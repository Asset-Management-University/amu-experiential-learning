Why this matters

You will have watched an audit programme quietly slip, one reasonable deferral at a time, until the audits that did happen were measured against an incomplete account of what actually binds the organisation. This course does not blame the people who deferred them — an internal audit genuinely is the easiest thing in any calendar to move. Instead it teaches you to give the audit programme standing within a real assurance structure that says who checks what; know what you are assuring against and keep that current; hold the schedule as a governed register so a deferral becomes a visible decision rather than a quiet one; plan and conduct an audit that tests whether the plant does what the documents say; and write findings that describe what the system does, never what a person did, so they can be acted on rather than closed. What changes for you is the ability to run assurance that finds out, rather than assurance that reassures.

Where this begins
The audit programme is a spreadsheet with dates on it, and the dates move. Three of last year's six audits happened; the other three were deferred by people who were not being obstructive, because the plant was busy and an internal audit is the easiest thing in any calendar to move. And when the three that survived were conducted, they audited against a folder of standards assembled in 2019 - so even the audits that happened were measuring the organisation against an incomplete account of what binds it.

What you will be able to do
By the end of this module you can give an audit programme standing and an assurance structure that says who checks what, know what you are actually assuring against and keep that current, govern the schedule so a deferral is a visible decision, plan and conduct an audit that finds out whether the plant does what the documents say, and write findings somebody can act on rather than close.
