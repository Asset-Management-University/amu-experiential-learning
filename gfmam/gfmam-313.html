GFMAM Landscape Subject 1.3
Why this matters

You will be asked what an asset is worth and get three correct, defensible, contradictory answers in the same week. This course does not resolve that by picking a winner. It teaches you to build a register that can actually carry a valuation; keep books that produce reportable expenditure and a unit cost that means something; speak about the same asset in capital and operating language without being argued out of a sound decision; write the financial policies that make finance, insurance and engineering numbers reconcile; appraise a decision over its whole life, including the cost of the ending nobody budgets for; and take a defended figure into a room with people who can say no, being honest about which parts you verified yourself and which you were told. What changes for you is that "what is it worth" stops being a contest of opinion and becomes a question you can answer on purpose.

Where this begins
Somebody asked what the pump station is worth and got three answers in the same week. Finance said one number, because that is what is left of it on the books. Insurance said another, because that is what it would cost to put back. The engineer said a third, because that is what it would cost to build something that does the same job today. All three are correct, all three come from a defensible method, and the person who asked now believes that nobody in the organisation knows what it owns.

What you will be able to do
By the end of this module you can build a register that can carry a valuation at all, keep books that produce reportable expenditure, cost a service so a price means something, speak about the same asset in capital and operating terms, write the interlocking financial policies that make the three numbers reconcile, appraise a decision over the whole life rather than the purchase, cost the end as well as the beginning, and take the result to a committee separating what you verified from what you were told.

Between problems

Between

Go back to the three numbers.

Finance is right: that is what remains of the original cost after years of depreciation, and it is the number the annual statements are built on. Insurance is right: that is what replacing it would cost, and it is the number that matters at two in the morning after a fire. The engineer is right: that is what an equivalent capability would cost to build today, and it is the number a renewal decision turns on.

Nobody has made an error. Three questions were asked and three questions were answered.

What is missing is a document saying which number this organisation uses for which decision, and why. Without it, every one of those three people is quietly assumed by the others to be wrong — and the person who asked walks away believing the organisation does not know what it owns.
