GFMAM Landscape Subject 3.2
Why this matters

You will meet a demand forecast that has been "last year plus two per cent" for nine years, quietly absorbing a housing development, a departing industrial customer, two tariff changes and a shifted rainfall pattern, because a line that grows by a fixed percentage can absorb anything without anyone noticing. This course teaches you to build a forecast somebody can actually argue with: work with more than one plausible future instead of a single line, state the service level in the words of the people receiving it, forecast what the assets themselves will realistically be able to deliver, and — at the end — manage demand rather than only meet it, which is usually cheaper than the capital a rising forecast implies. What changes for you is that your forecast stops being unfalsifiable.

Where this begins
The demand forecast is last year's consumption plus two per cent, and it has been last year's plus two per cent for nine years. In that time a housing development went in, an industrial customer left, the tariff structure changed twice and the rainfall pattern moved. The line on the chart absorbed all of it, because a line that grows by two per cent absorbs anything. Every capital decision in the last nine years has been sized against it.

What you will be able to do
By the end of this module you can build a demand forecast somebody can argue with, work with more than one future rather than a single line, state what the service level actually is in the words of the people receiving it, manage demand rather than only meeting it, and forecast what the assets themselves will be able to deliver.
