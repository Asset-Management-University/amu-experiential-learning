I can't tell if it's working - or dying

Movement 1 - The Felt Problem

You are Muhammad, 21. The test said yes, so you started for real. Tank You has been running a few weeks now - and it is quietly driving you mad.

Monday, three orders and a customer who calls you a lifesaver; you float home certain you have built something. Tuesday, silence - not one order - and you lie awake certain you are burning your family's savings on a dream that is already dying. Wednesday, a regular does not reorder and you cannot tell if that means anything at all. Every day swings you from genius to fool and back.

And the worst part is you cannot tell which feeling is true. Should you push harder - more flyers, more hours - or change something? And if so, what? You are pouring in effort and money on a gut that flips every twelve hours. You feel less like a founder and more like a leaf in the wind.

Ibraaheem finds you grim over the order book and asks how it is going. You say, honestly, "I have no idea. Some days great, some days nothing. I genuinely cannot tell if this is working or dying." And there it is - the real question:

How do I actually KNOW whether to keep going or change course - and if I change, what exactly do I change?

Movement 2 - Your Attempt

You stare at the order book - good days, bad days, all jumbled. You realise you have been grading the whole business on whatever happened in the last few hours. That cannot be right; one day is just noise.

Before you read on, work out what is actually missing. You have weeks of results in front of you, yet you still cannot read them. What two things would you have needed - set up beforehand - to turn this jumble of days into a clear answer?

I keep judging the whole thing by whatever happened today. A good day, I'm a genius; a quiet day, I'm a fool. But one day is just noise. What would let me read the WEEK instead of the mood?
Two things are missing. I never decided, up front, what 'working' would even look like - a number to aim at. And I count how many say no, but I never ask them WHY.
So: set a line in advance (by month-end, this many regular orders), and ask the people who didn't come back one honest question. Then the results stop being a feeling and start being a map.

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

Here is why you cannot read your own results: you are judging by mood, with no line and no way to listen. So every day reads two opposite ways at once - "it just needs time" and "it is dying" both fit the same facts. You will never settle it by feeling. You settle it by building two simple instruments - before you look.

  • The trouble: you're judging by mood: with no line and no way to listen, every result reads two ways at once - "give it time" and "it's dying" both fit the same day. So you spend effort and money on a feeling.
  • 1. Set a success line - in advance: a specific number or sign, decided BEFORE you look, that turns a result into a decision (e.g. 10 standing weekly orders by month-end). Not a grade - a trigger.
  • 2. Build a listening post: the yeses pay the bills; the NO's teach you. Ask the ones who did not come back one gentle question: "what would have made this worth it for you?"
  • 3. Read it, then change ONE thing: cleared the line? Hold course. Missed it? Change only the single thing the answers point at - not everything - then run it again with a fresh line.
  • The point: success is not hitting the line - it is running the loop. Hitting it says persevere; missing it says exactly where to learn. A "no" is data, not a verdict on you.

Notice the quiet revolution in step 2. Everyone counts how many said no. Almost nobody asks them why. But the people who did not come back are carrying the exact lesson you need - and most will tell you kindly, if you ask one honest question. Your yeses pay this month's bills; your noes build next month's business.

And hold on to this, because it is the part that frees you: the line is not a pass-or-fail grade on you. Its only job is to force a decision. Clear it, and you have proof to persevere. Miss it, and you have not failed - you have earned the right to ask exactly where to learn. Success is not hitting the line. Success is running the loop.

Movement 3.5 - The Guided Step

So you and Ibraaheem stop flying on feeling and build the two instruments.

How the brothers read the market on purpose

The line (set first): "By month-end, we want 10 households on a standing weekly order." A clear number, decided before, so the result becomes a decision instead of a mood.

The listening post: by month-end they have only 4. Old Muhammad would have quit, or grimly "given it more time". Instead they message every household that tried Tank You once and did not reorder: "Thank you for trying us. What is the one thing that would have made it worth keeping?"

What the noes taught them: people loved the service - but they never knew WHEN the next delivery was coming, so they topped up at the depot "just in case". The problem was not the price or the idea. It was one thing: unpredictability.

The one change: they fix a set day and area each week ("Thursdays in your street"), so customers can rely on it. They change that ONE thing - not the price, not the product - and set a fresh line for the next month.

That is the whole discipline: a line to read the result, a question to learn the reason, and the patience to change just one thing at a time. Panic changes everything at once and learns nothing. This changes one thing on purpose and learns every time.

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

The torture was never the bad days - it was not being able to read them. Now you can. You do not need more luck; you need two instruments and the nerve to listen.

For your own thing, once it is in front of real people:

  1. Set your line first. Before you look at results, decide the number that would mean "this is working" - by when, how many. A trigger, not a grade.
  2. Build your listening post. Find one cheap way to hear back - and above all, ask the people who say no the single question: "what would have made this worth it for you?"
  3. Read it, then change ONE thing. Cleared the line? Hold course. Missed it? Change only the one thing the answers point at, and run it again with a fresh line.
R

Write your line and your question down before you launch, and tell someone you trust. (Working with an AMU facilitator? Bring it there too.)

A "no" is not a door slammed in your face - it is a teacher who showed up for free. Persevere or pivot is no longer a torment you feel; it is a question your evidence answers. That is how you stop being a leaf in the wind and start being someone who learns on purpose.
