Movement 1 - The Felt Problem

You have come a long way with Sinegugu. She went out and sampled the world instead of waiting for it. She found, through the four corners, a direction she could love and do and sustain. She held to it when easier money called. She built the skill to be trusted, and she learned to charge fairly so the work could last. She is, at last, ready.

And standing at the edge of "ready", she feels the real question arrive - the one this whole journey has been walking toward. All of that was preparation. Now she actually has to do the thing: turn everything she has into a real service, that reaches real people, and genuinely eases something hard in their lives. And a quiet doubt comes with it: have I even chosen a real ache - a true struggle worth answering - or just something that suits me?

I feel the pull to serve, to build something that matters. But how do I find a real ache in the world - and know it is the right one for me? And how do I turn that into an actual, working service that people truly value?

There is a method for exactly this - as old as the first person who ever made a neighbour's life easier. And there is a true story that shows it working, start to finish: the story you are about to follow. Let us learn the method by meeting the two brothers who lived it.

Movement 2 - Your Attempt

Most people, asked to "find an opportunity", reach for one of two dead ends. They go grandiose - I will solve unemployment, I will fix the whole country - and freeze, because no one can lift that. Or they go inward - what do I want to sell? what could make me money? - and end up with a clever product nobody actually needs. Both miss the one place real opportunities live: in an ache in someone's ordinary day.

An opportunity is not a product you dream up. It is a struggle you notice and ease - the schlep, the worry, the wasted time, the small daily indignity. And almost every such struggle is one of five kinds of gap. Learn to see the five, and the world fills up with opportunities you were walking straight past:

  • QUALITY - it is not done well enough: A gap in how WELL the thing is done. (The only food nearby is cold and carelessly made.) The opportunity: serve it properly.
  • COST - it is more than people can afford: A gap in affordability. (A decent haircut costs more than a student can spare.) The opportunity: serve it for less - without a race to the bottom.
  • ACCESS - people cannot easily get it at all: A gap in reach. (The nearest gas depot is across a busy road - impossible for a frail gogo; the nearest library is a two-hour bus ride.) The opportunity: bring it within reach.
  • SPEED - it costs too much time and effort: A gap in time. (Certifying one form means a whole morning lost in a queue.) The opportunity: make it faster and simpler.
  • EXPERIENCE - the process itself is miserable: A gap in how it FEELS - its dignity and ease. (The clinic works, but the process is confusing, anxious, undignified.) The opportunity: make it feel human.

Tank You, you will see, did not fill the COST gap at all - it charged the very same price as the depot. It won completely on ACCESS, SPEED and EXPERIENCE. That is the quiet lesson hiding in the five: you do not always have to be cheaper - you can win by being dramatically better on a gap that matters more.

I keep trying to think up a clever 'business idea'. That is the wrong end. Look instead for an ACHE - a real struggle in a real person's day. Where do people around me sigh, complain, waste time, or quietly go without?
Run what I notice through five lenses: is the thing people struggle with too poor in QUALITY, too high in COST, too hard to ACCESS, too SLOW, or just a miserable EXPERIENCE? The gap names the opportunity.
The best ache to answer is often one I FEEL myself - because then I understand it from the inside, and I will not give up on it. An opportunity is simply a problem in someone's life that I can ease.

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

Meet Muhammad Cassim, 21, and his older brother Ibraaheem, 23, in the township of Nethmora View. Muhammad works the counter at their Uncle Yusuf's general-dealer shop. He is a people-person; Ibraaheem is the careful one with numbers. Neither set out to "start a business". They simply could not stop noticing one particular ache.

Like every home on their street, the brothers cooked on a 9kg gas cylinder - and they knew in their own arms the schlep of hauling it across the road to the depot to refill it, and the misery of it dying mid-supper. But Muhammad saw more than his own kitchen. Day after day, from behind Uncle Yusuf's counter, he watched the gas depot's customers across the road struggle with the same thing - the old man wrestling a cylinder onto a wheelbarrow, the mother who could not carry the tank and her baby both. He felt the ache himself, and he saw it everywhere. He did not see it because he was cleverer than anyone. He saw it because life had placed him at that counter, looking out - and he chose to truly look.

That noticing is where it began. Here is the method - the Ache-to-Service Sketch - worked exactly as the brothers lived it:

1. THE ACHE - what real struggle do people (and maybe you) keep facing?

Lugging a heavy 9kg gas cylinder to the depot across the road and back - and the dread of it running out mid-cooking. Muhammad and Ibraaheem felt it at home themselves; and from Uncle Yusuf's counter Muhammad watched the depot's customers struggle with it every single day. The ache was his own AND everyone's.

2. THE EASIER LIFE - what does life look like once the ache is gone?

Gas is simply there when you need it. No lugging, no trip across a busy road, no queue, no running out in the middle of supper. For a gogo with no car, it means never having to ask a favour or carry what she cannot lift. Cooking fuel becomes a thing you stop having to worry about.

3. THE SIMPLEST SERVICE - the most direct thing that delivers it

Bring the refill to the door, and take the empty away. That is the whole service. No shop, no fleet, no clever app to begin - just this: you carry the heavy thing, so they do not have to.

4. THE MEASURE - how you know you are serving well (money enters HERE)

Neighbours gladly pay the SAME R260 the depot charges - no extra for the convenience - because the help is real and they want the lads to keep going. The R260 retail minus the R205 trade cost = R55 a tank is not the goal; it is the measure that the value landed, and the fuel that lets the service keep existing.

5. THE MISSION CHECK - is this ache yours to answer, and a world you want?

Yes. Muhammad lived the ache, and cared most about the elderly neighbours who struggled hardest with it. It is a real, widespread, dignified need - not an insecurity invented to sell against. He would not lose heart on a grey, tired day. This ache is his to answer.

Read across those five and you have the whole founding move. Notice a real ache (the gas schlep). Picture the easier life (gas just there). Find the simplest service that delivers it (bring the refill to the door). Set the measure honestly (the same R260 - they win on service, not price - and the small spread is what lets the service live). And check it is truly yours to answer (it was - he lived it and cared). No grand plan. One real struggle, eased, for one real neighbour at a time.

The move: do not invent a product and hunt for buyers. Find an ache - ideally one you feel yourself - name the gap it lives in, and build the simplest service that eases it. The opportunity was never out in the clever distance. It was right where you already stand, waiting to be noticed.

Movement 3.5 - The Guided Step

This is the last stop in the founding journey, so let us tie the whole thread together - and answer the worry that has shadowed it the whole way.

"But where is the money in all this 'service' talk?"

Right where it should be: as the measure, not the master. Look again at box 4. The brothers did not start with "how do we make money?" - that is the question that builds things nobody wants. They started with an ache, and the money followed the service: the R55 a tank was not the goal, it was the proof that real value had landed, and the fuel that let them keep going and grow. This is the oldest truth in honest business: wealth follows service. And it joins to the unit just before this one - you learned to sustain yourself (the peach tree) SO THAT you would have the strength to serve. Here, now, is the serving. Sustain yourself so that you can serve; serve so well that you are sustained. The two are one circle. One warning guards it: ease real aches only. Never manufacture a fear or an insecurity just to sell the cure. A true founder lifts burdens off people - they do not invent new ones to profit from.

Sinegugu ran this same sketch on her phone-help service; Muhammad ran it on a gas cylinder. The aches were different; the method was identical. And that is the gift of it - it is yours now too, for whatever ache you were placed where you could notice.

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

This is where the whole founding journey comes to rest - and turns into something real. Take the ache you have been noticing, and run it through the same five-box sketch the brothers used. Be concrete; keep the service simple; let the money sit honestly in box 4, as the measure.
