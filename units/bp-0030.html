Step 1 of 5

I'm about to risk everything on a maybe

Movement 1 - The Felt Problem

You are Muhammad, 21. The gas idea has lit a fire in you that will not go out. You can see it - bringing full cylinders to people's doors, changing them over, taking the empties. People would love it. You burst into the back room where Ibraaheem is doing the books: "This is it. Let's start the gas business. We use our savings, buy a stack of cylinders, get going properly - this week."

Ibraaheem does not look up straight away. When he does, his face is careful. He puts down his pen and asks one quiet question:

"And what if no one buys?"

It stops you cold. Because that R10,000 the two of you have scraped together is not play money. It is the cushion. It is years of skipping things you wanted. It is the one shield your family has against a bad month - and you are proposing to spend most of it on a feeling that people will buy.

Your first reaction is heat - "You have to have faith, man!" But his question will not leave you alone. He is not wrong. And yet doing nothing, waiting forever until it is "safe," means the idea dies too. You are caught between two fears: losing everything, and losing the chance.

Is there a way to find out if this will really work - without having to be right the first time, and without betting everything we have?

Movement 2 - Your Attempt

You sit with Ibraaheem and his terrifying question. There seem to be only two roads, and both are bad. Go all in and pray. Or play it safe and never move.

But maybe there is a third road. What if you did not have to choose between "bet it all" and "do nothing"? What would it take to BUY THE ANSWER cheaply - to find out whether people will really pay, before you spend the shield?

Before you read on, try to design it yourself. If you wanted real proof - not a guess, not a hope - that neighbours will pay for this, what is the smallest thing you could actually do this week to find out?

Ibraaheem is not wrong - if I'm wrong, we lose the lot. But waiting forever is also losing. So the real question isn't 'should we risk it?' It's 'is there a way to find out if people will buy - WITHOUT betting everything first?'
What if I didn't buy ANY cylinders? What if I borrowed the bakkie, collected a few neighbours' OWN empty tanks with the cash up front, refilled them this Saturday - and watched whether they actually pay? Almost nothing at risk, a real answer out.
Asking 'would you buy?' gets a free, kind yes that means nothing. Asking 'will you pay R260 on Saturday?' costs them something - and only THAT answer is the truth. Test what people DO, not what they say.

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

The third road has a name: you test it small. You do not bet the shield, and you do not freeze. You risk a little to learn a lot - and you learn from what people DO, not what they say.

  • Trap 1 - the All-In Leap: bet the whole R10,000 shield on your belief. Be wrong, and you lose everything you and Ibraaheem saved.
  • Trap 2 - the Freeze: too scared to lose, so you never start - and the chance slips away while you wait for a certainty that never comes.
  • The move - a small, cheap, REAL test: risk a little to learn a lot. Borrow, do not buy; serve a few real customers at the real price; watch what they actually DO.
  • Opinions vs behaviour: "Would you buy?" is a free yes - worthless. "Will you pay R260 on Saturday, cash?" costs them something - and that answer is the truth. People vote with their wallets, not their words.
  • A "No" is not failure: it is cheap market feedback you bought for almost nothing - a signal, not a verdict. It just saved you from betting the whole shield on a losing hand.

This is the whole secret, and it is worth saying plainly: people vote with their wallets, not their words. A neighbour who says "lovely idea, my boy!" has told you nothing - saying yes was free and kind. A neighbour who hands you R260 cash up front for a Saturday delivery has told you everything. One is an opinion. The other is the truth.

And here is the part that frees you: you are not trying to SUCCEED on the test. You are trying to get a clear ANSWER, fast and cheap. A yes says "go." A no is not a death - it is feedback you bought for almost nothing instead of risking the whole shield. Either way, you walk away knowing something real.

Movement 3.5 - The Guided Step

So you and Ibraaheem design the smallest real test you can. Not 20 cylinders and flyers - just enough to get a true answer.

The test the brothers actually run

Borrow, don't buy: the parents' bakkie for one Saturday - no vehicle to pay for. No stock to buy: they collect the neighbours' OWN empty cylinders to refill - nothing of their own to purchase. Real money up front: go door to door first and take real orders - "I'll bring your gas back full this Saturday, R260 - the same as the depot - cash when I take your empty. Shall I put you down?" A clear signal, decided up front: if at least, say, 8 of the 10 they ask actually pay and hand over a tank, that is a YES worth building on.

Total at risk: almost nothing - no stock bought, the bakkie borrowed, and the refills paid for by the customers' own cash up front. The answer is real because real money changed hands - yet a "no" would have cost them next to nothing.

What makes a test trustworthy: (1) it costs the customer something, so their yes is honest; (2) it is small enough that being wrong does not sink you; (3) it is fast; and (4) you decide BEFORE you start what result counts as a yes - so your hope cannot rewrite the score afterwards.

(In Muhammad and Ibraaheem's case, the neighbours said yes - with their wallets. But notice: they would have been just as glad to learn a no this cheaply, before risking the shield.)

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

You came in with a fire and a fear - and a shield you could not afford to lose. The move you found protects all three: you do not have to bet everything to find out if your idea is real. You buy the answer cheaply first.

For your own opportunity (the one you spotted last time):

  1. Design the smallest real test. What is the least you could do this week to get real people to ACT - to pay, commit, or put their name down - not just nod and say "nice idea"?
  2. Make it cost them something. A yes only counts if giving it costs the other person money, time, or a real commitment. That is how you tell truth from politeness.
  3. Decide your signal first. Before you start, write down what result would mean "go" and what would mean "not yet." Then let the test - not your hope - give the verdict.
R

When you have designed your test, say it out loud to someone you trust - and then go run it. (Working with an AMU facilitator? Bring it there too.)

Remember: a "no" found cheaply is a gift, not a defeat. The entrepreneur who tests small never bets the shield on a maybe - and so is still standing, and still learning, long after the all-or-nothing gambler has lost everything.
