How much can I actually pay myself - without starving the business or me?

Movement 1 - The Felt Problem

You are Muhammad, and Tank You, your gas-delivery business, is starting to work - money is coming in. But your own money is chaos. One week the rent's due and you're short; you look at the Tank You account, fat with recent sales, and grab what you need. It feels like stealing from your own future. A few days later the gas supplier tries to take their payment and it bounces - you grabbed the working capital.

Guilty and scared, you swing the other way: for the next month you take nothing, telling yourself you're being disciplined. But you're eating two-minute noodles for supper and borrowing airtime from a friend - while the Tank You account sits fat with cash you're now too frightened to touch. You never know what you're allowed to take.

How much can I actually pay myself - enough to live, but without starving the business or getting ambushed by a bill I forgot was coming?

Movement 2 - Your Attempt

Your two attempts are the two sides of one broken coin. The grab: when personal pressure builds, you reach into the business account like it's your wallet - which quietly drains the venture's ability to operate (you spend next week's gas money, or the money the tax man will later demand). The starve: to punish yourself for grabbing, you take nothing and try to be the noble founder who eats last - but you're not separate from the business; if you starve, you lose energy, burn out, and make desperate decisions, while the account holds the very fuel you need.

Both come from the same two mistakes: there's no planned, written number for your pay, and you see your pocket and the business's account as one blurry thing. So: what would have to exist for you to pay yourself a fair amount AND keep the business safe?

My own money is chaos - some months I grab from the Tank You account (then the gas order bounces), other months I take nothing and eat noodles while the account sits fat. I've no planned figure, and my pocket and the business account are one blurry thing.
What if I treated me and the business as two separate parties - separate accounts - and paid the business's promises (costs + a tax set-aside + a cushion) FIRST, then paid myself a planned amount from what's left?
Two pots; pay the promises + a tax set-aside + a cushion first; size it on a NORMAL (not best) month; set ONE fair, sustainable wage and pay yourself it like a bill; take extra only as a deliberate bonus. Be a budgeted line, not a hand in the till.

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

The clarity comes when Ibraaheem sits you down with the books: "Muhammad, you and Tank You are not the same. You're the owner; it's the venture. Treat them as two separate parties." The goal is to make your pay a planned, predictable, budgeted line - not a hand reaching into the till. That gives dignity to you and stability to the business. Five moves:

  • Separate the two pots - forever: open a personal account that is only yours; the Tank You account is only the business's. Money moves between them only as a deliberate transaction - you never buy groceries with the business card again. You and the venture are two separate parties; until the pots are separate, you can't even see clearly what you're doing.
  • Pay the business's promises FIRST: before you think about your own pay, set aside what the business has already promised others, from a typical month's income: committed COSTS (gas, the driver, fuel, maintenance); a TAX SET-ASIDE (a slice moved to a separate pocket - it's not yours, it's the tax that's coming, so the bill never ambushes you); and a small SAFETY CUSHION for a slow week. These come out before you do.
  • Find what a NORMAL month can afford: don't size your pay on your best-ever month - hope is not a plan. Look at the last few months and take the LOWEST, not the best. After the promises come out of that, what's left is the real, sustainable pool your wage can come from. Size to the quiet months and you'll never have to claw it back.
  • Set ONE fair, sustainable wage - and pay it like a bill: from that pool, set a single fixed amount and pay yourself it on the same day every month, into your personal account, like any other bill. It must be FAIR - a real amount you can live on - and sustainable - one the business can pay month after month without getting hurt. (Muhammad's was R3,500: less than his old R4,500 wage, but real, planned, and dignified.)
  • In a great month, take a deliberate bonus: a fantastic month doesn't mean grab more. Stick to the plan: pay the promises, pay your set wage, then look at what's genuinely left over - and make a DELIBERATE choice to take a named 'bonus', leaving some to grow the cushion. Upside by decision, never by impulse - so the system stays intact.

The one place this breaks is step three, so guard it: when you size your wage, Ibraaheem makes you look at the last three months and ignore the best one - "hope is not a business plan." Size your pay to a quiet month and you'll never have to claw it back in a lean one. That's the whole secret - you stop being a hand reaching in, and become a budgeted line the business is strong enough, and proud, to pay.

Movement 3.5 - The Guided Step

This rescues anyone whose income arrives in lumps - watch it smooth out a freelancer's feast-and-famine.

See it steady a freelancer's lumpy income

You're a freelance designer - no steady salary. One month a big client pays you R50,000; the next two months you earn almost nothing. The old way: feel rich when the R50,000 lands (and spend it), then starve for two months - the same grab-and-starve. The new way:

• You have a business account and a personal account. The R50,000 lands in the business account.

• From it you immediately set aside the business's promises: software subscriptions (costs), a slice for tax (the set-aside), and a bit for your safety cushion.

• You've already worked out you need a fair, sustainable R15,000 a month to live.

• You pay yourself just R15,000 into your personal account - and leave the rest in the business account as buffer.

• Next month, when you earn nothing, you still pay yourself R15,000 - from that buffer.

You've turned lumpy, unpredictable income into a smooth, predictable personal salary. No longer a hand reaching in - a budgeted, paid employee of your own venture.

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

If you run anything that earns - a small business, a side-hustle, a freelance career - stop blurring the lines. Your venture and your personal life are two separate parties that must respect each other:

  1. Separate the pots. A bank account that's only for the venture - today.
  2. Pay the promises first. Each time money comes in, set aside what you owe for costs, for tax, and a small safety cushion - before anything else.
  3. Set a fair wage from a NORMAL month, and pay it like a bill. Size it on what the venture affords in a quiet or average month (not its best); pick one fixed, sustainable, fair amount; pay it to yourself on the same day each month. Then take extra only as a deliberate, named bonus.

This week, open (or name) the separate pot and work out your one fair number. (Working with an AMU facilitator? Size your wage together - a second pair of eyes guards against the optimistic month.)

You were never a thief for needing to be paid, and never noble for going without - you were just flying without a number. A planned, fair wage is not the scraps the business leaves you; it is a real, dignified cost the business exists to cover. Set it well, and you get the two things chaos stole: a life you can plan, and a business strong enough to keep paying for it.
