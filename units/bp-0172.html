One surprise, and I'm wiped out

Movement 1 - The Felt Problem

You are Muhammad, 21. Things are looking up, a little. You can see your money clearly now, you know your whole income rests on one thread, and you have promised yourself you will build a second one. For the first time in a while, you have a plan.

Then Tuesday happens. You are at the taxi rank before dawn, same as always - but the rank is dead quiet. A strike; no taxis on your route today. Your coworkers are already squeezing into a ride-hailing car, splitting the fare. "Get in, we'll sort it," one says. The ride is R150 you do not have. So you can climb in and promise to pay them back on Friday - feeling like a child as you say it - or you can be late, and the supermarket is not the only place that lets people go in a slow month.

You climb in. You pay them back on Friday, and that R150 leaves a hole you feel all week.

And here is what stings: you did nothing wrong. You were not careless. The world simply threw one small, ordinary problem at you - and you had no answer for it. One R150 surprise, and your whole careful plan wobbled.

That night you say it to Ibraaheem, half angry and half ashamed:

How are we meant to build anything, when one taxi strike can wipe us out? Other people just pay and move on. What do they have that we do not?

Movement 2 - Your Attempt

Ibraaheem does not answer straight away. He just asks one thing back: "So what would it have taken, this morning, for that R150 to be nothing?"

You sit with it. Not a bigger wage - you would have spent that by now too. Not "being more careful" - you were careful. It is something else. Something that would have been there already, before the trouble came.

Before you read on, try to name it yourself. What is it that lets some people take a R150 hit and just carry on - and what, exactly, would you have needed sitting at that rank this morning?

What would it have taken, at the rank this morning, to not feel that? Not more wages - I'd have spent those by now too. Something different. Something already there, waiting, before the trouble came.
The people who just pay and move on aren't richer. They have a little money set aside that they don't touch - kept for exactly this kind of bad-luck day.
So the answer isn't to earn more or be more careful. It's to put a little aside FIRST - on purpose, kept separate - so that when the next surprise comes, there's something waiting to catch it.

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

So what is the secret? It is almost annoyingly simple. The people who shrug off a R150 surprise are usually not richer or luckier than you. They just have a little money set aside before the surprise comes - a cushion.

  • What a cushion is: a small pot of money set aside BEFORE trouble comes, kept only for emergencies - for the day life shoves you, not for a want.
  • Pay it FIRST: the moment money reaches you, a little goes to the cushion before anything else. "Whatever is left at month-end" is always nothing.
  • Keep it SEPARATE: a different envelope, jar or account - somewhere hard to dip into on a whim.
  • Start absurdly small: even R100 a month, or R25 every Friday. The habit matters far more than the amount.
  • Aim for a first target: about R500 - enough to absorb a taxi strike, a small clinic fee, a broken tool. Not three months of savings - a starter cushion.

That is the whole trick. A cushion does not stop bad days - the taxis will still strike, the tooth will still ache. What it does is stop a small bad day from becoming a disaster that knocks over everything you are trying to build.

And hear this clearly, because it matters: starting a cushion is not "saving because you are poor." It is the opposite. It is what a strategist does - someone who has looked ahead, seen that surprises are certain, and quietly prepared for them. You are not a victim waiting for the next blow. You are an operator getting ready for a problem you know is coming.

Movement 3.5 - The Guided Step

The hard part is not starting a cushion. It is not raiding it. The day you have R400 in that envelope, the sale sneakers will whisper, or a friend's party will come up. So you and Ibraaheem agree on a simple gate before a single rand comes out.

The one test: is this really an emergency?

Before you touch the cushion, you must be able to say YES to one of these:

1. If I do not pay this right now, will I lose my job or my ability to earn? (getting to work, fixing a tool you work with)
2. Is this a real health or safety need, for me or someone who depends on me? (the clinic, medicine)

If it is neither, it is not an emergency - however much you want it.

This is not about never enjoying your money, and it is not about turning your back on people. Sale sneakers, a night out, a gift for someone you love - those are real and good, but they come from your everyday spending, never from the cushion. Helping family is one of the finest things money does - but it is a different job, with its own plan and its own money. The cushion has only ONE job: to keep you on your feet so you can keep earning. And someone who stays on their feet can carry their people for years - while someone who raids their cushion is knocked flat by the next bad Tuesday, and can then help no one. So the cushion is the one pot you never raid, so that it is actually there the morning the taxis stop.

And when you do use it for a true emergency - good, that is exactly what it was for. You simply start filling it again, the same way: a little, first, every time money comes in.

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

Your R150 morning was not bad luck you should be ashamed of - it was a certainty you had not prepared for yet. The fix is not to earn more or worry more. It is to build a small wall around your life, before the wind comes.

For your own life:

  1. Give it a name and a home. An envelope, a jar, a separate account - somewhere out of daily sight. Write the name on it. A cushion you can see every day is a cushion you will spend.
  2. Pay it first - and start tiny. Decide one small amount that goes in the moment money reaches you, before anything else. Even R25 a week builds the muscle. The amount can grow; the habit is the win.
  3. Guard it with the one test. Only a true emergency opens it (will I lose my ability to earn? is it real health or safety?). Everything else waits.
R

When you have named your cushion and decided what goes in first, say it out loud to someone you trust. (Working with an AMU facilitator? Bring it there too.)

The first time a surprise hits and you simply reach into your cushion and carry on - that is the day you stop being at the mercy of every bad Tuesday. That feeling is worth far more than the R500.
