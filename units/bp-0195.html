Two friends invested the same money in the same fund for the same years - so how did one end up with tens of thousands of rand more than the other?

Movement 1 - The Felt Problem

You are Tsholofelo, and you are hearing the story of two family friends, Tlotlo and Gontse. Twenty years ago, the two of them started their first jobs in the same week, and made the same good decision: each would invest a small, identical amount every month into the exact same low-cost index fund - the boring, cheap kind that just buys the whole market. They both stuck with it, untouched, for two decades. Same money in. Same fund. Same risk. Same years.

Today, they pull up their statements side by side. Tlotlo is genuinely pleased with how his has grown. But Gontse's final balance is tens of thousands of rand higher than his - so much more that Tlotlo keeps checking the numbers, sure he has misread.

He has not. They did the identical thing, and yet Gontse kept far more. And as you listen, you learn the one thing that was different between them: not the fund, not the amount, not the timing - the TYPE OF ACCOUNT each one used to hold it. For twenty years, in Tlotlo's ordinary account, a quiet slice of his growth and his dividends was shaved off for tax, year after year. In Gontse's account, nothing was. She kept every rand. It lands on you as something almost unfair - two people, the same effort, the same risk, and one is far poorer for a reason he never even saw.

They did the exact same thing - same money, same fund, same risk, same years. So how did one of them end up with tens of thousands of rand more than the other? Where did the other person's money quietly go?

Movement 2 - Your Attempt

Trying to make sense of the gap, three thoughts tempt you - and each one is exactly what kept Tlotlo poorer. "Surely a normal account is fine - the account doesn't really matter." But the account is the whole difference here: it is what stands between keeping all your growth and handing a steady slice of it to tax for decades. "A 'tax-free' account sounds too good to be true - probably a scam." But it is real, and government-made on purpose - an account designed to encourage ordinary people to save; using it is exactly what it is FOR. "I'll sort out a fancy tax-free account later, once I actually have money to invest." But the tax-free growth compounds over decades, so the early years you skip are the most valuable ones you will ever lose. The difference was never the investment - it was the wrapper around it.

So before you read on: the two friends held the identical fund - so what could the ACCOUNT have done, quietly, over twenty years, to leave one of them with so much less?

Stay with what is NOT different here. Same money in, same fund, same risk, same years. So the gap cannot come from the investment itself - it was identical. The only thing that differed was the TYPE OF ACCOUNT each one used. So what could an account do, quietly, year after year, that would leave one person with far less than the other?
Think about who takes a slice along the way. In an ordinary investment account, when your money grows and pays dividends, the taxman takes a cut - of the growth (capital gains tax) and of the dividends. What if there were a special account where he took NOTHING - not on the growth, not on the dividends, not even when you take the money out?
There is exactly such an account: a Tax-Free Savings Account (TFSA). It is a 'wrapper' you hold your investments inside - your same low-cost index fund - and inside it, ALL the growth, dividends, and withdrawals are 100% tax-free, forever (in the 2026/27 tax year you can put in up to R46,000, towards a R500,000 lifetime total - SARS sets these limits and does change them, so check the current ones before you contribute). Outside it, tax quietly takes a slice for decades. The rule: for long-term investing, fill your TFSA FIRST - and start young, because the tax-free growth compounds the most over time.

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

The fund was identical. The account is where the money quietly leaked. Five moves:

  • The account is the arena: The first shock is that the ACCOUNT you invest through - not just what you invest in - decides how much of your money you actually get to keep. Two people can buy the identical fund and take the identical risk, and walk away with very different amounts, purely because of the account that held it. The investment was the same; the arena it played in was not.
  • The taxman's quiet slice: In an ordinary investment account, the taxman takes a slice as you go. When your investments grow and you eventually sell, he takes a cut of the growth (called capital gains tax - up to about 18% of your gain). And along the way, he takes a cut of your dividends too (a 20% tax). None of it feels dramatic in any one year - but over decades, those quiet slices add up to a large pile of your money, gone to tax.
  • The tax-free wrapper: Now the fix: a special account called a Tax-Free Savings Account acts as a WRAPPER around your investments. You hold the SAME things inside it - your low-cost index fund - but inside this wrapper, ALL of it is tax-free: the growth, the dividends, and every rand you withdraw, forever. The taxman simply does not get a slice. In the 2026/27 tax year you can contribute up to R46,000, towards a R500,000 lifetime total. SARS sets these limits and they do change - the annual one rose from R36,000 in March 2026 - so check the current figures with SARS before you contribute, because going over the limit is penalised.
  • The power of tax-free compounding: Here is why it matters so much: that tax-free growth compounds, year on year, for decades. It is not a one-off saving - it is a slice the taxman never takes, growing on a slice he never takes, again and again. For the exact same money and the exact same risk, you can end up tens of thousands of rand richer - sometimes far more - simply because your investment grew inside the wrapper.
  • Name it - and fill it FIRST: This is your TFSA, and the rule is simple: for your long-term investing, fill your TFSA first, hold your low-cost index fund inside it, and start as young as you can - because the early tax-free years are the ones that compound the most. (Two notes: keep your emergency fund separate and reachable - this wrapper is for long-term growth, not emergencies; and the RA, the other big tax tool, comes next.)

The whole thing lives in one line: the account you invest through decides how much you keep - and a Tax-Free Savings Account lets you keep ALL of your growth, dividends and withdrawals, for ever, while an ordinary account quietly hands a slice to tax for decades. Tlotlo did nothing wrong; he simply never knew that the same fund, held inside a tax-free wrapper, would have kept tens of thousands of rand more in his pocket - money lost not to a bad choice or bad luck, but to the wrong account. These rules are not taught in school, which is exactly why knowing them is your quiet advantage. For your long-term investing, fill your TFSA first, hold your low-cost index fund inside it, and start young - and the taxman never gets to take a single slice of the freedom you are building.

Movement 3.5 - The Guided Step

Before you take this into your own life, see the size of the taxman's quiet slice, in plain numbers.

See two investors put the same R40,000 to work for 30 years

Two people each invest a single R40,000 into the exact same low-cost index fund, and leave it for 30 years. The fund grows at an illustrative 9% a year. In both accounts, the R40,000 grows to about R530,700 - a gain of about R490,700. So far, identical.

Person A used an ordinary account. When they finally sell, they owe capital gains tax - up to about 18% of their R490,700 gain, which is roughly R88,300 (and they have paid tax on dividends along the way too). They walk away with about R442,000.

Person B used a TFSA. Same R530,700. But inside the tax-free wrapper, they owe the taxman exactly R0. They keep the full R530,700.

Same money, same fund, same risk, same 30 years - and Person B keeps over R88,000 more, purely because of the account they chose on day one. (These are illustrative figures - tax rates and returns vary - but the quiet, steady power of the wrapper is very real.)

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

Now make sure the taxman never quietly takes a slice of your freedom:

  1. Separate your long-term money. This wrapper is for money you are investing for many years out, not your emergency fund (keep that safe and reachable, as before). Decide which of your future money is truly long-term - the money meant to grow for a decade or more.
  2. Plan to use a TFSA first - and hold your index fund inside it. For that long-term money, the TFSA is the first home to fill: a tax-free wrapper you hold your low-cost index fund inside, up to R46,000 in the 2026/27 tax year, toward a R500,000 lifetime total - always check the current limit with SARS, because it changes. Look into how you would actually open one (most banks and investment platforms offer them).
  3. Do it young, even if it is small. Because the tax-free growth compounds over decades, the early years are worth the most. A small amount started in a TFSA now beats a big amount started in one later - the same lesson as the snowball, made tax-free.
  4. Remember what the wrapper does - and does not - do. A TFSA removes the TAX, not the RISK. The index fund inside it still rises and falls with the market, so all your earlier rules still apply: spread wide, keep fees low, and stay the course. The wrapper just makes sure that whatever you do grow, you keep all of.
