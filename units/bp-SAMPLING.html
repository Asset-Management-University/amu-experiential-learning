Everyone says find an opportunity - but I look around and see nothing

Movement 1 - The Felt Problem

You are Sinegugu, 21. You finished school a year ago. At first you were full of plans - you sent your CV everywhere, walked into every shop on the high street, asked everyone you knew. Nothing. The CVs went quiet. The walks got shorter. These days you mostly stay home: the phone, the television, the long flat afternoons, waiting for something to come up.

Everyone keeps telling you the same thing. "Stop waiting for a job - find an opportunity. Start something." Your aunt says it, the pastor says it, the motivational videos say it. And every time, you look around your room, your street, your life - and you see nothing. No opportunity. Nothing to start. It feels like the opportunities are real, but they are somewhere else, for other people - the lucky ones, the connected ones, the clever ones.

And under that is the part you do not say out loud: a quiet, cold worry that maybe the empty feeling is not about your street at all. Maybe it is about you.

Everyone says "go find an opportunity" - but I look around and see nothing. How do I find one when there is nothing here, and I cannot just sit at home and wait for it?

You already half-know that waiting is not working. You just do not yet know what to do instead.

Movement 2 - Your Attempt

So you try harder at the only thing you know how to do: you sit and you think. You scroll for ideas. You wait for the right one to land - a clean, finished business idea that feels safe and certain. You ask yourself the big question everyone asks: what is my passion? And nothing comes. Every idea feels borrowed, or silly, or already done by someone else. The harder you think, the more stuck you feel.

Before you go further, do the simplest honest thing. Do not list ideas - list your week. Where do you actually go? Who do you actually talk to? What of the world do you actually see?

Where I actually go in a week
home to the spaza on the corner and back - most days I do not leave my street
People I really speak to
my two school friends and my mother - the same few faces, every day
New places, people or work I tried this month
none - I have been waiting for an opportunity to come up

Look at it. You were ready to blame your street for having no opportunities. But look how little of your street - of anyone's life - you actually touch in a week.

How am I going to spot an opportunity in the world, when this small a piece of the world is all I ever see?

Stop trying to INVENT an opportunity in my head - I cannot. Ask a different question: in a normal week, how much of the world do I actually SEE? How many new people, new places, new problems cross my path?
Look at my week honestly. Home, the corner shop, the same two friends, my phone. That is almost the whole of it. I have been waiting for an opportunity to find me - inside a world I barely leave.
An opportunity is just a problem I can see in someone's life. I can only see the problems I am EXPOSED to. So the fix is not to think harder - it is to go and stand somewhere new, and to look harder where I already stand.

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

So you looked. Here is what the page was quietly telling you.

  • My whole world, this week: three places, a handful of people, nothing new tried. You cannot spot an opportunity in a world you never leave - not because you are not clever, but because you have not yet seen enough of other people's lives.
  • My first small samples (cost: almost nothing): help cook at the community kitchen one morning; ask the spaza owner what is hard about running the shop; go with my aunt to the clinic and really watch the queue. The goal is to SEE, not to earn.
  • What the sampling does: two things at once - it shows me the real problems people keep having (what the world needs), and it shows me what pulls me in versus what bores me (what I care about). Direction is the residue of doing, not the prize of thinking.

The thing you felt but could not name is this: your problem was never that there are no opportunities. It is that you have been trying to FIND one without going to LOOK. An opportunity is just a problem in someone's life that you could ease - and you can only see the problems you are close enough to notice. Sitting at home thinking, you were searching a room with the lights off.

And notice the trap your mind jumped to first: I must wait until I find my passion, my one true idea, before I act. But it works the other way around. You do not think your way to a direction and then go. You go, and the direction appears. Clarity is the residue of action, not the price of admission.

The move: stop waiting for an opportunity to arrive. Start a sampling period - deliberately widen what you see, in small, cheap ways, and pay real attention. You are not hunting for the perfect idea. You are increasing the surface area of your life, so that real problems - and your own interests - can finally come into view.

There are two ways to sample, and you need both. Widen where you stand - go to a place, a person, a kind of work you have never met. And notice where you already stand - look with fresh, hard attention at the street, the shop, the family you see every day, because the opportunities you can spot are shaped by where life has already placed you. (Most great ventures began not with a far-off idea, but with someone finally noticing what the people right next to them were struggling with.) The mindset that makes it work is simple: stay curious, keep at it, stay open to surprises, and be brave enough to act before you are certain.

Movement 3.5 - The Guided Step

Your friend frowns when you tell her the plan. "But what if you go help at the kitchen and you hate it? What if you talk to the spaza owner and nothing comes of it? That's a wasted week." It is the fear that keeps everyone on the couch. Here is why it is wrong.

Think it through - can a sample really "fail"?

No - not if you are paying attention. A sample is not a bet you win or lose; it is a question you ask the world about yourself. If you help at the kitchen and love it, that is a direction. If you help and are bored stiff, that is also gold - you have just crossed one path off the map and saved yourself years. The only true failure is to stop sampling and go back to waiting. So keep two eyes open as you go: what problems do people here keep having? (that is what the world needs) and what pulls me in, versus what drains me? (that is what I might be built for). Write both down. Five small samples and you will know more about your direction than five months of thinking ever gave you.

And here is what no one tells you. The moment Sinegugu starts going out to look, her own street changes in front of her. Waiting at the clinic with her aunt, she watches an old man jab helplessly at his phone and then give up, asking a stranger to read his messages aloud - his pension notice, his clinic dates, all locked behind a screen he cannot work. She sees a gogo who has not seen her grandchildren in a year because no one has shown her how a video call works. She used to look straight past them. Now she sees people who would gladly welcome a patient hand. And for the first time, she sees herself: a young woman with the time and the kindness to be that hand.

That is not luck. That is what attention does once you go looking. The opportunities were always there - in the clinic queue, the kitchen, the walk past the shop. You just had to put yourself where you could see them.

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

The empty feeling was not a lie - but it was not the truth either. It was the view from one small room with the lights off. The move you found is yours now: do not wait for an opportunity to arrive; go and widen what you can see, and look harder at what is already in front of you.

For your own life:

  1. Map where you actually stand. Where do you go, who do you talk to, what do you see - in a real week? Be honest about how small that world is. Most of us are standing in one little room and calling it "there are no opportunities".
  2. Plan three small samples. Three cheap, low-risk exposures this week - a conversation with someone who does something different, an hour helping somewhere, one new place to sit and watch and listen. The goal is to SEE, not to earn. None of it has to lead anywhere yet.
  3. Go and notice - on purpose. As you sample, hunt for two things and write them down: the problems people around you keep having, and the things that pull you in. That is the raw material of a direction.

When you have named your small world and one new exposure you could try this week, say it out loud to someone you trust, and then actually go. (Working with an AMU facilitator? Bring it there too.)

This is only the first step of the journey. Once you start sampling and noticing, you will have something you have never had before: real problems and real interests to work with. Next, you will learn how to turn what you find into a direction worth giving yourself to - one whose attention you can actually sustain.
