Movement 1 - The Felt Problem

You are Mosa. You have just sold a pair of trainers you had outgrown on an online marketplace. The sale was for R200 - a good result, and you are pleased. You open your account, ready to see the money land.

But the amount that arrives is not R200. It is R185.

You stare at it. R15 is just... gone. You dig into the transaction and find the line: 'Service fee: R15'. A 7.5% cut. When you tapped 'accept' on the sale, that fee was there in the fine print, but it felt almost invisible - a tiny number you barely registered. Seeing R185 instead of R200, though, is real, and it stings a little.

And then a more unsettling thought starts to form. That was a small fee, taken once, on a small amount. But what about the savings you are planning to build - the gap you keep, growing for years? What happens when a 'small' percentage is taken not once, but every single year, off your entire pot - for decades?

That R15 fee on R200 stung - and it was just once. So what does a 'tiny' yearly percentage quietly do to my savings over a whole lifetime?

Movement 2 - Your Attempt

The fee is designed to look small and boring so you wave it away - and three thoughts tempt you to do exactly that. "It's only 1% - that's basically nothing." But 1% is taken every single year, off your whole pot, and it compounds against you - over a lifetime that 'nothing' becomes hundreds of thousands of rand. "I'll just pick whatever product my bank or an advert pushes at me." But the most heavily advertised products are very often the high-fee ones - that big marketing budget is paid for by your fees. "Checking fees is too boring and complicated to bother with." But the boredom IS the camouflage; you do not need to understand the fine print, you need just one number - the yearly percentage. None of these protects you from the silent thief.

So before you read on: a fee that looks tiny on a R200 sale - why could the same kind of small percentage be devastating when it is charged every year on your growing savings, and what is the one thing you should check before you ever invest?

Remember the R15 fee on your R200 sale - it felt almost invisible until you saw R185 land instead of R200. Now imagine a small cut like that taken not once, but EVERY single year, off your ENTIRE pot of savings, for decades. Does 'small' still feel small?
A 1% yearly fee does not just take 1% of your final pot once. It takes a slice of your money every year - so there is less left to grow the next year, and the year after that. It is the snowball from the compound-interest unit, but the fee is a leak draining it, every year, compounding against you.
See two savers: same R500 a month in, same 8% growth, for 35 years. One pays a 0.5% yearly fee and ends with about R1,015,000. The other pays 1.5% and ends with about R800,000. A 1% difference - a number so small it is easy to wave away - silently stole about R215,000. The thief is the percentage, taken every year. The defence is one question: 'what % am I paying a year?'

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

The R15 stung because you could see it. The yearly fee is far worse precisely because you cannot. Five moves:

  • A small % is not small: A yearly fee is not a one-off cost like the R15 on your sale. It is a percentage taken from your ENTIRE pot of money, EVERY single year - whether your investments went up, down, or nowhere. The bigger your pot grows, the bigger the rand amount that small percentage quietly takes.
  • The snowball in reverse: Remember how your money can snowball, growth feeding on growth? A yearly fee snowballs against you the same way. Each year it takes a slice, so there is less left to grow the next year - and less the year after that. It is a steady leak in the bottom of your snowball, and over decades that leak drains a shocking amount.
  • See the scale of the theft: Two people save the exact same R500 a month and earn the exact same 8% growth for 35 years. The ONLY difference is the fee: 0.5% a year versus 1.5% a year. The low-fee saver ends with about R1,015,000; the high-fee saver with about R800,000. That 'tiny' 1% gap quietly stole about R215,000 - a fortune, taken in slices so small no one noticed.
  • Find the one number that matters: So before you put money into ANY savings or investment product, hunt down its yearly fee. It is always a percentage, and it hides under names like 'expense ratio', 'TER' (total expense ratio), or 'annual fee'. You do not need to understand all the fine print - you need that ONE number: the percentage you pay every year.
  • Name the thief, claim your power: That yearly percentage is a silent thief, and it is made small and boring on purpose so you will not look. But once you know to ask 'what percentage am I paying every year?', the power is yours. A low-cost option (often a fraction of a percent) against a high-fee one (1-2% or more) is the difference of hundreds of thousands over a life. You just have to look - and choose the low one.

The whole thing lives in one line: a 'tiny' yearly percentage is a silent thief - charged on your whole pot every year, it compounds against you and quietly steals a fortune over a lifetime, but you defeat it with one question before you invest: 'what percentage am I paying every year?' The fee is made small, boring, and buried on purpose, so missing it was never your failing. But now you know it is there, and you know the one number to hunt for. A fraction of a percent instead of one or two percent is the difference of hundreds of thousands of rand that stay yours. The thief only works in the dark - and you have just turned the light on.

Movement 3.5 - The Guided Step

Before you take this into your own life, see what that 'tiny' 1% really does when you let it run for a lifetime.

See two savers, identical in every way but one

Meet Boikanyo and Reitumetse. They are as identical as two savers can be:

They both start at 25. They both save the exact same R500 a month. They both stay invested for 35 years. They both earn the exact same 8% average growth a year (an illustrative rate, just to compare them fairly).

There is only ONE difference between them. Boikanyo chose a low-cost investment with a 0.5% yearly fee. Reitumetse chose one with a 1.5% yearly fee - just one percent more.

After 35 years, Boikanyo's pot has grown to about R1,015,000. Reitumetse's, on the very same money and the very same returns, has grown to about R800,000.

That one percent - a number so small Reitumetse never thought about it - quietly cost her about R215,000. She did not lose it in a crash or a bad bet. It was taken from her in slices so small, year after year, that she never once noticed. That is the silent thief, and the only thing that separated the two of them was the percentage they each agreed to pay.

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

Now take the one question into your own life - the power here is not becoming an expert, it is knowing where to look:

  1. Pick a real product to inspect. Think of an actual savings or investment product - one your bank offers, one you have seen advertised, or one an older sibling or parent uses. You are going to practise finding its hidden number.
  2. Hunt for the one number: the yearly fee. Find its fees (usually on a 'fact sheet' or fee page). You are looking for a single percentage - the yearly fee, sometimes called the 'expense ratio', 'TER', or 'annual fee'. Ignore everything else; just find that one number.
  3. Compare, and make it a habit. Is it high (over 1%) or low (under 0.5%)? Seeing it makes the choice obvious. From now on, for anything you ever put money into, ask the same question first: 'what percentage am I paying every year?' - and choose the low one.
