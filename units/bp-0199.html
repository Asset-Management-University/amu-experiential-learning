My business is finally making real profit - but a huge slice has gone to personal tax, starving the money I needed to grow it. Is there a smarter, legal way to keep more of it working?

Movement 1 - The Felt Problem

You are Lesego, and for the first time, your side-business is really flying. What started as a few jobs for friends has become a steady stream of real work - and this year, for the first time, it made proper profit. You already know exactly what you want to do with it: reinvest. Better equipment, a bit of marketing, maybe paying someone to help - you can SEE the next level, right there, close enough to touch.

Then your first proper tax assessment lands. And the excitement drains out of you in a single breath. Because you trade in your own name, that profit was piled on top of your other income and taxed at your personal rate - and the top slice was taxed hard. A huge chunk of the exact money you had earmarked for growth is simply... gone, paid over in tax. The fuel you were counting on to grow the business has been drained out of the tank before you could use a drop of it.

It feels almost like being punished for doing well. You did everything right - you worked, you saved, you reinvested wisely - and yet the better the business does, the bigger the bite. You start to wonder if you can ever really build this into something, if the fuel keeps getting taken before you can burn it.

I worked so hard, the business is finally flying - but the tax bill has just eaten the money I needed to grow it. It feels like I'm being punished for success. Is there a smarter, legal way to keep more of my own profit working in the business?

Movement 2 - Your Attempt

Staring at the number, three responses tempt you - and each one misreads the situation. "This is just the price of success - accept it and grind harder." But resigning yourself means your business grows far slower than it could, starved of fuel you did not actually have to give up - the tax could have been structured differently. "Companies pay less tax - so I'll register one and put EVERYTHING inside it, my investments too, to be clever." But rushing everything into a company is its own mistake; it is the right tool for one job and the wrong tool for another, and you do not yet know which is which. "Tax is too complicated - I'll just ignore it and hope." But putting your head in the sand is how a growing business quietly bleeds money it could have kept, year after year. The real answer is not 'pay more', 'company-everything', or 'ignore it' - it is to understand which tool fits which job.

So before you read on: you are taxed in your own name, at your personal rate. What if the business could be its own separate 'person' in the eyes of the law, taxed at its own flat, lower rate - and when would that help, and when might it actually HURT?

You are being taxed in your OWN name, at your personal rate - which climbs as high as 45% on the top slice of a good year. But a business does not have to be held in your own name. What if it could be its OWN separate 'person' in the eyes of the law, taxed at its own, flat, lower rate - so that far more of each year's profit stayed inside the business, ready to reinvest? What might that separate 'person' be called?
It is a registered COMPANY. A company pays a flat 27% tax on its profit, while you personally can pay up to 45% - so for every R100 of profit, a company keeps about R73 to plough back into growth, where you might keep only R55. BUT do not rush to put EVERYTHING inside it. A company is the right tool for ONE job (keeping trading profit to reinvest) and the wrong tool for another. Which job might it be WRONG for - think about an asset you would hold for years and then SELL?
There is no single 'best' structure - you match the tool to the job. A COMPANY is the right tool for RETAINING and reinvesting business profit (27% vs up to 45%, so R73 kept vs R55), because it is taxed only on profit - income after the expenses of earning it. But your OWN HANDS are the right tool for holding a long-term growth asset (like an ETF) you plan to SELL one day - because a company pays a higher capital gains tax (about 21.6%) than you would personally (about 18%). Right tool, right job - and only worth it once your profit is real and being reinvested. Get proper advice before you act.

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

Your older friend Tirelo, who has built a business before, sits down with you. "You're not being punished, Lesego," they say. "You're just holding the wrong tool for the job - let me show you the toolbox." Five moves:

  • There is no single 'best' - match the tool to the job: The first shift is to stop hunting for one magic structure that is always best. There isn't one. A company, and trading in your own name, are like different tools in a toolbox - each is brilliant for one job and clumsy for another. The skill is not 'find the best tool', it is 'match the right tool to the job in front of you'. Two jobs matter most for a young builder: keeping profit to reinvest, and holding an asset you will sell.
  • A COMPANY: the right tool for keeping profit to reinvest: For the job of growing a business, a registered company is a powerful tool. It is taxed at a flat 27% on its profit, while you personally can be taxed up to 45% on your top slice of income. So for every R100 of profit, a company keeps about R73 to plough straight back into growth - new tools, stock, people - where trading in your own name might leave you only about R55. Year after year, that gap is rocket fuel for a growing business.
  • A business is taxed on PROFIT - expenses first, then tax: Part of why a real, growing business keeps more is this: it is taxed on its PROFIT, not its income - that is, on what's left after the legitimate expenses of earning that income (materials, tools, data, transport to a job). Expenses first, then tax on what remains. Run honestly and reinvested, a business keeps more of its money working than money simply earned and taxed personally first.
  • Your OWN HANDS: the right tool for a growth asset you'll sell: Now the twist that stops this becoming 'always use a company'. For a different job - holding a long-term growth asset, like a share ETF you plan to hold for years and then SELL - the company is the WRONG tool. When that asset is finally sold, a company pays a higher capital gains tax (about 21.6%) than you would pay holding it personally (about 18%). So a growth asset you'll sell one day usually belongs in your own hands, not inside the company. Same toolbox, different job.
  • Know when it's worth it - and get advice: This power only switches on once your business profit is REAL, steady, and being RETAINED to reinvest - not all drawn out as your salary (if you take it all personally, you're taxed personally anyway). A company also adds admin, cost, and its own rules (like tax when you take money OUT). This is the map, not the territory: it shows you the shape of the landscape, but before you register anything or move an asset, get proper advice for your own situation. Right tool, right job, right time.

The whole thing lives in one line: there is no single best structure - you match the tool to the job; a company is the right tool for keeping business profit to reinvest, but the wrong tool for holding a growth asset you'll sell, and it is only worth it once your profit is real and reinvested. What stung was not that you succeeded - it was that you were using your own name, a fine tool for many things, for a job it was never built for: housing a growing, reinvesting business. A company is built for exactly that job, and it would have kept thousands more of your profit working each year. But Tirelo stopped you before you made the opposite mistake - sweeping your long-term investments inside it too, where they would one day be taxed MORE when sold. Right tool, right job. That is not a trick or a loophole; it is simply building wisely, so the fuel you earn actually reaches the engine you are trying to grow.

Movement 3.5 - The Guided Step

Before you take this into your own world, see the two jobs side by side, in plain Rand.

See the same money handled by the right tool, and the wrong one

Job 1 - keeping R100,000 of business profit to reinvest. Say your business makes R100,000 of profit this year that you want to plough back into growth.

In your OWN NAME, sitting on top of your other income, the top slice can be taxed at about 45% - roughly R45,000 gone - leaving about R55,000 to reinvest.
Inside a COMPANY, that profit is taxed at a flat 27% - about R27,000 - leaving about R73,000 to reinvest.
That is R18,000 more fuel for growth, every R100,000 of profit, year after year. For this job, the company is clearly the right tool.

Job 2 - selling a long-held growth asset. Now say you also have a share ETF you've held for years, and you finally sell it, making a R200,000 gain.

Held in your OWN HANDS, the capital gains tax works out to about 18% - roughly R36,000.
Held inside the COMPANY, it works out to about 21.6% - roughly R43,200.
Here the company costs you about R7,200 MORE. For THIS job, your own hands are the right tool - the very same company that was perfect for Job 1 is the wrong choice for Job 2.

Same toolbox, two different jobs, two different right answers. (Illustrative and simplified - real rates depend on your full situation, there is also tax when you take money out of a company, and rules change. Always get proper advice before you act.)

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

You cannot register a company today - and you should not rush to - but you can carry the toolbox-thinking into every step you build:

  1. Name the job before you choose the tool. Whenever money or an asset is involved, ask first: what is the JOB here - keeping trading profit to reinvest, or holding something to sell later? The job decides the tool, not the other way round. This one habit will serve you for the rest of your building life.
  2. Watch for the day your profit is real and reinvested. The company tool switches on when your business profit is steady AND you are ploughing it back in (not drawing it all as salary, which is taxed personally anyway). When that day comes, that is your signal to look seriously at a company structure.
  3. Keep your growth assets in their right home - and get advice before you act. Plan to hold a long-term growth asset you'll sell (your ETF) in your own hands, not inside a trading company. And before you register anything or move any asset, get proper advice for your exact situation - this lesson is the map, not the territory.

You were never being punished for success - you were simply handed your money without the toolbox to hold it wisely. Now you have it.
