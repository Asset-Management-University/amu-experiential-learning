Thirty-four true things and no decision

T1: The Ache

You are Onalenna. The next planning cycle needs an environmental scan, so you run the workshop properly - six flip charts, a good turnout, the right people in the room.

By lunchtime you have 34 factors. Environmental 11, Technological 8, Economic 7, Social 4, Legal 3, Political 1.

Every single one of them is true.

The document goes into the plan, and nobody opens it again. Not because the workshop failed - it did exactly what workshops do. But when you read it back, not one line says what the works will do differently, and you cannot tell which of the thirty-four matter.

You also notice something you cannot immediately explain. The Environmental column has eleven entries and the Political column has one. You are fairly sure that is not because politics affects a municipal water works eleven times less than rainfall does.

I have thirty-four true statements about the world and no decision anywhere in them. How do I run a scan that changes what we do, and how do I know whether a thin column means nothing is happening or that we are not looking?

Before you read on, decide. The scan is finished: thirty-four statements about the world outside the works. Every one is true. Not one says what the works will do differently, and you cannot tell which of the thirty-four matter. Then you notice something you cannot immediately explain: the Environmental column has eleven entries and the Political column has one. You are fairly sure that is not because politics affects a municipal water works eleven times less than rainfall does. Write down how you would run a scan that changes what you do — and how you would read that thin column — your own answer, not a shortlist to choose from.

Now compare — the five answers, and how to read a silence

• “Fill in the thin columns — get them to a similar number.” Manufactures entries to satisfy a layout. You would end up with balanced columns and no more idea which movements matter, having added true and useless items on purpose.

• “Score every entry for impact and likelihood.” Ranks thirty-four true statements against each other and still yields no action — a prioritised list of observations is a list of observations.

• “Take the top five to the management meeting.” Top five by what? And the meeting will do with them exactly what you are doing now, because nothing in an observation tells anybody what to change.

• “It is a scan, not a plan — the decisions come later.” The comfortable answer that guarantees the document is filed. Nothing downstream will retrieve the meaning that was never put in.

• “Assume the empty column means that area is stable.” This is the trap. A near-empty column may mean nothing is happening, or it may mean nobody looked — and from the list alone the two are indistinguishable, which is precisely why the silence is unreadable.

• Two corrections. First, effort should follow movement rather than be spread evenly. Drawing six equal columns before anybody has thought about anything quietly decides how the work gets spent — and what matters is not how much information you gather but the fit between where the effort went and where your environment is actually moving. Scanning hard where the world is stable buys nothing; scanning lightly where it is moving costs everything. So choose the emphasis deliberately and write the reason down — which is also what makes a thin column afterwards a decision you can defend rather than an accident you have to explain. And note the trap in the shape of the dimensions: one that is quiet for long stretches and then decisive — politics, at tariff decisions and election cycles — is exactly the one a routine scan learns to skim. Second, the test that turns observation into decision: every surviving entry must answer “so what would we do differently?” Anything that cannot is a true fact with no place in a document meant to change behaviour. Applied ruthlessly, that is what turns thirty-four accurate lines into a short consequential one.

T2: The Tool in Action

This is the environmental scan - where to look, the ceiling, the cut, the test that makes it a decision, and the audit of the scan itself.

1. Scan where the environment is actually moving - not evenly across six columns

MOST SCANS BEGIN BY DRAWING SIX EQUAL COLUMNS, AND THAT DECISION - MADE BEFORE ANYONE HAS THOUGHT ABOUT ANYTHING - QUIETLY SETS HOW THE EFFORT WILL BE SPENT. The research is blunt about what actually matters: THE DECISIVE FACTOR WAS NOT THE VOLUME OF SCANNED INFORMATION, BUT THE FIT BETWEEN SCANNING EMPHASIS AND ENVIRONMENTAL DYNAMISM. In other words, scanning hard where your world is stable buys you nothing, and scanning lightly where it is moving costs you everything. SO ASK FIRST, BEFORE THE WORKSHOP: WHICH OF THESE SIX DIMENSIONS IS MOVING FOR US, AND HOW FAST? For a municipal water works the ENVIRONMENTAL dimension moves constantly - rainfall, discharge standards, catchment condition. TECHNOLOGICAL moves steadily. POLITICAL moves in step changes at election cycles and in tariff decisions, which means it is quiet for long periods and then decisive - AND A DIMENSION THAT IS QUIET FOR LONG PERIODS IS EXACTLY THE ONE A ROUTINE SCAN LEARNS TO SKIM. Write your emphasis down as a deliberate choice with a reason attached, so that a thin column later is a decision you can defend rather than an accident you have to explain. THE SIX CATEGORIES ARE A SEARCH PATTERN, NOT A FILING SYSTEM - they exist to send you looking where you were not looking, and if you use them to sort what you already know, they will return exactly what you already knew.

2. Gather and categorise - with a ceiling, and from outside the room

NOW GATHER, AND PUT A CEILING ON IT FROM THE START, because the failure mode of this technique is well documented: PESTLE EXERCISES FREQUENTLY PRODUCE LONG, UNDIFFERENTIATED LISTS ACROSS ALL SIX DIMENSIONS, AND WITHOUT A MECHANISM FOR PRIORITISATION THE VOLUME OF INFORMATION OBSCURES CRITICAL SIGNALS. The practical rule is A MAXIMUM OF EIGHT TO TEN FACTORS PER DIMENSION at this stage. AND RECORD WHERE EACH FACTOR CAME FROM, which is the step almost nobody takes and the one that decides whether the scan is worth anything. A workshop populated entirely from the knowledge already in the room is not a scan of the environment - IT IS A SCAN OF THE ROOM. Real sources look like: the regulator's published forward programme, the municipality's integrated development plan, the catchment management agency's reports, supplier and industry bulletins, court and tribunal decisions affecting operators like you, and conversations with people who are NOT in your organisation. BEWARE THE PREDICTABLE DISTORTION: CONFIRMATION BIAS CAUSES TEAMS TO OVERLOOK EMERGING THREATS THAT CHALLENGE PREVAILING ORGANISATIONAL ASSUMPTIONS - so a factor that makes the room uncomfortable is worth more attention than one that produces nodding, and if nothing in your list contradicts something the organisation currently believes, you have almost certainly scanned only for confirmation.

3. Condense to what is material - scored against a named decision

NOW CUT, AND CUT HARD, BECAUSE THIS IS WHERE THE ANALYSIS IS EITHER MADE OR LOST. CONDENSE TO THE THREE TO FIVE MOST RELEVANT PER DIMENSION, and treat MORE THAN 30 FACTORS IN TOTAL as the point at which YOU ARE PRODUCING VOLUME, NOT INSIGHT. A BRIEF, WELL-SCORED ANALYSIS OF FIVE MATERIAL FACTORS IS WORTH MORE THAN AN EXHAUSTIVE INVENTORY OF FIFTY OBSERVATIONS WITHOUT PRIORITISATION - and the fifty-item version is not merely less useful, it is actively harmful, because it buries the four things that mattered somewhere in the middle of a document nobody finishes. SCORE ON TWO AXES. LIKELIHOOD, stated for a period - and state the period, because a factor near-certain over twenty years and one near-certain over two are different animals. And IMPACT AGAINST A NAMED DECISION - not impact in the abstract, but impact on a decision this organisation actually has in front of it: the renewal programme, the tariff submission, the abstraction licence renewal, the treatment upgrade. AT CORATHYL THE WORKSHOP PRODUCED 34 FACTORS - already past the line - distributed as ENVIRONMENTAL 11, TECHNOLOGICAL 8, ECONOMIC 7, SOCIAL 4, LEGAL 3, POLITICAL 1. Every one of them is true. Being true was never the test.

4. The because-of-this-we-will test - a factor that changes nothing has not earned its place

HERE IS THE STRUCTURAL WEAKNESS OF THE TECHNIQUE, AND THE DISCIPLINE THAT REPAIRS IT. THE FRAMEWORK DOES NOT PRESCRIBE ACTION - IT IDENTIFIES AND DESCRIBES EXTERNAL FORCES BUT DOES NOT GENERATE STRATEGIC RESPONSES, LEAVING A SUBSTANTIAL GAP BETWEEN ENVIRONMENTAL INSIGHT AND STRATEGIC DECISION. Most analyses identify the right factors and then simply stop, with no decision attached. THE FIX IS TO INSIST THAT EVERY PRIORITY FACTOR PRODUCES A 'BECAUSE OF THIS, WE WILL' SENTENCE WITH AN OWNER AND A DEADLINE. Not 'we will monitor', which is what a factor says when nobody wants to act on it. Something testable: 'because the discharge standard tightens in 2029, we will complete the tertiary treatment options study by March, owned by the process engineer.' APPLY IT AT CORATHYL AND SEVEN OF THE 34 FACTORS SURVIVE - 20.6 PER CENT. The other twenty-seven were all true, and not one of them changed anything the works was going to do. THAT IS NOT A FAILED WORKSHOP; IT IS A NORMAL ONE, and the test is what turns it into an analysis. AND NOTICE WHAT THE TEST DOES TO THE ROOM: it converts an argument about whether something is a political or an economic factor - which is a filing dispute and consumes a surprising amount of any workshop - into an argument about what the organisation should do, which is the argument worth having.

5. Check the scan itself - horizons, thin columns, and what you refused to see

THE LAST STEP IS TO AUDIT YOUR OWN ANALYSIS, AND THERE ARE THREE CHECKS. FIRST, TIME HORIZONS: MOST PESTLE ANALYSES MIX THEM, placing a factor that bites next year beside one that bites in fifteen years as though they demanded the same response. Separate them, because they go to different plans and different people. SECOND, INTERROGATE EVERY THIN COLUMN, AND DO IT WITHOUT A PRESUMPTION EITHER WAY. A thin column has exactly two explanations: THAT DIMENSION IS GENUINELY STABLE FOR THIS ORGANISATION, or IT IS THE ONE NOBODY READS. Those look identical on the page and they are opposite in consequence, so ask the question explicitly and write the answer down. AT CORATHYL THE PATTERN IS WORTH LOOKING AT HONESTLY. The three well-populated dimensions took 26 OF 34 ENTRIES - 76.5 PER CENT OF THE EFFORT - and yielded 5 OF THE 7 MATERIAL FACTORS, 71.4 PER CENT OF THE VALUE: A RETURN ALMOST EXACTLY IN LINE WITH THE EFFORT SPENT. The three thin dimensions took 8 ENTRIES - 23.5 PER CENT - and yielded 2, WHICH IS 28.6 PER CENT. BE PRECISE ABOUT WHAT THAT DOES AND DOES NOT SHOW. The single political entry scoring one out of one is a statistical curiosity and proves nothing on its own. The defensible finding is the modest DISPROPORTIONALITY: the overlooked categories returned slightly more than the attention they were given, while the crowded ones returned exactly what was paid for. THE LESSON IS NOT THAT THIN COLUMNS ARE ALWAYS RIGHT - IT IS THAT THEY MUST ALWAYS BE INTERROGATED. THIRD, ASK WHAT IN THIS ANALYSIS CHALLENGES SOMETHING THE ORGANISATION CURRENTLY BELIEVES. If the answer is nothing, the scan has confirmed rather than scanned, and it is the one output of this technique that is worse than having done nothing - because it carries the authority of a method.

T3: The Blueprint

A PESTLE analysis earns its keep not by listing every external force, but by forcing a decision on the few that truly matter. Five questions:

  • Where is my world actually moving? Fit the emphasis to the dynamism. Six equal columns is a filing habit. The categories are a search pattern, not a filing system.
  • Where did this come from? A ceiling of eight to ten per dimension, and a source for each. A scan populated only from the room is a scan of the room.
  • Which of these are material? Three to five per dimension, thirty as the outer limit, scored on likelihood over a stated period and impact on a named decision.
  • What will we do differently? The because-of-this-we-will sentence, with an owner and a deadline.
  • What did I fail to see? Separated horizons, interrogated thin columns, and a deliberate search for the factor that contradicts what we believe.

The arithmetic that shows what the workshop actually produced. Thirty-four factors is already past the point where volume displaces insight - the practical rule is eight to ten per dimension at most, condensed to three to five, with more than thirty in total producing volume rather than understanding. Apply the because-of-this-we-will test and seven survive: 20.6 per cent. The other twenty-seven were all true and none of them changed anything. Being true was never the test.

And the pattern in the thin columns, stated carefully. The three well-populated dimensions took 26 of 34 entries - 76.5 per cent of the effort - and yielded 5 of the 7 material factors, 71.4 per cent of the value. That is a return almost exactly in line with the effort spent. The three thin dimensions took 8 entries, 23.5 per cent, and yielded 2 - which is 28.6 per cent. Be precise about what that does and does not show: the single political entry scoring one out of one is a statistical curiosity and proves nothing on its own. The defensible finding is the modest disproportionality - the overlooked categories returned slightly more than the attention they were given, while the crowded ones returned exactly what was paid for. The lesson is not that thin columns are always right. It is that they must always be interrogated.

Why the test changes the room, not just the document. Any environmental-scan workshop spends a surprising amount of its time on whether something is a political factor or an economic one. That is a filing dispute and it settles nothing. The because-of-this-we-will sentence converts it into an argument about what the organisation should do - which is the argument worth having, and the one people came for.

One honest boundary. You produce the scan. You do not set the strategy that responds to it - and you must not smuggle your preferred strategy in as an external factor, which is the quietest way this technique gets abused. A factor is something happening in the world, not an argument you wanted to make wearing a hat.

Does it scale? A radar operator does not sweep evenly across the whole sky. They know where traffic comes from, they weight the sweep accordingly - and then they deliberately check the quiet quadrant, because a quiet quadrant is either empty or the antenna is broken, and from the screen those look identical.

  1. Audit the method. Score the scan against the five boxes. Where does it fail?
  2. Find the real error. It is not in the method. Say precisely where it is.
  3. The scoring. They identified the right factor. What did they get wrong about it?
  4. The leadership table. Why did I give you the composition and tenure of the leadership team?
  5. Fix it. What would you change about how this scan was run - not what it concluded?

The test of a real answer: three things.

First, step 1 should come up nearly clean. Nineteen factors, condensed, sourced, horizons separated, a decision with an owner and a deadline. The method was followed correctly and the answer is still wrong, which is the entire exercise. A learner who finds a methodological flaw has found something minor and missed the point.

Second, the error is in the scoring of impact, and it was made by the organisation's own assumptions rather than by the technique. They read the shift to electric vehicles as a change in what the car is powered by. It may be a change in what a car is - and if the supply chain reorganises around a different kind of product, the threat is not that their engine components become obsolete but that the position they occupy in that chain disappears. They took a factor that questioned their business model and converted it into a search for new components to machine. That is confirmation bias with the paperwork done properly, and it is far harder to catch than a sloppy list.

Third, step 4 is the reason the leadership table is there. Seven mechanical engineers, nineteen years each, is not a criticism of anyone - it is a description of a room with a consistent way of seeing, and consistent seeing is exactly what scores impact wrongly. The fix in step 5 is therefore about how the scan is run: sources and voices from outside the organisation, a person whose job in the room is to argue the uncomfortable reading, and an explicit answer to box 5's last question - what here contradicts something we believe? Their scan had no such factor, and with that leadership table, the absence is the finding.

T5: The Guardrails

Environmental scans fail by being thorough.

The common traps - and how to dodge them

• Six equal columns. Drawn before anyone has thought, and they set how the effort will be spent. Fit the emphasis to where your world is moving.

• The exhaustive list. Fifty true observations without prioritisation are worse than five scored ones, because the four that mattered are buried in the middle.

• Using the categories to file what you already know. They are a search pattern. Used as a filing system they return exactly what went in.

• Scanning only the room. If no factor came from outside the organisation, you have surveyed your colleagues, not the environment.

• No owner and no deadline. "We will monitor" is what a factor says when nobody intends to act on it.

• Mixed time horizons. A factor biting next year and one biting in fifteen go to different plans and different people.

• Arguing about which column something belongs in. A filing dispute that settles nothing. Ask what it changes instead.

• Treating a thin column as automatically fine, or automatically a failure. Genuinely stable and never examined look identical on the page. Ask which, and write the answer down.

• Reading a threat through the business you already have. The most dangerous factor is the one you identify correctly and score against your existing model.

• A scan that contradicts nothing you believe. It has confirmed rather than scanned - and it now carries the authority of a method.

• Smuggling your preferred strategy in as an external factor. A factor is something happening in the world, not an argument wearing a hat.

Onalenna had thirty-four true statements and no decision. Decide where your environment is actually moving and weight the scan accordingly, since fit between scanning emphasis and dynamism matters more than volume; gather to a ceiling and record where each factor came from, because a scan built from the room surveys the room; condense to what is material, scored on likelihood over a stated period and impact against a decision you actually face; then make every survivor produce a because-of-this-we-will sentence with an owner and a deadline, and let the ones that cannot go; and finally audit the scan itself - separate the horizons, interrogate every thin column without assuming it is either fine or a failure, and find the factor that contradicts something the organisation believes. A scan earns its keep not by listing every external force but by forcing a decision on the few that matter. And if nothing in your analysis made anyone uncomfortable, you did not scan the environment - you confirmed it, with the authority of a method attached.
