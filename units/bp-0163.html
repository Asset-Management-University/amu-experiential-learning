I worked so hard to build all this - so how could one bad turn take ALL of it?

Movement 1 - The Felt Problem

You are Muhammad, and you built it with your own two hands. For years you worked, you saved, and you put every spare rand back into the one thing you knew and trusted most: your business and the gas trade it lives on. And it worked - you've built real security for your family, a good life. It feels safe precisely because you know it inside out and you control it.

Then, one Tuesday, a rumour: a big change coming to the energy market that could shake the whole gas sector. For the first time, your stomach drops. You look at the business, the savings, your family's future - and you see the truth you'd never let yourself notice. It isn't just the business at risk. It's everything. Your family's entire security is sitting in one single basket.

I've worked so hard and built so much - so how is it that one bad turn could take ALL of it?

Movement 2 - Your Attempt

Your mind races down two paths, both born of fear. The first is to abandon: "get out - sell it all, now!" Cash out, run for safety, sit on the money - but that means throwing away the very thing that built your family's security in the first place. The second is to cling: "it'll be fine - just hold on tight and hope." Ignore the rumour, dig in, pray the storm passes - but that's closing your eyes as you run toward a cliff edge.

Both feel like action, but look closely: both are still betting 100% on that one thing - one a bet it's doomed, the other a bet it's fine. So before you read on: if the danger is having it all on one outcome, what's the third path - the one that isn't a bet on the shock at all?

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

The shock isn't the real problem - it's only the symptom. The real problem is concentration: too much of your family's security resting on one single outcome. And the cure is not to predict the next shock (you can't) but to build so that no single shock can ever ruin you. Five moves:

  • See the concentration: name it plainly: 'almost all our family's security depends on this one thing succeeding.' While it does well the danger is invisible - which is exactly why it stays unseen until a shock. Seeing clearly how much rides on a single outcome is the first move.
  • Change the goal - don't predict the shock: your job is not to predict the rain; it is to build a roof. You can't reliably foresee which shock will come, or when - so stop trying. Change the question from 'what's the next crash?' to 'how do I build so that NO single event can ever ruin us?'
  • Spread across things that don't move together: move SOME (never all) of your wealth into a few different baskets - and choose ones that do NOT rise and fall together. That's the whole trick: when your main basket is hit, the others are likely steady or up, cushioning the blow. One down, another not.
  • Make the honest trade - peak for floor: this is the heart of it. Spreading means giving up the chance of the absolute highest peak - but in return you build a solid floor beneath your family that a single shock can't break. For your mission - your family's security - the floor matters far more than the peak.
  • Act calmly, in steps (and ask the right source): this is not a fire sale; you don't abandon the good thing you built. You calmly move a sensible slice out of the one basket into others, over time, starting now. And to find baskets that 'don't move with yours', don't take a hot tip from a mate - get guidance from a qualified, trustworthy source whose job is managing risk, not chasing hype.

For the life you're protecting, a floor that holds beats a peak that might vanish.

Movement 3.5 - The Guided Step

This isn't only about a business - it's any time your security piles onto one outcome. Watch it with a loyal employee.

See the same trap catch a 20-year employee

Grace has given one big company twenty good years - and, quietly, has let all her retirement savings sit in that same company's shares. It felt safe: "I know this company, I believe in it." But her wealth is just as concentrated as Muhammad's: her job and her life savings now both depend on one single company's fate. One bad turn could take her income and her retirement in the same blow.

Her move is exactly the same: calmly, over time, spread a slice of that retirement money into other baskets that don't rise and fall with her employer - trading a little possible peak for a floor she can depend on, whatever happens to the company.

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

You're not Muhammad, but the principle holds - and that drop in your stomach is a signal worth listening to. Wherever almost everything rides on one outcome:

  1. Name your concentration. Look honestly at your savings, your income, your work: how much of your whole security rests on one thing - one investment, one company, one industry, one earner?
  2. Stop predicting; start building. Don't try to guess the next crash. Your job is to be ready for any crash - to build so no single event can wipe you out.
  3. Plan a calm spread. What sensible slice (not all of it) could you move, over time, into one or two baskets that don't rise and fall with your main one - choosing "a floor that holds for my family" over "a fragile peak"? Take guidance from a qualified, trustworthy source, not a hot tip; take the first calm step, no panic-selling.

The danger was never that you backed something you believed in - that's how anything gets built. The danger was letting all of it ride on a single roll you can't control. Safety isn't knowing the future or controlling one thing perfectly; it's arranging things so that no single storm can take everything. Build that floor, calmly and in steps, and you can stop bracing for the crash - because whatever comes, your family will still be standing on solid ground.
