It was in my account and felt like my money - so how am I in debt for spending it?

Movement 1 - The Felt Problem

You are Muhammad, and it had been a good year. The money came in - whole, straight into your account - and it felt, completely, like yours. So you used it the way a careful person does: more stock, a repair the bakkie needed, a little for the family, the rest ploughed back into the business. Not one rand on luxuries.

Then the reckoning lands: an official bill for the share that was owed on that income all along - around R25,000 - on money that is simply gone. And because it's late, a penalty is growing on top, taking it to R27,500 - nearly eight months of what you pay yourself. You didn't waste it; you spent money that was never fully yours, without ever knowing a slice of it belonged to someone else.

It was in MY account, it felt like MY money - so how am I suddenly in debt for spending it?

Movement 2 - Your Attempt

You reach for a way out, and each one just deepens the hole. You promise to pay it from next year's income - but now you're running a full year behind, every year, with penalties quietly stacking. You panic-cut everything to the bone to scrape the lump together, starving the business that earns your living. Or you simply resent the system and look away - which is exactly how the next bill, bigger, is already forming.

So before you read on: the money arrived whole, and you spent it carefully. But was the whole of it ever actually yours to spend?

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

The trap was treating "arrived whole" as "all mine". The way out is a small, automatic discipline you run on every rand that comes in. Five moves:

  • See the hidden share: some of what lands in your hands carries a prior claim from the very moment it arrives - it was never yours; it is only passing through you. The money looked whole and complete, but a slice of it always belonged to someone else (here, the share owed on what you earn). 'Arrived whole' is not the same as 'all mine'.
  • Why it stays invisible: a wage hides this safely: the share is removed before you ever see the money, so you only hold what is truly yours. But when money comes to you WHOLE - as it does for the self-employed - the share is hidden inside it, and nobody removes it for you. You must remember it yourself, every time, or the whole amount feels spendable when it isn't.
  • Know your real share - from the proper source: find the true portion owed - ask a qualified person or the proper office; don't guess. As a rough picture, say it's about a quarter - but of what you actually EARN (your income after costs), NOT a quarter of every rand that passes through you. Your real figure will differ; getting this number right is not optional.
  • Separate it at the moment of receipt: this is the key action. The instant money comes in, move your share into a SEPARATE place you don't touch - automatically, before it ever feels like spendable money. A second account, a fixed transfer - the physical act of separating turns an invisible obligation into a visible, safe set-aside.
  • Spend only the remainder: run your life and business from what's left - because the remainder was always your true income, not the whole. (And the mirror: by facing the reckoning honestly and keeping clean records, you also get to claim back anything the same system owes YOU - the ones who hide forfeit that too.)

Watch the danger turn harmless. The R25,000 was never extra surplus you blew - it was a slice that was always owed on the roughly R100,000 the business truly earned you that year; it only became a crisis because it stayed hidden inside whole-looking money until the bill (plus a R2,500 penalty) made it real, too late. Separate that slice the instant each payment lands - into an account you treat as untouchable - and the reckoning becomes a non-event: the money is simply there, waiting. Money arriving whole is not the same as money that is all yours; your true income was always the remainder, and the slice that was never yours should never have felt spendable for a single day.

Movement 3.5 - The Guided Step

This isn't only about tax - it's any money that passes through your hands carrying someone else's claim. Watch it in a savings club.

See the same slice in a savings-club treasurer

You hold the money for a savings club - twenty members each paying in every month, all of it landing in your account, building toward the year-end payout. It sits there, whole, looking like a healthy balance. One tight month you dip in - "I'll replace it before payout, no one will know." Then a slow month, another dip. Payout day comes, and the pool is short: that money was never yours to touch for a single day.

The fix is identical:

• See the hidden claim: every rand in that account belongs to the members - it is only passing through you.

• Separate it at receipt: the moment contributions land, move them into a separate account you treat as untouchable - never your spending account.

• Spend only what's truly yours: your own money lives somewhere else entirely; the pool is sacred until payout.

Received whole, but never yours - so separate it the instant it arrives.

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

Think of money that passes through your hands carrying a prior claim - the share owed on what you earn, money you hold for others, a deposit that isn't yours. Take it out of the danger zone:

  1. Find your real share, from the proper source. Don't guess - ask a qualified person or the proper office. Remember it's a share of what you genuinely EARN, not of every rand that passes through you.
  2. Separate it the instant income arrives. Move that slice into a separate place you treat as untouchable - automatically, before it ever feels like spendable money.
  3. Live on the remainder. Run your life and business from what's left - that was always your true income - and keep clean records, so you can also claim back anything the same system owes you.

The cruelest debts are the ones you never knew you were building - money that felt like a reward for a good year, quietly turning into a bill you couldn't pay. But the cure is almost insultingly simple: decide, once, that the slice which was never yours never touches your hands as spendable money. Move it aside the moment it lands, live honestly on the remainder, and the dreaded reckoning becomes the easiest day of your year - because the money was always there, exactly where you put it, waiting.
