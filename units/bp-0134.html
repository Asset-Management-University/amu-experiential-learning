Why does fixing it never actually fix it?

Movement 1 - The Felt Problem

You are Muhammad, on an urgent delivery for a new customer you're trying to impress. Halfway there, on a busy road, the bakkie just dies. No warning - that sudden, sickening silence you've started to recognise. You coast to the verge, hazards ticking, a cold knot in your stomach.

You don't even have to think. You open the fuse box by your knee, pull the engine fuse, hold it to the light: the little wire inside is blown. Again. The third time in two months. You push in a spare from the glovebox, turn the key - the engine catches - and a wave of relief washes over you, chased straight away by dread. How long until the next one? At night? In the rain? Loaded, going uphill?

Each time it costs you: lost time, a customer left waiting, a chip off your own trust in the bakkie. And your logbook is no help at all - it just says "replaced fuse." What you did, never why it happened. You're spending five-rand fixes on a problem that's quietly costing you thousands.

The same fault keeps coming back, and every quick fix gets me going again - so why does fixing it never actually FIX it?

Movement 2 - Your Attempt

The pressure says: drop the fuse in, finish the delivery, deal with it later. It's fast, it's cheap, and the customer is waiting. You're running again - crisis over. Maybe it's just bad luck, or a cheap batch of fuses; maybe you'll buy a pricier brand next time. Taking it to an auto-electrician to "go looking for a problem" that isn't even happening right now? That sounds like hours of labour and a fortune you don't have.

But before you pull off: notice that you've done this exact thing twice already, and here you are again. What would it actually take to make this never happen again - not just get you home today?

I keep replacing the fuse and it keeps blowing. The fuse isn't the problem - it's a symptom. Something keeps MAKING it blow. What is it, and why?
When does it actually happen? Always after a bumpy stretch... vibration. So something is being shaken loose or rubbed through. I need to LOOK, not just swap the part - and keep asking 'why' past the symptom.
Find the real cause (a wire chafing because nothing ever checks it), fix THAT and add the missing check - then give it an owner and a date, and watch a couple of cycles to be sure it's truly stopped. Running again is not solved.

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

The breakthrough is a change of mind: "running again" is not "solved." A failure that keeps returning is a message from your asset - and the only way to truly answer it is to stop reacting and start investigating. Five moves do it:

  • First, the mindset: 'running again' is NOT 'solved': a failure that keeps coming back is a message. The quick patch ends today's crisis, not the problem - and the loss compounds every time it returns (lost time, a risked customer, your own confidence). Solving it means ending the recurrence, not surviving this one.
  • Pause and observe - gather the WHY the records never captured: don't just replace the part. Stop and look for the pattern: WHEN does it fail? (Always after a pothole - so it's vibration, not bad luck.) Your logbook says 'replaced fuse' - what was DONE, never WHY. You have to gather the clues no one wrote down.
  • Trace the 'why' chain to the system gap: keep asking 'why' until you reach the real cause: the fuse blew BECAUSE of a short, BECAUSE a bare wire touched the body, BECAUSE its cover rubbed through, BECAUSE it chafed on a bracket, BECAUSE nothing was clipping it or checking it. The root isn't a bad fuse - it's a missing check (a SYSTEM GAP).
  • Fix the system gap, not the symptom: repair the chafed wire AND remove the cause (clip it away from the bracket) AND close the gap that let it hide - add 'inspect the wiring for chafing' to the service list. You're fixing the system that produced the failure, not just the failure.
  • Close the loop: assign, schedule, VERIFY (the step everyone skips): give the fix and the new check an OWNER and a DATE - then VERIFY: watch a couple of cycles (drive the bumpy roads) and confirm it has actually stopped. Only when the recurrence is gone is it truly solved. A fix you never verify is just a hope.

Watch the chain come apart, one honest "why" at a time. The fuse blew - why? A power surge from a short. Why a short? A bare wire touched the metal body. Why bare? Its plastic cover had rubbed through. Why rubbed through? It was chafing against a sharp bracket. Why was it chafing? It was never clipped down - and nothing ever checked it. There it is: the root cause was never "a bad fuse." It was a missing check - a gap in the system, hiding in plain sight because the records only ever said what was done, never why.

So the real fix isn't another fuse. It's: mend the wire, clip it clear of the bracket, and add "inspect the wiring for chafing" to the service list - then give that an owner and a date, and watch the next couple of bumpy trips to confirm it has truly stopped. That last step - verify - is the one almost everyone skips, and it's the one that turns a fix into a solution.

Movement 3.5 - The Guided Step

This discipline isn't about engines - it's about any problem that keeps coming back. Watch it work somewhere completely ordinary.

See the same move solve an everyday recurring problem

The recurring failure: the kitchen bin always overflows the day before the rubbish is collected - smelly bags balanced on top or left on the floor.

• The symptom fix (again and again): squash it down hard, or start a new bag on the floor. Solved... for a few hours.

• Pause and observe: it overflows because it fills too fast - and looking inside, it's mostly bulky things: milk cartons, plastic bottles, flattened boxes.

• Trace the why-chain: the bin fills fast BECAUSE bulky recyclables go in it, BECAUSE there's nowhere else easy to put them. The root is a missing system: no convenient place to separate recycling.

• Fix the system + close the loop: put a second small bin beside it for recyclables (fix the gap); tell the family the new rule (assign); the evening before the next collection, check (verify) - the main bin has room to spare. It hasn't come back. Now it's solved.

Different world, same move: a recurring problem is never really about the part you keep replacing - it's about the system gap behind it.

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

What is the "blowing fuse" in your own life or work - the frustrating thing that keeps coming back no matter how often you patch it? A report that's always a last-minute scramble. A bill that's always late. A misunderstanding that keeps happening with the same person. A tool never where it should be. Here's how to end the loop:

  1. Refuse the quick patch - say it plainly: "running again is not solved." The recurrence is the real problem, not this one instance.
  2. Pause, observe, and trace the why-chain. Look for the pattern (when does it happen?), then keep asking "why does that happen?" past the symptom until you reach the system gap - the missing habit, check, rule, or part.
  3. Fix the system gap, then CLOSE THE LOOP. Change the system, not just the symptom - then give it an owner and a date, and verify over a cycle or two that it has actually stopped. A fix you never check is only a hope.
R

Name one thing that keeps going wrong, trace it to its real root this week, and make the one change that ends it - then watch to be sure. (Working with an AMU facilitator? Bring it there too.)

Most people spend their whole lives replacing fuses - patching the same problems over and over, mistaking a moment's relief for a solution. The ones who get ahead simply pause, find the real cause once, fix the system, and check that it's gone. Do that, and a problem solved is a problem solved for good - and your time and energy go to building forward, not bailing out the same leak again and again.
