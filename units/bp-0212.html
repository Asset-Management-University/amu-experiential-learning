I leave home before dawn in the freezing dark to catch the taxi to work - and it feels like I'll be doing this until I'm 60. Is there any way to be free sooner?

Movement 1 - The Felt Problem

You are Ntando, and your alarm goes off at half past four, into a dark that is still complete. You lie there for one stolen minute, then pull on every warm thing you own - the cold in the room has teeth this morning. Outside, the street is black and silent; your breath smokes in front of you as you walk the unlit road to the taxi rank. You wait there, hands jammed in your pockets, stamping your feet, until the minibus fills and pulls off, everyone pressed shoulder to shoulder, half of them still asleep. The sun will not be up for another hour.

It is not even the work itself that presses on you today. It is the maths of it. You are in your twenties. And if nothing changes, this exact cold dark morning is waiting for you tomorrow, and the morning after that, and every working morning - until you are in your sixties. Forty more years of leaving home before the sun, of this same taxi, this same cold. You do not want to wait until you are old and worn out before you are finally allowed to rest. There has to be another way. There has to be.

On Saturday - no shift - you walk past Aunty Reonet's house, and there they are on her sunny porch: Aunty Reonet, who taught at the primary school for forty years, and Uncle Kgosietsile from two streets over, who fixed cars his whole life. Coffee steaming between them, a little carved bird Uncle Kgosietsile is whittling, no rush in them anywhere. They have nowhere to be. No shift. No boss. Their mornings belong to them. And they are not rich, and they were never lucky - a teacher and a mechanic. But somehow, they got free. Aunty Reonet waves you over, and as you climb the bottom step, the longing in you is so big it aches.

You both did it - you don't have to work anymore, your mornings are your own. I want that more than anything in the world. But how did you even begin? Trying to save feels like filling a bucket with a hole in it - and do I really have to wait until I'm sixty, like you, before I'm free?

Movement 2 - Your Attempt

As you wait for them to answer, three quiet thoughts press in - and each one is a door that stays shut. "Freedom like theirs must be only for people who got lucky, or had money to begin with." But you just said it yourself - a teacher and a mechanic, ordinary people on ordinary pay; they did not FIND this freedom, they BUILT it, which means the path is open to you too. "I'll start properly one day, once I've saved up a big lump sum to begin with." But waiting throws away the one thing you have that they no longer do - decades of time; the engine grows from small, steady beginnings, not from one big magic moment that never quite arrives. "I'll just keep my money safe in a savings account." But cash sitting still quietly loses its power to buy things, year after year, and pays you almost nothing - it never becomes an engine that works for you. The shut doors all share one wrong belief: that freedom is a faraway mountain you must somehow leap to in one bound.

So before they answer, think: what is the ONE thing Aunty Reonet and Uncle Kgosietsile have that means their mornings are their own - and could you start building a small piece of it THIS month, instead of waiting forty years?

Look again at Aunty Reonet and Uncle Kgosietsile. They are not free because they are old, and not because they were lucky or rich - a teacher and a mechanic never are. They are free because something OTHER than their own two hands now earns for them. What could that something be - and is there any reason you would have to wait until you are old to start building one of your own?
You do not have to free your WHOLE life in one go - that is the thought that makes it feel impossible. What if you just freed ONE thing first - your phone bill - by having something else pay it for you, every month, forever? You would not need millions for that. You would only need a small money-engine that throws off enough each month to cover that one bill. How might you build the first piece of it?
Put a little, every month, into a capital-preserving asset that pays you an income - a REIT or an ETF that owns property and companies and pays you a share of the rent and profit. The moment you do, you already have passive income - small, but real, and yours. It builds every month as you add and reinvest. Cover your smallest bill first, then the next. Trim a little spending so you can invest a little more, and the two race to meet. You do NOT have to wait until you are 60 - freedom starts the first month you begin.

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

Aunty Reonet smiles, because it is the question she hoped you would ask. "You don't leap the mountain, Ntando," she says. "You climb it one step, and the first step is small enough for anyone." Five moves:

  • You already own a piece of freedom the moment you start: Here is the secret Aunty Reonet and Uncle Kgosietsile know: the day you put your first bit of money into an income-paying asset, something flips. That money starts earning FOR you - while you sleep, while you ride the taxi, while you rest. The first month it might pay you only R35. That feels like nothing. But it is not nothing - it is the first rand of your life that you did not have to stand on your feet for. It is proof your engine is running. Everything grows from there.
  • Free yourself ONE expense at a time: Forget freeing your whole life at once - that is the thought that crushes you before you start. Instead, line up your monthly expenses, smallest first, and aim your growing passive income at the FIRST one - say your phone bill. The day your assets cover that bill, you have freed it forever: you will never again work an hour just to pay your phone. Then you aim at the next. Freedom is not one far-off door; it is a staircase, and you can start climbing it this month.
  • You hold two levers - and you control BOTH: Two things, every month, are in your hands. (1) Trim a little unnecessary spending - that lowers the bar your passive income has to clear. (2) Invest a little more - that raises the income. Pull both and the two lines race toward each other: your costs stepping down, your passive income climbing up, meeting far sooner than you would think. And because you are starting YOUNG - decades before Aunty Reonet ever did - time does the heaviest lifting for you.
  • Build the engine from SOLID things, and never sell it: Uncle Kgosietsile puts it simply: your invested money is like a fruit tree. You never chop it down for firewood - you let it grow and live off the fruit it gives, season after season, while the tree itself only gets bigger. You live off what it PAYS you, never the tree itself - that is why this freedom lasts. Two things make a tree solid enough to live off for life, and you have met both before: it should be a whole ORCHARD, not one tree (a REIT owns a slice of many buildings, an ETF a slice of many companies - the 'don't put all your eggs in one basket' you already learned, so no single failure can stop your income), and it should be built of REAL things that hold their value as prices rise over the years, not cash that quietly fades (your own lessons on inflation and on choosing trustworthy instruments go deeper here). The point for now is simple: pick a solid, spread-out, reputable income asset - then live off its fruit, and let the tree grow.
  • Keep a freedom journal: Make it visible. Keep a simple journal or spreadsheet where every single investment you make - even R100 into the same fund - gets its OWN line, and the column adds up into a running total of how much passive income you have built. Track your monthly expenses beside it. Then watch, in your own handwriting, the two numbers move toward each other. It turns a far-off dream into a map of exactly what you have already done - proof that you are building your freedom, one line at a time.

The whole thing lives in one line: you do not have to wait until you are old to be free - the moment you start putting a little, every month, into a solid income-paying asset, you already have passive income, and you free your life one expense at a time until that income covers everything you need. "I did not get free at sixty," Uncle Kgosietsile says quietly. "I got my FIRST piece of freedom in my first month, when my little bit of money paid me back a few rand I did not work for. By the time it covered my water bill, I knew I had won - the rest was just time and patience." That is the secret the shut doors hide: freedom is not a far-off mountain you leap, it is a staircase you start climbing this month - and because you are starting decades before they did, your climb can be far shorter than theirs. You will still have cold mornings for a while. But now each one is building something that will, one bill at a time, set your mornings free.

Movement 3.5 - The Guided Step

Before you take this into your own life, watch the very first stretch of the staircase - in real numbers.

See what R500 a month could build, and meet someone already on the climb

Say you trim a little and put R500 a month into a solid income asset - for example a REIT ETF (it owns a slice of many buildings and pays you a share of the rent). Say it pays an income of about 7% a year (illustrative - real yields rise and fall, and the value can go down as well as up). Watch the passive income build:

After year 1 (you have put in R6,000): it pays you about R35 a month. Small - but that is R35 you did not stand in the cold for.
After year 2 (R12,000 in): about R70 a month.
After year 3 (R18,000 in): about R105 a month.
After year 4 (R24,000 in): about R140 a month.
Early in year 5 (about R25,800 in): about R150 a month - and just like that, your phone bill is covered. Forever. You will never again work an hour just to pay it. That is the first stair, and you climbed it.

And it is not only for old people. Phetolo, who is just 24 and started a few years ago, leans over the porch rail grinning: "My investments quietly pay my whole phone bill now - my phone pays for itself. I'm not free yet. But I've started, and I can SEE it working." From there you simply aim the growing income at the next bill, and the next - while every extra rand you invest, and every bit of spending you trim, pulls the finish closer.

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

You do not have to wait forty years, and you do not have to leap a mountain. You start the engine this month:

  1. Start a freedom journal, and list your expenses smallest-first. Open a simple notebook or spreadsheet. Write down what you spend each month - phone, transport, food, rent - smallest at the top. That smallest bill is your first target: the first piece of your life you are going to set free.
  2. Make your first small investment in a solid income asset - and log it. Even a little, every month, into a capital-preserving, inflation-hedging asset that pays an income (a REIT or a broad ETF - real property and companies, not cash). Choose a REPUTABLE one: well-established, low-cost, listed on a recognised exchange and properly regulated - something you can check and trust. Never hand your money to anything flashy that promises returns too good to be true, or that you cannot verify; if it feels like a get-rich-quick trick, it is. Give every single investment its own line in your journal, and total up the passive income it pays you. Watch that total grow, line by line.
  3. Pull both levers, every month. Trim one bit of unnecessary spending (it lowers the bar) and add a little to your investing (it raises the income). The two race toward each other - and starting this young, time does most of the lifting for you.

Think of yourself on tomorrow's cold dark morning - but knowing that something you own is already awake too, quietly earning, paying off your phone bill rand by rand while you ride that taxi. You do not have to wish your twenties, thirties, and forties away waiting for sixty. You can free your life one bill at a time, starting now, with two small acts you repeat every month: spend a little less than you could, and own a little more of something solid that pays you. That is the change you can be - not someday, but this month. Be the change. Develop the Capability. You can.
