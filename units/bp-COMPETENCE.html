I love this work and they need it - but I am not good enough at it yet to be trusted. How do I start?

Movement 1 - The Felt Problem

You are Sinegugu, and everything has finally lined up. You found your direction by sampling. You tested it against the four corners and chose it. You held to it when an easier-money offer tried to pull you away. You love this work - helping older people use their phones - and they genuinely need it. You are ready.

And then you sit with an old man at the clinic who asks, shyly, if you could help him check his pension on the banking app - and your stomach drops. Because here is the truth you have been stepping around: you are wonderful with people - patient, kind, you explain without making anyone feel small. But you are not actually an expert on the banking apps, the government grant sites, the scams that prey on the elderly. And this is not a photo or a quick message to a grandchild. This is his pension. His rent. One wrong tap, one scam you fail to spot, and you could cost him everything.

The fear that arrives is not the lazy kind. It is the honourable kind: I am not good enough at this yet to be trusted with it. And behind it, the question that threatens to stop you before you start:

I love this work, and they need the help. But who am I to touch their banking apps? One wrong tap and an old man's pension could be gone. How can I offer this service when I am not a real expert - when a mistake could cost them everything?

You are not imagining the gap. It is real, and on this kind of work it matters. The question is what an honest person does about it.

Movement 2 - Your Attempt

Two bad answers present themselves, and you can feel yourself reaching for both. The first: fake it - smile, pretend you know the banking apps, hope nothing goes wrong. The second: give up - decide you are not qualified, and wait, maybe forever, until you somehow become an "expert" before you dare to start. One is reckless; the other is paralysis. Neither is the work of a serious founder.

Before you read on, look squarely at the gap instead of flinching from it. Name it plainly.

I keep thinking 'I am not a real expert, so I have no right to do this.' Wrong frame. The question is not 'am I an expert yet?' - it is 'what part can I do brilliantly and safely RIGHT NOW?'
My hesitation about the banking apps is not weakness - it is INTEGRITY. I am right not to gamble with an old person's pension. That instinct is exactly what makes me trustworthy. The task is not to ignore the gap - it is to close it.
Skill is not something I am born with or wait to receive - it is BUILT, on purpose. I can start NARROW (what I do well today), BUILD the missing skill deliberately, and PARTNER for the high-risk part until I have earned it. I do not need to be perfect to begin - I need to be honest about where I am.

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

First, give your hesitation its proper name: it is not weakness, it is integrity. You are right not to gamble with a vulnerable person's money. That instinct is exactly what will make you trustworthy. So the task is not to ignore the gap, and not to let it stop you - it is to close it, deliberately. When you have a real direction but a real skill gap, there are three honest moves - and you usually use all three at once:

NARROW - what can you do brilliantly and safely TODAY?

I offer 'Phone Confidence for Connection' - messaging apps to reach family, photos, saving contacts, video calls, blocking scam callers. And I say plainly: I do not touch banking or government apps yet, for your security. Safe, genuinely useful, honest - and I can do it brilliantly today.

BUILD - how will you close the gap, deliberately?

A free online course on digital security and spotting scams; practise on my OWN banking and government apps until I can do them in my sleep; write simple checklists (how to safely check a balance). Skill built on purpose - not waited for, not faked.

PARTNER - who already has the skill, to serve the need NOW and safely?

When an elder needs banking help now, I do not fake it - I connect them safely. 'Let us call your daughter together', or 'let us visit the bank's own helper - I will come with you.' I partner with the trusted channel; I am the ally, not the flawed expert.

And hold onto the deeper truth underneath them:

  • The principle: skill earns trust: Get so good that people cannot ignore you. Skill is not the enemy of passion - it is how you HONOUR it. Deepening your skill is what makes your care valuable and trustworthy, and what earns you the right to charge. Passion gives the why; deliberate practice gives the how - and getting better at the work usually makes you love it more, not less.
  • The path to trust: Nadia: Nadia loved feeding her community and dreamed of catering - but she was disorganised, and her first big meal was chaos. She did not quit. She NARROWED to the one thing she did perfectly: a rich, slow-cooked lentil soup, sold on Fridays. Her reputation grew; the soup money and confidence let her BUILD - a kitchen-management course, an apprenticeship with a local caterer. Years on she runs a full catering company, trusted because she earned her competence one honest step at a time.
  • The caution: Tomas: Tomas, eager to help his elderly neighbours, saw a leaking tap and tried to fix it with tools he did not really understand. He broke a pipe and caused a small flood - good intentions, a costly mistake, and worst of all a loss of trust. On high-stakes work, enthusiasm without competence can do real harm. The answer is not to stop caring - it is to NARROW to what you can do safely, and BUILD the rest before you reach for it.

See what those three moves did together. They let you start now, honestly, serving real people safely (NARROW); they put you on a deliberate path to becoming the expert you are not yet (BUILD); and they let you serve the whole need today without faking it (PARTNER). That is how skill actually gets made - not by waiting to feel ready, but by starting where you can already win and building outward from there.

The move: do not fake the gap and do not freeze at it. NARROW to what you can do brilliantly and safely today, BUILD the missing skill on purpose, and PARTNER for the high-stakes part until you have earned it. Competence is not a gift you wait for - it is a thing you make.

Movement 3.5 - The Guided Step

One worry deserves answering before you go, because it can sound like this unit is undoing the last one.

"Wait - I thought I just had to find what I love. Now I need to be skilled too?"

Both are true, and they are partners, not rivals. The match-quality unit helped you find the work you love - the thing your attention will sustain. This unit shows you how to honour that love by becoming good enough to do it well. Passion gives you the why; deliberate practice gives you the how. And here is the quiet gift: as you get better at work you love, you usually love it more, not less - mastery and meaning feed each other. "Follow your passion" on its own is only half the road; the other half is the patient, unglamorous building of skill that turns a thing you love into a thing you can offer. You do not need to be perfect to begin. You need to be honest about where you stand, and willing to build.

So Sinegugu does the honest thing. She does not fake the banking, and she does not give up. She opens with what she can do beautifully today - "Phone Confidence for Connection" - and tells every client plainly that she does not yet handle banking, for their safety. Quietly, in her own evenings, she starts the security course and practises on her own accounts. And when an elder needs banking help now, she walks them to the bank's own helper, or sits with them while they call their daughter. Within months, she is the trusted one - not because she was an expert on day one, but because she was honest on day one and got better every week after.

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

You will have a gap too - everyone starting real work does. The point is not to have no gap. It is to be honest about it and to close it on purpose. Take the direction you have chosen and run your own skill gap through the three moves.
