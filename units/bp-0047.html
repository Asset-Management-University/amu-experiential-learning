Movement 1 - The Felt Problem

A gentle note before we begin. This unit is about extortion - criminals threatening a business - which can be frightening to read. If it brings up real fear about your own situation, be kind to yourself. The single most important thing in this unit is this: no business or amount of money is worth a life, and you do not have to face such a threat alone - there is help, through others. If you or anyone is in immediate danger, getting people to safety and contacting emergency services comes first.

You are Lwandle. The smell of metal and motor oil in your repair workshop usually calms you. Tonight, it does not. Everyone has gone home; it is just you, the quiet hum of the old fridge, and the number they gave you.

They did not shout. Two men, quiet suits. They came in this afternoon, looked around the workshop where you have worked for fifteen years, and explained the new "cost of doing business in this area" - a "protection" fee, payable every month. You look at your accounts book. The number they named is not a small piece of your profit; it is almost all of it, more than the rent. Paying it means you cannot buy new parts. It means letting Nolwazi go, then maybe Bonga too. It means the slow death of your business.

But you also remember the way one of them looked at the small, framed photo of your family on the corner of your desk, and the smile with no warmth as he said, "It is a small price to keep everyone safe and sound."

Movement 2 - Your Attempt

Your mind races through the options, and each one is a dead end. "Maybe I just pay it, just this once, to make them go away and keep everyone safe for now." It feels like the path of least resistance - but it is the cruel design of the trap: paying does not buy lasting safety, it buys you a label, "this one pays", marking you as a reliable source of income, so the demand does not go away - it comes back, and it grows, and you have funded the very people threatening you. (The wish to pay and protect your people is human, and no shame - but it deepens the trap, it does not end it.) "This is my business - I built it, I will not be bullied; I should just tell them no, to their faces." Standing up feels strong - but a brave, lonely stand is exactly what criminals are prepared for and can crush; no workshop, no tools, no sum is worth your life, or Nolwazi's, or Bonga's - people must come before property, always. "I cannot tell anyone - not my family, not the other owners on this street; it is my shame, my problem, I will carry it alone." This is what they want you to feel: isolation is their greatest weapon, because they can threaten one frightened person far more easily than a whole community that is organised and looking out for each other.

So before you read on: the real question is not "how do I fight them alone?" - it is "how do I stop being alone in this fight?"

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

The breakthrough is realising two truths at once. First: you cannot buy lasting safety from criminals - paying them only feeds the threat and deepens the trap. Second: the way through is not a heroic lone stand, but the exact opposite - put people's safety first, and stop facing this alone. Five moves:

  • Name the trap and refuse the shame: before anything else, name what this is and put the blame where it belongs. This is EXTORTION - a serious crime - and the impossible position you have been forced into is entirely the fault of the criminals doing it, not yours. You did nothing to invite it; building an honest business is not a provocation. The shame and secrecy you feel are not a true verdict on you - they are part of how this crime works, because a person who feels ashamed stays silent and isolated, which is exactly what makes them easy to prey on. So set the shame down: you are a victim of a crime, not a failure, and whatever you have felt forced to do so far to keep people safe, you are not a coward - you have been placed in a trap designed to break anyone. Seeing it clearly, and without self-blame, is the ground you need to stand on to do everything that follows.
  • Understand why paying does not buy safety: the instinct to 'just pay it, this once, to make them go away' is human and understandable - it feels like buying peace and protecting your people, and there is no shame in the impulse. But it is the cruel heart of the trap, because you cannot buy lasting safety from criminals. Paying does not end the threat; it answers a question they were asking - will this one pay? - and the answer 'yes' marks you as a reliable source of income, so the demand returns, and it grows, and you have now funded and emboldened the very people threatening your family. What feels safest in the short term deepens the danger and the cost over time. This is not said to shame anyone who has paid under fear - it is said so you can see the trap clearly: there is no amount that buys you out, because paying is the thing that keeps you in.
  • Put people before property, always: hold this above every other consideration: your life, and the lives of your staff and your customers, are the only truly priceless things here - no workshop, no stock, no tools, no sum of money comes close, and no business is worth a single life. That single rule settles a great deal. It means you must NOT take a brave, lonely stand, confront them, refuse to their faces, set any kind of trap, or play the hero alone, because a lone stand is exactly what violent criminals are prepared for and can crush, and the person who would pay for it is you or someone who works for you. Courage here does not look like a dramatic refusal; it looks like the discipline to keep every person physically safe while you find the real way through. Property can be rebuilt; people cannot. Let that order - people first, always - govern every decision you make from here.
  • Do not face it alone - this is the heart of it: this is the most important move, and the one the criminals most want to deny you: stop carrying it alone, because the real protection is not a lone stand but a network. Isolation is their greatest weapon - they can threaten and control one frightened person, but it is far harder to prey on a community that is organised and watching out for one another. So reach beyond yourself. Report to the appropriate authorities where you can safely do so. And, knowing that in some places the authorities may be weak or compromised, lean also on collective and community power: a business or traders' association, other trusted operators (you may well find you are not the only one being squeezed), community leaders, and professional security and legal advisers who know these situations. Criminals fear organised, collective resistance and daylight far more than a single isolated owner. The first quiet conversation with one person you trust is the step that turns an impossible lonely choice into a shared, survivable problem.
  • Protect and prepare wisely, with support: once you are no longer alone, you can act with care instead of panic. With the help of your support network and advisers, make decisions together and for safety: quietly preserve what is safe to preserve (a private record of the demands and what was said), share information carefully with others who are affected so a pattern can be seen, and decide on a path collectively rather than in isolation and fear. Let trained people - authorities where safe, security and legal professionals - guide what is wise and what is risky in your specific situation, because they can see options and dangers you cannot. The aim is not a single dramatic act but a steady, supported response that keeps people safe and refuses to let you be picked off alone. You are in an impossible position created by criminals - but you are not powerless and you are not alone, and the path to safety begins with that first quiet conversation with someone you trust.

See the one safe first step. That evening, Lwandle does not go anywhere alone, and he does not confront anyone. Instead he picks up his phone and calls the owner of the bakery down the road, Ma Ngwenya, a woman he has known for years and trusts from the local business forum. "Ma Ngwenya," he says, his voice quiet but clear, "I need to talk. Something is happening - and I think it might be happening to others too." It is the first step away from being alone. Nothing is solved in that moment, and everything has begun to change: the impossible lonely choice has become a shared, survivable problem. You cannot buy lasting safety from criminals - paying only marks you as a payer and feeds the threat. The real protection is to put people before property and to stop facing it alone, drawing in the appropriate authorities, other businesses, community groups, and professional security and legal help. Organised together, you are far harder to prey on than you are by yourself - and no workshop, no asset and no sum is ever worth a life.

Movement 3.5 - The Guided Step

You already know this one - picture a single antelope grazing alone on the plain.

See why safety is in the herd, not the lone stand

The lone antelope is alert, but it is vulnerable: a predator sees an easy, isolated target. Now picture the whole herd - hundreds of eyes and ears, watching in every direction. When a predator approaches, they do not scatter in panic; they bunch together, a wall of bodies, and the predator often decides it is too much trouble, too much risk, and moves on to find an easier, more isolated target.

This is not about you turning to fight the predator alone - that is the dangerous mistake. It is about finding your herd, so that you are no longer the isolated one. The criminals' whole method depends on catching you alone and frightened; your safety is in no longer being alone. You do not become braver by yourself - you become safer together.

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

This situation is terrifying, and it is not your fault. Your priority now is the safety of people, and you should not act alone. If this is your reality - or you are supporting someone in it - here is where to begin, carefully and with others:

  1. Acknowledge the crime. What is happening is called extortion. It is a crime, the criminals are entirely in the wrong, and you have done nothing to invite it - so let go of any shame.
  2. People come first. Before any decision, remind yourself that no money or property is worth a human life. Take no action that puts you, your staff or your customers in physical danger - which means no lone confrontations.
  3. Do not decide or act alone. The feeling of isolation is a weapon used against you, and your first, most powerful move is to break it.
  4. Reach for help - your strength now comes from others: report to the appropriate authorities when you and your advisers feel it is safe; connect with your community (a local business or traders' association, other trusted owners - you may find you are not the only one); and seek professional security and legal advice where you can, from people experienced in these matters.

There is no step here you should take alone - only, when you are ready, the first quiet conversation with one person you trust. And if anyone is ever in immediate danger, your only priority is to get people to safety and contact emergency services.

You are in an impossible position created by criminals - but you are not powerless, and you are not alone. The path to safety does not begin with a brave lone stand; it begins with reaching out, and with refusing to be the isolated target they are counting on. People before property, always - and never by yourself.
