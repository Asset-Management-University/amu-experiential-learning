Nobody knows we exist yet

Movement 1 - The Felt Problem

You are Muhammad, in the very early days of Tank You. You and Ibraaheem have something you KNOW is better: you deliver the gas to people's doors, the cylinder is full with a plastic seal to prove it, and you swap the empty one for them - no lugging a heavy tank to a shop, no being cheated with a half-empty refill. It is genuinely better. You are sure of it.

And the phone sits silent. Days go by with almost no orders. Nobody knows you exist. You have no big name, no shop sign people pass, and no money to advertise the way the established suppliers do. The thought claws at you: maybe you should take your tiny savings and blow it all on flyers and a big advert across the township - get your name everywhere, fast.

But a colder thought sits underneath: a great offer that nobody hears about helps no one and dies anyway. And you cannot afford to be heard the way the big companies are. You have the better thing - and no way, it seems, to get it into people's hands.

This thing is better - I know it is. But the phone doesn't ring. How do I get the first person to just TRY it - how do I get from zero to one with no money?

Movement 2 - Your Attempt

Both of your instincts are quietly wrong. Waiting for people to discover you means more silence - the world does not beat a path to an unknown. And blowing your savings to advertise to everyone fails for one simple reason: a stranger does not buy from a name they have never heard. You would be shouting "trust me!" at thousands of people who have no reason to.

Before you read on, sit with that last word: trust. The big companies have it (a known name) or buy it (huge adverts). You have neither. But is there another way a small, unknown person earns trust - one you could afford?

My instinct is to spend my little savings shouting at everyone with flyers. But a stranger doesn't buy from a name they've never heard. Advertising wide, while I'm unknown, just burns money.
What I'm missing isn't money - it's trust. And trust isn't bought; it's earned, one person at a time. So instead of shouting at thousands, what if I won ONE person, completely?
Go to one street where everyone uses gas. Serve one household so well - sealed, full, swapped - that they're amazed. Then kindly ask them to mention me next door. One happy neighbour's word beats any advert.

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

You stop trying to BUY attention and start to EARN trust - the one currency you can afford, paid one person at a time:

  • The trap - and what you really need: unknown and broke, you're tempted to WAIT for people to find you (silence) or BLOW cash on broad ads (strangers don't buy from an unknown). But money isn't what you lack - it's TRUST, and trust is earned one person at a time, never bought.
  • Step 1 - go where YOUR specific people are: not 'everyone' - the exact people with the problem you solve. One street where every home cooks on gas. Pour your scarce energy there, not everywhere.
  • Step 2 - SERVE and PROVE, don't pitch: do ONE job so well it speaks for itself - full, sealed, swapped, on time. Don't TELL them you're good; SHOW them, once, undeniably.
  • Step 3 - turn a happy customer into your voice: ask them - kindly, and as a service to their neighbour - to mention you next door. One trusted neighbour's word outsells any advert you could buy. This is the unknown's superpower.
  • Step 4 - make the first YES easy: a small first try plus a clear guarantee (the sealed, full tank) - so there's no risk for someone in giving an unknown a chance.

Here is the quiet reframe that changes everything: being small and unknown feels like your weakness, but it hides your greatest strength. A giant company cannot personally delight one family on one street and have them tell the neighbours by name. You can. Your smallness lets you give something no advert can buy - a real, personal, trustworthy experience - and a delighted person telling their neighbour is the most powerful, and cheapest, selling there is.

One caution on Step 3, because it is where people freeze: asking for the word-of-mouth must never feel like begging. Frame it as a kindness, not a plea - something like: "We're a new little business trying to help families on this street. If we did right by you, would you mention us to your neighbour? We'd be honoured to help her too." That is an offer to serve, not a desperate ask - and people are glad to pass on something good.

Movement 3.5 - The Guided Step

So Muhammad puts his savings back in his pocket and does something far cheaper and far harder: he goes and serves.

See trust beat a budget he doesn't have

The path he DIDN'T take: spend it all on flyers across the township. Result: most thrown away, a few read by strangers who've never heard of "Tank You" and don't call. Savings gone, phone still silent.

The path he took instead:

• One street. He picks a single road where he can see every home cooks on gas - his exact people.

• One flawless delivery. Mrs Khumalo gives him a try. He arrives on time, hands over a full cylinder with the seal unbroken, swaps her empty one, carries it in for her. She is amazed - no schlep, no short measure, no fuss.

• The kind ask. "We're new and trying to help families on this street - if we did right by you, would you mention us to your neighbour? We'd be honoured to help her too." She gladly does.

• It compounds. Three neighbours call that week. Each gets the same flawless service and the same kind ask. Each tells more. Within a month the street is his, and the next street is starting - on R0 of advertising.

The lesson in numbers: flyers everywhere = money spent, trust earned = zero. One street served brilliantly = money spent = zero, trust earned = a growing round that feeds itself. Trust beat the budget he never had.

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

You didn't need a budget or a famous name - you needed to help one real person so well they couldn't help but talk. That is how almost every great small business actually begins. For whatever you're trying to sell or offer:

  1. Find your specific people, and go to them. Not "everyone" - the exact people with the problem you solve. Where are they already? Start there, narrow.
  2. Serve one of them brilliantly - prove it, don't pitch it. Make that first experience so good it speaks for itself.
  3. Ask, kindly, for the word. Invite a happy customer to mention you to someone who'd also benefit - as a kindness offered, never a plea.
  4. Make the first yes easy. Offer a small, risk-free first try with a clear guarantee, so giving an unknown a chance costs them nothing.
R

Name your specific people and where they are, pick ONE to serve brilliantly this week, and plan the kind words you'll use to ask for the next. (Working with an AMU facilitator? Bring it there too.)

You don't win your first customers by being the loudest or the richest. You win them by being, to one person at a time, the most genuinely helpful and trustworthy - and letting their word carry you further than money ever could. That is a door open to anyone, with nothing but the will to serve.
