I sold my investment to protect it when the market crashed - so why did I end up losing far more than if I'd just done nothing?

Movement 1 - The Felt Problem

You are Tlhalefo. A year ago you did something you were proud of: you saved your first R10,000 and you invested it - a real stake in your own future. For months it sat there, slowly growing, and it felt good.

Then the crash hit. The news became a wall of red - recession, markets in freefall, everyone shouting that it would only get worse. You opened your account: your R10,000 was now R8,000. An hour later, R7,000. Your heart was pounding. A voice in your head, raw with panic, was screaming: sell - sell NOW, before it all goes to zero, stop the bleeding. So you did. You sold at R6,000, and felt a wave of relief: at least it could not fall any further now.

But the story did not end there. Over the following months, from the sidelines, you watched the market steady itself - and then climb. And keep climbing. Within a year, the R10,000 you had invested would have been worth about R12,000. Instead, you are sitting on R6,000 in cash, too uneasy to get back in.

You did what felt right. You acted to protect your money from a terrifying drop. And somehow that very act is what cost you the most - you would have been better off doing absolutely nothing.

I sold to protect my money when everything was crashing - so how did I end up losing far more than if I had just done nothing at all?

Movement 2 - Your Attempt

In the middle of a crash, three moves feel like protecting yourself - and each one is the trap closing. "Sell now, before the loss gets any worse." But selling is exactly what turns a temporary drop in price into a real, permanent loss - and it almost guarantees you miss the recovery. "Wait safely in cash until it's clearly OK to invest again." But by the time the news 'feels safe', the recovery has already happened, and you buy back in higher than you sold - the worst of both ends. "Watch the price every hour and react to the headlines." But that is the very engine of panic; the news is loudest and most terrifying right at the bottom, pushing you to sell at the worst possible moment. Every one of these feels like control, and every one makes it worse.

So before you read on: while the price was falling, had you actually LOST anything yet - and what was the single act that turned a scary-but-temporary drop into a real, permanent loss?

Go back to the feeling in your stomach as the number dropped - that pounding, screaming urge to sell before it 'all goes to zero'. That panic is real and human. But ask yourself: at the moment you sold, had you actually lost anything yet - or did the selling itself turn a falling price into a real, locked-in loss?
There is a difference between a PRICE and a LOSS. While prices were falling, you still owned exactly what you bought - the value on screen was just low for now. When did that temporary low price become a real, permanent loss you could never get back? Only at the single moment you... did what?
A crash is a temporary drop in PRICE, not a permanent loss - it only becomes a real loss the instant you sell. The trap is your own brain: fear screams 'sell' at the bottom and excitement whispers 'buy' at the top - the exact opposite of the plan. The defence is not cleverness; it is a RULE you write in calm, before the storm: 'I will not sell in a crash.'

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

The crash did not lose your money. The selling did. Five moves:

  • See a crash for what it really is: A crash is a sharp drop in the PRICE of what you own - not a loss of the thing itself. You still hold exactly what you bought. The low number on the screen is just today's price, and it only turns into a real, permanent loss the moment you sell. Until you sell, a crash is a price, not a loss.
  • Spot the real enemy - your own brain: The danger is not the crash; it is you. Our brains are wired for survival, so fear SCREAMS 'sell!' when prices fall, and excitement whispers 'buy more!' when they soar - which is the exact opposite of a sound plan (buy low, sell high). This gap between what we KNOW we should do and what fear MAKES us do is called the behaviour gap, and it quietly costs ordinary investors more than fees or bad luck ever do.
  • Feel the double cost of panic-selling: Panic-selling hurts you twice. First, you lock in the loss - you make the temporary drop permanent and real. Second, you miss the recovery - and history shows the market's best days tend to cluster right after its worst ones, so the very people who flee in fear are sitting in cash exactly when the rebound happens. You sell low, then watch it climb without you.
  • Choose time IN the market, not timing it: The fix is not to become a genius who jumps out before every crash and back in at the bottom - almost nobody can do that, and trying is how people get hurt. The fix is to STAY IN through the storm. Staying invested through the ups and downs has, over long stretches of history, beaten trying to time it. Boring beats clever here.
  • Forge your defence - a rule made in calm: You cannot reason with your panicking self in the middle of a crash - the fear is too loud. So you beat that future self with a rule you write NOW, while it is calm: a plain instruction like 'I will not sell in a crash; a falling price is not a loss until I sell; I will hold, and if anything keep buying while it is cheap.' A simple written rule, decided in advance, is the most powerful tool you have against your own fear.

The whole thing lives in one line: in a crash, the biggest danger to your money is not the falling price - it is your own panic, and the defence is a rule you decide in calm, long before the storm. Feeling that terror as the number drops is not weakness; your brain is built to flee danger, and a crashing screen looks exactly like danger. Even the professionals know they cannot trust themselves in the moment - so they build rules and systems to protect themselves from their own fear, and you can do the same. A falling price is only a loss the day you sell. Stay in, hold your nerve with a rule you wrote when you were calm, and you let time do the work that panic would have stolen. Boring, steady, and unafraid beats clever and frightened almost every time.

Movement 3.5 - The Guided Step

Before you take this into your own life, watch the same crash hit two friends who did just one thing differently.

See Onkemetse and Rethabile in the same crash

Onkemetse and Rethabile each invested R5,000 in the same fund. Then a major crash hit, and the value of their investment plunged by 40% - down to R3,000 each. They felt exactly the same fear, the same sick drop in the stomach.

Rethabile panicked. The fear was unbearable, and he sold everything for R3,000 to 'cut his losses before they get worse'. In that one moment, he turned a temporary drop into a permanent, locked-in R2,000 loss.

Onkemetse held. She felt the very same panic - but she had written herself a rule, in a calm moment, long before: I do not sell in a crash. So she did nothing at all. It was hard. She just held.

Over the next eighteen months, the market recovered. The original R5,000 investment climbed back and then past where it began, to about R6,500. Onkemetse now has R6,500. Rethabile is still sitting on the R3,000 he pulled out, having missed the entire rebound. The only difference between them was a single decision made in a moment of fear - and the rule that protected one of them from it. No recovery is ever guaranteed, but this pattern - where staying the course beats panic-selling - has played out again and again across market history.

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

You cannot stop a crash from coming, and you cannot stop the fear - but you can decide, right now, who you will be when it hits:

  1. Accept that the panic is coming. One day an investment of yours will drop hard, and your brain will scream at you to sell. That is normal and human - even the pros feel it. Knowing it is coming is the first defence; you will not be surprised into a rash move.
  2. Separate price from loss, in advance. Hold this clearly now, while it is easy: a falling price is not a loss - it only becomes a real loss the moment you sell. In a crash you still own exactly what you bought; you are just being shown a low price for it today.
  3. Write your Crash Rule - your instruction for your future, panicking self. In your own words, decide now what you WILL and will NOT do when prices fall hard. Something like: 'I will not sell in a crash. A falling price is not a loss until I sell. I will hold, and only ever sell for my real plan - never out of fear.' Keep it where you will find it.

This week, write your Crash Rule in one or two calm sentences and save it somewhere you will see it later. (Working with an AMU facilitator? Talk through what your rule should say, and why.)

The terrifying thing about a crash is that it makes you your own worst enemy - fear turns the very instinct meant to protect you into the thing that does the damage. But you have a power your panicking self does not: you are calm right now. The rule you write today, in this quiet moment, becomes the steady voice your frightened future self can follow when everything is red and screaming sell. That is you, being the change - your calm self today standing guard over your wealthier self tomorrow.
