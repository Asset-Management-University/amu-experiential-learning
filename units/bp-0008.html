My savings are sitting safe and untouched - so how are they getting smaller?

Movement 1 - The Felt Problem

You are Muhammad, and you and Ibraaheem did the hard thing: you followed a plan, you were disciplined, and you built a real store of savings. It sits in your everyday account - R15,000 - and the number never drops unless you spend it, which you haven't. That feels like winning. It feels safe.

But a quiet unease has crept in. A year ago you spotted a good second-hand welding tool for the business at R12,000; your savings could cover it, but you decided to wait and save a little more first. Today you see the same tool, same condition - and the price is R13,500. The R15,000 in your account hasn't moved a cent. The number is identical. But what it can buy has shrunk. It feels like the floor is sinking under money that isn't even moving.

My savings are sitting safe and untouched - so how on earth are they getting smaller?

Movement 2 - Your Attempt

Faced with this silent shrinking, two reactions tempt you - and both are dangerous. Do nothing - leave it exactly where it is. It feels safest; the number is familiar and never drops. But that's the trap of the slow leak: you're quietly letting rising prices eat your money's buying power, year after year. Or panic - jump at the first big "opportunity". Someone hypes a way to "double your money fast", and in a rush to stop the leak you think about throwing the whole R15,000 at it. That's the other way to lose your savings - not a slow leak, but one fast flood.

So if standing still loses slowly and a wild leap can lose everything fast, what's the wise path between them?

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

The trap is mistaking a steady number for true safety. The way out is to redefine safety and act carefully. Five moves:

  • See the invisible erosion: idle money isn't sitting still - it's in a race against the rising cost of everything (food, fuel, tools, rent). That's inflation. If your money earns nothing, it's losing that race: every month its real buying power shrinks a little, silently, even though the number on the screen never moves.
  • Size the silent loss: make the invisible real. R10,000 sitting idle, with prices rising about 6% a year: after 1 year it buys what ~R9,400 buys today (~R600 of buying power gone); after 5 years, only ~R7,500 (a quarter gone); after about 12 years, half. (The 6% is just an example - real rates vary.) Put your own number to it, and the loss stops being invisible.
  • Redefine safety: the key shift. FALSE safety = a number in an account that stays the same. TRUE safety = buying power that's preserved or grows. Money earning a return that at least keeps up with rising prices is doing its job - that's the safe money. Idle money that merely holds its number is the one quietly failing.
  • Keep your buffer, move the excess: your emergency fund must stay exactly where it is - safe and instantly reachable for real emergencies. This isn't about that money. It's about the EXCESS you've built BEYOND your buffer - that's the money that shouldn't sit idle, eroding.
  • Take the smallest reversible step: don't bet the whole store, and don't chase a hyped 'double your money' tip - that's just the fast way to lose it. Take a SMALL portion of your excess, put it somewhere it can earn, keep it low-risk and REVERSIBLE (you can get it back), and be honest about access, fees and risk. You're not gambling - you're learning: moving a little from idle to working, then stepping again.

The whole shift lives in one line: a number that stays the same is not the same as value preserved. Real safety isn't the figure on your screen holding still - it's your buying power holding (or growing). Idle money quietly fails that test; money that's working, even gently, can pass it. And the way to start is not the boldest move but the smallest reversible one: keep your emergency buffer untouched and instantly reachable, and move only a small part of the excess from idle to working - learning, not gambling, one careful step at a time.

Movement 3.5 - The Guided Step

This isn't only a personal problem - it bites any pool of money left to sit. Watch it in a savings group.

See the same silent shrink in a community savings group

Twenty members each save R200 a month into a shared pool, paid out at year-end. The number in the box grows every month, and it feels like success. But the money is idle: the R200 saved in January has lost buying power by December, and the whole pool quietly shrinks in value even as it grows in Rands. When the payout comes, it buys less than everyone hoped.

The fix is the same, just shared: keep enough cash for near-term needs, but let the larger, growing pool of excess go carefully to work together - so its buying power is protected before it's shared out. Same principle: a steady (or rising) number is not the same as preserved value.

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

It's time to protect the wealth you worked so hard to build. Idle savings or cash you're holding - put it to the test:

  1. Separate buffer from excess, and name the idle number. Keep your emergency fund safe and instantly reachable; then see how much is sitting completely idle beyond it.
  2. Size your silent loss. At a rough rate for rising prices (say 6%), how much buying power does that idle money lose in a year? (R20,000 idle loses about R1,200; R50,000 about R3,000.) Make the invisible enemy real.
  3. Plan your smallest reversible step. No hot tips, no betting it all - move one small part of the excess somewhere it can earn at least with rising prices: low-risk, reversible, honest about access, fees and risk. Then learn, and step again.

You were disciplined enough to save - that's the hard part, and most people never get there. But saving is only half the skill; the other half is refusing to let what you saved quietly melt away. Redefine safety as buying power preserved, keep your buffer close, and give the rest a job to do - gently, reversibly, one step at a time - and the money you fought to build will actually still be there, in full, when you finally need it.
