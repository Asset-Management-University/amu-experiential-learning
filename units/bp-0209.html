I love my cousin and I help him again and again - but he's no stronger, he leans on me more than ever, and the good I do washes away every week. Why doesn't my help ever last?

Movement 1 - The Felt Problem

You are Thapelo, and it is late on a Sunday evening, and you have just finished your younger cousin Otsile's maths homework. For him. Again. The fifth Sunday in a row. When you started doing this, it felt like exactly what a good cousin should do - he was stuck and panicking, you could help, so you did, and it felt warm and right.

But tonight the warm feeling is gone, replaced by something heavy and tired. Because you have started to notice the truth. Five weeks of your help, and Otsile's own capability has not grown. He still cannot do the maths himself. He still panics the moment a test is mentioned. If anything, he leans on you MORE now than he did at the start - he barely tries anymore, because he knows you will catch it. And you can see, underneath, that he is quietly ashamed that he still cannot do it alone.

The good you do feels like writing in sand. Every Sunday you write it carefully, and every Monday the tide comes in and washes it clean away, and by the next weekend you are back at the start. You are exhausted, the problem never ends, and the cousin you love so much is no better off than the day you began.

I love Otsile, and I help him again and again, week after week - but he's no stronger, he leans on me more than ever, and the good I do just washes away every Monday. Why doesn't my help ever last?

Movement 2 - Your Attempt

Sitting with that heaviness, three thoughts tempt you - and each one keeps the tide coming in. "Just keep giving the same help - that's what a good person does." But giving the same help that never lasts is not kindness, it is a treadmill; you both run and run and arrive nowhere. "Maybe Otsile is just the problem - he's lazy or not trying hard enough." But that is not fair to him; he was never actually given the chance to learn and grow - the trouble is in the KIND of help, not in him. "Teaching him would take forever - it's faster and kinder just to do it for him." But faster for tonight is slower for his whole life; the quick rescue is exactly what keeps him needing rescuing. Each thought feels caring, and each one quietly keeps you both stuck. It leaves him without the chance to grow, and it leaves you on a treadmill of repeated effort.

So before you read on: what is the difference between handing Otsile the finished answer, and giving him something he could use to solve it himself - forever?

Look at what your help actually leaves behind. Every week you hand Otsile the finished answer - and every week he is no closer to doing it alone, because the one thing he never got was the chance to learn it himself. So ask: what is the difference between giving someone the fish, and giving them the ability to catch their own fish forever?
There is an old saying: give a person a fish and you feed them for a day; teach them to fish and you feed them for a lifetime. Doing Otsile's work FOR him is the fish - it solves today and nothing more. What would the 'teaching to fish' version look like - and why might it feel slower and harder in the moment, yet be the far kinder thing?
The longest-lasting help builds the other person's own CAPABILITY, instead of their dependence on you. The fish solves the problem once, then the need returns; teaching the skill, lending the tool, opening the door, sitting beside them while they learn solves it for a LIFETIME - because now they can do it themselves, and they carry the quiet pride of their own accomplishment. The kindest help is the help that, in the end, makes them no longer need you - and sets them free.

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

The good washed away every Monday for one quiet reason: you kept giving Otsile the fish, and never the fishing rod. Five moves:

  • Help that is consumed helps for a day: When you give the finished answer, the cash, or the quick fix, the relief is real but it is temporary. The very same need comes back next week, and the week after, forever - because the only thing that would END it, the person's own ability to handle it, never grew. You can give the fish a hundred times and they will still be hungry the hundred-and-first.
  • Always giving the fish can rob them of the skill - and the pride: Here is the part that aches: by always stepping in with the finished solution, you accidentally take from Otsile the one thing he most needs - the experience of struggling with it, working it out, and finally getting it right himself. And you take something else too: the deep, quiet pride of 'I did this on my own.' Your kindness, given this way, unintentionally denies him the chance to develop the capability you want for him.
  • The kindest help makes you unnecessary: This is the turn that changes everything: the true aim of lasting help is not to be someone's hero forever. It is to build their strength until, for this problem, they simply do not need you anymore. Love that wants the best for someone wants them FREE - free of the problem, and free of needing you to solve it. Making yourself unnecessary, here, is the most generous thing you can do.
  • A hand up builds capability for a lifetime: So instead of the fish, give the fishing lesson. Teach the skill. Lend the tool. Open the door and introduce them. Sit beside them and let them try, and fail, and try again, while you resist grabbing the pen. This is the help that does not wash away on Monday - it becomes part of who they are, and it serves them for the rest of their life.
  • It is slower - but it lasts, and it frees: Be honest with yourself: this path is harder. It takes more patience, it is far less instantly satisfying than swooping in to rescue, and you must bear watching someone struggle when you could just do it. But it is the only kind of help that truly frees a person and lasts. (One exception: in a real emergency, you throw the rope FIRST - you do not teach swimming to someone who is drowning. This is for building lasting strength, once the crisis has passed.)

The whole thing lives in one line: the longest-lasting help builds the other person's own capability instead of their dependence on you - help that is consumed feeds them for a day, but help that builds their own capability frees them for a lifetime and gives them the pride of their own accomplishment. You were never wrong to want to help Otsile; you only chose the kind of help that had to be repeated forever and left him no stronger. The deepest love does not want to be needed forever - it wants the person to become free. Teach him to fish, sit with him through the frustration, let him struggle and get it - and one day he does it alone, glowing, and the problem you have fought every Sunday for months is solved, completely and for good. That is the same truth you have learned for your own life: the goal is freedom - and the greatest gift is to help someone else reach theirs.

Movement 3.5 - The Guided Step

Before you take this into your own life, watch the two kinds of help play out for Thapelo and Otsile.

See the fish and the fishing lesson, side by side

The fish - doing it for him. Every Sunday, Thapelo does Otsile's algebra for him. The homework gets handed in, ticked, done. But Otsile still cannot do it. He dreads every test, freezes when Thapelo is not around, and feels smaller each week for needing the help. The instant Thapelo stops, the good vanishes - because nothing was ever left behind in Otsile himself. It is a problem that must be solved again, and again, and again, forever.

The fishing lesson - teaching him. One Sunday, Thapelo says, gently: "I'm not going to do it for you anymore - but I'll sit with you and show you how." The first session is slow and frustrating. Otsile gets it wrong, sighs, wants to give up; Thapelo has to bite his tongue and not just grab the pen. The second is a little better. Then, a few weeks in, Thapelo's phone rings - it is Otsile, not stuck, but bursting: "I did it. The whole thing. All by myself."

And that is the difference. The fish fed Otsile for one night and left him just as helpless. The fishing lesson cost Thapelo more patience for a few weeks - and then freed Otsile for the rest of his life. One had to be given forever; the other only had to be given once. The most loving help was the help that, in the end, Otsile no longer needed at all.

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

Somewhere there is someone you keep helping the same way, week after week, and it never quite sticks. This week, change the KIND of help:

  1. Spot the 'fish' you keep giving. Name one person you regularly help and the help that solves it only for today - the answer you give, the task you do for them, the form you fill in for them every single month. Notice that the need always comes back, because nothing was left behind in them.
  2. Find the 'hand up' hiding inside it. Ask: what skill, tool, or open door would let them handle this themselves next time? Teach the method instead of giving the answer; sit beside them while they practise the task you have always done for them; introduce them to the person who can open the door. Build the capability, not the dependence.
  3. Offer it patiently - and believe in them. Teaching is slower and harder; you must let them struggle and resist rescuing. But do it anyway, because it says the most respectful thing one person can say to another: 'I believe you are capable of standing on your own two feet.' (And remember - in a real emergency, you throw the rope first; this is for the lasting help that comes after.)
R

This week, pick one 'fish' you keep giving and offer the 'hand up' instead - then watch how differently it feels to build someone up rather than carry them. (Working with an AMU facilitator? Map one person's fish and hand-up together.)

This is where your whole journey comes full circle. You set out to build your own freedom; you learned to be the change you wished to see; you learned to care for yourself so your giving could last. And here is the deepest part of all of it: the greatest gift you can ever give another person is not a thing you hand them, but the capability to provide for themselves - to become free, just as you are becoming free, and to feel the quiet glory of standing on their own. Give the kind of help that stays - the skill, the tool, the open door - and the good you do will not wash away on Monday; it will last a lifetime, in someone who can now stand tall. That is the change you can be. Be the change. Develop the Capability. You can.
