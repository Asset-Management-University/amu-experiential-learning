The business is just me - so how do I keep a problem in the business from reaching my family's home?

Movement 1 - The Felt Problem

You are Muhammad, and Tank You - delivering 9kg gas cylinders to homes - is good, honest work you're proud of. The business is just... you. You take the orders, you make the deliveries, the money goes into your bank account. Simple. Then one evening a furious call: a customer claims the cylinder you connected has somehow damaged their expensive cooker, they're talking lawyers, and they're demanding R10,000 for a replacement and damages. You know you connected it correctly - you're sure it isn't your fault - but they're threatening court.

That night you can't sleep, and for the first time a cold truth sinks in. There is no "Tank You" - there is only you. The name on the van, the promises, the work - it's all legally just Muhammad. So this isn't only a threat to the business; it's a threat to you: there's a direct, open path from their claim to your personal bank account, your family's savings, the roof over their heads. You built this to provide for your family - and one dispute, even an unfair one, could take it all.

The business is just me - so how do I build a wall so a problem in the business stays IN the business, and can never reach my family's home?

Movement 2 - Your Attempt

Your first instinct is to panic - which leads to doing nothing useful. You could hope it goes away: you're careful and honest, surely this won't really happen - but hope is not a wall; one ordinary day, one accident or one determined customer is all it takes. You could decide you need an expensive lawyer and a full company today: the cost and complexity feel overwhelming, like something only for big rich businesses, so fear freezes you and you do nothing - leaving the open path exactly as it was. Or you could pull all the money out to "hide" it: but that starves the business and is no real wall against a claim anyway.

Each fails for the same reason - none touches the real problem: the open path between your business life and your home life. So before you read on: what could you put in that path - and could you build it one affordable step at a time, rather than all at once?

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

The problem is the open path; the answer is to build a wall across it - and you don't build the whole fortress in a day. You build it in affordable layers, in the right order. Five moves:

  • See the open path: name the real danger plainly: 'the business is just me, so any claim against it runs straight to my home and savings.' The problem was never only this one dispute - it's that there's no separation at all, so an ordinary business risk has a direct, open path to your family's life. Block the path, and the problem stays in the business.
  • Layer 1 - written terms, before the deal (the paper wall): your cheapest, fastest layer. Before any work or sale, a simple written agreement the customer accepts - not 20 pages of jargon, a clear one-pager: exactly what you'll do, what you are and aren't responsible for, the price and terms. It defines responsibility in advance and leaves a record - so a vague, angry claim has a far harder time becoming a ruinous one.
  • Layer 2 - separate the money (the money wall): open an account that is ONLY the business's: all business income in, all business costs out. Your personal account is now its own thing - the family's food, the rent, your life. That clear line means a business debt is paid from business money, and stops your family's savings from being seen as part of the same pot.
  • Layer 3 - a separate legal body (the legal wall): as you grow, the strongest layer: with a professional's help, make the venture its own legal 'person' - its own name, its own account, its own debts. Now a claim is against the business, not against you; it stops at the wall, putting the business's assets at risk but leaving your home and savings protected on the other side.
  • The honest truths + the timing: the wall is powerful, not magic. It has DUTIES - keep the line genuinely clean (never treat the business account as a personal piggy bank, or the wall is just paper) and meet its small admin/fees. It does NOT cover real negligence or fraud - doing good, safe, honest work is always the first protection. A personal guarantee you sign punches a hole straight through it (see bp-0013). And you must build it BEFORE the dispute: you can't build a wall in the middle of a storm.

So the wall rises in layers you can actually afford: a one-page written agreement before the next job (free, today); a separate business account this week; and, as you grow, a separate legal body so a claim hits "the business", not Muhammad. But hold the honest truths beside it: keep the line genuinely clean (mix the money and the wall becomes mere paper), do safe and honest work (the wall never covers negligence or fraud - that protection is yours to earn), and never let a casually-signed personal guarantee punch a hole through it. Above all, build it BEFORE the storm: you cannot raise a wall in the middle of a dispute - the day you most need it is the day before you discover you needed it. You built the business to create security for your family, not to risk it; the wall is how you honour that.

Movement 3.5 - The Guided Step

The open path runs under any venture where your work and your name are the same thing - not just deliveries. Watch a freelancer.

See the same wall built by a freelance writer

Anya writes articles for technology websites; her business is just her name. An unhappy client claims an error in her article cost them a big contract, and threatens to sue her for their lost profits. Like Muhammad, Anya sees there's no wall between that claim and her personal savings - so she builds one in the same affordable layers:

• Written terms: every project now starts with a simple agreement - the scope, the number of revisions, and a clear line that she isn't liable for the client's business outcomes.
• Separate money: a new account just for her writing income and costs.
• Separate legal body: as her income grows, she forms a small company - now the contracts (and any claims) are with the business, and her home sits safely on the other side of the wall.

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

Think of your venture - or even a side-income project. For most people starting out, the business is just them; that's the normal starting point, not a failing. Now build from there:

  1. See your open path. Name a plausible, ordinary risk in your work - an unhappy customer, an unpaid supplier, an accident - and picture it happening. Trace the path from that business problem straight to your personal savings and home.
  2. Build your first layer today, then plan the next. Draft a one-page written agreement (or a standard email) for your very next customer - a wall you can build in an hour, for free. Open a separate business account this week. Learn what a separate legal body costs and requires in your area, so you know when it's the right time for that strongest layer.
  3. Commit to the honest truths. This wall is for honest risk, never for hiding from what you genuinely owe or for unsafe work. Keep the money cleanly separate, do good and safe work, and read before you ever sign a personal guarantee.

Having no wall isn't foolishness - it's simply where every small venture begins, the part no one warns you about. The quiet danger is that the very thing you built to provide for your family could, on one ordinary day, be the thing that takes everything from them. So build the wall, in layers you can afford, while the skies are clear: written terms, separated money, and in time a separate legal body - kept honest and kept clean. A business is meant to shelter the people you love, never to put them in the path of the storm.
