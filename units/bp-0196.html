My uncle paid full tax for forty years and still faces a tight old age - how do I save for my own future without losing a chunk to tax I could have kept?

Movement 1 - The Felt Problem

You are Tankiso, and on a quiet Sunday you are helping your uncle Kefiloe sort through a box of old paperwork. Kefiloe is a good man - he worked as a technician his whole life, never missed a day he could help it, raised his children well. As you sort, he says it quietly, almost in passing: retirement is coming, and it is going to be tight. He saved a little over the years in an ordinary account, but after tax and life and the children, it was never much. Mostly, he says with a small shrug, he will lean on the State old-age grant when the time comes.

You have been learning about money lately, and something makes you pick up one of his old payslips. There it is, month after month, year after year: a big chunk taken off the top for tax - "PAYE" - before he ever saw a cent of his own pay. Forty years of those payslips are in this box. Forty years of full tax, paid faithfully, every month.

And a cold, horrible realisation settles over you. All that tax, paid in full for four decades - and then he tried to save from whatever was left. You think of what you have been learning, and a question rises that you almost do not want to ask him.

For forty years you paid your full tax, and then tried to save from what was left over? And no one ever told you that if you had saved in a special retirement account, the taxman would have handed a big slice of that tax back to you - every single year?

Movement 2 - Your Attempt

Wanting to make sure this never happens to you - or to the people you love - three thoughts tempt you, and each one is exactly how Kefiloe ended up here. "Retirement is decades away - I will worry about it much later." But the tax break and the sheltered growth are most powerful over long stretches, so the early years you skip are the most valuable ones you can ever lose - and old age comes for everyone, ready or not. "The State grant will look after me when I am old." But that grant is built for bare survival, not for a free or dignified life; leaning on it is not a plan, it is leaving your whole future to chance. "I do not want my money locked away until I am old." But the lock is the very thing that protects your future self from your present self - and there is a reason the taxman rewards you for accepting it. The real question is what that reward is.

So before you read on: why would the government actually WANT you to save for your own old age - and what deal might they offer to make you do it?

Sit with the unfairness of Kefiloe's payslips: forty years, full tax paid every month, and then he tried to save from whatever was left over. What if there had been a way to save for retirement where the taxman gave a big slice of that tax BACK to him each year - so the same saving cost him far less? Why might the government offer something like that?
Think about why a government would WANT you to save for your own old age. If you provide for yourself, you will not need to lean on the State when you are old. So they offer a deal: save for retirement in a special account, and we will let you deduct it from your taxable income - meaning we refund you tax on every rand you put in. What would you have to give up in return for a deal that good?
It is called a Retirement Annuity (RA). You deduct your contributions from your taxable income, so the taxman refunds part of every rand you save (at your tax rate) - put in R1,000 at a 26% rate and R260 comes back, so it only cost you R740. The money then grows tax-sheltered for decades. The catch - and the point - is that it is LOCKED until retirement, which is exactly what keeps it safe for the future you. It is how you provide for your own old age, instead of depending on the small State grant.

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

Kefiloe was never careless. He simply was never told about the deal. Five moves:

  • See the double gift: When you save for retirement in this special way, you get not one gift but two. First, the taxman gives you a REFUND on your contributions now - he treats you as if you earned less, so you pay less tax this year. Second, all the money inside the account - your contributions AND every bit of growth - is sheltered from tax for decades. A refund going in, and tax-free growth all the way along.
  • How the refund actually works: Your contributions are deducted from your taxable income. So imagine you are taxed at 26% on your top rand, and you put R1,000 into your retirement account. The taxman now treats you as if you earned R1,000 less, which saves you R260 in tax. That means R1,000 of saving for your future only cost you R740 out of your own pocket - the other R260 was tax you would have paid anyway, handed back to you for saving.
  • The power of decades: That refund, every single year, is real money you keep and can invest. And it sits alongside decades of tax-sheltered growth on everything inside the account. Over a working lifetime, the gap between someone who used this break and someone who paid full tax and saved the leftovers is not small - it is the difference between a tight, dependent old age and a free, dignified one.
  • The trade-off is the point: In return for the tax break, the money is LOCKED until you reach retirement age - you cannot dip into it for a holiday or a new phone. That can feel like a downside, but it is actually the protection: it keeps the money safe from your present self's wants, guaranteeing it is there for the one person who will need it most - you, when you are old. The rule: you can contribute up to 27.5% of your income, capped in the 2026/27 tax year at R430,000 a year. SARS sets that cap and does change it - it rose from R350,000 in March 2026, its first increase since 2016 - so check the current figure with SARS, or a qualified tax practitioner, before you rely on it.
  • Name it, and the dignity in it: This is a Retirement Annuity (RA), and it is the main way you provide for your OWN future. Using one is an act of dignity and self-care: you make sure that you arrive in old age free and provided for, never having to depend on a small State grant just to survive. Use it alongside your TFSA once you earn a taxable income - the TFSA for flexible tax-free growth, the RA for the tax refund and your retirement. Two tools, both keeping the taxman's hands off your future.

The whole thing lives in one line: a retirement annuity hands you a tax refund now for saving for your own future, then shelters that money from tax for decades - so you arrive in old age free and provided for, instead of dependent. The government offers this deal for a simple reason: if you take care of your own future, you will not need to lean on the State - so they reward you for it, with a slice of your own tax handed back every year you contribute. The lock until retirement is not a punishment; it is the quiet guarantee that the money will actually be there when the older you needs it most. What stung about Kefiloe's payslips was forty years of a refund he never claimed, and an old age that is tighter than it needed to be. You can be the one who claims it - who decides, now, that your future self will be looked after, with dignity, by you.

Movement 3.5 - The Guided Step

Before you take this into your own life, see the deal play out for two people who earn exactly the same.

See Boago and Rorisang save the same R2,000 a month

Boago and Rorisang are both 25, earn the same salary, and each decides to put R2,000 a month - R24,000 a year - toward their retirement.

Boago uses an ordinary investment account. He earns his salary, pays his full income tax, and then invests R24,000 a year of what is left. On top of that, he pays tax on the growth his investment makes along the way. Solid, honest saving - with no help from the taxman at all.

Rorisang uses a Retirement Annuity. She also puts in R24,000 a year - but because it is an RA, she deducts that R24,000 from her taxable income. Say her top rand is taxed at 26% (this is just for illustration). The taxman now treats her as if she earned R24,000 less, and refunds her R24,000 x 26% = R6,240. So her R24,000 of retirement saving only cost her about R17,760 out of pocket - and the whole amount then grows tax-sheltered inside the RA, with no tax on the growth.

Same R2,000 a month, same 40 years - but Rorisang saved the same amount for far less out of her own pocket, AND her money grew untaxed the entire way. By retirement, the gap between their two pots is life-changing. That is the power of getting a tax refund for providing for your own future. (Illustrative figures - your tax rate varies; the RA money is locked until retirement and still rises and falls with the market.)

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

You cannot open an RA until you earn a taxable income - but you can decide now what kind of old age you are building toward:

  1. Picture your future self. Hold a clear picture of you in old age. Do you want a life of freedom and dignity, choosing how you spend your days - or one of bare survival, leaning on a small grant? That older person is real, and they are depending on the choices the young you makes now.
  2. Know the tool, and plan to use it. Once you earn enough to pay tax, an RA lets you claim a refund on your retirement contributions (up to 27.5% of your income, capped at R430,000 in the 2026/27 tax year - a figure SARS changes, so check the current one) and grow them tax-sheltered. Plan to use it - and use it alongside your TFSA: the TFSA for flexible tax-free growth, the RA for the tax refund and your retirement.
  3. Make peace with the lock. The RA money is locked until retirement, and that is the point - it is the wall that keeps your future safe from your present wants. See it not as money taken away, but as money set aside, by you, for the one person guaranteed to need it: the older you.

What stung most about Kefiloe was not that he was foolish - he was never told. He paid every cent of tax he owed for forty years, and no one ever showed him the door the government had left open: save for your own old age, and we will hand a slice of that tax back, every year. You have just been shown that door. Providing for your future self is not fear, and it is not a burden you carry for others - it is the quiet, deep dignity of knowing you will be all right, and never having to depend on anyone. That is you, being the change - deciding, today, that the older you will arrive cared-for and free, by your own hand.
