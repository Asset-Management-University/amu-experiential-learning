You're busy spotting problems, but you aren't actually solving them - the same dangers keep coming back.

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

Spotting a problem is only half a loop - the easy half. The other half is landing the fix so it stays fixed. (A forgotten finding is a danger you saw and chose to leave in place for the people you serve; closing the loop is keeping faith with them.) Five moves:

  • See where the energy stops: you're good at the first half - spotting problems and writing them down. But that's where the energy stops: the fix is left as a vague wish ('someone should look into it'), with no owner, no date, and no one ever checking it was done. So the item gets marked 'closed' because a sentence was written or a meeting happened - not because anything actually changed.
  • Turn the wish into a real, physical task: a wish is 'someone should be more careful'. A task is specific and physical: 'paint a yellow square in the yard to show exactly where empty cylinders must be stored.' Vague wishes can't be done, owned, or checked - so make the fix concrete enough that anyone could see whether it happened.
  • Give it one owner and one date: 'someone' never gets anything done. A real fix needs ONE named owner and a date they agree to - that turns it into a promise. Not 'we should sort the yard' but 'Thabo, can you own this and have the square painted by Friday?'. One owner, one date: a fix with neither is a hope, not a plan (this is bp-0108's single accountable owner, applied to every fix).
  • Fix the real 'why', not the symptom: aim the fix at why it happened, not the surface. The cylinders weren't a 'careless driver' problem - the why was that there was no clear, official place for empties. The yellow square fixes the why. Tidy the symptom ('clear the cylinders today') and it's back tomorrow; change the cause and it stays fixed (this is bp-0102's why-not-who, carried into the fix).
  • Earn the word 'closed' - go back and verify: the most important step. It's not closed when the owner says it's done, nor even when you see the new paint. It's closed only after you go back - a week later - and see with your own eyes, on the ground, that the empties are inside the square and the walkway is clear: real evidence the problem is gone and staying gone. 'Closed' is a word earned with proof, not a box ticked to tidy a list.

Movement 3.5 - The Guided Step

The findings-graveyard forms wherever the energy stops at the meeting. Watch it at a clinic.

See the same half-loop at a health clinic

Every year an inspector finds the same problem at a small clinic: patient records aren't stored securely. And every year the manager writes the same action - "remind all staff about the privacy policy" - it's ticked "closed" at a meeting, and every year the problem comes back. The energy stopped at the meeting.

The missing half: a nurse finally says the real cause out loud - "the lock on the records cabinet has been broken for two years." So the real why is a broken lock, not forgetful staff; the real task is "order and fit a new lock"; the Practice Manager owns it, due Friday; and it is not closed when the lock is ordered, or even fitted - only when someone physically goes to the cabinet, finds it locked, and confirms the records inside are now secure. The fix is verified; the loop is closed.

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

Look at your own world - a work to-do list, a team's meeting actions, the jobs for your own home. Is there a problem that keeps coming back: a garage you keep "sorting", a report that's always late, a promise to "get organised" that never quite happens? You're stuck in the first half of the loop. This time, land the fix:

  1. Turn the wish into a real, physical task with a real why. Not "sort the garage" but "put up one shelf for the paint tins" - because the why is you need a shelf, not more tidying.
  2. Give it one owner and one date. It might be you: "I'll put the shelf up by Sunday at 4pm." One named owner, one agreed date - a promise, not a hope.
  3. Earn the word "Closed". Don't tick it off the day it's done - go back and check. A week later, are the tins still on the shelf and the floor still clear? When you see with your own eyes that the problem is gone and staying gone, you've completed the loop and earned the right to call it closed.

Being good at spotting and diligent at recording is real, valuable work - it's just the first half. The list that's full of "Done" but full of returning problems isn't a lie you told on purpose; it's the loop no one taught you to finish. And finishing it isn't more paperwork - it's less, because problems that are truly landed stop coming back. Every loop you close is a danger removed from someone's path for good, and a promise - to the people you serve - actually kept.
