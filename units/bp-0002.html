Am I building, or am I in a hole?

Movement 1 - The Felt Problem

You are Muhammad, 21. Since the month you finally saw where your money was leaking, something changed. You plugged the gap. You stopped the small things from swallowing your wage. And this month, for the first time you can remember, there's a little bit left over.

It's a quiet, careful pride. You're making it. You're handling your money like a grown man.

Then your phone buzzes. A message from your friend Themba:

"Yoh bro, hope you're good. Any chance you can sort me out with that R1,500 when you have a moment? No rush if not, just checking in."

The R1,500 you borrowed from him a while back. You feel that flicker of pride again - this is the new you. You can sort this. You open your banking app, ready to send it across and feel good about it.

The balance loads: R1,200.

You read it twice. You can't even cover the R1,500 you owe Themba - not from what's in your account. And just like that, the careful pride drains out of you. That feeling of I'm okay now - what was it, exactly? You're no longer sure it was ever true.

Your mind scrambles for a lifeline. Wait - Lwazi still owes me that R600. I'll call him, get it, send Themba. For a second, relief. Then the thought that follows turns your stomach: ...but if I'm chasing what people owe me, who's about to chase what I owe them? The store-card account. My uncle, for Dad in hospital. How deep does this actually go?

You stop walking. You're not asking can I pay Themba? anymore. You're asking something bigger - something you've never once sat down and answered:

What do I actually own? After everyone's paid off... what's truly mine - is there anything?

You get home, ignore everything else, and find a pen and the back of an envelope. You need to see the number. You have to know.

Movement 2 - Your Attempt

It's all here, jumbled together. The answer is too - but not as a single number you can read off. You have to make it. Before you move on, have a real go at working it out for yourself.

This is a jumble - all mixed up. How do I get one clear number out of it? Not like this. I'd have to bring some order to it first... and when I really look, there are only two kinds of thing here: money I have or am owed, and money I owe to someone else.

Okay - two piles now, that's clearer. But I still need ONE number, and I'm staring at two lists. What if I total each pile first?

So I have a total for what I own, and a total for what I owe. The question was: after everyone's paid back, what is mine? That has to be the one taken away from the other.

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

So, what did you find?

If you split the pile into things you OWN and things you OWE, totalled each, and took one away from the other - that's exactly the move. There was no shortcut. A scatter of money-facts only becomes an answer when you sort it into those two piles and subtract. Here's one honest way it lands:

What you own
  • Cash (bank)R1,200
  • LaptopR3,000
  • PhoneR2,500
  • Lwazi owes meR600
Total owned: R7,300
What you owe
  • Uncle (Dad's hospital)R8,000
  • Store-card accountR900
  • ThembaR1,500
Total owed: R10,400
Net worth = R7,300 − R10,400 = -R3,100

Take a breath. This number isn't a judgement. It's a starting point.

A negative net worth at 21 isn't failure - it's where most people quietly start. The strange thing is how it feels: not worse, but lighter. The dread was vague and bottomless. -R3,100 has edges. It's a hole of a known depth - which means it can be climbed out of.

And notice the trap you just stepped out of: your monthly cash flow turned positive - there was money left over - yet your net worth is negative. They're not the same. One is about the month; the other is about the whole position. Feeling okay this month told you nothing about whether you were truly building.

But before you trust that -R3,100, look again at one line on the OWN side.

Lwazi owes me R600. Is that really mine? Lwazi has a new baby; things are tighter for him than for you. If you needed it today, could you even ask - and would it come?

Cross it out, just to see. Own drops to R6,700. Your honest net worth is closer to -R3,700. The lesson underneath the lesson: not all assets are equal. Cash in your hand is solid; money someone owes you is a hope, not a fact. An honest score counts the difference.

The Method: Net worth = what you own minus what you owe. It's the real score - not what's in your account this week, but whether, all things counted, you're building or sinking.

So what does the picture really say?

The man who looks like he's winning is R16,100 in the hole - more than five times deeper than you. The very phone that makes him look rich is the thing sinking him. Net worth is not net appearance. You can't read someone's score off their photos - only off their own minus their owe.

The deep lesson: two people can look like opposites and BE the opposite of how they look. The score was never what you show the world - it's what you own minus what you owe.

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

Your problem was never really the R1,200 in your account. It was that you had no idea where you actually stood - so a good month could fool you into thinking you were building when you were sinking. The move you just practised - gather, split, total, subtract - is yours now.

Today you found your real starting line. The first sum put it at -R3,100 - but the truly honest score, knowing a debt owed to you is a hope and not cash, is closer to -R3,700. A net worth isn't a verdict - it's the distance between where you stand and where you want to be, finally made visible. So before you go, make it yours:

R

That goal is what the line climbs toward. Every debt you chip, and every rand you build, moves it closer.

Here's how to find your own real score:

  1. Gather your money-facts. Open your banking app, your account SMSes, your notes. Pull together everything you own or are owed, and everything you owe - the way you just did for Muhammad. A pen and paper is all you need.
  2. Total each side and subtract. Add up what you own. Add up what you owe. Take owe away from own. That number - positive or negative - is your starting line today. And be honest, like with Lwazi: a debt owed to you is a hope, not cash in hand.
  3. Move the line up by one. You don't fix it all at once. What's one move this month - one debt chipped, one thing owned - that nudges the line the right way? That's how building starts.

You've done the real work the moment you find your own number. Make it stick: say it out loud to someone you trust - what you expected, what it actually was, and the one move you will make. Naming it to another person is how a plan becomes a promise. (And if you are working with an AMU facilitator, bring it there too.) The understanding you carry into your own life is the real prize - and in time, the wisdom you gain is what makes this path clearer for those who come after you.
