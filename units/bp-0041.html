A dream contract - behind a wall of paperwork

Movement 1 - The Felt Problem

You are Muhammad, 21. Tank You has a rhythm now - real customers, real routes, a real little profit. Then the phone rings, and it is Fatima Salie from the Baraka Community Kitchen. She has heard you are reliable. Her gas supplier keeps letting her down, and she has new funding. She wants Tank You to be her standing supplier - two cylinders every week, every week.

For one bright second you could fly. Not just a hustle - a supplier to a kitchen that feeds people. Then she adds, gently: "You're registered, of course? My funders need a registered business - a proper, numbered invoice and a tax number. And I'd need it sorted by the first of next month." The first of next month. You count it off on your fingers. Four weeks.

And the bright second collapses. Registered business. Tax number. Proper invoices. The words stack up into one grey concrete wall in your mind, and behind it a hot flush of shame - you are not those things, you are two brothers and a bakkie. You hear yourself starting to say it, the safe thing, the small thing: "Ah, Sister Fatima, that's a wonderful opportunity, but I don't think we're quite ready..."

You catch yourself. Because turning this down would be turning down the exact thing you have been working toward - out of fear of a wall you have not even looked at properly. The real question stops you:

Is this wall actually impossible in four weeks - or does it just LOOK impossible because I've never broken it down?

Movement 2 - Your Attempt

You tell Fatima, honestly, "Let me call you back tomorrow." Then you sit with Ibraaheem and the wall. Right now it is just one word - "registered" - and one feeling: too big, too slow, no chance.

Before you read on, see if you can turn that wall into something you could actually measure. If you wanted to know - really know - whether four weeks is enough, what would you have to do to the requirements first? What questions would you ask of each piece?

I keep seeing 'get registered' as one giant wall. But it isn't one thing - it's a few separate things. What if I wrote them out, one by one, instead of letting them blur into one impossible mountain?
For each one, two questions: how long does it really take, and what has to be DONE before I can even start it? Some can't begin until another finishes - but some I could start today, right now, alongside the rest.
So I don't add up everything. I find the longest CHAIN of must-follow steps - that's my real timeline - and let the independent ones run in parallel. Then I compare that honestly to Fatima's deadline.

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

The wall was never one thing. It only felt impossible because it was a blur. The skill here is not "how to register a business" - it is something you will use for the rest of your life: how to map any pile of requirements against a deadline. Five moves:

  • 1. Break the wall into bricks: stop treating 'get formal' as one thing. Write out the actual, separate requirements - each on its own line.
  • 2. Cost each brick: time + what it needs first: for each, ask two things: how many days does it REALLY take, and what must be FINISHED before I can start it?
  • 3. Build the chain: put them in order - some cannot begin until an earlier one is done (you cannot open the business account before the registration papers exist).
  • 4. Run the independent bricks in parallel: anything that depends on nothing, start TODAY, alongside the rest (set up the invoices while you wait for the registration).
  • 5. Add the LONGEST path, compare to the deadline: your real timeline is the longest chain of must-follow steps - not the sum of everything. If it fits the deadline, the wall was a set of climbable hills.

This is the move that turns "I can't" into "by when." And it comes with one more skill - the honest answer you give while the clock runs. You do not lie ("We're already registered!") and you do not surrender ("We can't"). You give a truthful holding response: "We've begun putting the formalities in place, and we're on track to be ready by this date - may we proceed on that basis?" That is not faking it till you make it. It is planning it till you make it - and it is how professionals keep a door open with their integrity intact.

One honest warning, too: being formal is not a one-day task you finish. It brings ongoing duties - keeping records, paying tax. That is the real price of the bigger game, and it is worth paying. (Your separate books will carry that weight.)

Movement 3.5 - The Guided Step

So you and Ibraaheem stop staring at the wall and start mapping it - on the back of an order pad.

See the wall turned into a climbable map

You grab your phone, and a few quick searches give you a rough time for each (estimates, not prophecies). You write out the real bricks, and beside each, the time it takes and what it needs done first:

Register the business - needs: your ID documents - about 3 days.
Open a business bank account - needs: the registration done first - about 1 day.
Get a tax number - needs: the registration done first - about 10 days.
Set up numbered invoices - needs: nothing; do it now - about 2 hours.

The chain: registration must come first; then the bank account and the tax number can both run from it. The invoices wait for no one - start them today.

The longest path: registration (3 days) then the tax number (10 days) = about 13 working days - under three weeks. Your deadline is four. It fits, with a week to spare.

The mountain was four small hills in a sensible order. Now you can phone Fatima with a real date and a clear conscience.

Notice what the map gave you: not just an answer, but a defensible one - a plan you could show Fatima, a lender, or a partner to prove you think like a professional, not a chancer. That written-down chain is itself proof of how you work.

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

You almost said no to your own dream because a wall looked solid. It was not. It was a sequence - and sequences can be timed, ordered, and beaten. That skill is yours now, for every "you must be X before you can do Y" you will ever meet.

For your own wall:

  1. Break it into bricks. Write out every separate requirement the opportunity demands. Do not judge them - just list them.
  2. Time each, and find what it depends on. How many days does each really take? What must be finished before it can start? Order them into a chain, and start the independent ones today.
  3. Add the longest path and compare to the deadline. If it fits, go. If it is tight, send a truthful holding response with a real date - never a status you do not yet hold.
R

Map your wall, then make the honest call or the honest holding response - out loud, to the person on the other side. (Working with an AMU facilitator? Bring it there too.)

Most people stay small because a wall looked solid and they never walked up to it. The ones who grow learn this quiet truth: nearly every wall is a staircase you simply have not put in order yet.
