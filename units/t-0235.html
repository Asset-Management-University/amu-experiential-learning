The stage you are in

T1: The Ache

Your business is alive because of you. Customers are happy, money is coming in, the work is good. By every outside measure it is working - and yet you are drowning. Every decision, every problem, every customer runs through you; you cannot take a day off without it all quietly stopping; and the harder you push to grow, the more the whole thing strains at the seams.

Here is why. You are trying to grow a business that has no shape beyond you - and the work that got you here is not the work that will get you to the next stage. What you need is not more effort. It is to know exactly where your business is, so you can do the one thing that stage actually requires.

My business works, but everything runs through me, I cannot step away, and pushing harder to grow only strains it more. Where is my business actually, and what should I be doing?

Before you read on, decide. The business works. Customers are happy, the money comes in, and you are good at what you do. You are also the only person who can quote a job, the only one who knows which suppliers to trust, and the only one who can settle a customer when something goes wrong. You have not had a full week off in two years, and when your daughter was ill you worked from the hospital car park. Last quarter you pushed hard for growth — took on more work, tried to hire — and everything strained: quality slipped, you worked later, and you pulled back. The harder you push, the more it strains. Write down what you would do — your own answer, not a shortlist to choose from.

Now compare — the five answers, and the two markers that place you

• “Work harder and push through it.” You have just run that experiment and it strained the whole thing. Effort applied to the wrong stage's work tightens the constraint rather than releasing it, because the constraint is you.

• “Hire somebody to take the load.” You tried, and notice why it did not stick: there is nothing for a new person to step into. Everything they would need — how you quote, which suppliers, what good looks like — exists only in your head.

• “Get more customers — growth solves everything.” More demand through a channel that is already at its limit. Every additional customer arrives at the same single point, which is the thing already breaking.

• “Buy a system — software will sort it out.” A tool can hold a process and cannot invent one. Software over an undefined way of working digitises the dependency on you.

• “Accept it — this is what running your own business is.” Widely believed, and it is the belief that keeps a business at one person's size forever, including on the days you cannot be there.

• The diagnosis is not effort: the work that got you here is not the work that gets you to the next stage. Businesses move through three stages, and they are not about size or age but about two things you can read today — whether the revenue is consistent, and what you actually spend your time on. Those two markers place you, and the point is not a label: each stage has one job, and knowing yours gives you permission to stop doing everything and do the thing that matters now. In the first, income is unpredictable and the founder does absolutely everything — not a failure, the beginning — and the single job is to find a repeatable way to win and serve a customer, not to grow or hire or build systems. In the second, income is steadier and the founder becomes the constraint; the job is to build the shape, so the thing can run without one person holding every thread. Only in the third is growing the work. Almost every growth problem is a founder doing the wrong stage's job — and yours is the second stage's job being skipped while you attempt the third's.

There are three stages, and each has a different job. Let us find yours.

T2: The Tool in Action

This is how you find out which stage your business is in - survival, stability, or scale - and do the one job that stage requires, instead of the wrong stage's work.

1. Three stages, three jobs - placed by two markers

A business grows through THREE STAGES, and they are not about size or age but about TWO SIMPLE THINGS you can read today: IS THE REVENUE CONSISTENT? and WHAT IS THE FOUNDER ACTUALLY DOING? Those two markers place you. And the point of placing yourself is NOT a label - it is that EACH STAGE HAS ONE CLEAR JOB, and KNOWING YOUR STAGE GIVES YOU PERMISSION TO STOP DOING EVERYTHING AND START DOING THE ONE THING THAT MATTERS NOW. The three stages are Survival, Stability and Scale, and almost every growth problem you will ever have is really a founder doing the wrong stage's work - so the whole technique is to name your stage honestly and do its job, not the exciting next stage's job and not the last stage's.

2. Stage one - Survival: reach consistent income

STAGE ONE, SURVIVAL: the revenue is UNPREDICTABLE - a good month, then a lean one - and the founder does ABSOLUTELY EVERYTHING: sells, delivers, invoices, fixes, cleans up. This is where every business starts, and IT IS NOT A FAILURE; IT IS THE BEGINNING. The one job of this stage is singular and clear: REACH CONSISTENT INCOME by finding a REPEATABLE way to win and serve a customer. NOT to grow big, NOT to hire, NOT to build systems - just to find the repeatable sale that turns the lean months into steady ones. EVERYTHING ELSE IS A DISTRACTION UNTIL THE INCOME IS CONSISTENT. A founder in survival who spends time on org charts, branding polish or hiring plans is doing the wrong stage's work; the only question that matters here is how to make the next sale, and the one after that, reliably.

3. Stage two - Stability: document and delegate

STAGE TWO, STABILITY: the revenue is now steady and reliable - and a NEW, QUIETER PROBLEM has appeared: YOU ARE THE BOTTLENECK. Every job, every decision, every customer still runs through you; the business is really A DEMANDING JOB THAT YOU HAPPEN TO OWN, and it cannot grow beyond your own two hands and finite hours. The job of this stage is to DOCUMENT AND DELEGATE - to get the work out of your head and onto paper so someone else can do it to your standard. And hear this clearly: IF YOU ARE THE BOTTLENECK, IT IS BECAUSE YOU DO GOOD WORK AND PEOPLE TRUST YOU. THE BUSINESS IS ALIVE BECAUSE OF YOU. BUT A BUSINESS THAT DEPENDS ON ONE PERSON CANNOT GROW, AND IT CANNOT GIVE THAT PERSON A REST. THE JOB NOW IS NOT TO WORK HARDER, BUT TO BUILD A SYSTEM THAT CAN DO YOUR GOOD WORK EVEN WHEN YOU ARE NOT THERE. THIS IS HOW YOU BUILD SOMETHING THAT LASTS. (The how of documenting and delegating is its own craft - that is where the systemisation work begins.)

4. Stage three - Scale: work on the business, not only in it

STAGE THREE, SCALE: the systems are documented and OTHER PEOPLE DO THE WORK RELIABLY, so the founder steps OUT of doing and INTO directing - working ON the business (its strategy, its growth, its direction) rather than only IN it (delivering the day-to-day). The DEFINING TEST of this stage is simple: GROWTH NO LONGER REQUIRES MORE OF THE FOUNDER'S HOURS, because the business can add customers without adding you. This is a GENUINELY DIFFERENT KIND OF WORK - less doing, more building and deciding - and it ONLY BECOMES POSSIBLE ONCE THE DOCUMENTING-AND-DELEGATING OF STABILITY HAS ACTUALLY BEEN DONE. You cannot skip stability and leap to scale; the work of getting out of the bottleneck is exactly what makes scale possible, which is why the order of the stages cannot be cheated.

5. Diagnose, do the right job, and choose

Place yourself PLAINLY: inconsistent revenue and you-do-everything is SURVIVAL; consistent revenue but everything-still-runs-through-you is STABILITY; documented systems and you-work-on-not-in is SCALE. Then do THAT stage's job - because the TWO CLASSIC WAYS BUSINESSES COME TO GRIEF are both a founder doing the wrong stage's work. THE FIRST MISTAKE IS TRYING TO SCALE BEFORE YOU ARE STABLE: pouring on marketing and taking on more while the business still runs entirely through you and has no repeatable system - YOU DO NOT SCALE A BUSINESS, YOU SCALE THE CHAOS, AND IT BREAKS (how good businesses collapse from too much success, not too little). THE SECOND MISTAKE IS STAYING THE BOTTLENECK FOREVER: never documenting, never delegating, so you remain the central processor of everything - you have not built a business, you have built yourself THE MOST DEMANDING JOB YOU WILL EVER HAVE. And then choose, deliberately: THE GOAL IS TO CHOOSE YOUR STAGE DELIBERATELY, NOT TO BE TRAPPED IN ONE BY ACCIDENT. A STABLE BUSINESS THAT GIVES YOU THE LIFE YOU WANT IS A SUCCESS. IT IS NOT A FAILURE TO SCALE. Not every founder wants the work of scale, and choosing a stable business on purpose is wisdom, not a lack of ambition.

T4: Your Turn

Here is your situation. A founder runs a small cleaning-services business. She has clawed her way to a consistent R40,000 a month in regular contracts - the income is now steady and reliable. But she still personally does or checks every clean, writes every quote, handles every complaint, and works every hour; she has not had a proper week off in two years, and when she was ill last month, income dropped. She is thinking of spending on advertising to "grow".

  1. Diagnose the stage. Using the two markers (revenue consistency, founder's role), which stage is she in?
  2. Name the one job. What is the single most important job for her now, and what transition does it set up?
  3. Spot the risk. Which of the two classic mistakes is she at risk of making, and why?
  4. Scale or stay? Should she aim to scale, or deliberately choose to stay a stable business? Justify either - both can be right.

The test of a real answer: four things.

First, the stage: Stability - the revenue is consistent (the survival job is done), but everything still runs through her, which is the signature of the bottleneck.

Second, the one job: document and delegate - write down how a clean is done to her standard, and get someone else doing it reliably - which is the transition that makes either scaling or a real rest possible. Working harder is not the job; building the system is.

Third, the risk: the scale-before-stable mistake - spending on advertising to grow while she is still the bottleneck would win her more work than she can personally deliver, and she would scale the chaos and break. She must fix the bottleneck before she pours on demand.

Fourth, scale or stay: either is legitimate. A strong answer might have her delegate and stay stable (build a small reliable team so she can take a proper week off and keep a good living she runs herself) or delegate and then scale (systemise so the business grows without her hours) - but in both the immediate job is the same: get out of the bottleneck. The mark of a real answer is that it treats staying stable as a genuine, respectable choice, not a consolation prize.

T5: The Guardrails

Almost every growth problem is a founder doing the wrong stage's work. Name your stage. Do its job. Choose your destination.

The common traps - and how to dodge them

• Not knowing your stage. Without the diagnosis, every effort is a guess. Read the two markers - revenue consistency and the founder's role - and place yourself honestly.

• Scaling before you are stable. Pouring on demand while the business still runs through you scales the chaos and breaks it. Fix the bottleneck first, then grow.

• Staying the bottleneck forever. If nothing is documented or delegated, you built a job, not a business - one that cannot grow or run without you. Get the work out of your hands.

• Assuming you must scale. Scale is a choice, not a destiny. A stable business that gives you the life you want is a success in its own right.

• Blaming yourself for being the bottleneck. It is the result of doing good work people trust, not a flaw. The fix is a system, not working harder or feeling guilty.

• Confusing busy with growing. Being run off your feet is not progress. Progress is doing the one job your current stage actually requires.

• Chasing size before consistent income. In survival, the only job is the repeatable sale. Do not hire or build systems for an income you do not yet have.

• Documenting nothing. A business that lives only in your head cannot be delegated, sold, or rested from. Write down how the good work is done.

Avoid the two great mistakes - scaling the chaos before you are stable, and staying the bottleneck forever - and choose your destination deliberately, free to build a stable business you love just as much as one you grow.
