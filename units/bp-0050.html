I'm winning - so why does it feel like a trap?

Movement 1 - The Felt Problem

You are Muhammad, 21. By every measure you dreamed of, Tank You is winning. So many orders you turn some away. People asking for you by name. And then the biggest yet: a whole new housing development wants Tank You as their supplier - dozens of households, every week.

You should be celebrating. Instead, your stomach drops. Because you already start at 5am and stop at 8pm. The bakkie already runs all day. There is no room - not one more hour, not one more street - inside you and Ibraaheem. To say yes to the development, you would have to be in two places at once.

So you are about to turn down the very thing you have worked years for. And as you reach for the words, an old fear you thought you had beaten comes back, wearing a new face: if I get sick tomorrow, Tank You does not slow down - it STOPS. Every route, every customer, every trick of doing this well is inside my head and nowhere else. You diversified your income long ago - and somewhere along the way, you became the one thing everything depends on.

It makes no sense, and it is completely true:

I'm winning - so why does it feel like a trap? I cannot work more hours. How do I grow past my own two hands?

Movement 2 - Your Attempt

Your whole life, the answer to "too much work" has been the same: work harder, work longer. But you are already at the wall - there is no harder left. For the first time, the old answer is useless, and you have to find a different kind.

Before you read on, think it through. Sipho is keen to drive for you. So why can't you just hand him a route tomorrow and take the development yourself? What is actually stopping you - and what would you have to do first?

My answer to everything has always been: work harder. But I'm already at 5am to 8pm. There are no more hours. So 'try harder' is not a door - it's the wall I'm already against.
The real problem is that everything - the route, who pays cash, how to check the seal - is only in MY head. Nobody else can do it because I've never gotten it out of my head and onto paper.
So the way out isn't more of me. It's making the work exist OUTSIDE me - the how written down so Sipho can follow it, and the records clear enough to trust. Then I'm not the only one who can do it.

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

Here is the trap, named plainly - and it is a trap that only catches people who are good at what they do:

  • The trap of success: the work, the how, and the records all live in YOUR head - so it cannot grow past your hands, cannot survive you being away, and has no value without you. Your skill built it; now YOU are the ceiling - and the single point of failure.
  • Working harder is NOT the way out: you are already at your limit; more hours buys an inch. The answer is to make the work exist OUTSIDE you, so it no longer needs you for every step.
  • Move 1 - get the METHOD out of your head: write down HOW the work is done well - a simple recipe, route sheet or checklist someone else can follow. Turn your private skill into a shared one.
  • Move 2 - get the RECORD out of your head: keep a clear, simple log of what the work produces and earns - so its value is provable to an outsider, not trapped in your memory (your separate books already help).
  • The honest trade: a written-down, reliable 85% done EVERY time beats a heroic 100% that only you can do - and that drops to 0% the day you are sick. You are not losing your craft; you are promoting yourself from DOING the work to DESIGNING how it is done.

The reason you cannot hand Sipho a route is not that he is incapable. It is that the route does not exist anywhere he can reach it - the order of the streets, who pays cash, who needs a reminder call, how to check a seal is real - all of it lives only in you. The work was never written down, so it cannot leave your hands.

Now, the fear that will rise in you is real, and you should name it: "No one can do it as well as I can." Maybe true. But hear the honest maths: a documented, reliable 85% done every single time is worth far more than a brilliant 100% that only you can deliver - and that becomes 0% the day you are sick. Writing your method down is not admitting someone else is as good as you. It is taking the hard-won brilliance in your head and making it last - so it can grow, survive you, and one day even be sold. That is not a step down from craftsman. It is a step UP - to the one who designs how the work is done.

Movement 3.5 - The Guided Step

So instead of driving the Thursday route yourself for the thousandth time, you sit with Ibraaheem and pour what is in your head onto one page.

See Muhammad's head become a page Sipho can follow

What was only in Muhammad's head - the street order that saves fuel; Mrs Khumalo pays cash on delivery; the flat on the corner needs a reminder call the night before; always break the seal in front of the customer; check the bakkie's water before a hot day.

Becomes a one-page route sheet - the stops in order, a note beside each ("cash" / "call first" / "leave at gate"), and the three safety checks at the top. Plus a simple log: cylinders out, money in, anything odd.

And this one page is what makes hiring Sipho possible at all. Before it, handing over the route was a fantasy - the knowledge lived nowhere he could reach. Now it is a plan you can put in someone's hands. So you do. Day one Sipho is slower and asks questions; by week two he runs the Thursday route as reliably as Muhammad ever did - because the knowledge is now in his hands, not trapped in someone else's head. (The documenting came first; the hire only became possible because of it.)

The result: Muhammad is no longer IN the route - he is freed to set up the new development, train the next driver, and grow the business. He has stopped being the bottleneck. And if he gets sick now, Thursday still happens.

That is the whole move: take what only you know, and what your work really earns, out of your head and onto paper that someone else can trust and follow. It feels slow, and it is never quite finished - but it is the only door out of the trap.

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

Your skill built this - and then quietly caged you in it. The way out was never more of you; it was making the work able to run without you. That move turns a job that owns you into a business you own.

For your own work or venture:

  1. Find what only you can do. What would fall apart if you were sick for a week? That is your bottleneck - and your starting point.
  2. Get the METHOD out of your head. Write down how you do that one thing well - simply enough that someone else could follow it. A single page is enough to start.
  3. Get the RECORD out of your head. Keep a clear, simple log of what it produces and earns - so its value lives on paper, not just in your memory.
R

Pick one thing that is stuck in your head, write it down this week, and hand it to someone - then watch what it frees. (Working with an AMU facilitator? Bring it there too.)

The founder who can never step away has not built a business - they have built a cage that pays. The one who writes it down, hands it over, and steps up to lead has built something that can grow, outlast them, and set them free. That is the difference between working IN your work and working ON it.
