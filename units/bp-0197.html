I earn R8,000 a month more than when I started - so why am I still living payslip to payslip, no closer to free than I was two raises ago?

Movement 1 - The Felt Problem

You are Khumo. You have been working for a couple of years now - a mix of part-time jobs at first, and lately a proper role - and in that time you have had two solid pay rises. They felt like proof you were getting somewhere. The first raise meant moving out of the crowded shared flat into your own small place. The second meant a better phone, some brand-name clothes, and saying 'yes' to nights out without doing the sums first. For a while, you felt like you had arrived.

Tonight you open your savings account, half-expecting to feel proud of how much it has grown across two years and two raises. Instead, you go cold. The number is almost exactly what it was back when you started - two raises ago, on far less money. It has barely moved.

You sit with that, and it is not guilt you feel - it is a baffled kind of dread. Real money has come IN. You can see it on every payslip. And yet none of it seems to have stayed. You are earning thousands more a month than the day you began, and you are still living payslip to payslip, still no closer to free. You have been running harder and harder on the spot - faster legs, same place.

I earn R8,000 a month more than when I started, and I thought I would be saving loads by now. But I am still living payslip to payslip. How can I earn so much more and be just as stuck - where did it all go?

Movement 2 - Your Attempt

Trying to break free, three answers tempt you - and each one keeps you on the treadmill. "I just need to earn even more - the NEXT raise will finally do it." But if every raise so far got swallowed, the next one will too; the problem was never the size of your income, it is what happens to it the moment it lands. "I've earned these upgrades - I deserve to enjoy all of it." You truly do deserve to enjoy your work - but letting the WHOLE raise vanish into a nicer life just buys you a more expensive cage, not the key out of it. "I'll start saving the extra once things settle down a bit." But your lifestyle adjusts almost instantly and then stays there; once an upgrade becomes your new normal, that money is quietly gone for good. None of these is the thing that actually frees you.

So before you read on: a raise lifts what comes IN - so what must have happened to what goes OUT, for your gap to stay exactly the same? And what could you do to the raise itself to change that?

Notice the 'treat yourself' feeling that arrives the moment new money does - it is powerful, and it is not a flaw in you, it is marketed at you relentlessly. Now ask: each time your pay went up, what happened to your spending? If it rose to match - a nicer flat, a better phone, more nights out - then where, exactly, would the extra saving have come from?
Think about the gap from before - the space between what comes in and what goes out. A raise lifts what comes IN. But if your spending quietly rises to fill it, your OUT rises by the same amount, and the gap... stays exactly the same. You can double your income and, if spending keeps pace, never grow the gap by a single rand. So the cure cannot be 'earn more'. It has to be something you do to the raise itself.
It is called lifestyle creep: spending silently rises to swallow every raise, so the gap never grows no matter how much you earn. The cure is to claim a slice of each raise BEFORE your lifestyle adjusts - decide in advance to bank, say, HALF of every raise straight to your gap, and automate it so it never reaches your spending hands. Keep the other half for real joy - this is not about going without - but never let the WHOLE raise vanish, so the gap finally widens as you earn more.

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

The raises were real. They just never reached your gap. Five moves:

  • See the treadmill: Here is the trap you are caught in: if your spending always rises to match your income, you can earn twice, three times what you do now and still be no closer to freedom - forever. You run faster and faster, the numbers on your payslip climb, and yet the gap that actually builds your freedom never moves an inch. More income, same gap, same cage. That is why earning more, on its own, never set you free.
  • Understand the pull: This is not a failure of willpower - it is a current almost everyone is swept along by. The moment more money arrives, the world whispers 'you've earned it, treat yourself', and a hundred upgrades are waiting: the nicer car, the bigger flat, the brand instead of the basic. So spending quietly inflates to fill whatever you earn, automatically, every single time - unless you deliberately step in and stop it. Naming that current is the first step to swimming across it.
  • Claim the raise first: The cure is simple but it must be done EARLY: before the new money ever lands, decide that a big slice of it goes straight to your gap. You claim the raise for your freedom before your lifestyle gets the chance to claim it for a bigger cage. A good rule of thumb is to bank HALF of every raise the moment it starts - because half a raise still feels like a real lift to your life, while the other half quietly buys your freedom.
  • Automate the slice: Do not leave it to willpower each month - willpower loses to a hundred small temptations. Set up an automatic transfer so that, the day after each payday, the slice you have claimed moves itself out of reach, into your gap. The money you commit to your future should never even pass through your spending hands. What you do not see, you do not miss - and the gap grows on its own.
  • Name it - and find the balance: This is lifestyle creep, and beating it is not about freezing your life or never enjoying your success. The goal is not zero growth; it is MANAGED growth. Enjoy a real share of every raise - go out more, eat a little better, treat the people you love - but always send a meaningful slice to widen your gap. Do that, and something quietly wonderful happens: as you earn more, you both live better AND get freer, instead of just trading one cage for a pricier one.

The whole thing lives in one line: if your spending rises to swallow every raise, you can earn far more and never get one step freer - so you must claim a slice of each raise for your gap before your lifestyle claims it for a bigger cage. You were not careless, and you were not weak; you were swept along by a current that pulls at everyone the instant more money arrives - the endless, advertised whisper of 'treat yourself, you've earned it'. The trick is not to fight your way to a joyless life. It is to enjoy a real, generous share of every raise AND quietly send the rest to your freedom - automated, before you ever feel it. Do that, and as you earn more you finally start to live better AND get freer at the same time, instead of running harder on the spot for the rest of your life.

Movement 3.5 - The Guided Step

Before you take this into your own life, watch two people get the very same raise and end up in two completely different futures.

See Boitshepo and Mpumelelo each get the same R3,000 raise

Boitshepo and Mpumelelo work at the same place, and both get the same excellent raise: R3,000 more a month.

Boitshepo is thrilled, and upgrades to match. A nicer car on finance (an extra R1,800 a month), a couple of premium streaming services (R400), and takeaways three nights a week instead of one (another R800). And just like that, the full R3,000 is spoken for. Their life is a little shinier - but their savings gap is exactly what it was before the raise. A year on, they are no closer to freedom, just locked into more expensive habits they now have to keep paying for.

Mpumelelo is just as thrilled - but makes a plan before the first new payslip lands. They set up an automatic transfer to move HALF the raise - R1,500 - into their long-term savings the day after payday, before they can get used to it. The other R1,500 they spend freely on making life better: more time out with friends, nicer groceries, a treat here and there. They genuinely enjoy the raise too.

Both of them 'enjoyed' the same R3,000. But after one year, Mpumelelo's gap has grown by R18,000 - a real, visible step toward freedom - while still living a noticeably better life. Boitshepo is in the exact same financial spot as before, just with pricier habits to feed. Same raise, same day - one bought a slice of freedom, the other bought a bigger cage.

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

Now get ready for your next raise - and claim it on purpose, before it can vanish:

  1. Decide your split in advance. Do not wait until the money is in your hands - by then the upgrades are already calling. Decide NOW, in the calm before, how you will split your next raise (or extra shifts, or a growing side-hustle): how much goes straight to your gap, and how much you keep for genuine joy. Half-and-half is a strong, generous starting rule.
  2. Automate the freedom slice. Set up an automatic transfer so the saved share moves itself out the day after payday, before your spending hands ever feel it. The money you commit to your future should never sit in your everyday account tempting you. What you do not see, you will not miss.
  3. Spend the rest without guilt. This is the part most people get wrong in the other direction. The half you kept is YOURS to enjoy fully - treat yourself, lift the people you love. You are not choosing between a good life now and freedom later; you are deliberately buying a little of both with every raise.
R

Lock in your rule now, in the calm before the money arrives - a simple 'bank half, enjoy half, automatically' is a powerful start - and write it where you will see it when the next raise lands. (Working with an AMU facilitator? Use this moment to decide your raise-split rule together.)

It is one of the cruellest quiet tricks money plays: you work hard, you climb, the income grows - and the world stands ready to hand you a slightly bigger cage every single time, so you never actually get free. But you can see the trick now, and you do not have to fall for it again. Enjoy your success, generously and without guilt - and quietly send a slice of every raise to the freer life you are building, before your lifestyle can swallow it whole. That is you, being the change: deciding that from now on, every raise you earn will buy a little more of your freedom, and not just a more expensive place to be stuck.
