Every night I watch a problem that breaks my heart, and I keep waiting for someone to fix it - but no one ever comes. So why does it have to be someone else? Why not me?

Movement 1 - The Felt Problem

You are Bontle, and from your window every evening you watch the same thing. The younger children from your street, gathered under the one weak streetlamp on the corner, trying to do their homework in light that flickers and barely reaches the page. The spot is not really safe after dark. You can see it in their slumped shoulders, the squinting, the books held up close - they are trying so hard to build a future out of pages they can barely read.

You have watched this night after night, month after month. You have heard the grown-ups sigh about it too - "someone should really do something, the council should put in a proper light, somebody should make a place for these kids." Everyone agrees. Everyone waits. And nothing ever happens. The important people never come, the proper light never arrives, and the children keep squinting under that flickering lamp while another school year slips past them.

It sits in your chest like a dull, constant ache - this wrong thing that everyone sees and no one mends. You feel it more sharply than you can explain, and you feel, at the same time, completely powerless to change it. You are just one young person at a window. What could you possibly do that the whole council cannot?

Every night I watch those children struggle under that broken light, and I hear everyone say 'someone should fix this' - but the someone never comes, and it just keeps getting darker. How can something so wrong just be left broken? And why does the someone always have to be other people - never me?

Movement 2 - Your Attempt

As that ache settles, three thoughts feel like simple common sense - and each one keeps the light flickering. "It's not my job - someone in authority should be handling this." But that is exactly what everyone says, which is precisely why nothing ever changes; a problem everyone has handed to 'someone else' belongs to no one. "I'm too young and too ordinary to make any real difference." But the children under that lamp do not need a powerful stranger - they need light and a safe corner tonight, and you may be far closer to giving them that than the council ever will be. "I'll get involved one day - when I'm older, richer, or more qualified." But 'one day' is where good intentions go to fade, while the problem keeps hurting real people right now. Each thought feels reasonable, and each one quietly hands your power to a 'someone' who is never going to arrive.

So before you read on: why does this problem ache in YOU, specifically, when so many others walk past it - and what might that ache actually be telling you?

Notice WHY this particular problem hurts you so much, when the council and the busy adults walk past it every day without a second thought. You feel it because you actually SEE it - up close, clearly, the way no distant official ever will. What might that sharp, personal hurt actually be - not a weakness, but a kind of signal pointing somewhere?
You keep asking 'why doesn't SOMEONE do something?' But think about who is best placed to fix a problem: the people who live right inside it, who understand exactly what is needed and care enough to keep going when it is hard. That describes a distant stranger... or it describes YOU. What happens if you turn the question around - from 'why doesn't someone?' to 'why not me?'
The problems that touch you personally are the ones you are BEST placed to solve - you see them clearly, you understand them from the inside, and you care enough to persist. Waiting for a saviour is the trap; the saviour was always meant to be you. You do not have to fix the whole thing at once - just take the ONE small first step you, in your exact spot, are uniquely able to take this week. And this is what your financial freedom is FOR: the time, security, and means to be the change you most want to see.

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

The light never came because everyone was waiting for everyone else. But the ache in your chest was never powerlessness - it was an instruction. Five moves:

  • The waiting is the trap: Hoping that a faceless 'someone' - the council, an organisation, some important adult - will one day fix a problem you feel is not patience; it is paralysis. It keeps you powerless, and it quietly guarantees the problem stays broken, because everyone is waiting for everyone else. The 'someone' you keep waiting for almost never comes.
  • The sting is a signal: Ask why this problem hurts YOU so much when others walk past it. It is because you see it with a closeness and a clarity a distant official never will - you understand exactly who it harms and what is needed. That sharp, personal ache is not a weakness or oversensitivity; it is a compass needle, and it is pointing at you.
  • The closest are the best placed: The people best equipped to solve a problem are almost never the powerful and far away - they are the ones who live right inside it. You are not an outsider waiting for permission; you are an expert in your own corner of the world, in what is wrong and what would help. That makes you not the least able to act, but among the most.
  • Turn 'why doesn't someone?' into 'why not me?': The frustrated question is not a cry into an empty sky. It is the question turning around to face you. 'Why doesn't someone do something about this?' - well, why don't YOU? You do not need to be richer, older, or more important first; you need only to stop waiting and start, with one small step, exactly where you are.
  • Start small - and this is what freedom is FOR: You do not have to move the whole mountain - only the one small stone you are placed to move this week. Light one candle in your corner; the perfect, permanent solution can come later. And see the deeper thread: THIS is why you are learning to build financial freedom at all. Freedom buys you the time, the security, and the means to be the 'someone' you were waiting for - and money becomes simply the measure of how much your solution helps others, never the point itself.

The whole thing lives in one line: the problems you feel most deeply are the ones you are best placed to solve - so the frustrated question 'why doesn't someone do something?' is really asking 'why not you?', and the answer begins with one small step, exactly where you stand. You were never too young or too ordinary; you were simply the one close enough to feel it, which is the very thing that makes you able. You do not have to fix the whole street, or wait until you are powerful enough to. You only have to stop waiting and light one small candle in your own corner - and let that be the start. This, in the end, is what all this building of freedom has been FOR: not money for its own sake, but the time and the means to become the very 'someone' you kept hoping would come.

Movement 3.5 - The Guided Step

Before you take this into your own life, watch what 'why not me?' actually looks like - small, and real.

See Bontle take the one small step she was placed to take

Bontle stops waiting for the council. She asks herself a smaller, truer question: not 'how do I fix the whole street?' but 'what do I already have that could help, tonight?' And she realises - she has a home with a covered front stoep, a good light that works, and her grandmother, Resego, who is home in the evenings.

So she talks to Resego, and they agree on something tiny. From Monday to Thursday, between 6 and 8 in the evening, they will leave the stoep light on and let three or four of the younger children study there, where it is lit and safe. Bontle puts out an old grass mat and a jug of water. It costs them almost nothing. It does not fix the broken streetlamp, and it does not help every child on the street. But for two hours a night, for those few children, the problem is completely solved - they have light, and a safe place, and someone who noticed.

That is all it was: one small, dignified first step, taken by the person closest to the problem. And it is a seed. Maybe it stays exactly that - a quiet kindness on a stoep. Or maybe, in time, it grows into a real homework club, or a small tutoring service that one day even earns Bontle a living. If it does, the money will never be the point - it will only ever be the measure of how much her solution helps the children it serves.

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

Somewhere in your world there is a problem that aches in you the way that flickering light ached in Bontle. This week, stop waiting for it to be fixed by someone else:

  1. Name the problem you actually feel. Not the one you think you ought to care about - the one that genuinely bothers or heartbreaks you. That real feeling is your fuel and your compass; trust it. Write it down plainly.
  2. Find the one small stone you can move. Forget the whole mountain. Ask Bontle's smaller question: what do I already have - a skill, an hour, a space, a connection, a voice - that could help, even a little, this week? Make the first step small enough that you genuinely can do it, with what is already in your hands.
  3. Take it - and let it be a seed. Do the one small thing. It does not need to be perfect or permanent or grand. It only needs to begin. And remember why you are building freedom at all: so that one day you will have the time, the security, and the means to be this change on a far larger scale.

The hardest part was never your size or your age or your ordinariness. It was the belief that the 'someone' had to be other people. But the ache you feel that others walk past is the surest sign that the someone was always meant to be you - not to carry the whole world, just to light your one small corner of it. And to keep that light burning for the long run, you will first need to care for yourself - which is exactly the next step on this path. Then, as your capability and your freedom grow, you can light a little more. The change you keep waiting to see in the world is a change you are allowed to begin, today. That is the change you can be. Be the change. Develop the Capability. You can.
