Free, judgement-free help exists: the National Credit Regulator's Debt Help line on 0860 627 627 can connect you to a registered debt counsellor. You do not have to carry this alone.

Movement 1 - The Felt Problem

You are Sindiswa. It is the 20th of the month, and payday feels a lifetime away. Your phone buzzes with a message: the payment reminder for the R1,000 loan you took last month to cover a gap until payday. The repayment is R1,250. You look at your bank balance - you have R800 left to last until month-end.

A cold feeling spreads in your stomach. The repayment for the loan is more than the gap you had in the first place. To pay it, you will have to take another, bigger loan. The tool that was meant to help you for one month has become a trap that is taking over all of your months.

You find yourself searching for loan apps again, your thumb hovering over the 'apply' button.

Movement 2 - Your Attempt

The first instinct is to put out the immediate fire. "I will just take another loan to cover this month's repayment." But this is the very core of the trap: the new loan also has fees and high interest, so next month you will owe an even larger amount - maybe R1,600 - digging the hole deeper. It is not a solution; it is just a bigger crisis, delayed. "I will ignore the messages and hope it goes away." But debt does not vanish when you look away - the amount grows with penalties and interest, and the constant messages create a fog of fear that makes it impossible to think clearly; the problem you will not look at always grows bigger in the dark. "I will hide this - I feel so stupid, no one can know." But shame is the debt spiral's best friend: it keeps you isolated, and stops you talking to a friend, a family member or an adviser who could help you see a way out. The spiral spins fastest when you are alone.

So before you read on: the real question is not "how can I find R1,250 right now?" - it is "how do I stop this cycle from repeating every single month?"

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

The breakthrough comes when you see the truth, without shame: the high-cost loan is no longer a solution to a problem - it IS the problem, and the instinct to borrow again is the very thing that tightens the trap. The way out is not another loan. The way out is a clear plan, honesty, and asking for help. Five moves:

  • See the spiral - the debt is now the problem: the breakthrough begins with one honest, shame-free look: the high-cost loan is no longer the solution to a hard month - it has BECOME the problem, the thing whose repayment now opens a bigger gap than the one you first borrowed to fill. That is what a spiral is: the cure feeding the disease. This is not a flaw in you; debt spirals trap careful, hard-working people, because the system lends in a tap and collects in a vice. Naming it plainly - 'this debt is now the problem, not my character' - is the first step, because you cannot climb out of a trap you will not let yourself look at. Seeing it clearly is not despair; it is the start of taking back control.
  • Stop the bleeding - put down the shovel: the first rule of getting out of a hole is to stop digging. The instinct that feels like the only option - one more loan to cover this month's repayment - is exactly the shovel that deepens the hole: the new loan brings its own fees and interest, so next month you owe even more. So make one hard rule today and hold it: no new high-cost debt to pay off old debt. This is the hardest move and the most important, because nothing else you do can work while the spiral is still being fed. Freezing it does not fix everything, but it stops things getting worse - and stopping the fall is what makes the climb possible.
  • Face the whole picture: dread lives in the fog - the shapeless, growing monster called 'debt' that is scariest precisely because you will not look straight at it. So turn the monster into a list: find a pen and paper (or a phone note) and write down every single person and company you owe, exactly how much, and what each one truly costs in interest and fees. It is frightening to write, and it will feel worse for an hour and far better for a month, because a finite list is something you can actually plan against, while a fog is not. You may even find it is smaller, or more workable, than the terror in your head. You cannot make a plan for a number you refuse to know.
  • Make a plan and ask for help: with the list in front of you, make a simple plan: keep paying the minimum on everything so nothing spirals further, then aim any spare rand at the most expensive debt first (it is bleeding you fastest) - or at the smallest first if you need the momentum of an early win. Then do the thing shame begs you not to: ask for help, early. Talk honestly to the people you owe - many will far rather agree a smaller payment plan than chase someone who has nothing - and reach for real support, a free, non-profit debt adviser or counsellor and a trusted person to share the weight. Shame keeps the spiral spinning by keeping you alone and silent; help breaks it. Asking is not weakness - it is the single fastest way out.
  • Build a tiny buffer as you climb: the spiral started with a shortfall and a loan to fill it, so the way to stop it restarting is a small cushion of your own. Even while you are paying down debt, begin putting away a little - a jar, a separate account, whatever you can, even a tiny amount each week. It will feel too small to matter, and it is not: the first time an unexpected cost lands and you meet it from your own buffer instead of a new loan, you have broken the cycle at its source. The buffer is a seed of safety and of dignity - proof that you are no longer one surprise away from the trap, and that you are slowly buying back your own months.

Movement 3.5 - The Guided Step

You already know this one - picture being stuck in a deep hole.

See it as a hole you keep digging

Your instinct is to get out, so you start digging frantically with your hands, hoping to carve steps - but all you are really doing is making the hole deeper, kicking the loose dirt back onto yourself. The debt spiral is exactly this: taking another loan to pay a loan is just digging faster.

The first rule of getting out of a hole is to stop digging. Put down the shovel. Only then can you look at the walls, find a foothold, and build one small, solid step to climb on. Stopping the new loans is putting down the shovel; writing the list of your debts and making a simple plan is building your first step. You do not climb out in one leap - you climb out one steady foothold at a time.

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

Look at your own life. Is there a 'quick fix' that has slowly become the main problem - a solution that now costs you more than the trouble it solved? This is not about blame; it is about seeing clearly so you can take back control. Four steps you can take right now:

  1. Face the list. Get a piece of paper. Write down who you owe and how much. Turn the big, scary monster of 'debt' into a list of names and numbers - a list can be dealt with.
  2. Stop the cycle. Promise yourself today: you will not take on new high-cost debt to pay off old debt. This is the hardest and most important step.
  3. Make a simple plan. Look at your list. Pay the minimum to all, then pick one debt - the most expensive, or the smallest for momentum - to focus on clearing first. A plan, however small, gives you back power.
  4. Ask for help early. Shame is what keeps the trap locked. Talk to a trusted person, search for a free, non-profit debt adviser in your country (in South Africa, the National Credit Regulator's Debt Help line on 0860 627 627 can point you to a registered debt counsellor), and call the people you owe - many would rather agree a payment plan than get nothing at all. Asking for help is a sign of strength and the fastest way to break the spiral.
