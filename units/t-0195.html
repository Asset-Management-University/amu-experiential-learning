Eighteen per cent cheaper, and nobody left to judge it

T1: The Ache

You are Onalenna. The maintenance function has been to market, and the bid is 18 per cent cheaper than what Corathyl spends. The saving is real. You have been through the business case twice and you cannot fault the arithmetic.

What you cannot find is the plan for who judges the work in five years. There is one line about it: "The provider will supply performance reports."

You read it again and something settles. The people who would know whether those reports were true are the people in the transfer schedule. In five years, the only party at Corathyl who can say whether the maintenance is any good will be the company being paid to do it - and the only price anyone will be able to compare theirs against is their own.

The saving is genuine and I still think we are about to make a mistake. What exactly is being sold here that is not on the schedule, and what do I have to write down to stop it?

Before you read on, decide. The proposal is 18 per cent cheaper and the saving is genuine — you have checked it. The clause says the provider will supply performance reports. Then you read it again and something settles: the people who would know whether those reports were true are the people on the transfer schedule. In five years, the only party at this works able to say whether the maintenance is any good will be the company being paid to do it, and the only price anyone can compare theirs against will be their own. Nobody has done anything underhand. Write down what is actually being sold here, and what you would have to write down to stop it — your own answer, not a shortlist to choose from.

Now compare — the five answers, and the question that has the wrong shape

• “Take the saving — 18 per cent is real money.” It is, and it is measured against a service that in five years nobody here will be able to evaluate. A saving you will never again be able to verify is not a saving anybody can defend.

• “Reject it and keep everything in-house.” Forgoes a genuine saving on the strength of an instinct, and concedes the framing — that this is one decision about a whole function, which as the next paragraph shows is the actual error.

• “Write tighter performance standards into the contract.” Standards need somebody able to judge whether they were met. Write the finest specification you like and hand away everyone who could apply it, and you have written a document that reports on itself.

• “Add a break clause so we can exit.” Sensible on paper and hollow in practice — an exit right you cannot exercise is not reversibility. Taking the work back requires people who can do it, and they are on the schedule.

• “Benchmark the price against the market each year.” Useful and partial: it tests the price and not the quality, and the market comparison will itself be shaped by whoever holds the knowledge of your assets.

• The first error is that the debate is being held as one question about a whole function, and that question has no good answer because it is the wrong shape. Take the function apart, and three kinds fall out. Commodity work — done to a published standard by many possible suppliers, and it can move between them without harm. Asset-knowledge work — the work that both requires and creates knowledge of these assets: the failure history, why this pump was rebuilt rather than replaced, which setpoint changed after the flood and why. Its real output is the knowledge rather than the service, and whoever does it ends up holding it. And judgement work — deciding what should be done at all, what good looks like, what is acceptable. The rule is short: the work may go, the judgement may not. Then two tests. Could you take it back if it disappointed — which requires retaining enough capability that the answer is yes. And is the comparison honest, given that you are pricing a service nobody here will be able to assess?

Here is the policy that answers it.

T2: The Tool in Action

This is the sourcing policy for services and functions - the function taken apart, what must never leave, the reversibility test, the honest comparison, and the people.

1. Break the function into parts before you decide anything about it

THE FIRST ERROR IN EVERY SOURCING DEBATE IS THAT IT IS CONDUCTED AS ONE QUESTION - DO WE OUTSOURCE MAINTENANCE, YES OR NO - AND THAT QUESTION HAS NO GOOD ANSWER BECAUSE IT IS THE WRONG SHAPE. THE PROFESSIONAL PATTERN IS SELECTIVE SOURCING: BREAKING A FUNCTION INTO PARTS AND SOURCING SOME EXTERNALLY WHILE RETAINING OTHERS. Documented clients OUTSOURCE BETWEEN 20 AND 60 PER CENT OF THE RELEVANT BUDGET WHILE RETAINING A SUBSTANTIAL INTERNAL DEPARTMENT. So begin by taking the function apart, and sort every part into three kinds. COMMODITY WORK - grounds, cleaning, routine servicing to a published standard, statutory inspections anyone qualified can do. Many suppliers can do it, the standard is written down, and the work can move between them without harm. ASSET-KNOWLEDGE WORK - the work that both requires and creates knowledge of THESE assets: failure history, the reason this pump was rebuilt rather than replaced, which setpoint was changed after the 2019 flood and why, what the plant actually does when it is run hard. THE OUTPUT OF THIS WORK IS NOT THE SERVICE, IT IS THE KNOWLEDGE, and whoever does it will end up holding the knowledge. JUDGEMENT WORK - deciding what should be done at all, what good looks like, what is acceptable to defer, and whether the work that was done was any good. NOW WRITE THE PARTS DOWN SEPARATELY WITH THEIR ANNUAL COST, because the moment they are one line called 'maintenance', the decision will be made on the total and you will never see which part you were actually disposing of.

2. State what must never leave - the intelligent client, written as named roles

THE RETAINED IN-HOUSE CAPABILITY HAS AN ESTABLISHED NAME - THE INTELLIGENT CLIENT - AND IT MEANS THE CAPABILITY THAT KEEPS OWNERSHIP, MANAGEMENT AND SPECIFICATION OF A SERVICE WHETHER OR NOT THE SERVICE ITSELF IS DELIVERED INTERNALLY. YOUR POLICY MUST DEFINE IT AS FOUR CAPABILITIES, EACH WRITTEN AS A NAMED ROLE WITH AN ACCOUNTABILITY, NOT AS A PARAGRAPH OF INTENT. FIRST, THE ABILITY TO SPECIFY THE WORK - to say what must be achieved, in the organisation's own words, without the provider's help. If you cannot write the specification without the provider, you are not buying a service, you are buying their opinion of what you need. SECOND, THE ABILITY TO JUDGE THE PERFORMANCE INDEPENDENTLY - somebody who can go and look, who reads the provider's report as evidence rather than as fact, and whose job does not depend on the arrangement appearing to work. THIRD, CUSTODY OF THE ASSET KNOWLEDGE - the register, the configuration and setpoint record with the REASONS attached, the failure history, the drawings, the decisions and why they were made - HELD IN THE ORGANISATION'S OWN SYSTEM, IN A FORM ANOTHER PROVIDER COULD READ. This one decides whether you have a market later or a single supplier. FOURTH, THE ABILITY TO PRICE AN ALTERNATIVE - somebody who could build a credible re-tender or in-house option, which is the only thing that makes the next negotiation a negotiation. THE EVIDENCE ON WHY THIS IS UNDER-PROTECTED IS BLUNT: AN EXTENSIVE LITERATURE SURVEY FAILED TO UNCOVER EMPIRICAL RESEARCH CONSIDERING THE CONSEQUENCES OF THE LOSS OF INTERNAL CAPABILITY THAT INEVITABLY ACCOMPANIES ASSETS AND PEOPLE BEING DISPOSED OF BY THE OUTSOURCING ORGANISATION. Nobody has costed it, so nobody prices it, so it is the line that gets cut.

3. Test reversibility before you commit - how would we get this back?

BUYING AN ASSET IS REVERSIBLE - YOU STILL OWN THE THING, AND YOU CAN SELL IT, REPLACE IT OR RUN IT YOURSELF. OUTSOURCING A FUNCTION IS DIFFERENT IN KIND, AND THIS IS THE WHOLE REASON THE ARTEFACT EXISTS AS A SEPARATE POLICY. THE PEOPLE WHO KNEW HOW GO WITH THE WORK, AND THE KNOWLEDGE GOES WITH THEM, SO THE DECISION IS REVERSIBLE ON PAPER AND, WITHIN ABOUT ONE CONTRACT TERM, EFFECTIVELY IRREVERSIBLE IN PRACTICE. THE MECHANISM IS WELL DOCUMENTED: THE MORE COMPLEX THE TASK TRANSFERRED, THE HARDER IT IS TO SPECIFY WHAT IS BEING TRANSFERRED, BECAUSE THE KNOWLEDGE BECOMES MORE TACIT - and a focus on the explicit leads to the loss of the tacit. Worse, THE KNOWLEDGE THE PROVIDER ACCUMULATES AFTERWARDS, ESPECIALLY THE TACIT KIND, IS DIFFICULT FOR THE CLIENT TO IDENTIFY OR ACCESS AT ALL. So ask the four questions and write the answers into the policy as a REQUIRED SECTION OF ANY SOURCING PROPOSAL. HOW WOULD WE BRING THIS BACK? IN HOW LONG - and be honest, because recruiting and re-training a competent team is measured in years, not months. AT WHAT COST, including the cost of running the service badly while the new team learns. AND WHAT WOULD WE HAVE HAD TO KEEP IN ORDER TO BE ABLE TO - which is the question that turns box 2 from a good intention into a budget line. THE RULE THE POLICY SHOULD STATE: A FUNCTION WHOSE REVERSAL HAS NO ANSWER IS NOT A SOURCING DECISION, IT IS A DISPOSAL, AND IT SHOULD BE APPROVED BY WHOEVER IS ALLOWED TO DISPOSE OF THINGS. This is not an argument against outsourcing. It is an argument for knowing which of the two you are doing.

4. Price it honestly - the retained capability belongs inside the comparison

NOW THE COMPARISON, AND THE POLICY MUST FORCE IT TO BE MADE HONESTLY, BECAUSE THE DISHONEST VERSION IS NOT A LIE - IT IS SIMPLY THE OBVIOUS COMPARISON, AND IT IS WRONG. AT CORATHYL THE MAINTENANCE FUNCTION COSTS R40.0m A YEAR IN-HOUSE. A PROVIDER BIDS R32.8m: AN 18 PER CENT SAVING, R7.2m A YEAR. A PROPERLY STAFFED INTELLIGENT-CLIENT FUNCTION - people who can specify, judge, hold the asset knowledge and price a re-tender - COSTS R3.6m A YEAR, SO THE REAL SAVING IS R3.6m, 9.0 PER CENT. THE TOKEN VERSION - a contract manager and an administrator - COSTS R1.2m, AND THE SAVING LOOKS LIKE R6.0m, 15.0 PER CENT. THE TOKEN VERSION IS THE ONE THAT GETS APPROVED, BECAUSE IT IS THE ONE THAT WINS THE COMPARISON. FIVE YEARS LATER, AT RENEWAL, THE ORGANISATION FINDS OUT WHAT IT BOUGHT. It has banked an extra R2.4m a year, R12.0m over the term. It also cannot specify the work, cannot judge the performance and cannot price an alternative, and the provider knows all three. A PROVIDER WHO KNOWS YOU HAVE NO ALTERNATIVE MIGHT SEEK A 12 PER CENT RISE - ON R32.8m THAT IS R3.94m A YEAR. A CLIENT WITH A CREDIBLE RE-TENDER OPTION CAN HOLD THAT TO INFLATION, PERHAPS 3 PER CENT - R0.98m. THE DIFFERENCE IS R2.95m A YEAR, R14.76m OVER THE NEXT TERM. SO THE ORGANISATION BANKED R12.0m AND PAID BACK R14.76m, A NET LOSS OF R2.76m - AND THAT ASSUMES THE PROVIDER STOPS AT 12 PER CENT. THOSE PERCENTAGES ARE ILLUSTRATIONS OF THE STRUCTURE OF THE TRAP, NOT CONSTANTS YOU SHOULD QUOTE. THE STRUCTURE IS THE TEACHING: THE COST OF REMAINING ABLE TO CHANGE YOUR MIND IS PAID EVERY YEAR, VISIBLY, OUT OF THE SAVING - WHILE THE COST OF HAVING NO ALTERNATIVE IS PAID ONCE, LATER, BY SOMEBODY ELSE. That is why the cheapest-looking deal is so often the one that quietly disposes of the client's own ability to judge it. WRITE THE RULE INTO THE POLICY: NO SOURCING PROPOSAL MAY STATE A SAVING THAT HAS NOT HAD THE FULL RETAINED CAPABILITY SUBTRACTED FROM IT.

5. The people - which legal regime binds you, and what the organisation commits to say

THIS IS THE BOX ABOUT HUMAN BEINGS, AND IT IS THE ONE MOST OFTEN WRITTEN LAST AND SHORTEST. PEOPLE ARE NOT ASSETS TO BE TRANSFERRED; THEY ARE PEOPLE WHOSE EMPLOYMENT IS CHANGING. THEIR KNOWLEDGE IS THE CAPABILITY THE ORGANISATION DEPENDS ON. START WITH THE LEGAL GROUND, BECAUSE THERE ARE TWO ENTIRELY DIFFERENT WORLDS AND THEY PRODUCE COMPLETELY DIFFERENT POLICIES. IN MANY JURISDICTIONS, WHEN A SERVICE TRANSFERS, THE PEOPLE WHO PERFORM IT TRANSFER AUTOMATICALLY BY OPERATION OF LAW, ON THEIR EXISTING TERMS, WITH CONTINUOUS EMPLOYMENT AND NO TERMINATION - AND IN MOST OF THOSE JURISDICTIONS THEY MAY NOT REFUSE; TO DECLINE IS TO RESIGN. IN OTHERS, EMPLOYMENT DOES NOT TRANSFER BY LAW AT ALL, AND EVERY PERSON'S POSITION IS A SEPARATE DECISION SOMEBODY HAS TO MAKE AND DEFEND. IMPLEMENTATIONS DIFFER EVEN BETWEEN NEIGHBOURING COUNTRIES. SO YOUR FIRST QUESTION TO YOUR LEGAL ADVISORS MUST BE: WHICH OF THESE TWO WORLDS ARE WE IN? THE ANSWER DETERMINES THE ENTIRE PEOPLE-POLICY. THEN WRITE THE COMMITMENT, AND WRITE IT AS A COMMITMENT RATHER THAN AN ASPIRATION: THE POLICY MUST COMMIT THE ORGANISATION TO EARLY, HONEST, AND DIRECT COMMUNICATION WITH EVERY PERSON AFFECTED, LED BY THEIR DIRECT LINE MANAGERS, BEFORE ANY PUBLIC ANNOUNCEMENT. Nobody should learn that their employer is changing from a press release, a supplier's website, or a colleague in another department. AND FINISH WITH THE KNOWLEDGE, WHICH LEAVES EITHER WAY. If the people transfer, the knowledge transfers with them and is now held by a company you have a contract with. If the people do not transfer, the knowledge simply goes. EITHER WAY THE ORGANISATION LOSES CUSTODY OF IT UNLESS IT DELIBERATELY TAKES CUSTODY FIRST - which means the asset knowledge in box 2 has to be written down, in the organisation's own systems, with the reasons attached, BEFORE the transfer date and not as a clause requiring somebody to do it afterwards.

Box 3 is the one that changes what gets approved. Box 5 is the one that gets written last and matters most. Let us name the ideas.

T3: The Blueprint

An outsourcing decision is not a decision about who does the work. It is a decision about what you will still be able to decide afterwards. Five questions:

  • What are the parts of this function? Commodity, asset-knowledge, judgement. Sourced as one lump, the decision is made on the total and nobody sees which part was disposed of.
  • What must never leave? The intelligent client - specifying the work, judging the performance, holding the asset knowledge, and being able to price an alternative. Four named roles, not a paragraph of intent.
  • How would we get this back? In how long, at what cost, and what would we have had to keep? A function whose reversal has no answer is not a sourcing decision. It is a disposal.
  • What is the saving, honestly? Gross saving minus the true cost of remaining a competent client. Anything else compares an outsourced service against an in-house one nobody has to judge.
  • What happens to the people? Which legal world you are in, and what the organisation commits to say, who says it, and when.

The arithmetic of the deal that wins the comparison and loses the decade. Corathyl's maintenance costs R40.0m in-house. The bid is R32.8m - an 18 per cent saving, R7.2m a year. A properly staffed intelligent-client function costs R3.6m, so the honest saving is R3.6m, or 9.0 per cent. The token version - a contract manager and an administrator - costs R1.2m, and the saving looks like R6.0m, 15.0 per cent. The token version is the one that gets approved, because it is the one that wins the comparison. Five years on, the organisation has banked an extra R2.4m a year - R12.0m - and cannot specify the work, judge the performance or price an alternative. A provider who knows you have no alternative might seek a 12 per cent rise: on R32.8m that is R3.94m a year. A client with a credible re-tender option can hold it to inflation, perhaps 3 per cent - R0.98m. The difference is R14.76m over the next term against R12.0m banked. Those percentages are illustrations of the trap's structure, not constants to quote. The structure is the teaching: the cost of remaining able to change your mind is paid every year, visibly, out of the saving - while the cost of having no alternative is paid once, later, by somebody else.

Why a function is not an asset, which is the reason this is a separate policy at all. Buy an asset and the decision stays reversible: you own the thing. Outsource a function and the people who knew how go with the work, and the knowledge goes with them. The evidence names the mechanism precisely - the more complex the task transferred, the harder it is to specify what is being transferred, because the knowledge becomes more tacit, and what the provider learns afterwards is difficult for the client to identify or access at all. So the decision is reversible on paper and, within about one contract term, effectively irreversible in practice. And here is why it stays under-priced: an extensive literature survey failed to find empirical research on the consequences of losing the internal capability that inevitably goes with the people and assets disposed of. Nobody has costed it, so nobody prices it, so it is the line that gets cut.

The part about people, which is not the paragraph at the end. People are not assets to be transferred; they are people whose employment is changing. Their knowledge is the capability the organisation depends on. There are two legal worlds. In many jurisdictions employment transfers automatically, on existing terms, with continuous service and no termination, and in most of those the person may not refuse - to decline is to resign. In others employment does not transfer by law at all. Implementations differ even between neighbouring countries. Your first question to your legal advisors must be: which of these two worlds are we in? The answer determines the entire people-policy. Then the commitment, written as a commitment: the policy must commit the organisation to early, honest, and direct communication with every person affected, led by their direct line managers, before any public announcement.

One honest boundary. You write the policy and the criteria it forces every future proposal to meet. You do not make the sourcing decision for a particular service, and you certainly do not decide anyone's employment. What the policy does is make sure that whoever does decide has the long-term risks in front of them, priced, in writing, before they sign.

Does it scale? A landlord can hire someone to look after a building. What decides whether they are still a landlord in ten years is whether they kept the keys, the plans and their own opinion of what good condition looks like - or whether all three now live with the company they are paying.

T4: Your Turn

A different organisation, and this time outsourcing is the right answer. Do not spend the exercise arguing against it.

An operator runs 41 remote pump stations across a wide area. Its instrumentation and control work is done by an in-house team of four.

ElementDetail
In-house team costR6.4m a year, four people
Provider bidR4.9m a year - a gross saving of R1.5m, 23 per cent
What the provider bringsRound-the-clock cover, an accredited calibration facility, and a pool of instrument technicians
What the in-house team cannot doCover nights and weekends; calibrate to accreditation; recruit - two vacancies have stood unfilled for 19 months
The teamTwo mid-career technicians, one recent graduate, and one senior engineer who set up most of the control philosophy and retires in two years
Where the configuration livesOn the engineering laptops, plus a spreadsheet of setpoints with no reasons recorded
Proposed retained capability"Absorbed into the operations manager's existing duties"
  1. Take the function apart. Which parts are commodity, which carry the asset knowledge, and which are judgement?
  2. Say why this is right. The case for outsourcing here is not really the R1.5m. What is it, and why does saying so out loud change the policy?
  3. What must never leave? Be specific. One item on your list matters more than all the others - identify it and say why.
  4. Price it honestly. A retained instrumentation engineer costs R1.1m a year. Redo the saving. Does the decision survive?
  5. The people. What must the organisation establish first, and what does it commit to do for these four?

The test of a real answer: five things.

First, the split. Routine calibration and 24-hour callout are commodity - many suppliers, published standards, and the work can move. The control philosophy, the alarm setpoints and the record of why each one is what it is are asset knowledge. Deciding what should be monitored at all, what an acceptable false-alarm rate is, and whether a station needs upgrading is judgement. Those three do not have to go the same way, and the whole exercise is noticing that.

Second, the honest reason. Two vacancies unfilled for nineteen months, no night cover and no accredited calibration mean the organisation cannot sustain this capability, and that is the case. It matters that the policy says so, because a decision justified by cost gets governed on cost - and a decision justified by capability gets governed on whether the capability is actually there.

Third, the item that outranks the rest is the configuration and setpoint record, with reasons, in the organisation's own system, in a form another provider could read. Today it lives on laptops and in an unexplained spreadsheet. Transfer it as it stands and within one term the only party who understands your control philosophy is your supplier - at which point no competitor can bid, and re-tendering is not a lever, it is a threat you cannot carry out. Everything else on the retained list can be rebuilt. That cannot.

Fourth, the honest price. R1.5m gross minus R1.1m retained is R0.4m a year - about 6 per cent, not 23. And the decision still survives, which is the point: the honest number was never the enemy of the decision. It was only the enemy of the version of the decision that pretended a function can be handed over and still be somebody's job.

Fifth, the people. Establish first which legal world this is - whether employment transfers automatically or not - because everything else follows from that. Then commit to early, honest, direct communication from their own line managers, ahead of any announcement. And note the senior engineer with two years to go: the control philosophy is in that person's head, and this is the last window in which it can be written down by the person who knows it. Not as a handover clause on the transfer date. Now, deliberately, and paid for.

T5: The Guardrails

Sourcing decisions are not usually wrong on the day. They are wrong at the renewal.

The common traps - and how to dodge them

• Forgetting that the people you are transferring are in the room when you discuss the transfer. They are colleagues, not a schedule. A discussion held as though they were not there is a failure of leadership before it is a failure of process.

• All-or-nothing framing. "Do we outsource maintenance?" has no good answer. Break the function into parts and the question becomes answerable.

• Comparing gross saving against in-house cost. That compares an outsourced service to an in-house one nobody has to judge. Subtract the full retained capability first.

• The token retained function. A contract manager and an administrator can process invoices. They cannot specify the work, judge it, or price an alternative.

• No reversibility test. If nobody can say how the function comes back, in how long and at what cost, the organisation is not sourcing it. It is disposing of it.

• Letting the provider help you write the specification. Then you are not buying a service. You are buying their opinion of what you need, and paying to have it confirmed.

• Asset knowledge left in the provider's systems. If no competitor could bid without your incumbent's records, you do not have a market, and re-tendering is a threat you cannot carry out.

• Assuming knowledge comes back at the end of the contract. What the provider learned about your assets is tacit, and a handover clause does not extract it. Take custody before the transfer date, not after it.

• Judging the arrangement using reports written by the party being judged. Evidence, not fact. Somebody independent has to go and look.

• A retained-capability owner whose position depends on the deal looking successful. You have appointed a judge with an interest in the verdict.

• Writing that people are the organisation's greatest asset in the same document that transfers them without a word about what they were told, by whom, or when.

Onalenna had a business case she could not fault and a decision she did not trust. Take the function apart before you decide anything about it, because commodity work, asset-knowledge work and judgement work do not have to go the same way; name what must never leave - the ability to specify, to judge independently, to hold the asset knowledge with its reasons in your own systems, and to price an alternative - and write it as roles with budgets rather than as intent; test reversibility in writing, because a function whose reversal has no answer is a disposal wearing a sourcing decision's clothes; price the whole thing honestly, with the retained capability subtracted, since the cost of remaining able to change your mind is paid every year out of the saving while the cost of having no alternative is paid once, later, by somebody else; and settle the people first, not last - which legal world binds you, and what the organisation commits to tell them, through their own managers, before anyone else hears it. People are not assets to be transferred. They are people whose employment is changing, and their knowledge is the capability the organisation depends on. Take one service your organisation currently buys and ask who inside the organisation could write its specification without the provider's help. If the answer is nobody, you have found the first line of your policy.
