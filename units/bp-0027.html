How do I provide for an old age that feels too far off to be real?

Movement 1 - The Felt Problem

You are Muhammad, young and strong, and for the first time earning real money - Tank You is profitable, the stipend feels good in your pocket. Life is now. Retirement is a word for other people, a far-off country you might visit in forty or fifty years.

Then one cold dawn you really see old Mr Solomon, who has run his market stall as long as you can remember - a good man, decent, respected, his hands swollen with age - still out setting up his wares. Not because he loves it. Because he must. Decades of honest work, and nothing ever put by for this season of his life. A thought hits you like a block of ice: that is me, in forty years - still hauling cylinders with an aching back, because I have to. Unless something changes. But that future feels a lifetime away, and the money in your pocket is real today - there's so much to spend it on now. Putting it away for a 65-year-old version of you feels like burying treasure you'll never dig up.

How do I provide for an old age that feels too far away to be real - and why does starting always feel like it can wait?

Movement 2 - Your Attempt

The default path is paved with good intentions. "I'll start when..." - when I earn more, when the business settles, after the next big thing. But "when" never arrives; there's always another real need for the money now, and because the future need is a vague fear with no number, the present always wins. Or you do start - a little put aside - but then the bakkie needs tyres, a relative needs help, an opportunity comes up, and you dip in. "I'll pay it back," you say, and you don't. You've treated a 40-year pot like a short-term tin, and every raid resets the clock.

A vague far-off fear, plus a bucket you can always raid, compounds over forty years into exactly what you saw at the market. So: what would actually change that?

I'm young and earning, so retirement feels like someone else's word. But I saw old Mr Solomon still hauling his stall at dawn because he must - and that's me in 40 years unless I act. Putting money away for an old age that feels unreal never feels urgent.
What if I made future-me a real person to provide for - gave the need a target number, then worked BACKWARDS to a small amount I start now?
Future-you is a real person: give the need a target, work backwards to a small regular amount, start NOW (the earliest money does the most work - delay is the costliest choice), and protect it from leaks (automatic, separate, never raided).

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

Stop treating this as a vague fear; treat it as a concrete project, and work backwards from the future to the one action you must take today. Five moves:

  • Make future-you a real person - give the need a target: stop treating 'retirement' as a vague word. Picture 65-year-old you as a real person you're responsible for: a place to live, food, doctors - and a body that can no longer haul cylinders. You owe him. Give his needs a rough number to live with dignity each month. That TARGET turns a fear into something you can actually aim at.
  • Work backwards to a small amount you start NOW: a huge, scary future number, spread back over the many years you have, becomes a small, manageable regular amount to put away today. Don't be frightened by the big number - be freed by the smallness of the monthly step. Working backwards turns dread into one doable action.
  • The engine: start early - the earliest money does the most work: time is the most powerful ingredient. The very first money you put away grows the longest, so it does the heavy lifting. Every year you delay isn't just a year lost - it's the most valuable year gone. DELAY IS THE SINGLE MOST EXPENSIVE CHOICE you can make; starting small today beats starting big later.
  • The killer: protect the pot from leaks: this pot is not your emergency fund and not your everyday savings - it is for future-you, and you must protect it from present-you. Make the contribution AUTOMATIC (it leaves the day you're paid) and keep it SEPARATE and HARD TO TOUCH. You don't raid it, you don't 'borrow' from it - every raid resets the engine.
  • Track it, and never stop: check it against your target now and then, and adjust the amount as life changes - earn more, give more; a hard season, trim it - but never raid the seed and never stop feeding it. A protected pot, started early and left to grow, is how future-you finally gets to rest.

Here's why when you start beats how much. Picture two savers, each putting away R500 a month, each earning the same steady growth. Amogelang starts at 25, saves for just ten years, then stops completely at 35 - she puts in R60,000 in total. Ben starts at 35 and saves right through to 65 - thirty years, R180,000 in total, three times as much. At 65, Ben has about R1.1 million. Amogelang - who put in a third of what Ben did and stopped thirty years earlier - has about R2 million. Her early money simply had more years to grow, and nothing could catch it. That's not a trick; it's the whole secret: the earliest money does the most work, so the best time to start is always now.

Movement 3.5 - The Guided Step

This isn't only for retirement - it's the shape of any big, far-off need. Watch it provide for a child.

See the same move fund a child's education

You have a newborn, and you want them to study in eighteen years.

• Vague fear: "University is so expensive - I hope we'll manage."

• The target: you find the likely cost of a course in eighteen years - a real number to aim at.

• Work backwards: spread that target over the 216 months you have (18 years x 12) -> a small required monthly amount.

• Start early: open a separate, dedicated pot the month the child is born. The first month's money has eighteen full years to grow; the last has only one. The early money does almost all the heavy lifting.

• Protect from leaks: when a squeeze hits in year seven, you don't raid the education pot for a holiday - you protect it, because you have a commitment to a real future person. Exactly like providing for future-you.

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

You have a big, important, far-off goal - your own old age, a child's education, a home of your own. Right now it's probably just a vague worry. This week, turn the fog into a plan:

  1. Name it and give it a target. Who or what are you providing for - "future-me at 65", "my daughter's studies" - and a realistic target number.
  2. Work backwards to a small step now. Use the years you have to find the small regular amount to start putting away today. Don't be scared by the big number; be freed by the smallness of the monthly step.
  3. Start this month, in a protected pot. Open a separate, hard-to-touch account for this one purpose, set an automatic transfer - and make it sacred: you do not raid it, borrow from it, or stop.
R

Open the separate pot and start the first small amount this month - whatever your age, today is the earliest you'll ever be. (Working with an AMU facilitator? Work the target and the backward step out together.)

You aren't just "saving" - you're providing for someone you're responsible for, who can't earn for themselves yet: future-you. The most powerful step is the first one, because the earliest money always does the most work. Mr Solomon's story isn't a warning that it's hopeless; it's a reminder that a small amount, started now and never raided, is the difference between an old age of grinding need and one of rest and dignity.
