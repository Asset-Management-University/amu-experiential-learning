Money moves through my business all day - how much of it is actually mine, and did we make any this month?

T1: The Ache

Your business takes money every day. Money comes in from sales; money goes out for stock and bills. It never stops moving. Customers leave deposits. Your cousin lends you something to get through a slow week. You pay yourself when you can.

And at month-end you cannot answer the simplest question anybody could ask you: did this business actually make money - or were you just busy? You look at the account balance and it tells you nothing, because you know some of what is sitting there is not really yours, and you could not say how much.

It feels like juggling in the dark. You can feel everything moving, but you cannot see the balls - and you are fairly sure one of them belongs to somebody else. It is all one tangled pot.

Money moves through my hands all day and some of it is not mine to keep. How much of what I am holding is actually the business's - and did we make anything this month?

Before you read on, decide. One account, money in and out every day, customer deposits, a loan from a cousin, and a month just ended. Write down how you would work out whether the business made money - your own answer, not a shortlist to choose from.

Now compare - the five answers, and what each one does with the money that is not yours

• "Look at the balance - it went up, so we made money." The one nearly everybody uses, and it is why deposits are so dangerous: a balance that rose because customers left deposits looks exactly like a balance that rose because you did good work. Both feel the same on the day you check.

• "Add up everything that came in, take off everything that went out." Much closer, and it counts a loan and a deposit as income. Those are other people's money sitting in your hands, and every rand of it will leave again.

• "Ask the person who does my books." Fine if you have one, and they can only sort what you gave them - if the deposits and the takings went into one account with no note of which was which, nobody downstream can untangle it either.

• "Wait for the year-end and let the accountant tell me." Eleven months late for every decision you have to make before then, and it treats your own business's health as somebody else's specialism.

• "I don't take a wage, so anything left over is profit." The most sincere answer and the most costly. A business that only breaks even because the owner works for nothing is not breaking even - it is being subsidised by you, and the figures will never show you that until you put your wage in them.

• What the first five miss is the sorting that has to happen before any sum is worth doing. Money in your hands comes in two kinds: money you earned, and money you are holding for somebody else. Until every rand is in one pile or the other, and until the business's money is somewhere your groceries cannot reach, "did we make money" has no answer - and neither has "how much do I actually have".

T2: The Tool in Action

Tank You had the very same month-end dread. Here is the sorting, and the surprise waiting at the end of it:

1. One pot for the business - and what is actually in it today

Tank You has its own account. Today it holds R4,000. Muhammad pays himself a wage out of it into his personal account and buys nothing personal from it - so when he looks at R4,000, he is looking at the business, not at his life and the business mixed together.

2. Sort the month's money in: EARNED, or just PASSING THROUGH?

R31,200 came into the account this month. Sorted: EARNED = 100 deliveries x R260 = R26,000. PASSING THROUGH = R3,200 of cylinder deposits (the customers' money, owed back when they return the cylinders) + a R2,000 loan from Muhammad's cousin (owed back to him) = R5,200. Count the whole R31,200 as income and the month looks like a R6,200 profit - six times the truth, and R5,200 of it belongs to other people.

3. This month's score: earned, minus what the business spent

Earned R26,000. Spent: gas R15,000 + bakkie instalment R2,000 + the two wages R7,000 + petrol and airtime R1,000 = R25,000. PROFIT: +R1,000. A profitable month - small, but real, and everybody has been paid.

4. The reading: what is actually yours, and your one action

Profit +R1,000 - the business works. But of the R4,000 in the account, R5,200 is money that is only passing through: R3,200 of customer deposits, owed back whenever a cylinder comes in, and the R2,000 owed to Muhammad's cousin. Take both out and the business's own position is -R1,200. A profitable month, and it is holding R1,200 less than it owes. My action: move the deposit money into a separate pocket this week so it stops flattering the balance, and agree a repayment date with my cousin rather than leaving it as a debt nobody has counted.

Look at what the sorting did. R31,200 came in, and only R26,000 of it was Tank You's to keep - the rest was R3,200 of customer deposits and a R2,000 loan, both owed back to somebody. Count the lot as income and the month reads as a R6,200 profit; sort it honestly and the profit is R1,000. Six times smaller, and the real one. Then the sting, which is the same lesson from the other end: the account holds R4,000, but R5,200 of what came through is money Tank You is holding for other people - R3,200 of deposits owed back whenever a cylinder returns, and R2,000 owed to a cousin. Take both out and the business's own position is minus R1,200. A profitable month, everybody paid, and it is holding R1,200 less than it owes. Nothing has gone wrong here and nobody has been careless - this is simply what money looks like before it has been sorted, and it is why the balance in the account can never answer either question on its own.

T3: The Blueprint

Four moves take you from one tangled pot to knowing what is yours.

  • 1. One pot, and nothing personal out of it. The business gets its own account, or at least its own tin. Pay yourself a wage out of it into your own money, and buy your groceries from there. Mixing the two blinds you to both, and no later sum can unmix them.
  • 2. Sort every rand in: EARNED or PASSING THROUGH. Earned is what the business worked for and keeps. Passing through is what is in your hands but belongs to someone else - deposits, loans, money held for a supplier. This one sorting decides whether every figure after it is true.
  • 3. Score the month: earned, minus everything the business spent - with your own wage counted as spending. That is profit, and it tells you whether the business earns its keep.
  • 4. Read two numbers, not one: the profit, and what is in the pot minus the money that is only passing through. They answer different questions and they often disagree.

What you have just done has a name - bookkeeping - and it is worth saying plainly what it actually is, because the word sounds like paperwork and it is not. It is the discipline of knowing whose money you are holding. The two ideas underneath do all the work. The separate pot makes the question answerable at all. Earned against passing through is the sorting that turns a balance into a fact: a deposit is a debt, a loan is a debt, and money that must leave again was never income however long it sits with you.

And the reason it matters more than it sounds: spending money that is passing through is how a business quietly digs a hole it cannot see. The deposits get spent on stock, the stock sells, the cylinders come back, and there is nothing to return - not through dishonesty, but because the balance said it was there. Sorting it once a month keeps that hole from ever opening.

Two limits. This is a look backwards: it tells you what happened and what you are holding today, and it says nothing about next Tuesday. And profit is not the same thing as cash in hand - Tank You's R1,000 profit sat alongside an own-cash position of minus R1,200, which is the clearest possible warning that a good month and a survivable week are two different questions. The forward-looking half - laying out the cash you expect in and the bills due over the weeks ahead, and finding the lowest point before it arrives - is bp-0024, and a business with money moving through it needs both.

T5: The Guardrails

A few principles that protect a small business.

The traps - and how to stay true

• The separate pot is sacred. Never buy groceries from the business account. Pay yourself a wage, then spend that from your personal money. Mixing the two blinds you to both, and no amount of care later will separate them again.

• A deposit is a debt, not income. Money passing through - a customer's deposit, a loan, cash held for a supplier - is owed to somebody. Spending it on running costs is how businesses dig a hole they cannot see, and it happens honestly, because the balance said it was there.

• The account balance is not your money. It is your money plus everybody else's, minus nothing. Subtract what is passing through before you decide you can afford anything.

• Your own wage left out of the sums. A business that only works because you are unpaid is not working, it is being subsidised by you - and the figures will never say so until your wage is in them. Put it in even in the months you cannot draw it.

• Sorting left until year-end. The sort takes half an hour a month and is nearly impossible eleven months late, when nobody can remember which R500 was a deposit. Do it monthly, while you still know.

• A profitable month mistaken for a safe one. Profit says the business earns its keep; it does not say you can pay Friday's bill. Those are two questions and this method answers one of them.

You were never bad with money - you were juggling in the dark, with everything in one pot and no way to tell whose money you were holding. Two moves switch on the lights: keep the business's money separate, and sort every rand that comes in into what you earned and what is only passing through. Then the score is real - Tank You made R1,000, honestly, with everyone paid - and so is the harder fact beside it, that R5,200 of the money that came through belongs to other people, which leaves the business itself R1,200 short rather than R4,000 up. Knowing which is which is the whole of it, and it is the difference between running a business and hoping. (Working with an AMU Facilitator?
