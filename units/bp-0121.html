Movement 1 - The Felt Problem

You are Lonwabo, the new asset manager at a regional water board, four weeks into the job. It is 08:10 on a Tuesday in a cramped boardroom at a bulk water treatment works; through the window you can see the high-lift pumps that push treated water up to the town reservoirs. Today you have to sign off the capital plan that decides which pumps and pipelines get money next year.

On the table are three printouts that were meant to tell one story. They do not. The first, from the maintenance system (the CMMS), lists 5,400 pieces of equipment your teams look after. The second, the finance fixed-asset register, lists 6,200 assets and a total book value of R1.84 billion. The third is a tag list pulled off the SCADA system that runs the plant. None of the three totals match, and nobody in the room can tell you why there are 800 more assets in finance than in maintenance.

The CFO, Thembi, taps her pen on the finance printout - calm, but her jaw tight. "I have to give this number to the board on Thursday. Last year the auditors flagged the register. If they flag it again, it's a qualified opinion, and that lands on me. So tell me the R1.84 billion is real." You flip to the page you marked last night and your stomach drops: a pump listed three times - Line 3041 "HL Pump 3, R410,000"; Line 4117 "Pump, High Lift, Stn 3, R0"; Line 5588 "HLP-03 motor & pump set, R388,500" - three names, three values, for what you are fairly sure is one physical machine humming through the wall. And a worse page the fitter, Zukile, slid to you: a work-order history for an inlet screen - 41 jobs over six years, R220,000 spent - that, when you searched the finance register this morning, isn't there at all. "And there's a clarifier out back we rebuilt in 2022," Zukile murmurs. "It's not on any list. Not one."

So you know two things at once: there are assets on the register that aren't real, or are counted twice - and there are real assets, running, costing money, that aren't on the register at all. Thembi wants a number by Thursday; the capital plan wants a decision today, and every priority on it is ranked by the same register you no longer trust.

Three printouts that should tell one story all disagree - the same pump is on the books three times, a screen we've maintained for six years is on no list; where do I start before any of it can be trusted?

Movement 2 - Your Attempt

The pull is to fix the numbers directly. "Pick the system that looks most official - finance - and make the others match it." But finance is the one with the triple-listed pump and no inlet screen; every list can be wrong, so trusting one because it looks tidy just enshrines its errors. "Compare list against list until they agree." But finance can double-count, maintenance can miss things, and SCADA can keep a tag for a machine that was scrapped - list A versus list B can never break the tie, because there is no referee among them. "It's a maths problem - reconcile the 6,200 lines by Thursday." But you cannot, and even if you could, you would be reconciling names to names with nothing tying any of them to a real thing - the duplicate pump and the missing screen would survive the exercise untouched.

So before you read on: stop arguing about the totals. Name what all three lists are missing that lets one pump live three lives and a real screen live none - and the one reference that is not another list.

None match, and nobody can say why finance has 800 more assets than maintenance. The CFO, Thembi, must give the R1.84 billion to the board on Thursday - and last year the auditors flagged the register, so a repeat flag is a qualified opinion that lands on her. Then it gets worse: one pump is on the register THREE times (R410,000, then R0, then R388,500 - three names, three values, one machine I can hear humming through the wall). And the opposite - the fitter, Zukile, slides me a work-order history for an inlet screen: 41 jobs, R220,000 spent over six years, and it is NOT on the finance register at all; there is also a clarifier rebuilt in 2022 that is on no list. So both errors run at once: assets on the register that aren't real or are double-counted, and real running assets that aren't on the register. Where do I even start?
Stop arguing about the totals - the totals are a SYMPTOM. The disease is that the three systems have NO SHARED, UNIQUE IDENTITY for each asset: finance calls the pump 'HL Pump 3', maintenance calls it 'HLP-03 motor & pump set', SCADA calls it 'PS3-PMP-003', and nothing links those three names to ONE thing in the real world - so the same pump can live three lives and the inlet screen can live none. The argument in the boardroom is not really about money; it is about IDENTITY. And you cannot break the tie by comparing list A against list B, because finance can double-count, maintenance can miss things, and SCADA can keep a tag for a machine that was scrapped - every list can be wrong, so another list is never the referee. The only thing you can fully trust is the one you can walk up to and TOUCH: the real physical plant. So make the physical asset the master, give every real asset ONE unique tag that travels with the steel (not with any system), and reconcile with one question that works on every line: 'which physical, tagged asset does this record refer to - and can I stand next to it?'. The real question is not 'is R1.84 billion right?' but 'does every line point to one real thing I can verify?'
Five moves pull the register back from the edge. (1) ANCHOR TO THE PHYSICAL MASTER - stop arguing about the lists; the only thing you can trust is the asset you can touch, so make it the single source of truth. (2) ASSIGN ONE UNIQUE, VERIFIABLE IDENTITY - stamp a single tag on the steel (one asset, one ID that travels with the machine); finance's three lines, maintenance's record and SCADA's tag all point at it or are retired. (3) INTERROGATE EVERY RECORD WITH ONE QUESTION - 'which real, tagged thing does this refer to, and can I stand next to it?'. (4) SORT EACH LINE - duplicate (the same pump counted twice -> keep one, retire the rest, removing R388,500 that does not exist twice), ghost (a stub 'Pump R0' opened and never finished -> retire), or invisible (the inlet screen and the 2022 clarifier are real -> tag, create on the register, value: a R4.1 million clarifier that was invisible is now on the books). (5) SET THE SUCCESS LINE, TALLY HONESTLY, AND STOP THE REGROWTH - you cannot verify all 6,200 lines by Thursday, so verify a deliberate sample (the 40 highest-value, most-disputed assets), write your expectation IN ADVANCE, and let the walk write the finding. The surprise: you went in certain the register was inflated, but the duplicates took only R0.9m out while the invisibles put R6.7m back in - net +R5.8 million; on the assets that matter most it was UNDERSTATED, not padded, exactly the omission an auditor catches. Hand the CFO not a perfect R1.84 billion but an HONEST number with the working shown, asset by asset - that is what survives an audit. (To keep the register true going forward through change,.)

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

You stop arguing about the totals - the totals are a symptom. The disease is that the three systems have no shared, unique identity for each asset: finance calls the pump "HL Pump 3", maintenance "HLP-03 motor & pump set", SCADA "PS3-PMP-003", and nothing links those three names to one thing in the real world. The argument is not about money; it is about identity. Five moves pull it back:

  • Anchor to the physical master - name the real disease: you stop arguing about the totals, because the totals are a symptom; the disease is that the three systems have NO SHARED, UNIQUE IDENTITY for each asset. Finance calls the pump 'HL Pump 3', maintenance calls it 'HLP-03 motor & pump set', SCADA calls it 'PS3-PMP-003' - and nothing links those three names to one thing in the real world, so the same pump lives three lives in finance while the inlet screen lives none. That is the whole problem in one sentence, and the argument in the boardroom is not about money, it is about identity. So you choose your master deliberately: not the smartest-looking system, not finance, not SCADA, but the only list you can walk up to and TOUCH - the real physical plant. Every system of record can be wrong (finance double-counts, maintenance misses things, SCADA keeps a tag for a scrapped machine), which is exactly why comparing one list against another can never break the tie; only the steel itself can. The physical asset becomes the single source of truth, and everything else must answer to it.
  • Assign one unique, verifiable identity: now give every real asset one identity that belongs to the THING, not to any system. You and Zukile walk to the plinth at High-Lift Pump Station 3, see one pump set, and fix a stamped metal tag to it: a single unique asset number, say WB-HLPS3-PMP-001. From this moment, THAT number is the asset - finance's three lines, maintenance's record and SCADA's tag must all point at it or be retired. The identity travels with the steel: if the pump is moved, the number goes with it; if a record cannot be tied to a tagged physical thing, the record is suspect, not the thing. This single unique identifier is the missing link every downstream report, plan and valuation was built without, which is why they all inherited the same confusion. You are not inventing a tidy new numbering scheme for its own sake; you are giving reality a name the paperwork has to use, so that for the first time a pump and its record can be the same fact.
  • Interrogate every record with one question: with a physical master and a single identity, you finally have a question that works on every line of every list: 'which real, tagged asset does this record refer to - and can I stand next to it?'. This one question does the work that arguing over totals never could, because it cannot be answered from a spreadsheet - it can only be answered at the asset, with your hand on the steel. It instantly exposes the records that point at nothing real, the several records that point at the same real thing, and the real things that no record points at. You do not need to verify all 6,200 lines to use it; you need to ask it honestly of each line you touch, and let the answer - verifiable, physical, undeniable - settle what a year of mismatched printouts could not. A record that cannot survive that question does not describe an asset; it describes a belief, and beliefs are what broke the register.
  • Sort each line - duplicate, ghost, or invisible: as you run the question down the sample, every disputed line falls cleanly into one of three buckets, and each bucket has a fixed action. A DUPLICATE is one real thing counted twice under different names - Line 3041 (R410,000) and Line 5588 (R388,500) are the same pump set, so you keep one and retire the other, removing R388,500 of value that does not exist twice. A GHOST is a record with nothing real behind it - Line 4117 ('Pump, High Lift, Stn 3, R0') is a stub someone opened and never finished, pointing at the same pump, so you retire it. An INVISIBLE is a real thing with no record at all - the inlet screen with R220,000 of history and the 2022 clarifier are both real (you put your hand on them), so you tag them and CREATE them on the register at their assessed value, and a R4.1 million clarifier that was invisible is now on the books and in the maintenance plan. Three buckets, three actions: retire the duplicates and ghosts to remove false value, create the invisibles to add true value - and now every line you have touched points to one tagged, physically-verified asset.
  • Set the success line, tally honestly, and stop the regrowth: you cannot verify all 6,200 lines in hours, so you verify a deliberate sample - the 40 highest-value, most-disputed assets, where being wrong does the most damage - and you write your expectation DOWN IN ADVANCE so reality can prove you wrong: 'the register is mostly inflated by duplicates; walking the top 40 will mostly remove value; the number should come down.' Then you walk the steel with a clipboard and a stamping tool - the cheapest honest instrument there is - and tally what you find. And it surprises you: the duplicates and ghosts take only about R0.9 million OUT, but the invisibles (the screen, the clarifier, two more invisibles Zukile walks you to) put about R6.7 million back IN - a net of +R5.8 million. The register was not padded; on the assets that matter most it was UNDERSTATED. So you change the message (not 'we over-counted, trim it' - which an auditor would catch as the very omission that triggers a qualified opinion - but 'we were missing real, running, money-spending assets, and here they are') and you re-point the work (hunt the invisibles first, especially maintenance spend with no asset behind it). The method held; your belief about the answer changed - the loop working, letting the walk write the finding. You hand the CFO not a perfect R1.84 billion but an HONEST number with the working shown, asset by asset, which is what survives an audit. And because a reconciliation is only true on the day you do it, the tag-and-verify rule becomes part of how the plant runs: no asset is real until it has one identity anchored to the steel (and to keep it true through every future change,).

See where it lands. You hand Thembi not a perfect R1.84 billion but something better - an honest number with the working shown: about R0.9 million out from duplicates and ghosts, about R6.7 million in from the invisibles, a net of +R5.8 million, and every line you touched now pointing to a tagged, physically-verified asset. She can stand in front of the board and say, "The number moved - up, not down - and here is exactly why, asset by asset." That is what survives an audit; a round number you cannot defend does not. The pump is still humming through the wall, but now when someone says "HL Pump 3", everyone means the same machine, and the rand for it appears exactly once. When asset registers disagree, stop trying to reconcile the lists with each other; the disease is not the totals but the missing shared identity. Anchor every record to a single, unique identity physically verified on the asset itself, ask of every line "which real, tagged thing does this refer to, and can I stand next to it?", sort each into duplicate, ghost or invisible, and let the walk - not your hunch - write the finding. Trustworthy records require a single verified identity for each real thing, and no record is real until it has one.

Movement 3.5 - The Guided Step

You already know the clean version - one pump screaming from three lines. Real life rarely shouts.

See the shop whose stock count never settles

Zinzi owns a small hardware shop. No finance system, no SCADA - just a point-of-sale (POS) that tracks stock, and the shelves themselves. Every quarter she does a stock count, and every quarter the POS says she has more stock than is actually on the shelf - a few thousand rand out, never the same amount twice. Her bookkeeper shrugs: "It's shrinkage. Theft, breakage. Every shop has it." Zinzi half-believes it, but it nags her, because the gap never settles.

This looks like a different problem from the water board - no third list, no machine listed three times, no missing clarifier; it looks like a maths or a theft problem. But it is the same shape, smaller: a record (the POS) that is supposed to describe a real set of things (the stock on the shelf) and doesn't - the water board had three drifting records, Zinzi has two, and the drift is the same disease. So she does what you did at the plinth: she stops trusting the list and walks the shelf. On the cordless drills she finds the same drill under two product codes - one entered when a supplier rep loaded it last year, one already there: a DUPLICATE, one real thing counted twice, quietly inflating the count, the triple-listed pump shrunk to a shop shelf. Then on the bottom shelf, twelve bags of a cement additive she buys cash and sells fast, never loaded into the POS at all: an INVISIBLE, real stock with no record, deflating the count in the other direction - which is exactly why the gap never settled on one number, a duplicate pushing one way and an invisible the other. "Shrinkage" was a story that explained the gap without fixing it. The real cause was the water board's cause: no single verified identity tying each record to the one real product. Her fix is your fix at a different scale - make the shelf the master, give each real product one code, merge the duplicate, capture the invisible, and let nothing be sold until it has one code. The disease doesn't depend on size or industry or how many lists you have; whenever records aren't anchored to one verified identity for each real thing, they drift, and the drift hides as something else.

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

The water board's problem was three lists that wouldn't reconcile because no record was anchored to one real, verified thing. That exact problem-type - records that have drifted from reality because nothing ties them to a single, verified identity - shows up far beyond a pump station. Find it where you are:

  1. Find your lists. Name a place where the same thing is recorded in two or more systems that don't agree - a stock count versus the shelf, a customer list versus who actually pays you, a fixed-asset register versus what is in the building. Which numbers do people argue about?
  2. Hunt for a duplicate, a ghost and an invisible. Find one record that is the same real thing counted twice; one record with nothing real behind it; and one real thing with no record at all. What did each cost you - in money, time, or a wrong decision?
  3. Find your "steel". What is the one thing you could physically go and touch (or unambiguously point at) that is more trustworthy than any of the lists - and how would you give it a single identity that every list has to point back to?
  4. Pick your first forty, and stop the regrowth. Which handful, if you got their identity right first, would remove the most error or settle the biggest argument? And what rule would stop a new thing entering your world without one clear identity tied to reality?
R

Run the smallest test this week: pick the one disputed record that bugs you most, write down - before you check - which way you expect it to go (a duplicate inflating your count, or an invisible deflating it?), then go and physically verify it. You'll know it worked when you can say which bucket it fell into and watch your number move for a reason you can name. (Working with an AMU facilitator? Build the reconciliation for your three-lists example together.)

Your "assets" might be customers, stock, tools, files, or money - but the trap is identical, and so is the way out: anchor every record to one real, verified thing, fix the highest-stakes ones first, and never let a new record exist without an identity. The water board did it with stamped tags on pumps; trustworthy records require a single verified identity for each real thing.
