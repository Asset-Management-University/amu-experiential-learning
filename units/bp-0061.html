My operations manager handed me a careful, defensible R1.45m asset plan where every single line is justified on its own - but the year's number-one strategic aim has zero rand against it, and she has never even seen our strategy; do I sign it before the supplier's price expires on Friday?

Movement 1 - The Felt Problem

You are Muhammad. It is a Tuesday morning in the Tank You depot office, and the business is not two friends and a car any more: you and Ibraaheem now run four depots across the region, eleven delivery vehicles, and just over 6,400 cylinders moving in and out every month. The whiteboard on the wall still has the old line written across the top in marker: find a way to make people's lives easier.

Two documents are open on the desk. The first is a single page - the strategy you and Ibraaheem agreed in January, three lines in his handwriting: 1. Grow - open a fifth depot in the northern town by December. 2. Win back trust - be the supplier people recommend, after that bad month when late deliveries cost us regulars. 3. Protect cash - no big new debt this year; the bank is watching us. The second document is fatter - eleven pages, the maintenance-and-buying plan your operations manager, Nosizwe, handed you yesterday. It is a good plan, careful, depot by depot: a new compressor for the coastal filling line (R380,000), requalification of 1,900 cylinders that are due (R310,000), two replacement bakkies (R540,000 together), and a second filling bay at the inland depot (R220,000) so trucks stop queueing in the mornings. You add it up: R1.45 million.

"It's all justified," Nosizwe says from the doorway, mug in hand. "Every line." And it is - that is the strange part. The compressor is old and slow. The 1,900 cylinders genuinely fall due; by law you cannot fill an out-of-date vessel. The bakkies have high mileage. The second bay would clear the morning queue. Every item makes sense on its own. But you pick up the one-page strategy in your other hand and something is wrong. Where is the fifth depot in Nosizwe's plan? Not one rand. Eleven pages keep the four depots you already have running well, and say nothing about the fifth one you decided to open. Where is "win back trust"? Nothing in the plan is aimed at the thing that actually lost you those regulars - late deliveries. And R1.45 million is most of your spare cash for the year, almost none of it moving you toward where you said you wanted to be.

You realise, with a small cold feeling, that Nosizwe has never seen the one-page strategy. Why would she have? Nobody gave it to her. She built her plan the way a careful operations manager does: keep what we own working, stay legal, fix the loudest pain. It is a good answer - to a different question from the one you asked in January. Two documents on one desk, living in two different worlds, with no line you can draw between them. The pen is in your hand. "So," Nosizwe says, "can I order the compressor? The supplier's price holds until Friday."

Every line of her R1.45m plan is justified on its own, but the year's number-one strategic aim has zero rand against it and she has never seen our strategy - do I sign it before the price expires on Friday?

Movement 2 - Your Attempt

The pen is right there and saying no feels like saying Nosizwe failed. "Just sign it - it's detailed, defensible, she worked hard." But every rand is justified line by line and not one rand moves you toward the fifth depot, winning back trust, or anywhere you said you were going; defensible is not the same as aligned. "Cross out the items I don't like and sign the rest." But on what basis - your mood that morning? Without a rule, you will cut the quiet important things and keep the loud ones, exactly as the plan already does. "Send her away to redo it." But she will rebuild the same plan, because she is answering the same question she was given - keep the assets healthy, stay legal - and nobody has handed her the one she should be answering. Each move treats the symptom; none names why a careful plan points nowhere you meant to go.

So before you read on: do not look for a villain. Name the thing that is missing BETWEEN the two documents, and the one rule you would write so this cannot happen again.

I am Muhammad. Tank You is not two friends and a car any more - Ibraaheem and I run four depots, eleven delivery vehicles, and just over 6,400 cylinders a month. The whiteboard still says our old line: find a way to make people's lives easier. Two documents are on my desk. The first is the one-page STRATEGY we agreed in January: GROW (open a fifth depot in the northern town by December), WIN BACK TRUST (be the supplier people recommend, after a bad month when late deliveries cost us regulars), and PROTECT CASH (no big new debt this year, the bank is watching). The second is the eleven-page maintenance-and-buying plan my operations manager, Nosizwe, handed me - a good, careful plan: a new compressor for the coastal filling line (R380,000), requalification of 1,900 cylinders due by law (R310,000), two replacement bakkies (R540,000 together), and a second filling bay at the inland depot (R220,000) to clear the morning queue. R1.45 million, and every line is justified on its own. But the fifth depot - the year's number-one aim - has not one rand against it; nothing attacks what actually lost us the regulars (late deliveries); and that is most of our spare cash. Nosizwe has never seen the one-page strategy - nobody gave it to her - so she built a sensible plan answering a different question: keep what we own working, stay legal, fix the loudest pain. She is in the doorway: can she order the compressor before the price expires Friday? Where do I start?
Don't look for a villain - Nosizwe did not do a bad job; she answered a different question from the one you and Ibraaheem asked in January. Look for the JOIN. You have two documents that live in different worlds: one says WHERE THE BUSINESS IS GOING, the other says WHAT WE WILL DO TO THE ASSETS, and there is no line you can draw between them. That missing line is the whole problem: when the things you decide to do with your assets are not DERIVED FROM the strategy - pulled out of it on purpose and reconciled against it - your money flows to wherever the loudest local pressure points (old compressor, cylinders due, queueing trucks), all real, none chosen BECAUSE of where the business is going. So don't sign, and don't send her away. Name the gap out loud, then build the missing line one aim at a time: for each January aim, ask 'what must our assets actually DO for this to come true, and what does that cost?' Grow needs a filling setup, stock and a vehicle for the fifth depot (~R600,000, completely absent from her plan); win-back-trust needs whatever attacks late deliveries (the worst bakkie, the second bay). Then reconcile honestly against the cash you have. The real question is not 'is each line justified?' (they all are) but 'which strategic aim does each line serve - and if none, why is it eating the money the aims need?'
Five moves draw the missing line. (1) NAME THE DISCONNECT - the strategy and the asset plan were built by different people answering different questions, and nobody connected them; there is no line of sight from where you're going down to what you'll do to your assets. (2) BUILD THE LINE OF SIGHT, AIM BY AIM - for each strategy aim, derive the costed asset work it needs: GROW -> ~R600,000 for the fifth depot's filling setup, stock and a vehicle (absent from the plan); WIN BACK TRUST -> one replacement bakkie (R270,000, the worst one - high-mileage bakkies cause the missed slots) and the second filling bay (R220,000 - the morning queue is why deliveries run late); PROTECT CASH -> spends nothing, it CONSTRAINS the rest. (3) RECONCILE HONESTLY against ~R1m no-debt cash: the fifth-depot setup R600k IN (the priority), one bakkie R270k IN, requalify R310k IN (serves no aim but is a legal/safety MUST) - those three already total R1.18m, over the limit, so the second bay phases to mid-year and the compressor (slow, not broken) and second bakkie defer; almost every rand now points at growing and winning back trust, and the requalification still goes first because law and safety set a floor strategy cannot override. (4) BUILD THE GATE AND PILOT IT ON A REAL PLAN - 'no spend is approved unless it names the aim it serves', set a success line, run it on the existing eleven pages; the surprise: it wrongly tries to BLOCK the legally-required requalification, so you add a second tag ('or is it a must - safety/law?') and it sorts all six lines cleanly. (5) OWN THE JOIN - each January agree the aims first, then build the asset plan FROM them with every line naming its aim or its must. Having a plan is not the same as having a plan derived from your goal.

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

Muhammad did not sign it. He also did not send Nosizwe away. He put both documents flat on the desk and said the thing he had only felt: our strategy and our asset plan were built by different people, answering different questions, and nobody ever connected them. Five moves over the next two days:

  • Name the disconnect - two plans that never met: Muhammad does not sign, and does not send Nosizwe away. He puts both documents flat on the desk and says the thing he had only felt: our strategy and our asset plan were built by different people, answering different questions, and nobody ever connected them. The strategy said where the business was going; the asset plan said what to do with the equipment; neither was wrong, but there was no LINE OF SIGHT from one to the other - no way to trace a path from what we are trying to achieve down to what we will do to our assets. That missing line is the whole problem. When the things you decide to do with your assets are not DERIVED from the strategy - pulled out of it on purpose, reconciled against it - your money goes wherever the loudest local pressure points: the old compressor, the cylinders falling due, the queueing trucks. All real, all justified on their own, none of them chosen BECAUSE of where the business is going. Nosizwe built a sensible plan answering 'keep what we own working, stay legal, fix the loudest pain' - a good answer to a different question from the one agreed in January, because nobody had ever put the one-page strategy in her hands.
  • Build the line of sight, aim by aim: Ibraaheem draws three columns on the whiteboard, one per strategy aim, and for each asks: what must our assets DO for this to come true, and what does it cost? GROW INTO THE NORTHERN TOWN: a fifth depot needs a small filling setup, cylinders to stock it, and a vehicle to serve it - call it R600,000, and notice it was completely absent from the asset plan, because that plan was sensibly about keeping the four existing depots healthy and nobody had told Nosizwe the fifth depot was the year's first priority. WIN BACK TRUST: ask what actually lost the regulars - late deliveries - then ask what asset spend attacks late deliveries; the high-mileage bakkies genuinely cause the breakdowns and missed slots, so one replacement bakkie (R270,000, the worst one) now serves the strategy directly, and the second filling bay (R220,000) earns its place because the morning queue is exactly why some deliveries run late - not just because queues are annoying. PROTECT CASH: this aim spends nothing; it CONSTRAINS the other two, saying the total cannot blow the budget or force a loan. This is the move that turns a strategy into asset objectives: each aim, translated into what the assets must deliver, and costed - so that every rand can later be traced back up to an aim.
  • Reconcile honestly against what you can afford: with roughly R1 million of spare cash for the year and no new debt, every line now gets weighed against both the aims and the cash constraint. The fifth-depot setup and stock (R600,000) is Aim 1, the priority - IN. One replacement bakkie (R270,000) is Aim 2, killing late deliveries - IN. The requalification of cylinders due by law (R310,000) serves no strategy aim directly but is non-negotiable - IN as a must. Those first three already total R1.18m - over the R1m no-debt limit - so even the must plus the two strategy items strain the cash aim, and something has to move: they hold the second bakkie and the compressor (slow, not broken - they will watch it and act if it fails), and phase the second filling bay to mid-year when cash recovers. That keeps the year inside the no-debt promise AND points almost every rand at growing and winning back trust. Notice the honesty: the requalification serves no aim and still goes FIRST, because law and safety set a floor strategy does not get to override - a real trade-off, not a failure of the method; and the compressor, a careful manager's top priority, becomes a 'watch and wait', because on its own it is justified but against the strategy it is not this year's fight. That stings, and Nosizwe had to hear it.
  • Build the gate, then pilot it on a real plan: what they built has a name: asset-management objectives and plans DERIVED FROM and RECONCILED WITH the strategy - an unbroken line of sight from what the business is trying to achieve down to the specific things it will do to its assets and the money behind them (in asset-management language, the Strategic Asset Management Plan is the bridge; the name matters less than the line you can now draw). But a rule on a whiteboard is a guess about how next January will go, so Muhammad tests it cheaply on the plan already in his hand. He sets a success line first: this gate works if, run against the existing eleven pages, it would have blocked the spending that serves no aim and passed the spending that does - AND a careful planner who has never seen the strategy can fill it in (a gate only Muhammad could operate is no gate at all). He walks all six lines through it with Nosizwe in twenty minutes - no money spent, fully reversible, run on real lines. And it throws a real surprise: the gate does its obvious job (the compressor and second bakkie hit the wall) but BREAKS on the requalification - the rule said 'name the aim or be blocked', requalification serves no aim, so the gate wanted to block R310,000 of work the LAW requires. Run mechanically, it would have built a dangerous, illegal plan. So they change ONE thing rather than abandon the gate: a second tag - every line must answer 'which aim does this serve?' OR 'is this a must - safety/law?', and passes if it can honestly tick one. Run again, all six lines sort cleanly, and Nosizwe fills the second pass herself - the other half of the success line, met.
  • Formalise the process - own the join: the fix is not a one-off. They make one small permanent change: the next plan starts FROM the one-page strategy, in Nosizwe's hands. Each January, Muhammad and Ibraaheem agree the aims first; then Nosizwe builds the asset plan with those lines pinned to the top of her document, and every spend item has to answer one question in writing - which aim does this serve, or is it a must (safety/law)? - and if neither, why is it still in? The legal-floor items like requalification get a clear 'must - safety/law' tag so they are never quietly dropped and never wrongly blocked. The gap had opened because nobody owned the join between the going-where plan and the doing plan; now someone does, and the rule that owns it has been tested against a real plan, not just hoped over. The honest trade-off: this is slower (the old way - she builds a plan, he signs it - took one morning; the new way took two days of argument and meant parking a proud colleague's top item), and line of sight is only as honest as the strategy at the top of it - vague January lines give a vague chain. But the alternative is what they nearly did: spend R1.45m, carefully and legally, and arrive in December no closer to anywhere they meant to go.

See where it lands. Almost every rand now points at the fifth depot and at winning back trust; the legal floor is honoured; the year stays inside the no-debt promise; and the gap that opened because nobody owned the join cannot quietly reopen, because each January the plan now starts from the strategy with someone owning the line between them. Having a plan is not the same as having a plan derived from your goal. A careful, defensible, fully-costed plan can balance perfectly and still point nowhere you said you wanted to go - because it was built to keep the present running, not to reach the future you chose. The fix is never to work harder on the plan; it is to draw an unbroken line of sight from each strategic aim down into the plan (every spend naming the aim it serves, or the safety/law must it answers), reconcile it honestly against what you can afford, and give someone ownership of the join. Line of sight is only as honest as the strategy at the top of it.

Movement 3.5 - The Guided Step

You watched a clean version, where the gap was glaring - the year's number-one aim had zero rand against it. Real life rarely shows you a zero that big.

See the household budget that balances perfectly and still goes nowhere

Onke is not running a business - she is running a household. On her phone tonight she has two things open. One is a note she wrote in January: "This year: clear the store card, and start a small emergency fund." The other is her actual monthly budget spreadsheet - and here is the twist: Onke HAS a budget, updates it every month, neat, and it balances. Most people would say she is doing everything right. She earns about R18,000 a month: rent, food and transport R12,500; "subscriptions and treats" R2,500; sending money home R2,000; and "whatever's left, R1,000, goes to the store-card minimum". There is no line for the emergency fund, and the store-card balance has barely moved in a year.

Start where Muhammad started: don't look for a villain, look for the join. Onke's note says where she's going; her spreadsheet says what she actually does with her money. Put them next to each other and here is the thing to see: a budget exists, and goals exist - but the budget was not built FROM the goals. That is the trap, and it is sneakier than Muhammad's, because Onke can honestly say "I have a budget" and "I have goals" - both true - and the missing thing is the line between them. Her budget answers "where does my money currently go?"; her note answers "where do I want it to go?" - two different questions that never met. Now do Muhammad's middle move: for "start an emergency fund", ask what the money must actually do. The fund needs, say, R500 a month to begin; there is no spare R500, because the budget already balances - so the fund can only come from REDIRECTING something already in the plan. The honest reconciliation looks exactly like Muhammad's: some lines are MUST (rent, food, transport - the floor, like requalification), some are CHOSEN (subscriptions, treats - and one of those defers so a goal line can finally appear). Paying only the store-card minimum is the "old compressor" of this story: defensible on its own, serving no stated aim, quietly eating the money that should be clearing the card faster. Same place, wildly different scale - R1.45 million and R500 - identical structure. Having a plan is not the same as having a plan derived from your goal; a neat budget, a careful maintenance plan, a full calendar can all balance and still point nowhere you said you wanted to go.

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

You have just watched a business spend its way sideways - every decision sensible on its own, none of them pulling toward where the business said it was going. That is the problem-type: decisions about your assets or resources, made with no traceable line back to what you are actually trying to achieve. It is almost never a bad person; it is two plans, built by different people answering different questions, that never met. Find it where you are:

  1. Find your two documents. What says where you are going (a strategy, vision, goals - even one on a whiteboard)? And what says what you will actually do and spend (a budget, work plan, maintenance schedule, roster)? Put them side by side - were they built by the same people, answering the same question?
  2. Run the missing-aim test. Take your most important goal and look through the plan for the money and effort aimed at it. In Muhammad's case the year's number-one aim had zero rand against it. Where is your fifth depot?
  3. Separate the "must" from the "chosen". Some spend is forced - safety, law, keeping the lights on - and some is chosen. Can you tell which is which? The trap is letting the musts, plus the loudest local pain, eat all the money before the chosen, strategy-serving items get a look in.
  4. Find who owns the join. Whose job is it to make sure the doing-plan is built FROM the going-where plan, not separately from it? If the answer is "nobody", you have found why the gap keeps opening.
R

Run the smallest test this week: take the gate - "no spend is approved unless it names the aim it serves, or is a genuine safety/law must" - and run it over your current plan's lines, the way Muhammad ran it over Nosizwe's eleven pages. Count the lines it blocks: if it blocks nothing, your plan is already aligned or your rule is too soft; if it blocks a line that is actually a legal or safety must, your rule needs the second tag, exactly as Muhammad's did. (Working with an AMU facilitator? Build your aims-to-spend grid and pilot your gate on a real plan together.)

Your world is not a gas business and your numbers are not Muhammad's - but if you have any gap between what you say you want and what you actually spend your time, money or attention on, and no one whose job is to close it, then this is your problem too. The method is the same at every scale: name the gap, build the missing line one aim at a time, reconcile honestly against what you can afford, and give someone ownership of the join.
