Step 1 of 5

The strategy does not say, so I will say what I assumed

T1: The Ache

You are Onalenna, and the municipality has just released its new five-year integrated plan. Your director wants the works' asset management objectives "fully aligned to it" by Friday.

You read it twice. It promises reliable service to all households. It also requires a 15 per cent cut in operating expenditure. It says nothing about which of those yields if they meet.

So you do the arithmetic, because you are an engineer. Operating expenditure is R48.6 million. A 15 per cent cut is R7.29 million, leaving R41.31 million, and after committed costs your discretionary pool is R7.41 million. The reliability programme that the first promise requires costs R11.4 million.

They are R3.99 million apart. Not rhetorically. Arithmetically.

You cannot align with a contradiction, and you cannot ask the board to resolve it by Friday. But you also cannot simply choose - because choosing between reliability and cost on behalf of a municipality is not your decision to make.

The corporate plan is vague in one place, contradictory in another, and silent where I need it most. How do I produce asset management objectives that are genuinely aligned to it, without quietly setting strategy I have no authority to set?

Before you read on, decide. You have to produce asset management objectives aligned to the corporate plan. That plan promises to be a world-class utility, which cannot be wrong and cannot be measured. It commits to a reliability improvement and to a cost reduction which, worked through on the same programme, are R3.99 million apart — not rhetorically, arithmetically; both cannot be met. And on the question you most need answered it is silent. You cannot align with a contradiction. You cannot get the board to resolve it by Friday. And you cannot simply choose, because choosing between reliability and cost on behalf of a municipality is not your decision to make. Write down what you would do — your own answer, not a shortlist to choose from.

Now compare — the five answers, and where an interpretation belongs

• “Pick the objective that seems more important and proceed.” Sets municipal strategy from an engineering desk, invisibly. It may even be the choice the board would have made — and nobody will ever know it was made, which is the whole problem.

• “Split the difference — partially meet both.” Looks even-handed and is the same overreach with the evidence smeared. You would have invented a third position nobody authorised and made it harder to see.

• “Refer it to the board and wait.” Correct in principle and unavailable — the objectives are due, and an escalation with no proposal attached usually returns as “use your judgement”, which puts you back here with time lost.

• “Quote the corporate wording verbatim so we are demonstrably aligned.” Produces objectives that inherit the vagueness and the contradiction, which is alignment in appearance and nothing at all in substance.

• “Align to the parts that are clear and leave the rest.” Sensible-sounding, and silence about the gaps reads to every later reader as though there were none.

• The escape is to notice that an interpretation is unavoidable, so the honest move is to make it visible rather than to make it quietly. An assumption written down in a document that goes upward can be corrected by the people with authority to correct it; the same assumption applied silently is strategy set by whoever was drafting. Two disciplines make it rigorous. Treat the corporate plan as raw material rather than an instruction, and sort every statement by whether it is testable — naming something measurable or a constraint that could be breached — because only a testable assertion can generate an objective, and treating an aspiration as one manufactures a mandate nobody gave. Do not dismiss the rest: values and codes bound how an objective may be pursued, and a code forbidding you to hit a cost target by deferring a safety inspection is load-bearing. And trace both ways — down from each corporate assertion to the objectives serving it, and up from every objective to the assertion it serves. The downward trace finds what you ignored; the upward trace finds objectives that serve nothing, which are the ones somebody invented.

Here is the traceability and interpretation memorandum that answers it.

T2: The Tool in Action

This is the memorandum - what the documents actually assert, the trace down, the trace up, the flaws named, and the interpretation put where it can be corrected.

1. Read the corporate documents for TESTABLE assertions - and mark what is not one

A CORPORATE VISION IS NOT AN INSTRUCTION. IT IS RAW MATERIAL, and your first act is to sort it. Read every corporate document you are expected to align with - the vision and mission, the strategic or business plan, the corporate objectives including any environmental and sustainability commitments, the organisational values and the code of conduct - AND MARK EACH STATEMENT AS TESTABLE OR NOT. A TESTABLE ASSERTION names something that could be measured, or a constraint that could be breached: 'reliable service to all households', 'reduce operating cost by 15 per cent', 'meet the discharge standard at every outfall'. A NON-TESTABLE ASSERTION cannot be wrong: 'be a world-class utility', 'strive for excellence', 'put people first'. DO NOT SNEER AT THE SECOND KIND - values and codes of conduct do real work by setting the boundary on HOW an objective may be pursued, and a code of conduct that forbids you from meeting a cost target by deferring a safety inspection is telling you something load-bearing. But be exact about which kind you are holding, because ONLY A TESTABLE ASSERTION CAN GENERATE AN ASSET MANAGEMENT OBJECTIVE, and treating an aspiration as if it were a mandate is how a plan fills up with work nobody asked for. At Corathyl the municipality's integrated plan carries FOUR corporate objectives. THREE ARE TESTABLE. ONE - 'be a world-class utility' - IS NOT, and no amount of goodwill will make it yield an objective without you inventing the content yourself.

2. Trace DOWN - derive asset management objectives from each testable assertion

NOW TRANSLATE. For each testable corporate objective, ask what the assets must do for it to be met, and write that as an asset management objective with a measure attached. AT CORATHYL: 'RELIABLE SERVICE TO ALL HOUSEHOLDS' yields THREE - supply continuity at the reservoir zones, pump-set availability on the critical duty, and a limit on unplanned interruptions per connection. 'FINANCIAL SUSTAINABILITY' yields TWO - operating cost per kilolitre delivered, and renewal expenditure held at a level that does not build a backlog. 'ENVIRONMENTAL COMPLIANCE' yields TWO - discharge quality at every outfall, and energy intensity per kilolitre. 'BE A WORLD-CLASS UTILITY' YIELDS NONE, and you write that down rather than quietly skipping it. SO THREE OF FOUR CORPORATE OBJECTIVES - 75 PER CENT - YIELD SEVEN ASSET MANAGEMENT OBJECTIVES, AND ONE OF FOUR YIELDS NOTHING. THE POINT OF RECORDING THE ZERO is that it is not your failure and it is not nothing: it is a finding about the plan, and it belongs in front of the people who wrote it. A corporate objective that yields no asset management objective is either aimed at something other than the assets, which is fine and should be said, or it is hot air, which is also worth saying - politely, once, in writing.

3. Trace UP - audit what you are already doing for its mandate

THE TRACE DOWN TELLS YOU WHAT YOU SHOULD BE DOING. THE TRACE UP TELLS YOU WHY YOU ARE DOING WHAT YOU ARE DOING, AND IT IS THE MORE UNCOMFORTABLE OF THE TWO. Take every asset management objective already sitting in the works' plans and walk it upward: which corporate objective does this serve, and by what chain? At Corathyl there are ELEVEN asset management objectives in the current plans. SEVEN TRACE TO A CORPORATE OBJECTIVE. FOUR DO NOT - 36.4 PER CENT OF THE PLAN EXISTS BECAUSE SOMEBODY THOUGHT IT WAS A GOOD IDEA. NOW BE CAREFUL, BECAUSE THIS IS WHERE THE INSTRUMENT IS MOST OFTEN MISUSED. AN ORPHAN IS A QUESTION, NOT A CRIME. Some orphans are legacy work that has outlived its reason and should stop. Some are genuinely good ideas that predate the current plan and deserve a mandate rather than a deletion. AND SOME ARE THE MOST IMPORTANT WORK YOU DO - a statutory obligation, a safety commitment, a licence condition - which appear as orphans only because the corporate plan forgot to mention them, and those must be escalated rather than dropped. So for each orphan, record WHICH KIND IT IS and what you propose. Deleting the fourth kind because a matrix said orphan is how a traceability exercise causes harm.

4. Name the flaw - vague, contradictory or silent - and write the interpretation you are acting on

THE DOCUMENTS WILL NOT BE GOOD ENOUGH. THAT IS THE NORMAL CASE, NOT A FAILURE, and the whole professional content of this technique is what you do next. THE THREE FLAWS: VAGUE - the assertion is not testable, as with 'world-class utility'. SILENT - the plan says nothing about a thing you must decide. CONTRADICTORY - and this is the hard one, because the plan means both things and cannot have both. AT CORATHYL THE CONTRADICTION IS ARITHMETIC RATHER THAN RHETORICAL. Operating expenditure is R48.6 MILLION; the plan requires a 15 PER CENT CUT, which is R7.29 million, leaving R41.31 million. After committed costs the DISCRETIONARY POOL IS R7.41 MILLION. The reliability programme that objective 1 requires NEEDS R11.4 MILLION. THE TWO OBJECTIVES ARE SHORT OF EACH OTHER BY R3.99 MILLION, and no amount of alignment language closes that. NOW THE MOVE THAT MATTERS. THE INCOMPETENT RESPONSES ARE TO FREEZE UNTIL SOMEBODY CLARIFIES, WHICH THEY WILL NOT, OR TO PICK ONE SILENTLY, WHICH IS SETTING CORPORATE STRATEGY WITHOUT THE AUTHORITY TO DO IT. THE COMPETENT RESPONSE IS TO WRITE DOWN THE INTERPRETATION YOU ARE ACTING ON AND ATTRIBUTE IT TO YOURSELF. Not 'the strategy implies reliability comes first' - that puts your words in the board's mouth. But 'THE PLAN DOES NOT RANK RELIABILITY AGAINST COST, AND THEY ARE R3.99 MILLION APART. I HAVE ASSUMED RELIABILITY TAKES PRECEDENCE AND PLANNED ON THAT BASIS. IF THAT IS WRONG, TELL ME AND I WILL REPLAN.' Same decision. Visible, attributed, and reversible.

5. Issue the traceability and interpretation memorandum - and route it where it can be corrected

THE DELIVERABLE IS ONE DOCUMENT IN TWO PARTS, AND NEITHER PART WORKS ALONE. PART ONE, THE MATRIX: the two-directional trace, showing every corporate objective and what it yielded, and every asset management objective and what it traces to. A matrix alone looks like tidy administration. PART TWO, THE NARRATIVE: the untraceable corporate objectives, the contradictions with their arithmetic, THE INTERPRETATIONS YOU HAVE ADOPTED AND WHO ADOPTED THEM, and the orphans sorted by kind with a proposal for each. A narrative alone looks like an opinion. TOGETHER THEY ARE AN ENGINEERING ARGUMENT. THEN ROUTE IT PROPERLY, because an interpretation nobody can correct is just a private decision with better paperwork. It goes to whoever can actually rule on the contradiction - not filed, not attached to a plan nobody opens - and it carries a DATE BY WHICH YOU WILL PROCEED ON YOUR STATED ASSUMPTION IF YOU HEAR NOTHING. Silence is the most likely response and you must be able to act through it. FINALLY, THE PART THAT MAKES THIS WORTH DOING TWICE: your findings are the best contribution you will ever make to the NEXT corporate plan. You now know, with evidence, which of its objectives were untestable, which contradicted each other and by how much, and which of your work it forgot to mandate. Bring that to the next planning round and you are no longer only a reader of the strategy.

T3: The Blueprint

Strategic alignment is not a state you find. It is an act of translation you perform and then defend. Five questions:

  • What did the documents actually assert? Testable against non-testable. Only a testable assertion can generate an objective. Values and codes of conduct are not testable and are still load-bearing - they bound how an objective may be pursued.
  • What does each corporate objective require of the assets? Trace down. Three of four yielded seven asset management objectives here. The fourth yielded none, and that zero gets written down.
  • Why am I doing what I am already doing? Trace up. Eleven existing objectives, seven with a mandate, four orphans - 36.4 per cent.
  • What is wrong with the plan, and what am I assuming? Vague, silent, contradictory - and the named, attributed interpretation that lets you proceed anyway.
  • Where does it go? Routed for correction, with a date on which you proceed if nobody answers.

The trace runs both ways, and the second direction is the one people skip. Trace-down answers what should we be doing. Trace-up answers why are we doing this - and it exposes legacy work, pet projects and unmandated good ideas. But handle the orphans carefully, because this is where a traceability exercise causes harm. An orphan is a question, not a crime. Some have outlived their reason and should stop. Some are good ideas that deserve a mandate rather than a deletion. And some are the most important work you do - a statutory obligation, a licence condition, a safety commitment - which appear as orphans only because the corporate plan forgot to mention them. Deleting that fourth kind because a matrix said "orphan" is the failure mode of this technique, so sort every orphan by kind before you propose anything.

And the move that the whole unit exists for. The documents will not be good enough. That is the normal operating environment, not a sign that something has gone wrong. There are two incompetent responses and one competent one. You can freeze until somebody clarifies, and they will not. You can choose silently, which is setting corporate strategy without the authority to set it - and it is the more dangerous option precisely because it looks like getting on with the job. Or you can write the interpretation down and put your own name on it. Not "the strategy implies reliability comes first", which puts your words in the board's mouth. But: "the plan does not rank reliability against cost, and they are R3.99 million apart. I have assumed reliability takes precedence and planned on that basis. If that is wrong, tell me and I will replan." It is the same decision. The difference is that it is visible, attributed and reversible - and you have converted the ambiguity from a hidden career risk into a visible demonstration of competence. Be clear-eyed about what that does and does not buy you: in a punitive organisation, putting your name on an assumption that cuts across a board promise can still be held against you. What the memorandum changes is that the risk is now transparent and professional rather than hidden and personal - and if it is held against you, the record shows an engineer who asked rather than one who guessed.

One honest boundary. You do not author the vision, the values, the code of conduct or the corporate strategy, and this unit will not teach you to. You are a recipient of those documents whose professional duty is to read them correctly, translate them into testable objectives, and feed what you learn back into the next planning round. Mastering the current plan is the qualification for contributing to the next one.

Does it scale? A translator working from a damaged manuscript does not invent the missing lines and does not refuse to translate. They render what is there, mark the gap, and state in a footnote what they took the passage to mean - so a later reader can check the reading rather than inherit it unknowingly. Your memorandum is that footnote.

T4: Your Turn

A district municipality's roads department asks you to align its asset management objectives to the council's new term plan.

Corporate objectiveAs written
1"Every household within 500 m of an all-weather road by the end of the term"
2"Become the most respected roads authority in the province"
3"Reduce the roads maintenance backlog by half"
4"Hold the roads budget flat in nominal terms for five years"
Other materialDetail
Values statement"Safety first, always"
Code of conductForbids deferring a safety-critical intervention to meet a financial target
Existing objectivesNine, of which: 3 trace to objective 1, 2 trace to objective 3, 1 is a provincial bridge-inspection mandate, 1 is a pothole-response time set after a council complaint two terms ago, 1 is a fuel-efficiency target for the plant fleet, 1 is a depot refurbishment
BudgetR92.4m a year; inflation assumed at 5 per cent; backlog reduction costed at R31.8m a year for five years
  1. Sort the assertions. Which corporate objectives are testable, and which are not?
  2. Trace down. Derive asset management objectives for the testable ones, and record any that yield nothing.
  3. The contradiction. Find it, and put arithmetic on it rather than adjectives.
  4. Trace up. Sort the nine existing objectives, and classify every orphan by kind.
  5. Write the interpretation. Two or three sentences, attributed, with what you will do if nobody replies.

The test of a real answer: three things.

First, objective 2 is not testable and must be recorded as yielding nothing. Objective 4 is testable but it is a constraint rather than a goal - and noticing that distinction is worth as much as the trace itself, because a constraint bounds every other objective rather than generating its own.

Second, step 3 is the exercise. Holding R92.4m flat in nominal terms against 5 per cent inflation is a real-terms cut of R4.62m in year one, rising to R25.5m in year five, and about R74m of cumulative purchasing power across the term, while objective 3 requires R31.8m a year of backlog reduction. Objectives 3 and 4 cannot both be met, and saying so with a number is what makes the memorandum unanswerable. If your answer says the objectives are "in tension", you have written an adjective where the arithmetic was available.

Third, step 4 contains the trap. The provincial bridge-inspection mandate is an orphan - it traces to no council objective, because the council plan forgot it. It is also statutory, and it is about bridges falling down. A learner who lists it beside the depot refurbishment as "unmandated work to be reviewed" has used the instrument to do damage. Meanwhile the values statement and the code of conduct are not decoration: they tell you that when objectives 3 and 4 collide, the resolution may not be to defer a safety-critical intervention. The code of conduct has quietly resolved part of your contradiction for you, and most learners miss that because they filed it under "not testable" and stopped reading.

T5: The Guardrails

Alignment work goes wrong in ways that look diligent from the outside.

The common traps - and how to dodge them

• Treating an aspiration as a mandate. "World-class utility" cannot be wrong, so it cannot be traced. Record the zero rather than inventing content to fill it.

• Choosing silently between contradictory objectives. The most dangerous option, because it looks like getting on with the job. You have just set corporate strategy without the authority to set it.

• Freezing until somebody clarifies. They will not. The interpretation with a date on it is what lets you proceed honestly.

• Putting your words in the board's mouth. "The strategy implies" attributes your reasoning to them. "I have assumed" attributes it to you, where it belongs and where it can be corrected.

• Deleting orphans because the matrix said orphan. Sort them by kind first. A statutory obligation the corporate plan forgot looks identical to a pet project until you look.

• Describing a contradiction with adjectives. "In tension" changes nothing. R3.99 million apart is a question somebody has to answer.

• Skipping the trace up. It is the uncomfortable direction and it is where the unmandated third of the plan lives.

• Filing values and codes of conduct under "not testable" and stopping. They bound how an objective may be pursued, and they sometimes resolve the contradiction you are stuck on.

• Sending the memorandum where it cannot be answered. An interpretation nobody can correct is a private decision with better paperwork.

• Doing it once. The findings are your contribution to the next corporate plan - which objectives were untestable, which collided and by how much, and what it forgot to mandate.

Onalenna could not align to a contradiction and could not resolve it either. Sort the corporate documents into what is testable and what is not, and do not discard the values and the code of conduct because they bound how you may pursue everything else; trace down from each testable objective to the asset management objectives it requires, recording the ones that yield nothing; trace up from what you are already doing and sort every orphan by kind before you propose anything, because the corporate plan forgets statutory work more often than you would like; name the flaw as vague, silent or contradictory, put arithmetic on it, and write the interpretation you are acting on with your own name attached rather than the board's; and route it to someone who can correct it, with the date you will proceed anyway. Strategic alignment is not a state to be found. It is an act of translation to be performed and defended - and the sentence that makes you a professional is not "the strategy says", it is "the strategy does not say, so here is what I assumed". If you are working with an AMU Facilitator, take your organisation's strategic plan and find the objective that yields nothing.
