How do I stop my money disappearing on things that don't even make me happy?

Movement 1 - The Felt Problem

You are Nandi. You are sitting at your desk with your phone, scrolling back through your spending from the last month. You decided to be brave and actually add it up - not the big, obvious things, but the small stuff. The little leaks you never normally count.

There it is, line by line. R60 for an extra data bundle you bought in a panic - but you were on Wi-Fi most of that week anyway. R45 on a fizzy drink and chips at the till, gone before you even got home. R350 for a grey hoodie you ordered online because it looked cool in the photo - you already have two almost exactly like it, and you have not worn it once. R1,200 on the limited-edition sneakers that everyone was posting about; you felt the pressure, you bought them, and you have worn them exactly one time. R180 on Friday's takeaway that, if you are honest, was not even that nice. R95 on an in-app purchase you had forgotten about by the next morning.

You tap the numbers into your calculator. The small, forgotten, joyless things. The total stares back at you.

R1,930.

It lands like a gut punch. Nearly two thousand rand. Gone. Vanished on things that gave you almost no real, lasting joy at all. Money that could have been the deposit on that weekend trip you keep talking about, or most of the way to the drawing tablet you actually want. Money that could have been the start of your freedom. Instead it is just... gone, drip by tiny drip, on nothing you can even remember enjoying. You stare at the number, and a single raw question forms.

How do I stop my money from disappearing on things that don't even make me happy?

Movement 2 - Your Attempt

Staring at that total, three answers tempt you - and each one quietly fails. "Right, that's it - a total spending freeze, cut absolutely everything." But that is just misery, and misery never lasts: you white-knuckle it for two weeks, then rebound and binge worse than before. The enemy was never ALL spending. "They're only small amounts - they don't really matter." But you just watched those 'small' amounts stack into nearly two thousand rand; the leak is small, repeated many times, and it adds up to exactly the total that just winded you. "I'll just earn more, then I can spend freely without worrying." But a leaky bucket only leaks faster when you pour more in - if the joyless habit stays, more income just means more vanishing. None of these is the real answer.

So before you read on: the problem is clearly not that you spent money - some of it you would happily spend again. What is the real difference between the spending that hurt to see and the spending that did not - and how would you use that difference?

Sit with how it felt to see that total. The problem is not that you spent money - it is that so much of it left you with nothing: no joy, no memory, no use. Go back down the list and ask, honestly, of each one: did this actually bring me real, lasting joy - or did it just vanish?
What if the test was not 'spend less' at all, but 'get more real joy out of every single rand'? Some of those spends earned their place - they made you genuinely happy. Others gave you nothing back. The skill is not cutting everything; it is telling those two apart.
Split your spending into two columns: REAL JOY and NO REAL JOY. The second column is the enemy - the quiet leak. It is not about giving up the things you love; it is about stopping the spends that give you nothing, and sending that freed money toward what you actually want. Cut the joyless, keep the joyful.

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

The total hurt because most of it bought you nothing. That is the clue to the whole thing. Five moves:

  • Isolate the real enemy - the joyless leak: The enemy was never 'spending'. It is the R1,930 that slipped away and gave you nothing back - the bundle you did not use, the hoodie you do not wear, the snack you forgot by the time you got home. The spends you love are not the problem. The spends you cannot even remember are. Name that, and you have found exactly what to fight.
  • Run the joy-and-value test: For every rand, the real question is not 'was it cheap?' but 'did this bring me real, lasting joy or genuine value?' A R500 concert ticket that becomes a memory you keep for years is a brilliant use of money. A R50 impulse buy you forget by morning is a loss, no matter how small. Judge each spend by the joy or value it actually gave back, not by its price.
  • Spot the 'should' spend - the FOMO trap: A huge share of joyless spending is not even your own want - it is pressure. You buy because you feel you SHOULD, or because everyone online has it. Those sneakers were not about joy; they were about keeping up, and they gave you almost nothing once the post stopped getting likes. This is a trap set FOR you by people selling the fear of missing out - it is not a flaw in you. Spotting that motive is half the cure.
  • Cut the joyless, keep the joyful: This is the opposite of going without. Once you can see the joyless leaks, you plug them - and that frees up MORE money to spend, guilt-free, on the few things you genuinely, deeply love. You are not aiming to spend less for its own sake. You are aiming for more joy per rand: less on the empty stuff, more on what truly lights you up.
  • Redirect the leak to your gap: Every rand you stop leaking is not 'gone' - it is now fuel. Send it straight to the gap from the last lesson: the space between what you earn and what you spend. That R1,930 of joyless spend, redirected, is R1,930 moving you toward the trip, the tablet, the freedom you actually want. Cutting the joyless is not loss; it is taking the controls.

The whole shift lives in one line: the enemy of your freedom is not the money you spend on what you love - it is the money that leaks away on what gives you nothing. This is not about going without, and it is not about being 'good with money' as some character test you keep failing. A joyless leak is something everyone has, and a lot of it is pushed on you on purpose, by people who profit from your fear of missing out. The relief is this: you can simply SEE it now. Mark each spend joy or no-joy, plug the joyless leaks, keep spending freely on the few things that truly light you up - and send the freed money to your gap, toward the thing you actually want. More joy per rand, and freedom getting closer at the same time. That is not deprivation. That is taking the controls of your own money.

Movement 3.5 - The Guided Step

Before you take this into your own life, watch it work for someone who was sure they 'had no money' - and prove that this is about MORE joy, not less.

See how Karabelo found R1,100 a month without giving up a thing he loved

Karabelo was frustrated. His part-time job paid decently, yet he always felt broke - and he could not work out where it went. So he ran the joy-and-value test on his own spending, and two leaks jumped out.

First, his streaming services. He was paying for three: R120, R99 and R80 a month - R299 in all. But when he was honest, he only ever watched ONE of them. The other two were for single shows he had finished months ago. Pure joyless leak: R200 a month for nothing.

Second, the fancy R45 coffee he bought on the way to college most mornings - about R900 over a month. He realised it was not joy anymore; it was just a habit he did without thinking. Another leak.

So he acted. He cancelled the two streaming services he never watched - and kept the one he genuinely loved, so he lost no joy at all. He let the daily coffee go (and still treated himself to a proper one on a Saturday, which he actually savoured). Just like that, he had freed up R1,100 every month. He started sending it straight toward a drawing tablet for his design course - something he had wanted for a year. He ended up both richer AND happier, because his money was finally working for his real joy instead of leaking away on things he never even noticed.

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

Now turn the test on your own money - the real spending you did this last month:

  1. Mark every spend joy or no-joy. Go back through last month, line by line, like Nandi did. Beside each one, ask the only question that matters: did this bring me real, lasting joy or genuine value? Be honest. Seeing the no-joy column add up is the whole jolt that makes change easy.
  2. Catch the FOMO ones. Look hardest at the spends you made to keep up, or because everyone online had it. Those are usually the biggest joyless leaks - and naming the pressure that drove them takes away most of its power over you next time.
  3. Close one leak and redirect it. Pick just ONE joyless leak you can cut this month - a subscription you do not use, an impulse habit, one FOMO buy you can skip. Then send that exact amount straight to your gap, toward the thing you actually want. You are not losing it; you are aiming it.

This week, run the joy-test on last month and close one leak - then point that money at your real goal. (Working with an AMU facilitator? Bring your two columns and pick the first leak to close together.)

This was never about punishing yourself or giving up joy. It is about power - the power to see the leak that everyone has, name the pressure behind it, and turn your own money back toward your own joy and your own freedom. You do not need more income to start, and you do not need to go without the things you love. You only need to look honestly, once - and you can be the change in your own spending starting today.
