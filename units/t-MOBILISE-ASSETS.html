You have more to work with than you think

T1: The Ache

You want to start something, fix something, build something. And then you look around at what you have to work with, and your heart sinks: no money, no equipment, no premises, no resources. The idea dies right there, on three small words: "I have nothing."

But here is what that feeling hides. You are not standing in an empty room - you are standing in a world quietly stuffed with idle value: things sitting unused, skills going to waste, spaces empty half the week, time and trust nobody has put to work. You have stopped seeing it, because you have been staring at what is missing instead of what is already there.

I have nothing to work with - so how can I even begin? What if "nothing" is just a trick of where I'm looking?

Before you read on, decide. You have an idea worth doing, and no money, no equipment and no premises to do it with. Write down how you would begin anyway. Your own answer, in your own words, not a choice from a list — and if your honest answer is “I couldn’t”, write that down too, because it is where nearly everybody starts.

Now compare — the four answers, and what each one is really assuming

• “Save up until I have enough.” Sensible, honest, and for many people it means never — because the amount needed is calculated from doing it the expensive way, which is the only way you have imagined so far.

• “Get a loan, or find an investor.” Sometimes possible, and notice what it assumes: that the only way to use something is to own it. Most of what you would buy with that money is already sitting somewhere near you, doing nothing.

• “Ask people to help me out.” Warmer, and it puts you in the weakest position there is — asking for a favour, which runs out fast and costs you something every time you spend it. Somebody helping you is doing you a kindness. Somebody gaining alongside you is doing business, and that lasts.

• “Start smaller — do what little I can afford.” Genuinely good, and it still counts only what you own. It leaves out the largest thing you have, which is not in your pocket: your hours, your one real skill, and the fact that people who know you already trust you.

Before you turn the page, look once more at the place you are sitting in and ask a different question: what is around me right now that somebody owns, and nobody is using?

This is a tool for changing where you look - for becoming an organiser of value: someone who spots idle things and connects them so everyone wins. Watch how Muhammad used it to build Tank You out of what looked like nothing.

T2: The Tool in Action

Muhammad had no money and no gas business - just an idea and a feeling of having nothing. So instead of counting what he lacked, he scanned his world for what was sitting idle. Here is the tool he filled in:

1. Scan for dormant value

Empty gas cylinders sitting idle in neighbours' kitchens between refills; the parents' bakkie, idle on weekends; my own spare hours; the trust of my own street; the depot's unused reach to households too far to come in.

2. Map the asset (pick ONE)

The neighbours' own empty cylinders. Each neighbour owns theirs; it sits idle between refills, and getting it refilled is a heavy, time-eating schlep they dread.

3. The owner's win (how do THEY benefit?)

The neighbour's idle empty comes back full - the same price as the depot, but delivered to the door with no schlep. Their dead weight becomes effortless gas. They win plainly.

4. The barrier (what's in the way?)

They must trust me with their cylinder and their cash. So I start on my OWN street, with people who already know me - trust I've already earned - and prove myself one tank at a time.

5. Devise the mobilisation (fair plan + first small ask)

Collect the empties and the cash, refill them all in one bulk run, return each one full. First small ask: 'Hand me your empty - I'll bring it back full, same price, today. Try me once.'

Look what appeared once he changed his lens. He owned almost nothing - yet he was surrounded by dormant value: the neighbours' own empty cylinders gathering dust between refills, a bakkie idle on weekends, his own spare hours, the trust of his street, even the depot's unused reach. "Nothing" was an illusion. He picked one dormant asset - the neighbours' empties - found the way it could serve them (full gas, no schlep, same price), and devised a simple way to put it to work. That is the whole move: see the idle value, then connect it so everyone wins.

T3: The Blueprint

The world is not as empty as scarcity makes it feel. It is full of value that is simply idle - and your job is to organise it. Three steps:

  • 1. Scan for dormant value. Look around and list what is idle, under-used, or trapped: tools, spaces, skills, time, trust, a shared need. Crucially, include your own - your spare hour, your one real skill, your phone, the trust your family and neighbours already place in you. These are assets most people never count.
  • 2. Map the asset - and the owner's win. Pick one. Who owns it, and why does it sit idle? Then the most important question of all: how does the OWNER win when you put it to work? A fee, a share, a problem solved, goodwill, simply seeing their idle thing do some good. This is the heart of the tool - mutual win or no deal. You are an organiser of value, never a taker; if there is no real gain for the owner, you stop and find another way.
  • 3. Devise the mobilisation. Make a simple, fair plan to put that asset to work on a problem you have - and start with one small ask, not a grand demand. (If the asset belongs to a powerful or formal party - a business, a council, a gatekeeper - winning their yes is its own skill: that's the deal-making tool, where you map their gap and lead with their win.)

Notice the lens flip: you stopped asking "what do I lack?" and started asking "what is already here, sitting idle, that I could organise so everyone gains?" That question turns an empty-handed beginner into a creator.

T4: Your Turn

Dormant value is everywhere once you look - and it is not only physical things. A retired neighbour's expertise. A church hall kitchen empty all week. A bakkie idle on Sundays. A garage nobody parks in. And your own: a free hour each morning, a knack for organising people, the camera in your pocket, the trust your name already carries. Now scan your own world and put one idle asset to work.

T5: The Guardrails

This is a tool for organising value into the open, not for taking. A few lines keep it honest and strong.

The traps - and how to stay true

• Mutual win or no deal. The owner of the asset must genuinely gain. If your plan only helps you, it isn't mobilising value - it's taking it. Walk away and find a fairer way.

• Ask; never just take. Idle is not the same as free. Respect who owns the thing - approach them openly, propose the win, and let them say yes. The whole point is value brought into the light, with consent.

• Count your OWN dormant assets too. Your time, your skills, your phone, your reputation - these are real capital. Self-reliance starts with mobilising what is already yours before you ever reach for someone else's.

• Start with one small ask. Don't ask a neighbour to bet big on an untested idea. A tiny first step lets them see it work, and trust grows from there.

• Idle does not mean unwanted. Check why something sits unused before you assume it's free for the organising - sometimes there's a good reason, and respecting it keeps your name clean.

The feeling of having "nothing to work with" is almost never true - it is a lens, not a fact. Change the lens, and an ordinary street becomes full of idle value waiting to be organised into something good: cylinders, hours, skills, spaces, trust. See it, honour the people who own it, and connect it so everyone wins - and you will never again be stuck for want of resources, only for want of looking. You have far more to work with than you think. (Working with an AMU Facilitator? Bring your scan - it's a wonderful thing to widen together.)
