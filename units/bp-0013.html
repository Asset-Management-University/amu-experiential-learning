I signed before I understood

Movement 1 - The Felt Problem

You are Muhammad, with a little money coming in at last - and that makes you a target. In a bright shop, a friendly salesperson shows you a lovely new phone. "Only R299 a month! Everyone's on this deal. But I can only hold this price today." It feels like arriving, like progress. He slides the tablet across: "just sign here." You hesitate - you don't really follow all the small print - but you'd feel foolish slowing it down, and everyone signs these. So you do.

Months later the statement makes your stomach drop. That "R299" turned out to be R299 every month for two years - R7,176 - plus fees, for a phone worth maybe R3,000. You're locked in; leaving early carries a penalty. The sums, now clear on the page, settle the cold truth: you agreed to something you were never actually shown plainly. The friendly five minutes will cost you for two years.

And it isn't really about the phone. It's the store card, the "easy" loan, the policy, the next "opportunity" - the same trap, again and again, whenever a confident voice says "sign here" before you've understood.

It sounded good and everyone signs these - but I'm trapped in something costing far more than I thought. How do I stop agreeing to things I don't actually understand?

Movement 2 - Your Attempt

First, drop the shame - it's the very thing they use. You didn't sign because you're foolish; you signed because the game is rigged. The seller has given that pitch a thousand times and is paid to get your name on the line; you heard it once, in a hurry, not wanting to seem difficult. That's not a fair contest - so the answer is to stop fighting it on their terms.

Before you read on, think: what did the seller have that you didn't, in that moment? And what could you take BACK that would change everything - that costs you nothing and that they can't stop you from taking?

I signed because it sounded good, everyone has one, and the salesperson was nice and in a hurry. None of those are reasons to agree to something - they're just feelings the seller knows how to create.
The deal will look completely different once I know the TOTAL it'll cost me, what I actually get, and what happens if I want out. The seller knows all that; I didn't ask. The fix is to slow down and ask - before I sign, not after.
Take it away, sleep on it (a good deal survives the night), and ask four plain questions: what's the TOTAL cost? what do I get and NOT get? what if I can't pay or want out? who profits, and what aren't they saying? No clear answer = no signature.

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

Here is the truth to carry for life: a signature on something you don't understand is handing someone a weapon and trusting them not to use it. You never have to do that. You hold a shield, and it has two parts - the two things the seller had that you didn't: time and understanding.

  • Why we sign blind (it's not stupidity): the seller has practised the pitch a thousand times; you're hearing it once. They're paid to get your signature; you're rushed, trusting, and embarrassed to ask 'dumb' questions. The game is unfair - so stop playing it on their terms.
  • Shield 1 - TIME (slow it down): real urgency is rare; 'sign today only' is a tactic. Say: 'Thank you - I'll take this away and read it properly.' A good deal survives a night's sleep. If it vanishes the moment you walk out, it was never a good deal.
  • Shield 2 - the FOUR questions: ask of ANY money-over-time product: (1) what will it cost me in TOTAL, to the end? (not the monthly number) (2) what exactly do I get - and what's excluded? (3) what if I can't pay, or want out - what's the penalty? (4) who profits if I sign, and what aren't they telling me?
  • No clear answer is YOUR answer: if you can't get plain answers, or don't understand them - that IS the answer: don't sign. Confusion is not your fault to push through; it's a reason to walk.
  • Borrow a second pair of eyes: before signing anything big, have a trusted, sensible, independent person read it - or a free advice service. Asking for help is the smartest move you can make, never the weakest.

One honest balance: this is not "never sign anything" - some financial products are genuinely useful, and you will need a few in your life. The skill is not fear; it is understanding before agreeing. A deal you fully understand and choose with a clear head is fine. A deal you don't understand is the trap - every time, no matter how friendly the face selling it.

(And note: AMU is teaching you how to THINK about this, not which products to buy or avoid. For a specific document, a trusted, sensible person - or a free, independent advice service - is worth more than any salesperson's smile.)

Movement 3.5 - The Guided Step

So this time, Muhammad doesn't sign. He smiles and says, "Thank you - I'll take it home and read it, and come back tomorrow." And that night he runs the four questions.

See the four questions strip the "R299" bare

1. What's the TOTAL cost? R299 x 24 months = R7,176, plus a sign-up fee and monthly charges. For a phone the same shop sells for cash at about R3,000. He'd pay more than double - for the convenience of paying slowly.

2. What do I get - and NOT get? A mid-range phone (not the flagship the poster implied) and a small monthly data bundle that runs out in a week. Read aloud, it's far less than it sounded.

3. What if I can't pay, or want out? Locked for 24 months; leaving early means paying out most of what's left, plus a black mark on his credit record. A missed month adds penalties fast.

4. Who profits, and what aren't they telling me? The shop and the network make their money on the R4,000+ gap between R3,000 and R7,176 - which is exactly why "today only" and "everyone's on it" came out before any of these numbers did.

The decision: the deal that felt like arriving was really a two-year leak. Muhammad goes back, says "no thank you," and buys a decent phone for cash. His friend, who signed in five minutes, is still paying - and will be for a year and a half more. The questions, not cleverness, saved him.

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

Nothing changed about how clever you are - only that you stopped letting a stranger's hurry decide your money. That single habit will protect you for the rest of your life. The next time anyone asks you to sign for money over time - a loan, a card, a policy, a contract, a "scheme":

  1. Take back time. "I'll read it and come back." Never sign in the moment. A real opportunity survives a night's sleep; pressure to sign now is the warning, not the deal.
  2. Ask the four questions. What's the TOTAL cost? What do I get and NOT get? What if I can't pay or want out? Who profits, and what aren't they saying? If you can't get plain answers, that IS your answer - walk.
  3. Borrow a second pair of eyes. Have a trusted, sensible person (or a free advice service) read anything big before you sign. Asking is strength, not weakness.
R

Find one thing you're being offered (or already signed), work out its TRUE total cost, and run the four questions on it this week. (Working with an AMU facilitator? Bring it there too.)

Your signature is one of the most powerful things you own - it can build your future or quietly drain it for years. Never give it to something you don't understand. Understand first; sign second; or don't sign at all. That is how you stay free.
