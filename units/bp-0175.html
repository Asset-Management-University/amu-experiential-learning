Movement 1 - The Felt Problem

Before you begin - a note of care. This unit asks you to think about difficult events: your own serious illness, accident, or death, and the effect on your family. These are heavy thoughts. The goal is not to cause worry, but to build the clarity that lets you protect the people you love. Go at your own pace, and feel free to pause. If these topics feel too close right now, you do not have to face them alone. SADAG's free helpline is there to talk, 24 hours a day, on 0800 567 567.

You are Zaid, and you work hard for your two young children. You're a responsible person: you're paying off the bond on your home, you put food on the table, and you even keep up a small funeral policy that pays out R50,000 - your parents always said it was the right thing to do, so you tell yourself you're "covered".

Then one night, after the children are asleep, a quiet thought surfaces: if something happened to you - a sudden illness, a serious accident, your death - what would actually happen to them? The funeral policy would pay for your burial. And then? The bond still needs paying for years; the children still need to eat, be clothed, and finish school for a decade or more; the R50,000 would be gone in a few months. You realise you've never truly looked at the numbers - you've held on to the feeling of being covered without ever asking the real question.

Movement 2 - Your Attempt

Your first instinct is to push the thought away - it's grim, it's uncomfortable, and it's easier to trust the feeling of "covered" and not look closer. That's the most common path, and the one that leaves the biggest hole. Or you let it take hold and feel a wave of panic - the numbers seem impossibly huge, it feels hopeless and too expensive to even think about, so you freeze and do nothing, which leaves your family just as exposed. Or you tell yourself one thing will solve everything: "the funeral policy will help" (but a burial policy just buries you), "my savings will see them through" (but savings that feel big today might last six months against a family's real costs), "my business will provide" (but a business is worth little if your own hands stop).

The trap is believing "covered" is a feeling. It isn't - it's a number. So before you read on: what is the number, and does the danger really only come through one door?

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

A policy is not a plan, and a feeling is not a number. The way through is to see the problem clearly, size it, and then close the gap deliberately - one step at a time. Five moves:

  • See the trap - 'covered' is a feeling, the gap is a number: the comfort you felt from a small policy was real, but it was never based on an actual calculation - and comfort is not coverage. The first step is simply to accept that true protection comes from putting two honest numbers side by side, not from the warm feeling of having 'a policy'. You can't protect what you've never sized.
  • Put the two numbers side by side: on one side, what your family would truly NEED if you were gone: debts that must still be cleared (say a R400,000 bond), the years of living costs they'd still face (perhaps 10 years at R30,000 = R300,000), the education still to finish (say R100,000) - an illustrative total NEED of R800,000. On the other side, what your cover would actually PAY OUT: a R50,000 funeral policy. The gap is R800,000 minus R50,000 = R750,000. Seeing that number isn't cause for panic - it's the first time the problem has a size you can plan around.
  • The danger comes through more than one door: the biggest mistake is planning only for death. What if a serious illness or injury stops you working for years? The family's need is still there - but now you are alive and need care yourself, and a burial policy is useless for that. The danger comes through several doors - death, disability, serious illness - so size the gap for EACH; the kind of protection for 'I died' is different from the kind for 'I can't work', and a plan built for one leaves the others wide open.
  • Close the gap - cheapest and highest-leverage first: you don't need R750,000 in cash today, and you don't have to close it all at once. Match each part of the need to the right KIND of protection, and start where the biggest gap can be covered for the least cost. The job of this thinking is not to pick products - it's to know exactly what problem you're solving, so you can take it to qualified, professional help and start, one step at a time.
  • Face the honest tension: protection is money spent now on a month that may never come, so in good times it will always feel like a bad deal - and that feeling is exactly the trap, because it gets more expensive the longer you leave it, and the bill for waiting is never paid by you. It's paid by the people you love, in the hardest moment of their lives. Sizing it in calm, today, is how you make sure they aren't the ones who pay.

Movement 3.5 - The Guided Step

This isn't only about a salaried breadwinner - the same unmeasured gap hides behind "my work is my cover". Watch a self-employed designer.

See the same gap behind "my business is my cover"

Suri is a brilliant self-employed graphic designer. Asked about her protection, she says, "My business is my cover - it provides for my family." But the business is Suri: if an accident stops her using her hands or her eyes for a year, the business stops too, and the income vanishes overnight - just when her family's needs, and her own medical bills, are higher than ever.

Like Zaid, she has planned for only one door (the business working) and never sized the gap for the others (disability, illness). The move is the same: put the two numbers side by side for each door - what the family would NEED versus what would actually come in - see the gap as a number, and close it deliberately, cheapest and highest-leverage first.

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

This isn't about fear - it's about love, and taking responsibility for the people who depend on you in the most practical way there is. Take a quiet half-hour and a piece of paper:

  1. Write what they'd truly NEED if you weren't there to provide - debts that must be cleared (bond, car, loans), the years of living costs until they can stand on their own, the cost of the children's education. Add it up.
  2. Write what your cover would actually PAY OUT - any cover from your job, any policies you pay for yourself. Add it up. Then subtract cover from need: that is your gap - and do it for the different doors (what if you die? what if illness or injury stops you working?).
  3. Look at the number - don't look away, don't panic. It turns a vague fear into a solvable problem with a known size. You don't have to close it all today, but you can start today: take the sized gap to qualified help and say, "this is my gap - help me find the most effective way to start closing it."

Holding on to a small policy and believing you were covered was never foolish - it was a responsible person doing what they were taught, and the not-looking is only human, because who wants to picture being gone? But the people you carry deserve more than your hope; they deserve a number, and a plan. Sizing this gap is not an act of fear or a confession of failure - it is one of the most loving, capable things you will ever do: turning a silent dread into a shield you build, deliberately, one affordable step at a time, so that whatever comes through whichever door, the people you love can carry on. That is the surest foundation you can ever lay for them.
