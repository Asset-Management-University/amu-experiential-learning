Out of what they want, buried in what they don't

Movement 1 - The Felt Problem

You are Muhammad. A customer pulls up: "I need a standard two-metre hose and a new regulator." Your heart sinks - you sold the last ones yesterday. "Sorry, I'm out." He sighs: "Fine, I'll try the big hardware store." And he drives off with R250 of easy profit you'll never see. It happens two or three times a week.

Later, when it's quiet, you look at your accessory shelf. Three fancy gas lamps sit there under a film of dust - bought six months ago because the supplier had a "great deal." R1,500 of your cash, doing nothing. Beside them, an open box of rubber seals; you pick one up and it cracks in your fingers - hard, perished, useless. Another R200 turned to rubbish on the shelf.

The rent is due Friday. Your cash isn't in the bank ready to help - it's stuck right there on that shelf, in dusty lamps and crumbling rubber.

How can I be OUT of what everyone wants, and BURIED in what nobody wants, at the very same time - with no cash free to fix either?

Movement 2 - Your Attempt

Your first instinct is to fix the "out of stock" side by buying MORE of everything. But you haven't the cash - and even if you did, more rubber hoses would just perish before they sold. Your second instinct is the travelling rep at the door with an "unbeatable" 30%-off deal on a fancy gas heater. Tempting - until you glance at those dusty lamps: a deal on something that doesn't move isn't a saving, it's a faster way to bury your money. Your third instinct is to "try harder to remember" what sells - but your gut is exactly what got you here: you remember the one person who wanted a rare fitting and forget the twenty who wanted a standard hose.

So before you read on: what would you need to actually KNOW - really know, not guess - to stock the right things and stop burying cash in the wrong ones?

I'm out of the common hose AND buried in dusty lamps and perished rubber - at the same time, cash stuck on the shelf. The items aren't the same, but I've been treating them the same: buying by gut and by whatever deal comes to the door.

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

The breakthrough is simple but it changes everything: your items are not all the same - so stop treating them the same. Your money has been turned into items, but they return it at wildly different speeds. Some are racehorses; some are donkeys. You must know which is which - and stock each by its own rule:

  • Stop treating every item the same: your money has been turned into items - but they return it at very different speeds. Some are racehorses (they fly off the shelf and bring the cash straight back); some are donkeys (they sit, tie up your money, and a few quietly rot). The whole fix starts with seeing they are NOT the same - and refusing to stock them as if they were.
  • Observe and record what ACTUALLY moves: stop trusting your gut - it's what built the trap (you remember the one person who wanted a rare fitting and forget the twenty who wanted a standard hose). Get a notebook; for a few weeks make a tally mark every time an item sells. Record what truly leaves the shelf, not what you imagine sells.
  • Find your vital few: after a few weeks the truth is plain: a SMALL number of items carry most of your sales and cash - your vital few - while a long tail barely moves. Manage the vital few perfectly (never run out of your money-makers); don't pour attention or cash into the tail.
  • Match the rule to the item: now set simple rules. Fast and doesn't perish (brass fittings, metal parts): BUY AHEAD - keep a healthy stock, never run out. Fast and perishable (rubber hoses, seals): BUY LITTLE AND OFTEN - so stock stays fresh and nothing is wasted. Slow movers (dusty lamps, odd parts): CLEAR THEM OUT at a discount to free the cash, then STOP reordering.
  • Two golden disciplines: PERISHABILITY IS A CLOCK - anything that can spoil, expire, or go out of date is a ticking bomb; overstocking it isn't a risk, it's a guaranteed loss. And THE DEAL TRAP - a discount on something that won't sell is not a saving, it's your cash buried. Buy what MOVES, not what's cheap at the door.

Notice what just happened. You didn't need more cash, a bigger shelf, or a clever deal - you needed to see what was already true: a few things make your money, a few things quietly eat it, and a notebook reveals which in three weeks flat. That's the whole craft - watch the vital few, match a rule to each, and respect the clock on anything that can spoil. Most shops never do this; they buy by gut and by the loudest deal at the door, and stay forever both empty and buried.

Movement 3.5 - The Guided Step

This isn't about gas accessories - it's about anything you hold that costs money and moves at different speeds. Watch it in a kitchen.

See the same move in Fatima's community kitchen

Fatima has the very same trap, but with food.

• The felt problem: her samp and beans sell out by 1 p.m. every day (lost meals, lost money), while the expensive imported spices she bought for a "gourmet week" sit in the cupboard with their use-by dates creeping up (cash trapped, about to spoil).

• The old way: buy a bit of everything at the market, by what looked good or was on special.

• The new move: she tallies her dishes and sees her vital few - samp and beans, and her chicken stew - carry the kitchen. Then she matches a rule to each ingredient:

  - Samp, beans, onions (fast, don't spoil quickly) -> buy ahead, in bulk once a month.
  - Fresh chicken, vegetables (fast, perishable) -> little and often, bought fresh each morning.
  - Imported spices, fancy flour (slow, perishable) -> clear and stop: don't stock them at all; for a special order, take a deposit and buy them for that job only.

She stopped treating every ingredient the same - so now she never runs out of her bestsellers, and her cash stops expiring on the pantry shelf.

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

Look at any collection of items you manage - shop stock, parts for your tools, materials for what you make, even the supplies in your kitchen. Your money and effort are tied up in them. Here's how to free the cash and stop the gaps:

  1. Record what actually moves. For two weeks, change nothing - just tally what you truly sell or use. Let the real numbers replace your memory.
  2. Find your vital few. The small number of items behind most of the activity - those are the ones to manage perfectly and never run out of.
  3. Give each item a rule. Fast and lasts -> buy ahead. Fast and perishable -> little and often. Slow -> clear it out and stop reordering. And never overbuy a perishable on a "deal".

Start the tally this week, and pick one slow, cash-trapping item to clear. (Working with an AMU facilitator? Bring your tally - it's powerful to read together.)

This was never about complex spreadsheets or having more money - it was about seeing what's real. Stock stops being a pile of anxiety and becomes a reliable engine: your money flows instead of freezing, your bestsellers are always there, and nothing quietly rots away your hard-earned cash. You buy what moves - and you're never again empty and buried at the same time.
