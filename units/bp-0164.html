We're bigger and busier than ever - so why is the very thing that made us good falling apart, and why are the people who built it walking away?

Movement 1 - The Felt Problem

You are Muhammad, founder of Tank You, and for the first time in years the business isn't just surviving - it's growing fast: more bakkies on the road, more drivers, more families getting their 9kg gas than ever. It should feel like victory. But something precious is slipping through your fingers, and you hear it in small, painful ways: a regular, Mrs Khumalo, says a new driver was curt and didn't check her connection properly; a bakkie missed its weekly safety check; the easy, respectful way Sipho and Thabo always carried themselves feels diluted. The warmth that made Tank You Tank You is fading.

Then the blow lands. Jabu - one of your first and best, a man who helped build this from the ground up - quietly tells you he's leaving, for a job that pays a few hundred rand more a month. You look in his eyes and you know that isn't the real reason. It feels like a piece of the company is breaking off.

We're bigger and busier than ever - so why does it feel like the very thing that made us good is falling apart, and why are the people who built it with me the ones walking away?

Movement 2 - Your Attempt

Your first thought is to fix it with money: you offer Jabu a raise, more than the other firm. He's grateful - but gently shakes his head; his mind's made up. The money doesn't hold him, because money was never the real problem. Your next thought is a speech, or a poster in the depot - "The Tank You Way" - but even as you think it you know paper is just paper; if the way isn't lived, every day, the words are hollow and people feel the difference. Or, in frustration, you blame the new drivers - "they're just not as good as the first team" - but is that fair? Did anyone ever truly sit with them and show them the unwritten way Sipho, Thabo and Jabu simply caught from being beside you when the whole team fit in one room? You hired them to drive; you never taught them how to serve.

Each attempt treats the symptom, not the cause. So before you read on: Jabu's leaving is the fever, not the illness - what is the illness, the thing that quietly stopped happening as you grew?

See the leaver as a symptom (a warning light), not the cause; the real cause is that the way stopped spreading once the team grew past closeness. Money is a hiding place. Make the way explicit and build it into how people ARRIVE, how the DAILY WORK is done, and who you THANK - and live it yourself, because a way no leader lives is hollow.

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

The unwritten way you do things - which grew by itself when you were small - will not scale by itself as you grow. You must now do on purpose what used to happen by closeness. Five moves:

  • See the leaver as a warning light, not the cause: Jabu leaving isn't the problem - it's the bright, painful warning light telling you the engine's in trouble. He's going because the place he helped build no longer feels like the place he works; the few-hundred-rand 'reason' is the surface, not the cause. Don't curse the warning light - thank it, and look under the bonnet for what it's telling you.
  • Find the real cause - the unwritten way stopped spreading: when it was just you, Sipho, Thabo and Jabu, the 'Tank You way' (never leave a family stuck, double-check every connection, be a guest in a customer's home, look after each other) spread by itself - new people caught it from being close to you every day. Grow from four drivers to fifteen and that closeness is gone; the new drivers never got to catch it. It's not that they're worse - the message simply stopped reaching them.
  • Make the way explicit - build it into how people ARRIVE: when a new driver joins, don't just hand over keys and a route - welcome them by teaching the way out loud: 'At Tank You we never leave a family without gas for the night if we can help it; we double-check every connection; we're guests in our customers' homes - this matters as much as the delivery.' What you used to pass on by closeness, you now pass on, on purpose, from day one.
  • Build it into the daily work and into who you thank: weave the way into the job: make the standard of care part of the daily checklist (not just the tyres), share good customer feedback in team meetings and say WHY it's the Tank You way - show it, don't just tell it. And make your thanks and promotions the loudest signal: praise a driver specifically for living the way, and lift up the guardians of it - not only the fastest.
  • Live it from the top: this only works if you, the leader, live it most of all - if you say respect matters but you're short with a driver, the whole thing collapses. Your actions are the real standard, the poster everyone actually reads. (And remember: money is a hiding place - a raise can't replace belonging or pride in how you serve, so don't let the pay-reason hide the real one.)

See what really happened. When it was you, Sipho, Thabo and Jabu, the way you serve - never leave a family stuck, double-check every connection, be a guest in a customer's home, look after each other - spread by itself, caught from being close to you each day. Grow to fifteen drivers and that closeness is gone; the new drivers were handed keys and a route but never the way. Jabu didn't leave for money - he grieved a place that no longer felt like the one he built, and his leaving is the warning light. So you do deliberately what closeness once did for free: you say the way out loud and build it into how new drivers arrive, into the daily work, and into who you thank - and you live it yourself, hardest of all. The way that grew by itself will not scale by itself; the leaver is the symptom, the broken passing-on is the cause; money is a hiding place - so make the way explicit, lived not framed, and carry on purpose the soul that once carried itself.

Movement 3.5 - The Guided Step

This isn't only about a gas company - it's any growing thing with something precious at its heart. Watch a cafe.

See the same loss (and fix) at a beloved cafe

Maria's little cafe was famous because she knew every regular's name and their usual order - it felt like a second home. It grew so popular she opened a bigger second cafe across town, hiring new staff who were efficient and polite. But the new place felt different: the staff didn't know the regulars; coffee was served, but the warmth was gone, and soon some loyal customers stopped coming.

The problem wasn't the coffee or the staff - it was that the "Maria way" (knowing and caring for your people) didn't transfer; it stopped spreading. So Maria does on purpose what closeness once did: a new training day where she doesn't teach the coffee machine but tells the story of her first customers; a little daily game of "learn one regular's name"; and she pointedly thanks the staff member who remembered someone had a hard week and asked after them - building her way into arrival, daily work, and thanks.

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

This is about any growing group with something precious at its heart - a team, a business, a charity, even a family. Where growth is straining the unwritten way:

  1. Read the symptoms, find the cause. Are standards quietly drifting in small everyday signs? Are good, long-standing people starting to drift away "for money" or "a new opportunity" in a way that feels deeper? See their leaving not as betrayal but as a warning light - and ask what really stopped happening.
  2. Name the way out loud, and build it in. Be the one to say it plainly - "this is who we are, this is how we treat people, this is what matters most" - then build it into how new people are welcomed, how the work is really done each day, and who gets thanked and lifted up.
  3. Live it yourself. The way is kept not in words but in actions, starting with yours - a stated way no leader lives is worse than none.

It is no failure that your closeness didn't scale - that warmth was the very thing that made you worth growing, and almost every good, growing thing meets this moment. But the soul of why you serve - the care that made customers trust you and good people proud to stay - does not pass itself on once you grow past one room. So carry it on purpose: name it, build it into every arrival and every ordinary day, reward the people who keep it alive, and above all live it yourself. Growth that hollows out how you serve is not success; growth that carries the mission forward, intact, into more hands and more homes - that is the whole point.
