Step 1 of 5

My dream is bigger than I am. How do I reach it when I have only ever known how to rely on myself?

Movement 1 - The Felt Problem

You are Lethabo, and you have a dream worth having - a business, a project, a thing you want to build that would matter. It is real, and it is good, and it is big. Bigger, if you are honest with yourself, than one person could ever build with two hands and twenty-four hours in a day.

And you are trying to build it entirely alone. Not because you are hoarding tasks you could hand over, but because you have always done things yourself. Life taught you early that if you wanted something done, you did it - no one was coming to do it for you, so you learned to rely on yourself, and that self-reliance carried you all the way here. It is who you are. So now, faced with a goal too big for one person, your answer is the only one you know: work harder, do more, push through alone. Asking anyone for help feels like admitting you are not enough - or like taking on a debt you will owe forever.

But the dream is not moving. It cannot, because it is simply larger than one pair of hands, and you are exhausting yourself throwing those two hands at a mountain. Hear this before anything else: the strength that got you here is real and necessary. But it will not get you there. To build something bigger than yourself, you must add a new strength to the one you already have: the strength of building and accepting help.

Movement 2 - Your Attempt

You are standing at the foot of something bigger than you, and every instinct you have ever trusted pulls you toward a move that feels right - and quietly keeps the dream out of reach. Three tempt almost everyone here.

1. Work harder: "I will just push harder and longer, and will it into being on my own." This is the move that got you everywhere so far, so it feels like the answer. But you cannot out-work a goal that is genuinely bigger than one person. Effort is not the thing you are short of - capacity is. Push harder against that wall and the wall does not move; only you break.

2. Shrink the goal: "I will just make the dream smaller, cut it down to what I can do by myself." This feels safe and honest. But look at what you are really doing: you are quietly abandoning the very thing that was worth doing, and settling for something smaller than you are capable of - not because the big dream was wrong, but because you would not reach for the help it needed.

3. Wait for resources: "I will get help later - once I can afford to hire people, then I will bring others in." But help is not only paid staff you cannot yet afford, and waiting for the perfect moment means the dream sits still forever. The mentor who has walked this road, the partner who shares the vision, the community who would rally - they are available now, and they cost not money but the courage to ask.

There is a fourth path, and it does not ask you to abandon your self-reliance. It asks you to build something on top of it.

Is the problem a lack of effort, or a limit on capacity? You have out-worked every obstacle so far - but what if you have already hit the ceiling of what one person can do, and no amount of extra hours can raise it?
When you shrink the goal to fit your own two hands, what is the true cost? You are not being realistic - you are quietly abandoning the part of the dream that was worth reaching for. What did you let go of, and was it really impossible, or just impossible alone?
What if the most valuable help you need right now cannot be hired? What if it is not paid staff at all, but a mentor's hour, a partner who shares the vision, or a community who would rally - available now, for the price not of money but of the courage to ask?

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

The way to build something bigger than yourself is not to become a bigger self. It is to stop being alone. No one has ever built something great with one pair of hands - and the sooner you accept that, the sooner the dream starts moving. Five moves:

  • 1. Face the arithmetic: Your capacity is a hard limit - your hours, your skills, your energy. A goal bigger than that limit cannot be reached by one person, no matter the effort. This is maths, not a moral failure, and refusing to accept it just breaks you against the wall.
  • 2. Reframe self-reliance: The pride of 'I did it myself' is real and often hard-won - it is the engine that got you here. But past a certain size of goal it becomes the cage that holds you. The new strength is not doing more alone; it is building with others, added on top of the strength you already have.
  • 3. Map the faces of help: Help is not only employees you pay. It is a partner who shares the vision, a mentor who has walked the road, a supplier, a collaborator, a community, someone who owes you nothing but a good answer to one good question. Map the help your goal actually needs, in all its forms.
  • 4. Ask with trust: A specific, honest ask is not an admission of weakness - it is an act of trust. You are not saying 'I am not good enough'; you are saying 'I believe you are capable, and I trust you with a piece of my goal.' That honours the other person, and most people are glad to help when asked clearly.
  • 5. Build the web both ways: This is not leaning on others - it is building a web where support flows in both directions. Give help freely; offer your skill and time to others building their dreams. The strongest structures are not lone pillars but interconnected webs, and every hand you lend strengthens the web that holds you.

The whole thing lives in one line: your own two hands are a hard ceiling, and the art of doing something bigger than yourself is to stop trying to be enough on your own and start building a web of help, given and received. This is not about leaning on others and it is not about becoming a bigger hero - it is interdependence, which is neither lonely independence nor helpless dependence, but the higher strength of we-can-do-together-what-none-of-us-could-alone. A person becomes more through other people, and a dream becomes real the same way.

And this is the first step of a longer road. Accepting you need a web of help is where it starts - here. The next step, for many, is overcoming the fear of letting others actually do the work. And once you have people around you, the work becomes learning to lead them well and to grow their capability. But it all begins with the willingness this problem is about: to stop building alone.

Movement 3.5 - The Guided Step

Before you take this into your own dream, watch two builders with the same big vision - and see that the difference is not talent or luck, but whether each one chose to stay alone or chose to build a web.

The same dream, two builders

The first builder goes it alone. Proud and fiercely self-reliant, she insists on doing every part herself. She works to exhaustion, year after year, and the dream stays small - not because she lacked talent or grit, but because it was always bigger than one person and she would never let it be more than her. In time she grows bitter that no one ever helped her - though the truth is she never truly asked, and turned away the hands that offered. She built a project the size of herself, and it cost her everything she had.

The second builder builds a web. She has the same dream and the same self-reliance - but she faces the arithmetic honestly: this is bigger than me. So she finds a mentor who has walked the road and asks for an hour of their wisdom. She brings in a partner who shares the vision and carries the half she is weakest at. She asks her community for the things a community can give, and she gives freely in return - her own skill, her own time, to others building their dreams. The web grows, and the dream grows with it, into something none of them could have built alone - and it lifts every person in the web as it rises. She did not do something bigger than herself by becoming bigger. She did it by no longer being alone.

Same dream. Same strength at the start. One built a cage the size of herself; one built a web with no ceiling at all.

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

You do not build a web by deciding to trust everyone tomorrow. You build it one honest ask and one freely given hand at a time. Start now, while the dream is still ahead of you:

  1. Name the gap between your goal and your capacity. Be honest about the size of the dream and the size of one pair of hands. Where exactly does what you want to build exceed what you alone can reach? That gap is not your failure - it is simply the shape of the help you need.
  2. Map your web of help. List the kinds of help the dream actually needs - a skill you lack, a door you cannot open, wisdom you do not yet have, more hands for the work - and beside each, who could give it. Look wider than paid staff: a mentor, a partner, a supplier, a community, someone who has done this before and would answer one good question.
  3. Make one ask, and give one hand. This week, make one specific, honest ask of one person on your map - clear about exactly what you need. And offer one specific help to someone building their own thing. Start the web flowing in both directions, so it is a web and not a favour.
R

The self-reliance you carried here was real and hard-won - there is no shame in it, and you do not lay it down; you build on it. But you now know the truth that lets your dream finally grow: a goal bigger than one person is not reached by one person trying harder, but by many people, held together in a web of help given and received, doing together what none could do alone. A specific, honest ask is not a confession that you are not enough - it is an act of trust that says I believe you are capable, and I will trust you with a piece of my goal. Build the web, give as much as you ask, and watch the thing you could only dream alone become real through many hands. That is you, being the change.
