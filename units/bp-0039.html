My business is real and it works - so why can't I prove it exists?

Movement 1 - The Felt Problem

You are Muhammad, and Tank You is real - you can feel it in your bones. Every day you and Sipho deliver gas, your customers trust you, and the cash is real: it pays Sipho, buys stock, supports your family, and leaves a little to save. The business works, and it makes money. You can see the next step too: a second bakkie would let you take on Thabo and double your rounds - about R40,000. You have some savings, but not enough, so you walk in to borrow the rest.

A polite gatekeeper listens, nods, and asks the simple question: "Can I see the business - your records, bank statements, proof of income?" And you have... nothing. It lives in your head, in the worn notes in your pocket, in your customers' trust; your business money and personal money are one tangled account. The gatekeeper cannot see a business at all - only a person asking for money with no proof of how it comes back. The answer is no.

My business is real, it's good, it makes money - so why can't I prove it even exists?

Movement 2 - Your Attempt

You feel the sting of the "no" and you react. First, you want to argue: "just trust me - it's a great business, I've never missed a delivery!" But a stranger can't run on your word; their job is to verify, not simply believe. Then you think, give up and stay small: save cash bit by bit, maybe afford the bakkie in two years - and your growth is rationed to whatever you can scrape together alone. Then comes the dangerous one - the shortcut: someone who lends with no paperwork, no questions. It feels like a way around the gatekeeper, but that lender takes their price in other ways - unfair terms and no protection if anything goes wrong. That's not a short road to growth; it's a short road that strips away your safety.

So the real question isn't "why don't they trust me?" It's something else - what is it the gatekeeper actually can't do, and what would let them?

Tank You is real - busy, profitable, customers trust me. I went to borrow for a second bakkie, and the gatekeeper asked to see the business: records, proof of income. I have nothing - it's all cash, in my head, tangled with my own money. They said no. My business is real - so why can't I prove it exists?
They're not doubting my value - they just can't SEE it. What if I made the business legible to a stranger: its own clean identity, records made on purpose, and a neutral source to confirm them?
Separate identity (own account/name); deliberate records (income written down on purpose, outsider-readable); independent confirmation (a bank statement backs your claim); start the track-record clock TODAY (it can't be back-dated); refuse the no-paperwork shortcut that strips your protection.

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

The gatekeeper can't see it. The value of Tank You is already there - the answer is not to become more valuable, but to make that existing value legible to a stranger. With Ibraaheem, you take five deliberate steps:

  • Separate identity - give the business its own life: open a simple, separate account in the business's own name, so its money is no longer tangled with your groceries and family expenses. It costs a little each month - that is the price of legibility. The business now has its own clean existence that a stranger can look at on its own, instead of hiding inside your personal life.
  • Deliberate records - make evidence on purpose: stop relying on memory and scattered scraps. Keep a simple, consistent record - date, customer, amount - of every payment, and put the day's takings through the business account. It needn't be fancy; it must be on purpose and readable by an outsider. You are producing evidence deliberately, not leaving accidental traces.
  • Independent confirmation - turn a claim into proof: this is the master move. Your record says 'we earned R18,000 last month' - that is your claim. When the business account's statement shows R18,000 of deposits, a neutral, trusted source has confirmed it. The story becomes evidence: not 'trust me', but 'see for yourself' - and a stranger can act on what they can verify.
  • Start the track-record clock TODAY: the most powerful proof of all is a track record - a gatekeeper wants to see six months, a year, of clean records. You cannot build that retroactively; it can only grow over time. So the single most important move is the one with no instant reward: start the records TODAY, so that in six months the proof simply exists.
  • Refuse the protection-stripping shortcut: there is always a tempting shortcut - a no-questions, no-paperwork deal that skips the slow work. But it takes its price in other ways: unfair terms and no protection if things go wrong. The slow, legible road is not the obstacle - it is the thing that unlocks real, safe growth and keeps you protected on the way up.

Watch a claim become proof. After a few months your record says, in plain ink, "we earned R18,000 last month" - and the business account's statement shows R18,000 of deposits to match. The same value that was invisible is now something a stranger can verify in thirty seconds. That's the whole secret: a gatekeeper does not say yes to a good thing - it says yes to a good thing it can SEE. Your business was always good; now it is also legible - and legibility, built patiently from today, is what quietly turns a good business into a fundable one.

Movement 3.5 - The Guided Step

This isn't only about borrowing money - it's any time your real value is invisible to a gatekeeper. Watch it with a skilled craftsperson.

See the same three moves win a carpenter the contract

A carpenter is genuinely brilliant, but works informally. A hotel wants custom furniture built and asks for a portfolio and references - and suddenly her real skill is invisible; she has only random photos on her phone and her own say-so. Same problem, same cure:

• Separate identity: she builds a clean, professional portfolio - her best work, presented on purpose, apart from the clutter of her phone.

• Deliberate records: for each piece she records what the client wanted, what she made, and the result, with good photographs - evidence created deliberately.

• Independent confirmation: she asks past clients for a short written reference ("delivered on time, excellent quality") - a neutral witness turning her claim into proof.

Her competence didn't grow - it became visible. Now the hotel's gatekeeper can see it, and gives her the contract.

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

Look at your own venture or work. What part of it is genuinely valuable - your earnings, your skill, your reliability - but invisible on paper? What opportunity stays shut because a gatekeeper can't see the proof? Make that value legible - not next year, not when you feel "ready", but today:

  1. Separate it. Give the valuable thing its own clean identity - a separate account, a separate folder, a proper name - untangled from your personal life.
  2. Record it deliberately, and start the clock. Begin a simple, consistent, outsider-readable record of how it performs - on purpose, from today - because a track record can only be built over time, never back-dated.
  3. Get it confirmed - and refuse the shortcut. Back your records with a neutral source (a statement, a receipt, a written reference) to turn claim into evidence; and turn down the no-paperwork shortcut that strips your protection.

A gatekeeper is not there to stop you - they are waiting for you to hand them the evidence that lets them say yes. The value is already in your hands; the only thing missing is a form a stranger can read. Start building that proof today, quietly and on purpose, and the door that was shut because no one could see your worth will open the moment they finally can.
