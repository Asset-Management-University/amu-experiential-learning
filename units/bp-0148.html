The money in the account was a liar

Movement 1 - The Felt Problem

You are Muhammad, and it is a tight month. Then the worst timing: a bakkie's gearbox goes, and the mechanic needs R5,000 by Friday or the bakkie - and its deliveries - stay locked in his yard. Heart sinking, you open the banking app. The balance reads R18,000. A wave of relief washes over you. Thank God. We have it. You pay the R5,000 and get back to work.

But that R18,000 was lying to you. Buried inside it was about R7,000 of tax you'd added to sales all quarter and must hand to the tax office - and a R4,000 deposit a school paid you for a big gas-system installation for their kitchen next month. Money sitting in your account, yes - but not yours. Your own spendable money had only ever been about R7,000.

You don't feel it yet. The account still shows R13,000; you feel fine. Then the tax deadline arrives. R7,000 due to the tax office. The school's R4,000 must stay safe for their job. That's R11,000 of your R13,000 already spoken for - leaving R2,000 for wages and materials. The cold dread rises as the truth lands: the business looked healthy the whole time, and you have been quietly spending money that was never, ever yours.

There was money sitting in my account, so I used it - but it turns out it was never mine to spend. How do I tell what's TRULY mine from what I'm only holding for someone else?

Movement 2 - Your Attempt

The instinct that got you here was natural: the money was there, the bill was real, so you paid it. But notice the hidden error - you treated the whole balance as "what I have," when part of it was money you were only holding for the tax office and the school. And "I'll just remember which part is theirs" never survives a tight Friday.

Before you read on: if the account balance can't tell you what's truly yours, what could? How would you make the not-yours money impossible to spend by accident?

I saw a healthy balance and felt safe - but a big chunk of it was tax I'd collected and a customer's deposit. The account number wasn't telling me what's MINE; it was just adding up everything sitting there, including what I owe.
The problem is that the money I'm holding for others looks exactly like my own. The only way to stop mixing them up is to physically pull them apart - the instant the not-mine money lands.
So: the moment money that isn't fully mine arrives, I move that part into a separate 'not mine' pot and treat it as already gone. I run the business only on what's left - and I never, ever borrow from the held pot.

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

Remember the wall you once built between your business money and your own pocket? This is the second wall - and it runs inside the business: between the money that is truly the business's, and the money the business is only holding for others.

  • The trap: the balance is lying to you: the number in your account includes money that isn't yours - tax you collected, a deposit, money owed onward. It looks like wealth; part of it is a debt you're holding.
  • Move 1 - separate it the INSTANT it arrives: the moment money that isn't fully yours lands, move the not-yours part to a separate place (a second account, or an envelope marked NOT MINE). Don't wait, don't 'do it later'.
  • Move 2 - treat it as already gone: that money belongs to someone else; you're only its security guard. Don't look at it, don't count it, don't let it tempt you. To you, it's spent the day it arrives.
  • Move 3 - your true income is what's LEFT: run the whole business only on what remains after the not-yours is set aside. Your real cash is the balance minus everything you owe.
  • Move 4 - NEVER borrow from the held money: however tight the month, never 'borrow just this once' from the held pot. The bill is certain, and that first borrow is the start of a spiral that has ended countless businesses.

The whole thing rests on one act you can do today: physically pulling the not-yours money out of reach the moment it lands, and never touching it. It is the difference between a business that meets the tax deadline with a shrug and one that meets it with panic. And this is not about being clever with money - it is about being honest, including with yourself. A business that never quietly spends what it owes is a business that can finally sleep at night.

(One plain note: this is the principle, not tax advice. The "tax you add to a sale" simply means the bit you add to your price, collect for the government, and must pay onward. For the exact rules where you are, ask the tax office or a bookkeeper.)

Movement 3.5 - The Guided Step

So you set up a second account - you label it, only half-joking, "NOT MINE" - and you make one rule about every rand that comes in.

See the one habit that kills the trap

A single sale, seen truly: a customer pays R1,150. It is tempting to read that as R1,150 earned. But R150 of it is tax you're holding for the tax office - so only R1,000 is actually yours. The moment it lands, R150 goes straight to the "NOT MINE" account. Same with any deposit.

Path A - the old way (mix it all): the main account looks fat and healthy, so it gets spent. Tax day comes, the money's gone, a penalty lands on top, and you "borrow" from next month's tax to cover this month's - the spiral that sinks businesses.

Path B - the new way (separate on arrival): the main account honestly shows only what's truly yours. It looks smaller - but it's the truth. Tax day comes and the money is already sitting in "NOT MINE," waiting. You pay it in thirty seconds and feel nothing but calm. The school's deposit is there for their job. No penalty. No spiral. Ever.

Same business, same sales - opposite lives, decided entirely by whether the not-yours money was separated the day it arrived.

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

The account balance felt like the truth, and it was lying. The fix wasn't more discipline in your head - it was one wall, built once, that makes the right thing automatic. Anywhere money passes through your hands on its way to someone else:

  1. Name the "not mine" money. Tax you collect, deposits for work not yet done, money owed onward, even cash you hold for a group or a savings club. It is not income - it is a duty.
  2. Separate it the instant it arrives. A second account or an envelope marked "NOT MINE." Move it the same day, and treat it as already gone.
  3. Live only on what's truly yours - and never borrow the rest. Your real income is what's left after the held money is set aside. The held pot is sacred, no matter how tight things get.

Many businesses that look perfectly healthy are quietly insolvent - alive only because they're spending money they owe. The ones that last learn early that not every rand in the account is theirs, and they guard the difference like their life depends on it. Because, in the end, it does.
