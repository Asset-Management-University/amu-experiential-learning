I can't buy the skill my business needs

Movement 1 - The Felt Problem

You are Muhammad, 21. Tank You is growing faster than you can hold it together. Between the Baraka Kitchen and the new housing development, your phone never stops - a customer says nobody came, a driver needs a new address, two orders clash for the same hour. You spend your whole day untangling it, which means you have no time left to find new customers or even think. You are not running the business; you are firefighting it.

You know what you need: a real operations person - someone who can hold all the routes, drivers and clients in their head and keep the whole machine turning smoothly. So you go to find one.

And the market laughs at you. The experienced ones want R25,000 to R30,000 a month - more than Tank You clears. So you take a chance on a sharp young man at R15,000, and for two months you pour everything into teaching him the routes, the clients, the way you do things. Just as he gets good, a big logistics firm offers him R20,000 and a company car. He is gone in a week. Two months and a small fortune in wages - vanished. And you are back on the phone.

You sit there, exhausted and stuck:

I keep trying to buy the skill I need, and I keep losing - it's too rare, too dear, or they leave. How do I ever get the people to grow?

Movement 2 - Your Attempt

Your whole plan has been the same move on repeat: go to the market, buy a finished expert. And it keeps failing the same three ways - the skill is rare, or it costs more than you can bear, or the moment you have built it, someone bigger buys it away from you.

Before you read on, ask yourself what you have NOT tried. You keep reaching outside for the skill. Is there another place a skill can come from? And look around you - who is already here, who has never let you down?

I keep trying to BUY a finished expert off the market, and the market keeps beating me - too rare, too dear, or they leave. Maybe the problem is that I'm only thinking about BUYING the skill.
Sipho isn't a trained dispatcher. But he's reliable, he knows the routes, the drivers trust him - and he hasn't left. I can't buy that loyalty anywhere. What if I grew the skill on top of the trust I already have?
So: build the scarce skill in someone I already trust. But a bigger firm can always outbid a salary - so I also have to give him a reason to stay that money alone can't beat: a real role, a real share of what he helps build, in a fair deal we both sign.

Movement 3 - The Worked Solution

The market keeps beating you because you keep playing its game - trying to BUY a finished, scarce skill in open competition with people who can outbid you. There is a second way to get a skill, and it is one no rival can take from you: you BUILD it, in someone you already trust.

  • The trap: thinking you can only BUY it: a scarce, dear skill keeps slipping away - too rare, too costly, or poached the moment they're trained. Each miss burns wages, weeks, and your growth.
  • Buy common skills; BUILD scarce ones: buy a skill when it's common and cheap (another driver). Build it when it's scarce and dear - grow it in someone already TRUSTED and reliable. You can't buy reliability; you can grow a skill on top of it.
  • Bind 1 - a PATH: a real title and real authority ("Operations Lead"). Respect and a future - things a bigger wage somewhere else doesn't give.
  • Bind 2 - a STAKE: a real share of the upside they help create (e.g. a small slice of the profit on the contracts they run). A partner in the reward, not just a wage - so when the business wins, they win.
  • Bind 3 - a FAIR AGREEMENT: one page, plain words: what you invest in training them, what they commit in return, how their share grows. A pact made openly - dignity and trust, never a trap.

The split is simple. A common, cheap skill - another driver - just buy it. A scarce, dear skill - someone who can run the whole operation - build it, in a person whose reliability you already know. Because here is the thing the market cannot sell you: loyalty. You can train a skill into a reliable person far more easily than you can train reliability into a skilled stranger.

But building is only half the move - and skipping the other half is how you lost your last hire. Never build a skill without also building a reason to stay - one that a bigger salary somewhere else cannot simply beat. That reason has three parts: a PATH, a STAKE, and a FAIR AGREEMENT. And hear this clearly, because it matters to who we are: a stake is not a trick to chain someone. It is fairness - you share the reward with the person who helps you build it. You are not buying a tool; you are making a partner.

Movement 3.5 - The Guided Step

Ibraaheem, the numbers brother, does the sum that settles it - and then you make the offer.

See the maths, and the offer to Sipho

The cost of buying-and-losing (what just happened): two months teaching the young man at R15,000 = R30,000, plus weeks of your own time recruiting and the work you dropped, call it R10,000. About R40,000 gone - and then you start again, and likely lose the next one too.

The cost of building-and-binding Sipho: Sipho is reliable, knows the routes, and has never left. You train him into the operations role over a few months. His STAKE: a small share - say 2% - of the profit on the contracts he runs. Right now that is about R200 a month, growing as he grows those contracts. His path: the title and authority of Operations Lead. The agreement: one page you both sign.

The comparison: R40,000 lost every time a bought hire walks - versus about R200 a month for a loyal, capable Sipho who stays AND has his own reason to grow the contracts (his share grows with them). The stake is not a cost. It is the cheapest, surest thing you will ever buy: a partner who wins when you win.

The offer: "Sipho - I don't want to just keep you driving. I want you to run our operations. I'll teach you everything. You'll have the title, real say, and a share of the profit on the contracts you run - growing as they grow. Here it is, one page, for both of us to sign."

Notice what the stake did: it turned an employee who might leave into a partner who is pulling the same direction as you - because now, when Tank You grows, Sipho grows too. That is something no rival's salary offer can match.

Movement 4 - In Your Own World

You stopped trying to win a bidding war you could never win, and built the skill you needed inside your own circle - then gave the person a real reason to stay. That is how small ventures grow strong teams: not by out-paying giants, but by growing and keeping their own people.

For your own venture:

  1. Name the scarce skill - and decide buy or build. Is it common and cheap (buy it) or scarce and dear and keeps walking out the door (build it)?
  2. For a build, start with TRUST, not skill. Who around you is reliable and has never let you down? Skill can be grown on top of that; loyalty cannot be bought.
  3. Build a reason to stay - the three binds. A path (a real role), a stake (a fair share of what they help create), and a fair agreement on one page. Make a partner, not just a hire.
R

Name your scarce skill and the trusted person, and sketch the one-page offer - then say it out loud to someone you trust. (Working with an AMU facilitator? Bring it there too.)

What goes in a fair one-page agreement (no lawyers needed)

Just be clear. Cover four things:

1. The role - the new title.
2. The path - the main responsibilities and the real say that comes with them.
3. The stake - exactly what they get a share of (e.g. "2% of the net profit on the housing-development contract") and when it is paid.
4. The understanding - a fair commitment both ways: they commit to give the role a real go (say, a year), and either of you can part with honest notice if it truly is not working. A pact, not a cage.

That is it. Two signatures. Clarity creates trust.

Giants win talent with money. You will rarely beat them at that. But you can do something they cannot: see the potential in a loyal person, grow it, and share the reward fairly - and build a team that stays because it is winning with you, not just working for you.
