## 1. The Purpose of This Guide

AMU's Business Problems teach through a continuous story. A learner who walks through this library lives inside one world, alongside the same people, as one business grows from an idea into an institution. A story a learner can inhabit teaches what a list of topics cannot.

This guide is the constitution of that story. It gives you, the contributor, the world and its rules — the people, the place, the business, and the principles that keep the canon whole. It provides the context for every problem you write. It does not provide the solution to any of them.

Every contribution must protect the learner's work: to live each problem and find their own way through. This guide shows you how.

---

## 2. The World

**The business is Tank You.** It is a 9kg gas-cylinder delivery and refill service in a township called **Nethmora View**. The service is a direct response to a lived difficulty: the struggle to carry and refill heavy cylinders. Tank You adds the delivery and convenience layer — a quick message prompts a door delivery, a full-for-empty swap, and a plastic seal on every cylinder to prove it is full and untampered. It sells convenience, reliability, and trust, for a R50 delivery fee.

**The places are real.** The story begins at Uncle Yusuf's general dealer and spaza, with its informal gas-cylinder exchange depot. It finds its footing in conversations at the Engen garage, where the founders hold their planning meetings. It serves real needs, like those of Fatima Salie's Baraka Community Kitchen.

**The operational world is grounded.** The business model is a like-for-like branded cylinder exchange. LPG safety is always respected — upright, ventilated, extinguisher near, no smoking. Viability lives and dies on delivery density. Load-shedding is a constant operational factor; the grid is available about 80% of business hours in the recorded reference month.

## 4. The Shape of the Story

The storyline runs in six phases. This is the shape of the journey, not a list of outcomes. Each phase is a container for Business Problems that a learner must resolve.

1.  **The Employee Era.** Muhammad earns a wage at his uncle's counter, at the centre of the community's daily life.
2.  **The Spark.** An idea forms. It must be tested against the honest questions: is this a business, who is it for, and what would it take to begin with almost nothing?
3.  **The Test.** The idea must prove itself small, before real money moves. The first paying customers arrive.
4.  **The Foundation.** The business becomes real and formal. It must set a price that carries its costs, manage its money, and keep its promises.
5.  **The Scaling.** Growth arrives, bringing more hands, more wheels, and more risk. The money, the systems, the partnerships, and the founders are all tested.
6.  **Maturity.** The business is established. The hardest questions remain: what holds it up when its founders step back — and what was it all for?

The guiding theme is constant: an entrepreneur serves by making people's lives easier. Money is the measure of serving well, never the goal itself.

## 5. The Rules of Contribution

We welcome every contribution that honours the story and the learner. To write for this library is to accept these rules.

1.  **We honour the canon.** The people, places, and chronology in this guide are fixed. A contribution set in one phase must not assume events of a later one. The given figures — Muhammad's R4,500 wage, the R50 delivery fee, the R260 refill price, the R4,750 test-stage outlay, the R40,000 bakkie, the R10,000 of combined savings — are the ground on which the story stands.
2.  **We reuse the existing character universe first.** Propose a new character only when nobody existing fits. A new character may not claim a pre-existing relationship with the Cassim family without approval.
3.  **We protect the learner's work.** A contribution must never reveal another problem's resolution — its worked figures, its discovery, or its answer. A unit may end on an open question; it never signposts another unit's answer.
4.  **We write from evidence.** Every felt problem must be one people actually face, never invented for teaching convenience.
5.  **We honour the dignity of every person.** No scenario judges a person's choices, frames poverty as character, or measures a life against an implied norm. We meet the learner as they are: whole and welcome. (Per `voice-and-tone.md` §1; C10).
6.  **We write in UK English.** The language is plain, readable by a young learner, and locally grounded in a way that remains globally understandable.

## 6. How a Contribution Enters the Canon

This is the airlock that keeps the story whole.

1.  **Propose.** Open a pull request with your Business Problem, character proposal, or story element, written from this guide alone. Figures in a proposal are illustrative.
2.  **Continuity Check.** AMU's Custodian checks the proposal against the full internal storyline for contradictions and for fit with the learning arc. Feedback references this guide.
3.  **Canonisation.** An accepted contribution is built to the published standard for Business Problems. Its canonical figures are set, and any new world-facts it adds are folded back into this guide, so the next contributor sees them too.

The guide grows as the world grows. If something you need is missing here, ask. An unclear canon is a defect in this guide, not in your proposal.
