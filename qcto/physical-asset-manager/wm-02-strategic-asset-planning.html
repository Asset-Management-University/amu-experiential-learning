§1 Purpose of This Work Experience Module

WM-02 assesses your ability to contribute to or lead strategic asset planning in a real workplace — demand analysis, scenario development, investment appraisal, SAMP section development, or annual plan compilation. Evidence must demonstrate scenario-based thinking, defensible assumptions, and a clear chain from strategic requirement to investment plan.

Strategic asset planning is where the PAMS connects to the organisational future. A PAMS that only manages current assets reactively will always be surprised by future needs. Strategic planning identifies future demand, the assets required to meet it, the investment needed, and the risks that could disrupt the plan — and develops responses before those risks materialise.

Three horizons, one system: Strategic asset planning operates across three horizons simultaneously — short-term (immediate needs, within budget), medium-term (2–5 years, requires planned investment), and long-term (5+ years, requires strategic decisions about the organisation's future). A plan that only operates in the short term is not strategic — it is operational scheduling. WM-02 evidence must show planning across at least two horizons.

§2 What Workplace Evidence Is Required

Minimum evidence:

  • One logbook entry describing a strategic or investment planning contribution you led or made — covering at least two planning horizons
  • A planning document you produced or contributed to materially: SAMP section, investment appraisal, demand analysis, or 3-year asset plan — with your specific contribution identified
  • Supervisor verification of your substantive role

Your entry must demonstrate: the strategic requirement that drove the planning, the methodology applied (scenario development, demand analysis, risk-based prioritisation), the investment decisions recommended, and the outcome.

§3 Worked Example Logbook Entry

Model Logbook Entry — WM-02 | PAM NQF Level 7

Date of workplace event: February–March 2026
Organisation: KD Auto Workshop, Tembisa
My role: Kagiso Dlamini — owner/manager
Module: WM-02 — Strategic Asset Planning

In February 2026, Sipho Nkosi indicated that he intended to grow his fleet from 15 to 22 taxis over the next 24 months — a 47% increase in the primary revenue source. This triggered the need for a strategic asset plan to determine whether KD Auto's current equipment can support 22 taxis, what investment is required, and over what timeline.

Demand Analysis

Current workshop throughput with 2 operational bays: approximately 4–5 fleet vehicles per day (including routine services and reactive jobs). At 22 taxis, estimated demand: 6–7 fleet vehicles per day, plus non-fleet walk-in work. Current capacity is insufficient for the projected demand at full fleet size.

Three-Horizon Investment Plan

Scenario Analysis

I developed three scenarios to test the plan assumptions:

ScenarioFleet growthImplication
Base caseSipho grows to 22 taxis by end 2027Medium-term investment (3rd bay) required by mid-2027
Slow growthSipho reaches only 18 taxis by end 20273rd bay not needed until 2028 — delay medium-term investment
Fast growth (and competitor risk)Sipho reaches 22 taxis by mid-2026; second fleet operator approaches KD Auto3rd bay needed by Q3 2026 — accelerate short-term completion and front-load medium-term investment

Key sensitivity: The medium-term investment decision is sensitive to Sipho's actual growth rate. The plan includes a review trigger: if fleet reaches 20 vehicles before December 2026, commence 3rd bay procurement. If fleet has not reached 18 vehicles by December 2026, defer medium-term investment by 12 months.

Outcome: I presented the plan to Kagiso in March 2026. He approved the short-term investment (R24,520) immediately from the current cash reserve (R8,000 supplemented by fleet contract income over 2 months). The medium-term investment is contingent on the review trigger. He noted the long-term plan but did not commit to it.

What I would do differently: I based my demand analysis entirely on Sipho's stated intention to grow his fleet. I did not independently verify whether this growth was plausible — by checking local taxi industry trends, route competition, or Sipho's financing capacity. A more rigorous demand analysis includes external validation of the growth assumption, not just the client's stated plans. For any plan where a single client's growth drives the entire investment case, independent demand verification is essential.

Strategic planning insight: The three-horizon plan with scenario analysis shows that the plan is not a single point forecast — it is a range of possible futures with explicit triggers and decision points. This is the difference between a budget and a strategic plan: a budget allocates resources for a known future; a strategic plan prepares the organisation for an uncertain one. The review triggers (fleet reaches 20 or 18 by December 2026) convert the plan from a document into a management tool.

§5 Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Confusing a budget with a plan: A budget says "we will spend R24,520 this year." A strategic plan says "we will spend R24,520 this year because we need 2-bay capacity to serve 15 taxis, and R38,000 in 2027 if the fleet grows to 20+ as projected." The plan has reasoning; the budget has numbers.
  • No uncertainty acknowledgement: All strategic plans contain assumptions about the future. The plan must explicitly acknowledge which assumptions are most uncertain, and what the investment implication is if those assumptions are wrong.
  • Single-client dependency without external validation: If your entire investment case depends on one client's growth, you must independently validate that client's plans where possible.

§6 Connect to Your Qualification

WM-02 applies PM-02 (Long, Medium, and Short-term Planning) skills to a real strategic planning challenge. The planning methodology is identical. What WM-02 adds is the strategic consequence dimension: the investments in this plan shape the organisation's capacity for years. A poor plan produces either under-investment (lost revenue, capacity constraints) or over-investment (cash tied up in assets not yet needed). Your evidence must show that you understood and managed this trade-off.
