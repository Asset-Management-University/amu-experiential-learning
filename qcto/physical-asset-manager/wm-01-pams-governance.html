§1 Purpose of This Work Experience Module

WM-01 assesses your ability to design, improve, or govern an element of a Physical Asset Management System (PAMS) in a real workplace. This requires evidence that you have moved beyond applying others' systems — you have designed or improved the system itself, evaluated it against ISO 55001 requirements, and implemented or proposed measurable improvements.

At NQF Level 7, PAMS governance means understanding why the system is designed the way it is, what its weaknesses are relative to the standard, and how to improve it systematically. The workplace evidence for WM-01 should demonstrate strategic thinking about the PAMS — scope definition, policy development, accountability framework design, or information system architecture — not just compliance checking or operational execution within the existing system.

NQF Level 7 expectation: An NQF Level 6 practitioner applies a PAMS. An NQF Level 7 manager designs and governs one. The difference is not experience — it is analytical depth and system-level thinking. Your evidence must show that you evaluated the system against a standard, identified systemic gaps, and designed improvements — not just identified things that needed fixing.

§2 What Workplace Evidence Is Required

Minimum evidence:

  • One logbook entry (minimum 500 words) describing a situation where you designed, improved, or governed an element of a PAMS — scope definition, policy development, information system design, or accountability framework
  • A document you produced: PAMS scope definition, policy draft, accountability framework, AMIS architecture, or gap analysis — with your role explicitly identified
  • Supervisor or client verification confirming you played a lead role (not merely a supporting role)

Your entry must demonstrate NQF Level 7 analysis: evaluating the system against ISO 55001 requirements and designing improvements, not just describing what exists.

§3 Worked Example Logbook Entry

Model Logbook Entry — WM-01 | PAM NQF Level 7

Date of workplace event: January–March 2026
Organisation: KD Auto Workshop, Tembisa
My role: Kagiso Dlamini — owner/manager
Module: WM-01 — PAMS Governance and Implementation

In January 2026, following the series of asset failures in 2025 (HY-002 hoist failure, JK-001 cracked housing, SL-001 calibration drift), I decided to design and implement a formal Physical Asset Management System for KD Auto. Until that point, asset management was entirely reactive — maintenance happened when things broke, the asset register had not been audited since 2020, and there was no documented policy or accountability framework.

Gap Analysis — ISO 55001 Baseline Assessment

I conducted an ISO 55001 gap analysis covering the seven primary clauses. For each clause, I rated the current state on a 1–5 maturity scale (1 = absent, 3 = partially implemented, 5 = fully implemented and embedded).

Average maturity: 1.3/5. The primary gaps were the absence of any documented governance elements — policy, scope, objectives, and measurement. The organisation was managing assets operationally (§8, score 2) but had no governance structure to direct that management or evaluate its effectiveness.

PAMS Design — Phase 1 Governance Elements

I designed and documented four governance elements in the first quarter of 2026:

  1. PAMS Scope Document: Defines the asset portfolio in scope (HY-001, HY-002, JK-001, SL-001, PE-002 + all equipment ≥ R500 acquisition cost), the organisational boundary (KD Auto Workshop, 14 Mokoena Street, Tembisa), and the stakeholder relationship (Sipho Nkosi fleet contract as primary AM obligation driver).
  2. AM Policy (one page): States KD Auto's commitment to safe, reliable, and cost-effective asset management; defines accountability (Kagiso Dlamini as accountable person); references OHSA compliance and the fleet contract as the primary performance obligation.
  3. Accountability Framework: Assigns AM responsibilities: Kagiso (strategic decisions, fleet accountability), Bongani (daily condition monitoring, job card completion), me (PAP qualification — quarterly audits, register maintenance, performance reporting during qualification period).
  4. Performance Measurement System: Five KPIs tracked monthly: bay availability, fleet vehicle turnaround time, rework rate, compliance status (% obligations current), and maintenance cost as % of asset replacement value.

Outcome: By March 2026, KD Auto had a documented PAMS scope, an AM policy, an accountability framework, and a live performance measurement system. The gap analysis score for §4 (Context) improved from 1 to 3; §5 (Leadership) from 1 to 3; §9 (Performance evaluation) from 1 to 2. Average improved from 1.3 to 1.9 — not yet a well-functioning PAMS, but a structured foundation.

What I would do differently: I designed the PAMS elements sequentially — scope first, then policy, then accountability, then measurement. In retrospect, I should have designed the measurement system alongside the policy, because the KPIs must reflect the AM objectives stated in the policy. Designing them sequentially meant I had to revise the measurement system after the policy was finalised. Future PAMS implementations should design these elements simultaneously in a workshop, not sequentially as separate documents.

System design insight: A PAMS is not a collection of separate documents — it is a system where each element references the others. The scope defines what is managed; the policy defines how it will be managed and why; the objectives define what success looks like; the measurement system evaluates whether success is being achieved. If these elements are designed independently, they will not form a coherent system. The WM-01 evidence must show that the learner understands this — not just that they produced documents.

§5 Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Describing someone else's PAMS: WM-01 requires evidence of your contribution to PAMS design or governance — not a description of the system your employer uses. If you worked within an existing PAMS, show how you improved or governed a specific element of it.
  • Governance documents without evaluation: Producing a policy or scope document is the output, not the achievement. The achievement is identifying what the organisation needed, evaluating the gap against a standard, and designing a document that fills that gap effectively.
  • No measurement of impact: A PAMS governance improvement should produce a measurable change. If you cannot point to what improved — maturity score, compliance status, performance indicator — the improvement was not substantive.

§6 Connect to Your Qualification

WM-01 is the highest-level application of PM-01 (Design and Establish a PAMS). Where PM-01 tested your ability to design PAMS elements in a structured learning context, WM-01 requires evidence that you have done this in a real organisation, with real stakeholders, under real constraints. The standard is NQF Level 7 — strategic, evaluative, and systemic.
