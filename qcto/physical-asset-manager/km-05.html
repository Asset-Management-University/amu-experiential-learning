Financial Management and Business Engineering Principles III

KM-05 · a journey assembled from felt Business Problems

Money, information and law are the three things that will constrain every asset decision you take, and this is the course that gives you all three. You will build the financial case for an asset decision; work out when equivalence, financing or replacement is the right instrument; author the policy set that governs the money; design the information systems and the knowledge inside them; and meet the property law that attaches to an asset. Two of its five modules are heavily Director-reserved - property valuation and specialist property law - and they are declared rather than approximated.

The financial case for an asset decision

Where this begins
You have been asked to make the case and you have made it three times. Each time the answer was that there is no money. And you have never been able to test that answer, because you do not know how this organisation decides what money there is - which budget it comes from, what return it expects, what it costs to hold, or what it would have done instead.

What you will be able to do
By the end of this module you can argue a financial case in the language the decision is actually taken in: the budget, the time value of money, the forecast beneath it, the probability around it, and the interpretation of the result.

Equivalence, financing, and when to replace rather than repair

Where this begins
The compressor can be refurbished for R900,000 and will run another eight years, or replaced for R3.1 million and will run twenty-five. Somebody in the meeting says the refurbishment is obviously cheaper. Somebody else says the replacement is obviously better value. Both are using the word 'obviously' to mean 'I have not done the arithmetic that would settle this'.

What you will be able to do
By the end of this module you can settle that argument: equivalence, the interest formulae behind it, the comparison of alternatives with different lives, and the appraisal of economic life, refurbishment, replacement and retirement.

The policy set that governs the money

Where this begins
There are eleven policies and you can find nine of them. Two of the nine contradict each other on who may approve an unbudgeted repair. Nobody has noticed, because in practice the question is settled by ringing the finance manager - which works, and has worked for years, and will stop working the day she is on leave and something fails.

What you will be able to do
By the end of this module you can appraise a policy set as a set - financial, operational, maintenance, replacement and safety - against the legislation, the protocols, the sensitivities and the expectations that all bear on it, and design the process by which policy is actually developed.

Information systems, and the knowledge inside them

Where this begins
The information exists. Somebody has it. That is the honest state of the organisation's knowledge: every question you ask can be answered by a particular person, and none of them can be answered by a system - so the organisation's memory is a set of people, and it leaves at the rate they do.

What you will be able to do
By the end of this module you can build the information side of an asset organisation deliberately - the system development, the access and security, the accuracy, and the knowledge management strategy, governance and records that outlive the people.

Property law and the rights attached to an asset

Where this begins
The depot has been operating from that yard for nineteen years and the lease was signed by somebody who has since died. You need to know whether the organisation can build on it, whether the access road is theirs, and what happens if the owner sells - and the honest answer is that nobody in the organisation knows, and the file is a folder with three documents in it.

What you will be able to do
By the end of this module you can meet property law where it touches an asset portfolio - the basic principles, the kinds of property, the rights that attach, and the justification of a valuation basis.
