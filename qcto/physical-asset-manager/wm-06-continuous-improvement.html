§1 Purpose of This Work Experience Module

WM-06 assesses your ability to lead or contribute to a PAMS management review, process quality assessment, compliance review, or risk mitigation effectiveness review — and to demonstrate that the review produced a specific, implemented improvement. A finding without an implemented improvement does not satisfy the WM-06 standard.

Continuous improvement is the ISO 55001 §10 obligation that gives the PAMS its learning capacity. Without a systematic improvement process, the PAMS stabilises at whatever level it reached at implementation and never advances. WM-06 evidence must show that you closed the improvement loop — not just that you reviewed performance.

The improvement loop: Review → Find → Analyse → Decide → Implement → Verify → Review. Most organisations manage the first three steps reasonably well. The failures are at "Decide" (recommendations made but not acted on), "Implement" (action started but not completed), and "Verify" (no check that the improvement actually improved anything). WM-06 requires evidence that you completed the entire loop — including verification.

§2 What Workplace Evidence Is Required

Minimum evidence:

  • One logbook entry describing a PAMS management review, process quality assessment, or risk effectiveness review you led or contributed to
  • The review record (management review minutes, process audit report, or improvement register extract) that you produced or led
  • Evidence of the improvement that resulted: an updated process, a changed practice, a closed risk, or a verified KPI improvement — not just a recommendation
  • Supervisor verification of your lead role

Your entry must demonstrate: what you reviewed, the method you applied, the findings, the improvement decision you made, the implementation, and the verification of improvement.

Q2 2026 Improvement Priorities

  2. Written accountability reports: Q1 accountability report to Sipho was verbal with paper summary. Produce a formal one-page written Q2 report, signed by both parties. Owner: Kagiso. Target: Q2 report produced July 2026.

Verification — Q1 improvement effectiveness: Of the 5 Q1 improvements I committed to (WM-01 through WM-05), I implemented all 5: PAMS scope documented ✓, AM policy documented ✓, HY-002 replacement ordered ✓ (in progress), SL-001 recalibrated ✓, Bongani hoist training completed ✓. The April management review confirmed this as the basis for the Q1 improvement score of 4/5.

What I would do differently: I scored "improvement effectiveness" highly (4/5) because I completed all actions — but I did not verify that the improvements achieved their intended outcomes. For example, I recalibrated SL-001 but have not yet confirmed that the Q2 rework rate has returned to ≤5%. Completion of an action and effectiveness of an action are different things. From Q2 onwards, each improvement action will include an effectiveness criterion — what I will measure to confirm the improvement worked — not just a completion date.

Improvement loop closure: The Q1 management review produced three Q2 improvement commitments (formal register, written accountability reports, close-out timeliness target). I will review these at the Q2 management review — completing the loop. Each management review begins by checking whether I implemented the previous review's improvements and whether they were effective, then adds new improvements for the next cycle. This is how a PAMS learns.

§5 Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Review without improvement: A management review that produces only findings and no improvement decisions does not satisfy WM-06. The review must produce a specific implemented improvement — not just a list of problems.
  • Improvement without verification: An unverified improvement is not an improvement. WM-06 requires closing the loop: you implemented the improvement, and evidence shows it achieved its intended effect.
  • Single-dimension review: A PAMS review that only covers performance against KPIs is incomplete. Include governance quality, capability, improvement effectiveness, and risk management in the review scope.

§6 Connect to Your Qualification

WM-06 is the final module of the PAM qualification and the perpetual engine of PAMS improvement. Every other WM module (WM-01 through WM-05) feeds into this one: the governance (WM-01), the strategic plan (WM-02), the team capability (WM-03), the lifecycle management (WM-04), and the accountability reporting (WM-05) — the management review assesses all these areas, and the improvement loop that WM-06 drives improves them. A Physical Asset Manager who masters WM-06 has mastered the PAMS learning cycle.
