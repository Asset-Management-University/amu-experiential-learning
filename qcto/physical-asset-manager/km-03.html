Why this matters

Strategy, risk and money are usually taught as three subjects and lived as one problem. This course teaches them as one. You will find the risks that matter and get them to somebody who can act before the event rather than after it; build a strategic plan that survives contact with a regulator, a customer and a community who were not in the room; run the work as a project so a slip is visible while it is still small; and put a policy, a budget and a value-for-money test on the same page - which is what breaks the deadlock where a standard is affirmed every year and quietly not met.

Where this begins
The risk register has ninety-one entries and has not changed anybody's behaviour in two years. It is accurate. It is reviewed quarterly. And when the transformer failed in March, the failure was on it - rated medium, owned by a person who had left, with a mitigation described as 'monitor'. Nobody was negligent. The register had become a place risks were put rather than a thing anybody did.

What you will be able to do
By the end of this module you can find the risks that matter, rank them so the ranking means something, choose controls that actually intervene, and get the information to the person who can act before the event rather than after it.

Where this begins
The strategy was signed off in November and by March nobody quotes it. Not because it was wrong - it was careful, and it was right. It simply had no answer for the three things that actually happened: a regulator changed a clause, a large customer changed what it wanted, and the community next door started asking questions nobody had a process for. The plan was a description of an intended future. What was needed was something that could take a hit.

What you will be able to do
By the end of this module you can build a strategic plan that survives contact - grounded in a real needs analysis, weighed against alternatives, tied to what binds you legally, and answerable to the people whose expectations will move.

Where this begins
The project is eleven weeks late and nobody can say when it went wrong. Every status report said amber. Every one was honest. And the schedule that would show you the slip - the same forecast date moving four times, always for a defensible reason - was never drawn, because each report replaced the last one rather than sitting beside it.

What you will be able to do
By the end of this module you can run asset work as a project - the breakdown, the schedule, the contracts, the quality method and the control that shows a slip while it is still small.

Where this begins
The policy says the organisation will maintain its assets to a defined standard. It has said so for six years. Nobody has ever been able to say what it costs to comply with it, and so every budget round it is quietly not complied with, and the policy is never changed - because changing it would require somebody to say out loud that the standard is unaffordable.

What you will be able to do
By the end of this module you can put a policy, a budget and a value-for-money test in the same room - implementing policy rather than publishing it, and appraising an investment against criteria somebody signed.
