Why this matters

This is the course where you stop describing the organisation and start seeing it. You will measure how mature your asset management really is before anybody writes down how mature they think it is; find out who is genuinely answerable for an asset system rather than who sits above whom; design controls that hold in the moment somebody is asked to sign something they should not; run an audit that finds what is true rather than what is filed; hold an asset from the day it is specified to the day it is justly let go; and turn a two-year irritation into research somebody would fund and read. It is called Physical Asset and Facilities Management III. What it actually gives you is the ability to tell the difference between a system that runs and a system that is documented.

Where this begins
You have the organogram. You have the ISO 55001 manual, and it is a good one. And a pump station has just failed in a way that six different people each partly own - the operator who logged it, the planner who scheduled around it, the engineer who owns the design, the contracts officer who holds the service agreement, the finance business partner who holds the budget, and a manager whose name is on the policy. Every one of them acted correctly. Nobody was answerable for the pump station.

What you will be able to do
By the end of this module you can see the management system the organisation actually runs - its processes, its roles, its accountabilities and its governing cycle - rather than the one its documents describe, and say honestly how far apart the two are.

Where this begins
The specification for the new asset information system runs to sixty pages and every page of it is about what the system will DO. Screens, reports, integrations, response times. Nobody has written down what the system is FOR - which decision, taken by which person, is currently being taken badly. So the build will succeed and the decision will not improve, and in three years somebody will propose replacing it.

What you will be able to do
By the end of this module you can take a system from a stated requirement to a verified delivery - modelling it formally, placing every activity in its life cycle stage, and planning the verification that proves it does what was asked.

Where this begins
Every control the auditors asked for is in place. There is a delegation of authority, a segregation of duties, an approval workflow and an annual attestation. And a manager has just told an engineer to sign off a pressure vessel the engineer believes is unsafe - and every one of those controls is silent, because none of them was ever about whether somebody would be asked to do the wrong thing by a person who could end their employment.

What you will be able to do
By the end of this module you can design internal controls that hold where it matters, judge honestly what a certification is worth, and run an audit that finds what is true rather than what is documented.

Where this begins
The blower was signed for in March. Nobody has asked since whether it is doing what it was bought to do - not because anybody is careless, but because the question has no owner. Acquisition belonged to the project. Operation belongs to the plant. Utilisation is a number in a report nobody reads. And disposal will belong, one day, to whoever is unlucky enough to be there. Seventeen assessment criteria in this topic; a single asset walks through every one of them, and in most organisations no single person walks with it.

What you will be able to do
By the end of this module you can hold an asset across its whole working life - specify what it must arrive with, measure what it actually delivers, verify utilisation against the design, and justify letting it go.

Where this begins
You have been irritated by the same thing for two years. The bearings on that line fail earlier than the identical bearings on the other one, and everybody has a theory, and nobody has ever measured it. You could find out. You have the records and you have the access. What you do not have is any idea how to turn a two-year irritation into a question somebody would fund, a method somebody would trust, and a document somebody would read.

What you will be able to do
By the end of this module you can take a felt dissatisfaction to a defended manuscript - framing the question, earning the method, designing the study so its fatal flaw is cheap to find, and writing it so a reader can tell what you did from what you found.

Where this work meets another profession

Where the house style belongs to the house

Writing is judged by one test: did the reader do the right thing because of what you wrote.

You will develop the capability to write so that they can — to structure an argument, keep a citation honest all the way through to its reference list, and cost a proposal and put it to the person who has to approve it. That is the part that travels with you between organisations.

Two things here are set by whoever you are writing for: the numbering, layout and typography conventions a particular institution requires of a research project, and the form and structure a particular funder requires of an external application.

These differ between institutions and between funders, and they change. AMU develops the writing; the house style belongs to the house. When you need it, it is in your institution’s style guide and in the funder’s published guidance — and knowing to go and find the right one, rather than assuming last year’s, is itself part of doing this well.

Where this work meets another profession

Where the software gets built

You will be accountable for information systems you did not build. That is the normal condition of this work, and it is a strong position if you know what you are doing.

You will develop the capability to specify what a system has to do, design the asset data model it will carry, interrogate what is in it, govern the estate it holds, and audit whether it is telling you the truth.

Describing how an information system application is built is software development — a discipline of its own, held by the developers and vendors you will work alongside.

You are not training to write the application. You are training to be the person who can tell a developer precisely what is needed, judge honestly what comes back, and stand behind the data inside it. When that person is missing, the system is built to the wrong specification and nobody notices for two years.

Every step above is a Business Problem a person works through, not a topic to be covered. Steps marked callback revisit capability developed earlier. This is the real AMU curriculum, shared freely in the spirit of Ubuntu — the belief that we all grow stronger when we help each other thrive.
