§1 Purpose of This Work Experience Module

WM-04 assesses your ability to operationalise the PAMS — aligning business processes to the PAMS strategy, designing or applying a Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) framework, or applying lifecycle stage logic to a portfolio of assets. Evidence must show that the activity produced a system-level change, not just a process document.

PAMS operationalisation is the step that converts governance documents into daily practice. A PAMS scope definition and policy (WM-01) is only valuable if the operational processes align with it. WM-04 evidence should show that you looked at how the team actually manages assets day-to-day and made systemic changes to bring that practice into alignment with the PAMS.

Operationalisation vs documentation: Creating a process document that no one follows is not operationalisation. Operationalisation means the team uses the process, decision-makers make lifecycle decisions using the defined methodology, and the system captures the evidence of that usage. A process document plus a verification that the team applies the process is the minimum standard for WM-04 evidence.

§2 What Workplace Evidence Is Required

Minimum evidence:

  • One logbook entry describing a lifecycle management or PAMS operationalisation activity you led or contributed to
  • A document you produced: M&E framework, process redesign document, process manual section, lifecycle stage assessment — plus evidence of application (meeting minutes, decision record, or updated system data)
  • Supervisor verification confirming your lead role

Your entry must demonstrate: the PAMS alignment gap that triggered the work, what process or system change you designed, and evidence that you applied the change (not just documented).

§3 Worked Example Logbook Entry

Model Logbook Entry — WM-04 | PAM NQF Level 7

Date of workplace event: March–April 2026
Organisation: KD Auto Workshop, Tembisa
My role: Kagiso Dlamini — owner/manager
Module: WM-04 — Lifecycle Management and PAMS Operationalisation

In March 2026, following the PAMS design (WM-01) and investment planning (WM-02), I designed and implemented the lifecycle stage management process for KD Auto's asset portfolio. The PAMS §8 requirement — that lifecycle decisions are made using a documented methodology — had not been operationalised in practice: the team made lifecycle decisions ad hoc when assets failed, not proactively based on stage-appropriate management strategies.

M&E Framework — Lifecycle Stage Review

To operationalise the lifecycle stage approach, I designed a quarterly lifecycle review process:

  1. Trigger: First working day of each quarter, Bongani completes the asset condition checklist (5 assets, 30 minutes).
  2. Assessment: Kagiso reviews the checklist, assigns lifecycle stage to each asset using the defined stage criteria, and updates the asset register.
  3. Decision: Any asset showing a stage change (e.g., from "Maintain" to "Modify or dispose") triggers a formal lifecycle decision — documented in the asset record with the analysis and chosen option.
  4. Verification: At each quarterly review, the previous quarter's triggered decisions are reviewed for implementation progress.

I conducted the first quarterly lifecycle review on 1 April 2026, assessing all five assets. The SL-001 "Maintain — monitoring" stage was confirmed; calibration drift had not worsened since recalibration in February. JK-001 was marked "Disposed" after the replacement floor jack was installed.

Evidence of application: I have attached the quarterly lifecycle review record (completed 1 April 2026). This shows I conducted the assessment, not just that I designed the process.

What I would do differently: The lifecycle stage criteria I defined (the conditions under which an asset transitions from one stage to another) were subjective — "condition is good/fair/poor" without specific measurement criteria. For HY-001, "good condition" means load test current and no visible structural defects. For SL-001, "maintain vs dispose" depends on annual calibration cost exceeding R8,000. These are good criteria but I defined them after designing the process rather than building them into the design. Future lifecycle frameworks should define quantitative transition criteria for each asset type before the first assessment cycle.

Operationalisation insight: The lifecycle stage assessment table is not the evidence of operationalisation — it is the design artifact. The evidence is the quarterly review record: the team used the process. A process that exists only on paper is an aspiration. A process with a completed review record is a management practice. WM-04 requires the latter.

§5 Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Lifecycle decisions without a process: Making a one-off repair/replace decision is PM-02 level evidence. WM-04 requires evidence that you designed and operationalised a repeatable lifecycle management process — not just made one decision.
  • Process documents without evidence of use: The assessor must see that you applied the process — not just that you created it. Include the completed assessment or review record, not just the blank template.
  • No measurement criteria: "Good/fair/poor" condition assessment without defined criteria is not a reliable management tool. Define what each level means for each asset type.

§6 Connect to Your Qualification

WM-04 applies PM-04 (Manage the Whole Asset Lifecycle) skills to full portfolio management. Where PM-04 assessed each asset individually, WM-04 requires a portfolio-level approach with a repeatable process. The lifecycle stage logic is the same; the operationalisation requirement makes this a management standard, not just an analytical skill.
