§1 Purpose of This Work Experience Module

WM-05 assesses your ability to produce, review, or govern a performance report for a stakeholder — applying the correct operational and financial ratios, communicating in a way that serves the stakeholder's decision-making needs, and demonstrating accountability for PAMS performance. Evidence must demonstrate Board-quality communication: honest about gaps, specific about causes, clear about remediation.

Stakeholder accountability reporting at NQF Level 7 differs from performance reporting at NQF Level 6 in the governance dimension. A PAP practitioner (WE-03) produces a report. A PAM manager designs the reporting framework — what to report, to whom, how often, in what format — and then produces reports within that framework. WM-05 evidence should show both the framework and its application.

Accountability vs reporting: A report that says "availability was 96.8%" is performance reporting. A report that says "availability was 96.8%, which is below the 98% commitment in the fleet contract. The shortfall is attributable to the HY-002 failure that was identified in the asset register audit. The replacement is in progress and availability will return to ≥98% from Q2 2026. We have committed an investment of R22,000 for this purpose" is accountability reporting. The second holds the asset manager accountable to the commitment, explains the failure, and demonstrates that remediation is underway.

§2 What Workplace Evidence Is Required

Minimum evidence:

  • One logbook entry describing an accountability report you produced for a specific stakeholder
  • The report itself (or an anonymised extract), showing the reporting framework applied, KPIs presented, narrative explanation of gaps, and remediation commitments
  • Evidence that the report was presented: meeting record, email, or sign-off confirming the stakeholder received and reviewed the report
  • Supervisor verification of your lead role

Your entry must describe: the stakeholder and their reporting requirements, the reporting framework applied, the content of the report (KPIs, narrative, remediation), how the stakeholder responded, and what you would change about the reporting approach.

§3 Worked Example Logbook Entry

Model Logbook Entry — WM-05 | PAM NQF Level 7

Date of workplace event: April 2026 (Q1 2026 formal accountability report)
Organisation: KD Auto Workshop, Tembisa
My role: Kagiso Dlamini — owner/manager
Module: WM-05 — Stakeholder Accountability and Reporting

As part of the PAMS implementation (WM-01), I established a formal quarterly accountability reporting commitment with Sipho Nkosi — the fleet operator whose 15 taxis generate R18,000/month. The reporting framework I designed had three elements: (1) performance against contracted commitments, (2) asset condition and investment transparency, (3) outlook and risk disclosure. Sipho and I agreed on this framework before Q1 reporting commenced.

Asset Condition and Investment Transparency

I included an asset condition section in the Q1 report — a brief summary of the workshop's asset portfolio health, since Sipho's fleet service quality depends directly on KD Auto's equipment condition:

  • HY-001: Serviceable. Annual load test current (completed March 2026).
  • HY-002: Replacement ordered (R22,000). ETA 15 April 2026. Funded from fleet contract income.
  • JK-001: Replaced March 2026 (R1,800). New unit serviceable.
  • SL-001: Recalibrated February 2026. Quarterly calibration schedule established.
  • PE-002: Commissioned February 2026. Bongani trained and certified.

This section was new — it was not part of Sipho's original expectation but I added it because the Q1 performance gaps were directly caused by equipment failures. A client who understands the workshop's investment in reliable equipment is better positioned to trust the Q2 outlook than one who only sees the Q1 numbers.

Outcome of Report Presentation

I presented the report at a 45-minute meeting with Sipho on 5 April 2026. His response was positive: "I appreciate you being honest about the problems and showing me what you did about them." He accepted the adjusted rework rate interpretation and confirmed the fleet contract for the next quarter. He asked for the asset condition section to be included in all future quarterly reports — it was new but he valued it.

What I would do differently: I presented the Q1 report verbally with a paper summary. A formal written report (even one page) that Sipho could file and refer back to is more appropriate for a commercial accountability relationship. I will produce a one-page written accountability report for Q2, signed by both parties, to create a formal record of the commitments made and the performance delivered. This protects both parties and formalises the accountability relationship.

Accountability reporting principle: The decision to include the asset condition section — even though Sipho did not ask for it — reflects understanding of what accountability means. Sipho's service quality depends on KD Auto's equipment. Disclosing equipment investment decisions is part of being accountable for the service promise. A manager who reports only the minimum required by the contract is complying; a manager who discloses proactively is demonstrating accountability.

§5 Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Reporting only the metrics, not the meaning: Numbers without narrative are a data dump. Every significant gap or achievement needs an explanation: why did it happen, what does it mean for the stakeholder, what is being done about it?
  • No accountability language: "Availability was below target" is passive. "KD Auto did not meet the availability commitment" is accountable. The language of accountability matters — stakeholders can tell the difference.
  • No evidence of stakeholder response: The stakeholder must have received and responded to the report. A report you prepared but never presented is not evidence of accountability reporting — it is evidence of report preparation.

§6 Connect to Your Qualification

WM-05 applies PM-05 (Report Accountability to Stakeholders) skills to a formal stakeholder accountability relationship. The reporting framework design, accountability language, and transparent disclosure principles are the same. What WM-05 adds is the management dimension: you designed the reporting framework, not just executed within one that already existed. Your evidence must show this design contribution clearly.
