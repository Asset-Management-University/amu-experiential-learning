§1 Purpose of This Work Experience Module

WE-01 assesses your ability to apply physical asset management activities in a real workplace — specifically, auditing and updating an asset register, conducting a risk or criticality assessment, and contributing to the governance of AM activities. This module mirrors PM-01 (PAM Activities) but requires real workplace evidence instead of simulation.

At NQF Level 6, you are expected to demonstrate more than operational execution. An asset register audit should reveal what governance standard the register is held to, what discrepancies exist between the register and physical reality, and what the consequences are for financial reporting, insurance, and maintenance planning. A risk assessment should connect asset condition to business consequence — not just score a risk matrix.

NQF Level 6 expectation: Where a junior technician identifies a broken floor jack, an NQF Level 6 practitioner asks: "Why is this asset still listed as serviceable in the register? What governance failure allowed this discrepancy? What is the financial, safety, and operational consequence of an inaccurate register? What process change prevents this from recurring?" Your evidence must show this level of analysis.

§2 What Workplace Evidence Is Required

Minimum evidence:

  • One logbook entry (minimum 300 words) describing an asset register audit or risk/criticality assessment you conducted or contributed to
  • A document you produced: audit finding report, updated asset register section, risk assessment extract, or criticality matrix — anonymised if commercially sensitive
  • Supervisor verification confirming your role (not merely observing)

Your entry must describe: the scope of the audit or assessment, the methodology applied, findings including discrepancies and their consequences, and the corrective actions you recommended or implemented.

§3 Worked Example Logbook Entry

Model Logbook Entry — WE-01 | PAP NQF Level 6

Date of workplace event: 8–10 January 2026
Organisation: KD Auto Workshop, Tembisa
My role: Physical Asset Practitioner (learner undertaking PAP qualification)
Module: WE-01 — PAM Activities (PM-01 practical skills applied in the workplace)

My employer (Kagiso Dlamini, owner of KD Auto) asked me to conduct an audit of the workshop's physical asset register after the hoist failure incident in August 2025. The register had not been physically verified since the workshop was established in 2020. I conducted the audit over three days using a visual inspection checklist and comparing physical assets to the register entries.

Audit Findings

AssetRegister statusPhysical conditionDiscrepancyConsequence
HY-001 (4-post hoist)ServiceableServiceable — annual load test due Feb 2026None — register accurate—
HY-002 (2-post hoist)ServiceableOut of service since August 2025 — failed sealsRegister shows serviceable; asset is not. Ghost asset risk.Insurance claim on inaccurate value; financial overstatement; safety risk if used
JK-001 (floor jack)ServiceableCracked housing — unsafeSafety defect not reflected in registerOHSA s8 liability; injury risk
SL-001 (alignment machine)ServiceableServiceable — calibration overdue by 4 monthsCalibration compliance gap — register has no calibration fieldDoL compliance risk; customer quality risk
PE-002 (tyre changer)ServiceableServiceable — commissioned February 2026None — register accurate—

Root cause analysis: The register was last updated at acquisition — there was no process requiring update at change of condition. No one was assigned responsibility for keeping the register current. The OHSA (s8) requires an employer to provide safe equipment; maintaining an accurate register of equipment condition is a prerequisite. The JK-001 defect was a OHSA s8 compliance failure waiting to happen.

Corrective actions I implemented:

  1. HY-002 status updated to "Out of service — awaiting repair/disposal decision." Kagiso was briefed that the asset is currently listed as an asset in the books at estimated value R5,000 but is not generating revenue — a ghost asset.
  2. JK-001 tagged "Out of service — do not use" and removed from the service bay. A new jack has been ordered. I added an immediate risk entry to the workshop risk register: JK-001 (cracked housing, P1 — safety hazard).
  3. SL-001 calibration: I contacted the OEM agent and scheduled a calibration visit for the following week. I added a calibration field to the asset register template and recorded the next calibration due date.
  4. Proposed an annual physical verification process: a brief checklist for Bongani to complete at the start of each month — condition, calibration due dates, statutory compliance status.

What I would do differently: I designed the monthly checklist after completing the audit — but the audit itself took three days because there was no prior checklist to work from. If the checklist had existed, the audit would have taken one morning. Building a simple monitoring tool at the start of a maintenance regime is more efficient than a large audit later. I recommend establishing the tool when the asset portfolio is small and maintaining it continuously rather than auditing periodically.

Why this entry demonstrates NQF Level 6 competence: The learner identified discrepancies, traced each to a consequence (financial, safety, compliance), conducted root cause analysis (no process for condition updates, no assigned responsibility), and implemented systemic fixes (updated register, risk entry, calibration scheduling, monthly checklist). This is not operational execution — it is governance improvement. That is what NQF Level 6 PAM activities look like.

§5 Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Audit without a standard: An audit compares reality to a standard. An inspection that only notes observations without comparison to a register or compliance requirement is not an audit.
  • Describing only what you found, not what it means: "The floor jack has a cracked housing" is a finding. "The floor jack is listed serviceable in the register but is unsafe, creating an OHSA s8 liability for the employer" is an analysis. NQF Level 6 requires the latter.
  • No improvement action: Finding a problem and documenting it is necessary but not sufficient. Show that you contributed to fixing it — or formally recommended a fix that was accepted.

§6 Connect to Your Qualification

WE-01 is the workplace evidence for PM-01. The PAP qualification prepares you to manage physical assets at an organisational level — not just maintain equipment. The asset register is the foundation of that management. If the register is inaccurate, every decision that relies on it (insurance, maintenance budgeting, financial reporting, replacement planning) is built on a flawed foundation. WE-01 demonstrates that you understand this — and that you can fix it.
