§1 Purpose of This Work Experience Module

WE-03 assesses your ability to interpret asset management performance data and produce a meaningful report for a stakeholder — a client, a supervisor, a compliance body, or an asset owner. This module mirrors PM-03 (Performance Interpretation and Reporting) but requires real workplace evidence at NQF Level 6.

Performance reporting at NQF Level 6 is not data transcription. It is interpretation: understanding what the KPIs mean in the context of the asset portfolio, identifying trends and root causes, and communicating findings in a way that allows the stakeholder to make informed decisions. A report that says "availability was 96.8%" gives the reader one number. A report that says "availability was 96.8%, which is below the 98% contractual commitment — caused by the HY-002 outage — and will require the HY-002 replacement (in progress) to be completed before the next reporting period" gives the reader the context, the cause, and the remediation status.

Reporting for accountability: At NQF Level 6, performance reports are accountability documents. They tell the asset owner whether commitments are being met, why gaps exist, and what is being done about them. A good performance report is honest about underperformance and specific about remediation — not a summary of achievements designed to make the author look good.

§2 What Workplace Evidence Is Required

Minimum evidence:

  • One logbook entry describing an AM performance report you prepared, contributed to, or presented
  • A copy (or anonymised extract) of the report, or a representative KPI table showing the data you compiled and interpreted
  • Supervisor verification of your role in producing or presenting the report

Your entry must describe: the stakeholder and their reporting requirements, the KPIs reported, the data sources, any significant trends or gaps identified, the root causes of underperformance, and how the report was used.

§3 Worked Example Logbook Entry

Model Logbook Entry — WE-03 | PAP NQF Level 6

Date of workplace event: April 2026 (Q1 2026 performance report)
Organisation: KD Auto Workshop, Tembisa
My role: Physical Asset Practitioner (learner)
Module: WE-03 — Performance Interpretation and Reporting

Kagiso asked me to compile the Q1 2026 performance summary for Sipho Nkosi — the fleet operator whose 15 taxis represent KD Auto's main commercial relationship. Sipho had raised concerns about turnaround time and wanted to understand how the workshop was performing against the informal SLA commitments Kagiso had made when the fleet contract was signed.

Narrative Interpretation

Q1 2026 saw two significant performance gaps, both linked to equipment failures rather than workmanship quality:

  1. HY-002 outage (8 days, February): The secondhand hoist failed due to hydraulic seal wear. This reduced workshop capacity by 50% during the outage and is the primary driver of the below-target bay availability. The replacement unit (R22,000 reconditioned) has been ordered and will be delivered by 15 April 2026. Bay availability is expected to return to ≥98% from Q2 2026 onward.
  2. SL-001 alignment calibration error: Three vehicles were found to have incorrect wheel alignment post-service. Root cause was a calibration drift on the SL-001 alignment machine that was not detected during routine use. SL-001 has now been recalibrated. I have proposed a monthly calibration verification check as part of the monthly asset inspection routine — the calibration error was present for an estimated 6 weeks before detection.

Adjusted rework rate (excluding SL-001 systemic fault): 2 of 60 = 3.3% — within the 5% target. The three SL-001 rework jobs should be treated as equipment-caused, not workmanship-caused, for a fair performance assessment.

How the report was used: I presented the Q1 report to Sipho at a 30-minute meeting at KD Auto. Sipho was satisfied with the transparency and with the remediation plans — specifically that HY-002 replacement was confirmed and SL-001 was recalibrated. He agreed to the adjusted rework rate interpretation. Kagiso participated in the meeting. This was the first formal performance review between KD Auto and Sipho since the fleet contract started.

What I would do differently: I compiled the KPIs from paper job cards and estimate calculations. This took approximately 4 hours. A simple tracking spreadsheet updated weekly would have reduced this to 30 minutes. I have since built a monthly KPI tracking sheet that Bongani updates weekly from job card data. The Q2 report will take significantly less time to prepare.

Reporting insight: The adjusted rework rate (3.3% vs apparent 8.3%) shows that performance reports must distinguish between systemic equipment faults and genuine workmanship quality issues. A report that treats all rework as equivalent misrepresents the workshop's actual quality level and leads to the wrong corrective actions (process retraining when the actual cause is equipment recalibration). Good performance reporting requires analytical discipline, not just data collection.

§5 Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Reporting only the good results: A performance report that highlights achievements and omits failures is not an accountability document — it is a marketing document. Stakeholders who receive sanitised reports lose trust when problems inevitably surface.
  • KPIs without targets: A KPI with no target is just a measurement. Show both the actual and the target — and explain the gap when one exists.
  • No remediation: Identifying a gap without describing what is being done about it leaves the stakeholder with a problem and no confidence in resolution. Always close the loop: here is the gap, here is the cause, here is what we are doing, here is when we expect it to be resolved.

§6 Connect to Your Qualification

WE-03 applies the PM-03 performance interpretation skills to a real stakeholder relationship. The skills — KPI selection, root cause analysis, adjusted interpretation, remediation reporting — are identical. What WE-03 adds is the dimension of real accountability: a real client, real gaps, real reputational and commercial consequences if the report is poor. Your evidence must show that you can perform to this standard.
