§1 Purpose of This Work Experience Module

WE-04 assesses your ability to manage new asset creation in a real workplace — specifically, contributing to the specification, procurement, pre-commissioning, and handover of a new physical asset. This module mirrors PM-04 (New Asset Creation) but requires real workplace evidence at NQF Level 6.

New asset creation is where the lifecycle begins. The decisions made at acquisition — specifications, hold points, acceptance criteria, initial PM plan, asset register entry — determine the quality of asset management for the entire life of the asset. A poorly specified asset generates operational problems from day one. A poorly commissioned asset may operate sub-optimally for years before the issue is traced back to the handover.

Hold points vs close-outs: A hold point is a condition that must be satisfied before commissioning proceeds — because the risk of proceeding without satisfying it is unacceptable. A close-out is a condition that may be acceptable post-commissioning. Distinguishing the two requires judgement: not everything that is not perfect at handover is a hold point, but some deficiencies are serious enough to stop commissioning entirely.

§2 What Workplace Evidence Is Required

Minimum evidence:

  • One logbook entry describing your contribution to a new asset acquisition, commissioning, or handover process
  • A document you produced or contributed to: specification document, pre-commissioning checklist, acceptance test results, or handover record
  • Supervisor verification of your role

Your entry must describe: the asset, the acquisition context, the pre-commissioning or acceptance checks you performed, any hold points or close-out items identified, and how the asset was formally accepted and entered into service.

§3 Worked Example Logbook Entry

Model Logbook Entry — WE-04 | PAP NQF Level 6

Date of workplace event: February 2026 (PE-002 tyre changer commissioning)
Organisation: KD Auto Workshop, Tembisa
My role: Physical Asset Practitioner (learner)
Module: WE-04 — New Asset Creation

In February 2026, KD Auto acquired a Hofmann Monty 3200 tyre changer (PE-002, R45,000 new from Ditsela Equipment). I was assigned to manage the pre-commissioning process and formal handover to service.

Hold Point 3 — Air Supply: The OEM specification requires a minimum of 8 bar at the machine inlet for safe bead-breaking operation. The workshop compressor was delivering 6.5 bar due to a pressure regulator setting. I treated this as a HOLD POINT because operating below the minimum specified pressure creates a risk of incomplete bead breaking — a safety hazard for the operator and for the wheel assembly.

Resolution: I contacted the compressor service agent who confirmed the regulator could be adjusted to deliver 8.5 bar (maximum for the compressor model: 10 bar). Cost: R680 service call. Kagiso approved. Compressor regulator was adjusted on 17 February; inlet pressure re-measured at 8.2 bar. HOLD POINT cleared.

No-load test (Check 6) was then completed successfully. PE-002 was formally accepted into service on 18 February 2026.

PM plan created: Based on the OEM maintenance schedule (Hofmann Monty 3200 Service Manual, Section 7), I created the initial PM plan for PE-002: 3-monthly lubrication (3 points), annual bead breaker cylinder inspection, and annual calibration of the inflation gauge. These were entered into the workshop's job card schedule.

What I would do differently: I created the pre-commissioning checklist from scratch using the OEM manual. For future acquisitions, I will build a standard pre-commissioning template as part of the specification stage — before the equipment arrives — so that checks are designed proactively rather than developed reactively. A template also makes it easier to train Bongani to manage future commissionings independently.

Commissioning insight: The compressor air supply shortfall was a hold point, not a close-out, because operating below specification creates an immediate safety risk. If I had treated it as a close-out and allowed PE-002 to enter service at 6.5 bar, Bongani would have been operating equipment that did not meet its own safety specifications — and KD Auto would have been liable under OHSA s8 for providing an unsafe work environment. Correct classification of hold points is a safety and legal obligation, not just a quality preference.

§5 Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • No acceptance criteria: A commissioning check without defined pass/fail criteria cannot determine whether an asset is ready for service. What does "good" look like for each check?
  • Overlooking the asset register and PM plan: The physical commissioning is only part of new asset creation. The asset must be formally entered in the register and a maintenance plan must be established. An asset that is physically operational but has no register entry and no PM plan is an unmanaged asset.
  • Treating every defect as a close-out: Some deficiencies prevent safe operation and must stop commissioning. The practitioner must exercise judgement about which category a deficiency falls into — not default to "we'll fix it later" for everything.

§6 Connect to Your Qualification

WE-04 applies PM-04 skills to a real commissioning event. The transition from simulation to workplace is significant here: in a simulation, hold points are scripted. In the real workplace, you must exercise judgement about what constitutes an unacceptable risk for this specific asset in this specific context. WE-04 is where that judgement is assessed.
