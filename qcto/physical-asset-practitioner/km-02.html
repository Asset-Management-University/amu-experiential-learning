Physical Asset and Facilities Management II

KM-02 · a journey assembled from felt Business Problems

A portfolio is not a list. This course teaches you to classify one deliberately - by criteria you can state, in a hierarchy that survives the fourth person who adds a building - and to see how an asset investment decision is actually made, including the risk that is really being taken when an asset is kept past its point and nobody names it.

Classifying a portfolio, and keeping the register true

Where this begins
The portfolio has four hundred entries and three of them are the same building. One is listed by its street address, one by the department that occupies it, and one by the year it was bought. Nobody did anything wrong. Three different people added it, at three different times, each answering a different question - and there was never a rule about which question the register answers.

What you will be able to do
By the end of this module you can classify a facilities portfolio deliberately - by criteria you can state, in a hierarchy that holds, against specifications that distinguish one kind of facility from another.

How an asset investment decision is actually made

Where this begins
The motivation was approved in eleven minutes. Nobody asked what it would cost to run, nobody asked what happens in year nine, and nobody asked what else the money could have done - because the paper answered none of those questions and the meeting had six more items on it.
