§1 Purpose of This Work Experience Module

WE-05 assesses your ability to identify, compile, and manage statutory and regulatory compliance obligations for physical assets in a real workplace. This module mirrors PM-05 (Statutory Reporting) but requires real workplace evidence at NQF Level 6.

Statutory compliance for physical assets includes safety inspections, environmental licences, load tests, calibration certificates, fire safety compliance, OHS Act requirements, and applicable industry-specific regulations. A Physical Asset Practitioner must understand which obligations apply to which assets, maintain a compliance calendar, and ensure that all required submissions are made on time to the relevant authority.

Compliance is not optional: Non-compliance with statutory requirements is not a planning failure — it is a legal exposure. An employer who allows an uncertified hoist to operate in a workshop is in breach of OHSA s8 and the employer's liability is personal, not just organisational. The practitioner's role is to ensure the asset owner understands what is required, when, and what the consequence of non-compliance is — not to assume someone else is managing it.

§2 What Workplace Evidence Is Required

Minimum evidence:

  • One logbook entry describing your contribution to statutory compliance identification or reporting for one or more physical assets
  • A compliance register, inspection certificate, submission record, or correspondence with a regulatory body that you produced or contributed to
  • Supervisor verification of your role

Your entry must describe: the applicable statutory obligation(s), the assets covered, the compliance status you found, any gaps identified, the corrective actions taken, and the current status at the time of submission.

§3 Worked Example Logbook Entry

Actions Taken Before the Inspection

  1. HY-001 load test scheduled with an approved inspection authority (LIFT-SA) — completed 19 March 2026. Certificate issued.
  2. Fire extinguishers serviced by Flame Guard on 15 March 2026. Service tags updated.
  3. First aid kit restocked. Kagiso enrolled in a 2-day first aid course (22–23 March 2026).
  4. All certificates and compliance documents compiled into a single compliance file, organised per asset.

Outcome: The DoL inspection was conducted on 28 March 2026. Two observations noted: (1) JK-001 replacement not yet arrived — the inspector accepted the out-of-service tag and new jack purchase order as satisfactory; (2) HY-002 out-of-service tag acceptable. No prohibition notice was issued. The inspector commended the compliance file as "better organised than most workshops this size."

What I would do differently: The compliance status was unknown before the DoL visit triggered the audit. A simple annual compliance calendar — listing each statutory obligation with the asset, due date, responsible person, and certificate storage location — would have allowed KD Auto to manage compliance proactively rather than reactively. I built this calendar after the inspection and it is now maintained monthly.

Compliance calendar principle: Statutory compliance is not an event — it is a continuous obligation. The two-month overdue load test on HY-001 and the 12-month overdue fire extinguisher service were both known gaps that would have been visible in a compliance calendar. The absence of a calendar meant neither was visible until a regulatory inspection forced the issue. The compliance calendar is a basic asset management tool — not an advanced one.

§5 Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Only citing OHSA s8 (general duty): OHSA s8 is the catch-all. The specific obligations for hoists, pressure vessels, fire extinguishers, and lifting tackle are in subordinate regulations (Driven Machinery Regulations, Pressure Equipment Regulations, etc.). Show that you know the specific requirements for your asset types.
  • No documentation: Statutory compliance is an evidence-based obligation. Claiming compliance without certificates, test records, or service tags is not compliance — it is assertion. Your logbook entry must reference or attach actual documentation.
  • No compliance calendar: Identifying and fixing current gaps is the minimum. Showing that you built a system to prevent future gaps demonstrates the practitioner-level thinking that this module assesses.

§6 Connect to Your Qualification

WE-05 is the real-workplace evidence for PM-05. The statutory reporting skills — identifying obligations, auditing compliance status, compiling documentation, and managing a compliance calendar — are the same. What WE-05 adds is the real consequence dimension: an overdue load test on an operating hoist is not a simulation finding — it is a live safety and legal risk. Your evidence must show that you treated it with the seriousness it deserves.
